Overview

Task 6.1

Develop a strategy to centrally deploy and manage AWS accounts.

The running example has used this structure since Task 1.1; this page explains how it's built and governed. Questions test the edges: which policies affect the management account, what Control Tower does and doesn't do since landing zone 4.0, which service a delegated administrator is registered through, and how to remove root credentials without losing the ability to recover an account. The associate-level account structure and SCP basics are in the Solutions Architect guide.

Running example. The same company: a management account (555555555555) used only for organization tasks; a Security OU with the security tooling account (222222222222) and the log archive account (444444444444); an Infrastructure OU with network, backup and identity accounts; Workloads OUs for prod (111111111111) and non-prod; a Sandbox OU; and procedural OUs for policy staging, exceptions and suspended accounts. The company is moving the organization onto Control Tower.

Status changes. Control Tower landing zone 4.0 (November 2025) made service integrations optional, stopped creating a Security OU and stopped applying mandatory controls by default, and renamed the log archive and audit accounts the CloudTrail administrator and Config aggregator accounts. Organizations created in the console since July 2026 start with SCPs on and a default SCP denying leaving the organization and closing accounts. New organization policy types cover Security Hub, Inspector, Bedrock guardrails, S3 Block Public Access and GuardDuty, and RCPs now support about 60 services.

Deploying and configuring an organization

Skill 6.1.1Deploy and configure organizations by using AWS Organizations.

Features and structure

All features, OUs and the management account

An organization in all features mode has policies, trusted access for AWS services and delegated administrators; consolidated billing mode only shares the bill. Moving to all features needs every invited account to approve. OUs nest up to five levels deep. The management account is the payer, can't be changed, and isn't restricted by SCPs or RCPs, so AWS's advice is to keep workloads and users out of it, limit and review who can reach its root email, password, MFA and phone, and delegate everything possible to member accounts.

Recommended OUs

AWS's multi-account guidance (republished April 2025) groups OUs by purpose:

  • Foundational: Security (log archive and security tooling accounts only; other security accounts go in a separate OU) and Infrastructure (network, identity, backup, operations tooling, shared services).
  • Application: Workloads, split into prod and non-prod.
  • Experimental: Sandbox, isolated from internal networks.
  • Procedural: Policy Staging (test policy changes), Exceptions (accounts needing documented exceptions, with account-level SCPs), Transitional (accounts arriving from acquisitions), Suspended (accounts being retired, locked down by SCP).
  • Advanced: Deployments, Business Continuity and others for larger organizations.

Accounts

Creating, inviting and closing

create-account (management account only, asynchronous) creates a member with an OrganizationAccountAccessRole that the management account can assume with administrator rights; invited accounts don't get that role unless you create it. In an organization governed by Control Tower, create accounts through Account Factory instead, or they aren't enrolled. Closed accounts stay in the organization (suspended) for 90 days, counting toward the account quota, and can be reopened in that time; you can close at most 20% of member accounts (or 250) per 30 days. A registered delegated administrator account can't be removed until it's deregistered.

Create a workload account (AWS CLI, management account) 
aws organizations create-account \
  --email aws-prod-payments@example.com \
  --account-name "prod-payments" \
  --iam-user-access-to-billing DENY
# Returns a request ID; check with: aws organizations describe-create-account-status --create-account-request-id car-...

Trusted access and two kinds of delegation

Trusted access (enable-aws-service-access, best done from the service's own console) lets an AWS service act across the organization. A delegated administrator for a service is a member account that runs that service for everyone (Skill 6.1.4). A delegated administrator for Organizations manages organization policies, granted by a resource-based delegation policy in the management account:

Let the identity account manage SCPs (Organizations delegation policy, applied with put-resource-policy) 
{
  "Version": "2012-10-17",
  "Statement": [
    {
      "Sid": "ManageScpsFromIdentityAccount",
      "Effect": "Allow",
      "Principal": { "AWS": "666600006666" },
      "Action": [
        "organizations:DescribeOrganization",
        "organizations:ListAccounts",
        "organizations:CreatePolicy",
        "organizations:DescribePolicy",
        "organizations:UpdatePolicy",
        "organizations:DeletePolicy",
        "organizations:AttachPolicy",
        "organizations:DetachPolicy"
      ],
      "Resource": [
        "arn:aws:organizations::555555555555:root/o-exampleorgid/r-examplerootid",
        "arn:aws:organizations::555555555555:ou/o-exampleorgid/*",
        "arn:aws:organizations::555555555555:account/o-exampleorgid/*",
        "arn:aws:organizations::555555555555:policy/o-exampleorgid/service_control_policy/*"
      ],
      "Condition": { "StringLikeIfExists": { "organizations:PolicyType": ["SERVICE_CONTROL_POLICY"] } }
    }
  ]
}

Without AttachPolicy the delegate can write policies but not attach them, permission to attach to one OU doesn't extend to its child OUs, and since June 2026 NotAction and NotResource aren't accepted in delegation policies. Creating, inviting, moving and removing accounts can't be delegated this way.

AWS Control Tower

Skill 6.1.2Implement and manage AWS Control Tower in new and existing environments, and deploy optional and custom controls.

The landing zone

What Control Tower sets up

Control Tower runs from the management account (it has no delegated administrator) and builds a landing zone: organization-wide CloudTrail and Config into dedicated accounts, IAM Identity Center, optional AWS Backup, cross-account security roles, and controls on registered OUs. Landing zone 4.0 changed the model:

  • Each service integration (centralizedLogging, config, securityRoles, accessManagement, backup) is optional and switched by an enabled flag in the manifest; a controls-only landing zone is possible.
  • Control Tower no longer creates or requires a Security OU; the integration accounts just need to share an OU directly under the root.
  • The log archive and audit accounts are now the CloudTrail administrator and Config aggregator accounts, with separate buckets for CloudTrail and Config logs.
  • Mandatory controls are no longer applied by default, and drift notifications go to EventBridge in the management account.
Create a 4.0 landing zone with logging, Config and security roles (AWS CLI and manifest, JSON) 
aws controltower create-landing-zone --landing-zone-version 4.0 --manifest file://manifest.json

{
  "governedRegions": ["us-east-1", "us-west-2"],
  "centralizedLogging": {
    "enabled": true,
    "accountId": "444444444444",
    "configurations": {
      "loggingBucket": { "retentionDays": 2557 },
      "accessLoggingBucket": { "retentionDays": 365 },
      "kmsKeyArn": "arn:aws:kms:us-east-1:444444444444:key/1234abcd-12ab-34cd-56ef-1234567890ab"
    }
  },
  "config": {

Enabling and customizing controls

Controls are mandatory (protect Control Tower's own resources), strongly recommended or elective, and the Control Catalog (formerly the Controls Library) lists them all with mappings to frameworks such as PCI DSS. Controls are enabled per OU through the console or API, and some take parameters, such as the configurable Region deny control:

Allow only the company's Regions, exempting a read-only role and a few global actions (AWS CLI, management account) 
aws controltower update-enabled-control \
  --enabled-control-identifier arn:aws:controltower:us-east-1:555555555555:enabledcontrol/EXAMPLE_REGION_DENY \
  --parameters '[{"key": "AllowedRegions", "value": ["us-east-1", "us-west-2"]},
                 {"key": "ExemptedPrincipalArns", "value": ["arn:aws:iam::*:role/ReadOnly"]},
                 {"key": "ExemptedActions", "value": ["cloudwatch:Get*", "cloudwatch:Describe*"]}]'

RCP-based controls can also exempt principals and resources. For your own guardrails, Customizations for Control Tower (CfCT) deploys CloudFormation templates and SCPs or RCPs from a pipeline, and you can now run your own CloudFormation hooks alongside the proactive controls. reset-enabled-control repairs drifted optional controls, but not SCP-based or mandatory ones.

New and existing accounts

Account Factory and enrollment

  • Account Factory creates governed accounts (through Service Catalog); Account Factory Customization applies a blueprint (CloudFormation or Terraform product from a hub account) at creation; Account Factory for Terraform (AFT) provisions and customizes accounts from a Terraform pipeline.
  • Existing accounts can be enrolled if they have the AWSControlTowerExecution role and no existing Config recorder or delivery channel (delete them first), and you should delete duplicate account trails. Existing OUs are registered by enabling the Control Tower baseline on them, which enrolls their accounts.
  • With auto-enrollment (October 2025) turned on, moving an account into a registered OU applies that OU's baselines and controls automatically; without it, moved accounts drift.

An account created directly with Organizations in a Control Tower organization isn't enrolled and gets none of the controls. And with landing zone 4.0, an organization upgraded or created without explicitly enabling the mandatory controls doesn't have them; check before assuming the log buckets are protected.

Organization policies

Skill 6.1.3Implement organization policies to manage permissions (for example, SCPs, RCPs, AI service opt-out policies, declarative policies).

Authorization policies: SCPs and RCPs

How they apply

SCPs cap what principals in member accounts can do; RCPs cap what can be done to resources in member accounts, whoever the caller is (Task 4.2 places them in evaluation). Neither grants anything, neither affects the management account or service-linked roles, and both do apply to member root users and delegated administrator accounts. An action must be allowed at every level from root to account, and a deny anywhere wins. Limits differ: SCPs 10 per target and 10,240 characters; RCPs 5 per target (including the undetachable RCPFullAWSAccess) and 5,120 characters. In SCPs, every statement needs Resource or NotResource, and Allow statements can only use "*".

Organization guardrail: approved Regions only, except global services and the platform role (SCP, shortened from AWS's sample) 
{
  "Version": "2012-10-17",
  "Statement": [
    {
      "Sid": "DenyOutsideApprovedRegions",
      "Effect": "Deny",
      "NotAction": [
        "iam:*", "organizations:*", "sts:*", "account:*", "support:*", "budgets:*", "ce:*",
        "cloudfront:*", "route53:*", "route53domains:*", "globalaccelerator:*", "shield:*",
        "waf:*", "wafv2:*", "health:*", "kms:*", "config:*", "fms:*", "sso:*", "trustedadvisor:*"
      ],
      "Resource": "*",
      "Condition": {
        "StringNotEquals": { "aws:RequestedRegion": ["us-east-1", "us-west-2"] },
        "ArnNotLike": { "aws:PrincipalArn": "arn:aws:iam::*:role/platform-admin" }
      }
    },
    {
      "Sid": "ProtectSecurityServices",
      "Effect": "Deny",
      "Action": [
        "guardduty:DeleteDetector", "guardduty:DisassociateFromMasterAccount", "guardduty:UpdateDetector",
        "securityhub:DisableSecurityHub", "securityhub:DisassociateFromAdministratorAccount",
        "config:StopConfigurationRecorder", "config:DeleteConfigurationRecorder", "config:DeleteDeliveryChannel",
        "cloudtrail:StopLogging", "cloudtrail:DeleteTrail",
        "organizations:LeaveOrganization"
      ],
      "Resource": "*",
      "Condition": { "ArnNotLike": { "aws:PrincipalArn": "arn:aws:iam::*:role/platform-admin" } }
    }
  ]
}

The NotAction list exempts global services that are called through us-east-1. AWS's full sample in aws-samples/service-control-policy-examples has a longer list.

Organization guardrail: no non-TLS access to key data services, whoever calls (RCP) 
{
  "Version": "2012-10-17",
  "Statement": [
    {
      "Sid": "EnforceTls",
      "Effect": "Deny",
      "Principal": "*",
      "Action": ["s3:*", "sts:*", "kms:*", "sqs:*", "secretsmanager:*", "logs:*", "dynamodb:*", "ecr:*"],
      "Resource": "*",
      "Condition": { "BoolIfExists": { "aws:SecureTransport": "false" } }
    }
  ]
}

Allow list or deny list, and testing

The default deny list strategy keeps FullAWSAccess at every level and adds Deny statements; an allow list replaces it with Allow statements for approved services at every level, which is stricter but needs updating as needs change. Disabling the SCP policy type detaches every SCP. Test changes in the Policy Staging OU first:

  1. Test in Policy StagingAttach the policy to the staging OU that mirrors the target, with representative test accounts.
  2. Check impactUse CloudTrail errors and IAM last accessed data for the OU.
  3. Attach to one real accountApply to a single account in the target OU.
  4. Attach to the OUThen remove the account-level attachment.

Declarative and service policies

Policies that set configuration

All other policy types are now grouped as declarative policies. Unlike SCPs and RCPs, they do apply to the management account when attached at the root, and describe-effective-policy shows the merged result for an account.

  • AI services opt-out: stop AWS AI services storing or using content to improve the service; default covers current and future services, and values are case-sensitive (optOut, optIn).
  • EC2 policies (formerly EC2 declarative policies): VPC Block Public Access, AMI and snapshot block public access, Allowed AMIs, IMDS defaults, serial console access and now VPC Encryption Controls, which account administrators then can't change. An optional custom error message points users to internal guidance.
  • S3 policies: organization-wide S3 Block Public Access.
  • Tag policies standardize tag keys and values (Task 6.2); backup policies deploy AWS Backup plans (Task 5.2).
  • Security Hub, Inspector and GuardDuty policies enable those services and their features in chosen Regions for every account; Bedrock policies enforce a guardrail on every model call (Task 3.2).
Opt the whole organization out of AI service data use, and lock it so no OU can change it (AI services opt-out policy, JSON) 
{
  "services": {
    "@@operators_allowed_for_child_policies": ["@@none"],
    "default": {
      "@@operators_allowed_for_child_policies": ["@@none"],
      "opt_out_policy": {
        "@@operators_allowed_for_child_policies": ["@@none"],
        "@@assign": "optOut"
      }
    }
  }
}
Block public sharing of snapshots and AMIs everywhere, with a pointer to internal guidance (EC2 policy, JSON) 
{
  "ec2_attributes": {
    "exception_message": { "@@assign": "Public sharing is blocked by company policy. See https://wiki.example.com/cloud-security" },
    "snapshot_block_public_access": { "state": { "@@assign": "block_all_sharing" } },
    "image_block_public_access": { "state": { "@@assign": "block_new_sharing" } }
  }
}
Turn on all four S3 Block Public Access settings in every account (S3 policy, JSON) 
{
  "s3_attributes": {
    "public_access_block_configuration": { "@@assign": "all" }
  }
}

"Prevent anyone, including administrators in the management account, from creating public snapshots" can't be done with an SCP, which never applies to the management account. An EC2 policy attached at the root does apply there. Conversely, an SCP is the only one of these that limits API actions by principals.

Centrally managing security services

Skill 6.1.4Centrally manage security services (for example, delegated administrator accounts).

The security tooling account

Who delegates what

AWS recommends one security tooling account as the delegated administrator for the detection services, and the per-service setup is in Task 1.1. The points that differ by service:

  • One administrator per organization: GuardDuty, Security Hub CSPM and Macie (keep them in the same account), Detective (in the same account as GuardDuty), IAM Access Analyzer, AWS Account Management. Regional services are delegated in every Region you use.
  • Several administrators: Firewall Manager (a default administrator plus up to 9 with a limited scope of accounts, Regions or policy types), CloudFormation StackSets (up to 5, each with full deployment rights that can't be scoped to OUs), AWS Health (up to 5), Config aggregators.
  • Registered differently: Security Lake registers its administrator through its own API, AWS Config needs both config-multiaccountsetup.amazonaws.com (organization rules and conformance packs) and config.amazonaws.com (aggregation), and Control Tower has no delegated administrator.
Delegate AWS Config to the security tooling account (AWS CLI, management account) 
aws organizations enable-aws-service-access --service-principal config-multiaccountsetup.amazonaws.com
aws organizations enable-aws-service-access --service-principal config.amazonaws.com
aws organizations register-delegated-administrator --service-principal config-multiaccountsetup.amazonaws.com --account-id 222222222222
aws organizations register-delegated-administrator --service-principal config.amazonaws.com --account-id 222222222222

Organization policies for Security Hub, Inspector and GuardDuty now let the administrator enable services by Region for all accounts as policy rather than per-service auto-enable settings; detaching the policy leaves services on but stops automatic enabling for new accounts. The Infrastructure OU accounts take the rest: the identity account for IAM Identity Center, centralized root access and Organizations policy delegation, the backup account for AWS Backup.

"Run GuardDuty, Security Hub and Macie for all accounts from one place other than the management account" points to a delegated administrator in the security tooling account. "Let the network team manage Firewall Manager WAF policies but only for their OU" points to a scoped Firewall Manager administrator.

Root user credentials

Skill 6.1.5Manage AWS account root user credentials (for example, by centralizing root access for member accounts, managing MFA, designing break-glass procedures).

Member accounts: no root credentials at all

Centralized root access

With centralized root access, member accounts don't need root passwords, access keys or MFA devices: the management account or its delegated administrator removes them and performs the few root-only tasks with short AssumeRoot sessions (Task 2.2). New accounts are then created without root credentials. If a member's root user is ever needed, the privileged "Allow password recovery" task lets whoever controls the root email reset it.

Turn on both capabilities and delegate them to the identity account (AWS CLI, management account) 
aws organizations enable-aws-service-access --service-principal iam.amazonaws.com
aws iam enable-organizations-root-credentials-management
aws iam enable-organizations-root-sessions
aws organizations register-delegated-administrator --service-principal iam.amazonaws.com --account-id 666600006666

If member root credentials remain, an SCP can deny root actions while still allowing centralized AssumeRoot sessions, which carry the aws:AssumedRoot key:

No direct use of member root users; centralized root sessions still work (SCP, from AWS's sample) 
{
  "Version": "2012-10-17",
  "Statement": [
    {
      "Sid": "DenyDirectRootUse",
      "Effect": "Deny",
      "Action": "*",
      "Resource": "*",
      "Condition": {
        "ArnLike": { "aws:PrincipalArn": ["arn:aws:iam::*:root"] },
        "Null": { "aws:AssumedRoot": "true" }
      }
    }
  ]
}

The management account root

MFA, ownership and the remaining root tasks

MFA is now required for the root user of every account type, within 14 days of first sign-in. Register several devices (up to 8), preferring FIDO2 security keys or passkeys, and split control: one group holds the password, another the MFA devices, with the root email and phone owned by separate groups too. Use a group mailbox used for nothing else, never create root access keys, and keep the password out of any tool that depends on the same account. Root is still needed for a few tasks, such as changing some account and billing settings, configuring S3 MFA delete, and fixing a bucket or queue policy that denies everyone (now also possible centrally for members).

Break-glass access

Break-glass access is the planned way in when normal access fails (the identity provider is down, Identity Center is misconfigured, an SCP locks out administrators). AWS's guidance gives the elements; the design below combines them:

  1. Prepare the pathA small number of emergency roles or users (or direct SAML federation to a few accounts, as in Identity Center's emergency access guidance), outside the normal sign-in path.
  2. Seal the credentialsStrong MFA, credentials held under multi-person control in a vault or safe.
  3. Alert on every useEventBridge rules on root and break-glass sign-ins page the security team.
  4. Test and reviewExercise the procedure regularly, rotate after every use, review who holds what.
Alert on any root user sign-in or API call (EventBridge event pattern, from AWS's root activity monitor sample) 
{
  "detail-type": ["AWS API Call via CloudTrail", "AWS Console Sign In via CloudTrail"],
  "detail": {
    "userIdentity": { "type": ["Root"] }
  }
}

Root console sign-in events can be recorded in us-east-1, us-east-2 or us-west-2, so the rule must exist in all three (or alert from the organization trail with a metric filter, Task 1.1). GuardDuty also reports root credential use (Policy:IAMUser/RootCredentialUsage).

Security contacts

AWS sends security notifications to each account's security alternate contact. With trusted access for AWS Account Management, the management account (or its one delegated administrator) sets it for every member:

Point every account's security contact at the security team (AWS CLI, run per member account) 
aws account put-alternate-contact --account-id 111111111111 \
  --alternate-contact-type SECURITY \
  --email-address security-team@example.com \
  --name "Security Operations" \
  --phone-number "+1-206-555-0100" \
  --title "Security"

Removing member root credentials is only safe if the privileged root actions capability is also on, so the organization can still recover root or fix a locked bucket policy. And centralized root access doesn't apply to the management account itself: its root user still needs MFA, multi-person control and alerting.

Key takeaways

  • All features mode for policies and delegation; keep the management account empty; OUs by purpose (Security, Infrastructure, Workloads, Sandbox, Policy Staging, Exceptions, Transitional, Suspended); console-created organizations now start with a deny-leave and deny-close SCP.
  • OrganizationAccountAccessRole only in created accounts; closed accounts linger 90 days; Organizations delegation policies let a member manage policies (attach needs its own permission; no NotAction).
  • Control Tower (no delegated admin): landing zone 4.0 with optional integrations, no required Security OU, CloudTrail administrator and Config aggregator accounts, mandatory controls not applied by default, drift to EventBridge.
  • Controls: preventive (SCP, RCP, declarative), detective (Config), proactive (CloudFormation hooks); mandatory, strongly recommended, elective; Control Catalog; parameters on Region deny; CfCT and custom hooks; enroll accounts with AWSControlTowerExecution and no existing Config recorder; auto-enrollment on OU moves.
  • SCPs and RCPs never affect the management account or service-linked roles; SCP 10 per target and 10,240 characters, RCP 5 and 5,120; allow at every level, deny anywhere wins; test through Policy Staging.
  • Declarative policies (AI opt-out, EC2, S3, tag, backup, Security Hub, Inspector, GuardDuty, Bedrock) do apply to the management account and have effective policy views.
  • Delegated administrators: one for GuardDuty, Security Hub CSPM, Macie, Access Analyzer, Account Management; several for Firewall Manager (scoped), StackSets (unscoped) and Health; two principals for Config; Security Lake through its own API.
  • Root: centralized root access removes member credentials (keep privileged root actions on), SCP with aws:AssumedRoot, root MFA required with FIDO keys and split custody, break-glass with sealed credentials and alerts, root events in three Regions, security alternate contacts set centrally.

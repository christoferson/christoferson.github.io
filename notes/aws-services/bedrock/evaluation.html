Model and RAG evaluation

Measure before you ship: Amazon Bedrock Evaluations runs jobs over your own prompt dataset that score a model, a prompt or a Knowledge Base with algorithms, an LLM judge or human reviewers, so you can pick models, compare prompt versions and catch RAG regressions with numbers instead of impressions.

Which evaluation to use

All job types use one API, CreateEvaluationJob on the bedrock control-plane client, with applicationType set to ModelEvaluation or RagEvaluation. Each job reads a JSONL dataset from S3 and writes scores to S3.

  • Automatic (programmatic): fixed algorithms score accuracy, robustness and toxicity for a task type (generation, summarization, question answering, classification), on AWS's built-in datasets or yours. Cheap and repeatable, but blunt: good for comparing raw models on standard tasks.
  • LLM-as-a-judge: a judge model rates each answer for quality (correctness, completeness, faithfulness, helpfulness and more) and responsible AI (harmfulness, stereotyping, refusal), with an explanation. Add custom metrics (your own judge prompt and rating scale) for things like brand voice. The usual choice for prompts and applications.
  • Human: your own work team (up to 50 workers) rates or compares the answers of up to two models with thumbs up or down, Likert scales or rankings. For subjective quality and for checking that the judge agrees with people.
  • RAG evaluation: scores a Knowledge Base on retrieval only (are the retrieved passages relevant and do they cover the answer) or on retrieve and generate (is the final answer correct, complete, faithful to the passages and properly cited).
  • Bring your own responses: for model, human and RAG jobs you can supply responses (and retrieved passages and citations) produced anywhere, by another provider or your own RAG pipeline, and Bedrock only scores them.

Metrics

LLM-as-a-judge (model evaluation)

Builtin.Correctness, Builtin.Completeness, Builtin.Faithfulness (no information beyond what's in or inferable from the prompt), Builtin.Helpfulness, Builtin.Coherence, Builtin.Relevance, Builtin.FollowingInstructions, Builtin.ProfessionalStyleAndTone, and the responsible-AI metrics Builtin.Harmfulness, Builtin.Stereotyping and Builtin.Refusal. A reference answer is optional; Correctness and Completeness use it when present.

RAG evaluation

  • Retrieve only: Builtin.ContextRelevance and Builtin.ContextCoverage (needs reference answers).
  • Retrieve and generate: Builtin.Correctness, Builtin.Completeness, Builtin.Helpfulness, Builtin.LogicalCoherence, Builtin.Faithfulness (compared with the retrieved passages, so it detects hallucination), Builtin.CitationPrecision and Builtin.CitationCoverage (use the two together), Builtin.Harmfulness, Builtin.Stereotyping and Builtin.Refusal.

Automatic (programmatic)

Builtin.Accuracy, Builtin.Robustness and Builtin.Toxicity, computed differently per task: real-world-knowledge score for generation, BERTScore for summarization, F1 for question answering, classification accuracy for classification (no toxicity there). Robustness re-runs each prompt with about five small perturbations (typos, case, whitespace, numbers as words), which multiplies inference cost. Built-in datasets (T-REx, BOLD, WikiText-2, RealToxicityPrompts, Gigaword, BoolQ, Natural Questions, TriviaQA, women's clothing reviews) are sampled to 100 prompts. Cohere models can't complete toxicity evaluation.

Reading scores

  • The judge rates each answer on a 2- to 7-point scale, normalized to 0 to 1; reports show the average and a histogram per metric.
  • For Harmfulness, Stereotyping and Refusal a higher score is worse; for programmatic robustness and toxicity, lower is better. Everything else: higher is better.
  • The judge prompts and rubrics are published in the documentation for each judge model, so you can see exactly what "correct" means.

Judge models

About 22 judges are supported, including Amazon Nova Pro, Nova Premier, Nova Micro and Nova 2 Lite, Claude models from 3.5 Sonnet to Sonnet 4.6 and Opus 4.8, OpenAI GPT-5.4 and GPT-5.5, Llama 3.1 70B and Mistral Large (the RAG list currently omits Claude Sonnet 4.5). One judge per job for built-in metrics, and one (which can differ) for custom metrics. The generator, the judge and the S3 data must be in the job's Region, and judges vary by Region.

The coherence metric is Builtin.Coherence in model evaluation but Builtin.LogicalCoherence in RAG evaluation. And the judge is tuned for English: scores on other languages are less reliable.

Datasets

JSONL files in S3 (one JSON object per line), up to 1,000 prompts per dataset and 4 KB per prompt, up to 5 datasets per job. An optional category gives per-segment scores.

Model evaluation, Bedrock calls the model (one line of the .jsonl file) 
{"prompt": "How do I reset my router?", "referenceResponse": "Hold the reset button for 10 seconds, then wait for the lights to settle.", "category": "devices"}
Model evaluation with your own responses (one modelIdentifier across the whole file) 
{"prompt": "How do I reset my router?", "referenceResponse": "Hold the reset button for 10 seconds.", "modelResponses": [{"response": "Press and hold reset for ten seconds.", "modelIdentifier": "support_bot_v2"}]}
RAG evaluation, Bedrock calls the Knowledge Base (retrieve-and-generate allows up to 5 turns; retrieve-only 1) 
{"conversationTurns": [{"prompt": {"content": [{"text": "What is the refund window for online orders?"}]}, "referenceResponses": [{"content": [{"text": "30 days from delivery."}]}]}]}
RAG retrieve-and-generate with your own pipeline's output, passages and citations 
{"conversationTurns": [{"prompt": {"content": [{"text": "What is the refund window?"}]}, "referenceResponses": [{"content": [{"text": "30 days from delivery."}]}], "output": {"text": "You can return items within 30 days of delivery.", "knowledgeBaseIdentifier": "my_rag_source", "retrievedPassages": {"retrievalResults": [{"content": {"text": "Refunds are accepted within 30 days of delivery."}, "metadata": {"source": "policy.pdf"}}]}, "citations": [{"generatedResponsePart": {"textResponsePart": {"span": {"start": 0, "end": 48}, "text": "You can return items within 30 days of delivery."}}, "retrievedReferences": [{"content": {"text": "Refunds are accepted within 30 days of delivery."}, "metadata": {"source": "policy.pdf"}}]}]}}]}
  • The reference for RAG is the expected answer, not the expected chunks.
  • Bring-your-own RAG output uses retrievedResults for retrieve-only jobs but retrievedPassages for retrieve-and-generate; knowledgeBaseIdentifier must be the same on every line and match the job's source identifier. Without citations, don't select the citation metrics.

A model evaluation job

LLM-as-a-judge with built-in metrics and one custom metric (Python, boto3; field names from the API reference) 
import json
import time

import boto3

bedrock = boto3.client("bedrock", region_name="us-east-1")

resp = bedrock.create_evaluation_job(
    # Lowercase letters, digits and hyphens only
    jobName="support-bot-judge-2026-11-09",
    roleArn="arn:aws:iam::111111111111:role/BedrockEvalRole",
    applicationType="ModelEvaluation",
    evaluationConfig={"automated": {
        "datasetMetricConfigs": [{
            # As in AWS's judge examples
            "taskType": "General",
            "dataset": {
                "name": "support-golden-v3",
                "datasetLocation": {"s3Uri": "s3://eval-111111111111/datasets/support-golden-v3.jsonl"},
            },
            "metricNames": ["Builtin.Correctness", "Builtin.Faithfulness", "Builtin.Harmfulness", "brand-voice"],
        }],
        # Judge for the built-in metrics (exactly one)
        "evaluatorModelConfig": {"bedrockEvaluatorModels": [{"modelIdentifier": "amazon.nova-pro-v1:0"}]},
        "customMetricConfig": {
            "customMetrics": [{"customMetricDefinition": {
                "name": "brand-voice",
                "instructions": (
                    "You are a brand editor checking support replies against our voice: friendly, plain words, "
                    "no jargon. Rate the response.\nPrompt: {{prompt}}\nResponse: {{prediction}}"
                ),
                "ratingScale": [
                    {"definition": "Off brand", "value": {"floatValue": 0}},
                    {"definition": "Partly on brand", "value": {"floatValue": 1}},
                    {"definition": "On brand", "value": {"floatValue": 2}},
                ],
            }}],
            # Judge for custom metrics (exactly one; can differ)
            "evaluatorModelConfig": {"bedrockEvaluatorModels": [{"modelIdentifier": "amazon.nova-premier-v1:0"}]},
        },
    }},
    # The model being evaluated (one per automated job)
    inferenceConfig={"models": [{"bedrockModel": {
        "modelIdentifier": "amazon.nova-2-lite-v1:0",
        "inferenceParams": json.dumps({"inferenceConfig": {"maxTokens": 512, "temperature": 0}}),
    }}]},
    outputDataConfig={"s3Uri": "s3://eval-111111111111/results/"},
    jobTags=[{"key": "project", "value": "support-bot"}],
)
arn = resp["jobArn"]
while (status := bedrock.get_evaluation_job(jobIdentifier=arn)["status"]) in ("InProgress", "Stopping"):
    time.sleep(60)
print(status)
  • To score your own responses instead of calling a model, use inferenceConfig={"models": [{"precomputedInferenceSource": {"inferenceSourceIdentifier": "support_bot_v2"}}]}, matching the dataset's modelIdentifier.
  • The model can be a foundation model, an inference profile (including application profiles), a custom, distilled or imported model, a prompt router, Provisioned Throughput or a Marketplace model; Responses API models on bedrock-mantle are API and CLI only.
  • Custom metrics: up to 10 per job. Variables ({{prompt}}, {{prediction}}, {{ground_truth}}, and for RAG {{context}}) go at the end of the instructions (up to 5,000 characters); define the rating scale separately, not in the prompt. A custom metric without a scale returns explanations only, no scores.
  • Status values: InProgress, Completed, Failed (see failureMessages), Stopping, Stopped, Deleting. Stop with stop_evaluation_job; delete old jobs with batch_delete_evaluation_job (up to 25; results in S3 stay).

jobName allows only lowercase letters, digits and hyphens (up to 63 characters), so names such as my_rag_eval in some AWS examples fail. The tags parameter is jobTags, not tags. An automated job evaluates one model: to compare models, run one job per model with the same dataset and judge, then compare.

A RAG evaluation job

Retrieve-and-generate evaluation of a Knowledge Base with hybrid search (Python, boto3) 
resp = bedrock.create_evaluation_job(
    jobName="kb-hybrid-top10-rag-eval",
    roleArn="arn:aws:iam::111111111111:role/BedrockRagEvalRole",
    applicationType="RagEvaluation",
    evaluationConfig={"automated": {
        "datasetMetricConfigs": [{
            # Ignored for RAG jobs
            "taskType": "General",
            "dataset": {
                "name": "kb-questions",
                "datasetLocation": {"s3Uri": "s3://eval-111111111111/datasets/kb-questions.jsonl"},
            },
            "metricNames": [
                "Builtin.Correctness", "Builtin.Completeness", "Builtin.Faithfulness",
                "Builtin.CitationPrecision", "Builtin.CitationCoverage",
            ],
        }],
        "evaluatorModelConfig": {"bedrockEvaluatorModels": [{"modelIdentifier": "amazon.nova-pro-v1:0"}]},
    }},
    inferenceConfig={"ragConfigs": [{"knowledgeBaseConfig": {"retrieveAndGenerateConfig": {
        "type": "KNOWLEDGE_BASE",
        "knowledgeBaseConfiguration": {
            "knowledgeBaseId": "KB_ID",
            "modelArn": "arn:aws:bedrock:us-east-1::foundation-model/amazon.nova-pro-v1:0",
            "retrievalConfiguration": {"vectorSearchConfiguration": {
                "numberOfResults": 10,
                "overrideSearchType": "HYBRID",
            }},
        },
    }}}]},
    outputDataConfig={"s3Uri": "s3://eval-111111111111/rag-results/"},
)
  • Retrieve only: use {"knowledgeBaseConfig": {"retrieveConfig": {"knowledgeBaseId": "KB_ID", "knowledgeBaseRetrievalConfiguration": {"vectorSearchConfiguration": {"numberOfResults": 5}}}}} with Builtin.ContextRelevance and Builtin.ContextCoverage. This isolates retrieval problems (chunking, embeddings, search type, number of results) from generation problems.
  • Your own RAG pipeline: use {"precomputedRagSourceConfig": {"retrieveAndGenerateSourceConfig": {"ragSourceIdentifier": "my_rag_source"}}} (or retrieveSourceConfig) with a bring-your-own dataset.
  • Change one setting per job (search type, number of results, chunking, generator model, prompt) and compare: that's how you find what actually moves the scores.

According to an official AWS sample, CreateEvaluationJob can't evaluate the new Managed Knowledge Bases directly (their retrieval configuration isn't accepted). Evaluate them by exporting answers and passages as a bring-your-own dataset, with AgentCore Evaluations on agent traces, or with an open-source framework such as RAGAS.

Permissions

Service role trust policy (as in AWS's evaluation service role pages) 
{
  "Version": "2012-10-17",
  "Statement": [{
    "Effect": "Allow",
    "Principal": {"Service": "bedrock.amazonaws.com"},
    "Action": "sts:AssumeRole",
    "Condition": {
      "StringEquals": {"aws:SourceAccount": "111111111111"},
      "ArnEquals": {"aws:SourceArn": "arn:aws:bedrock:us-east-1:111111111111:evaluation-job/*"}
    }
  }]
}
  • The role needs bedrock:InvokeModel plus bedrock:CreateModelInvocationJob and bedrock:StopModelInvocationJob (jobs run as batch inference) on both the generator and the judge (foundation models, inference profiles, provisioned and imported models as used).
  • S3: read the dataset (s3:GetObject, s3:ListBucket) and write results (s3:PutObject, s3:GetBucketLocation and multipart actions).
  • RAG jobs add bedrock:Retrieve and bedrock:RetrieveAndGenerate on the knowledge base ARN.
  • The caller needs bedrock:CreateEvaluationJob and iam:PassRole to bedrock.amazonaws.com. With a customer managed KMS key (customerEncryptionKeyId), the key policy allows Bedrock to decrypt and generate data keys for evaluation jobs.
  • Human jobs also need a SageMaker flow definition and work team, and CORS on the output bucket when created from the console.

Results and comparisons

  • The console report card (under Inference and assessment, Evaluations) shows each metric's average and histogram, and each prompt's response, reference and scores; hover a score for the judge's explanation. The console shows explanations for only the first 5 prompts: the full results are in S3.
  • Results are JSONL under s3://OUTPUT/JOB_NAME/JOB_UUID/models/MODEL/taskTypes/TASK/datasets/DATASET/, each line holding the input record, the model response and automatedEvaluationResult.scores (metricName and result). Judge answers the service can't parse are kept in S3 but left out of averages.
  • Compare in the console takes jobs that used the same metrics and shows a radar chart and score differences, for models or for RAG configurations.
  • Failed inferences are excluded from metrics: compare "prompts with responses" with the total before trusting an average.

Designing good evaluations

  • Golden dataset: real user questions plus edge cases (ambiguous, out of scope, adversarial), with reference answers where you can write them, and categories for the segments you care about. Over 1,000 prompts, sample by category.
  • Keep the judge fixed: use the same judge model and settings across every job you compare, set the generator's temperature to 0, and record the configuration. AWS requires a separate evaluator model from the generator; whether the same model may play both roles isn't stated, so keep them different.
  • Calibrate: have people rate a sample (a human job) and check the judge agrees before relying on it.
  • Quality gates in CI: there's no built-in pass or fail. Start a job, poll until complete, read the S3 results, and fail the build when an average drops below your threshold (for example faithfulness under 0.9 or harmfulness above 0.05). Watch the 20 concurrent-job limit and delete old jobs.
  • Prompt versions: render each Prompt management version into the dataset (or generate responses with each version and bring them), run one job per version with the same judge, and compare.
  • SageMaker Clarify's foundation model evaluation is closed to new customers; AWS names Bedrock Evaluations and Guardrails as its replacement.

Quotas, pricing and Regions

  • Quotas (not adjustable): 20 concurrent automatic jobs and 10 concurrent human jobs per account and Region, 5,000 jobs in total, 5 datasets and 1 model per automated job (2 for human jobs), 1,000 prompts per dataset, 4 KB per prompt, 10 custom metrics. The quotas page also lists 3 metrics per dataset for automated jobs, while AWS's judge and RAG examples use more, so test your metric count.
  • Pricing: no evaluation fee. You pay the generator's inference and the judge's tokens at on-demand Standard prices (the judge prompts themselves count as input), plus normal Knowledge Base charges for RAG jobs; algorithmic scores are free; human jobs add $0.21 per completed task. Robustness multiplies generator calls.
  • Regions: launched in us-east-1 and us-west-2 and has since expanded (including GovCloud, Seoul and Zurich, and judge and RAG evaluation in most major Regions); judge models vary by Region, so check the model list for yours.

What's changed

  • 2025 to 2026: newer judge models added to the documentation (Nova 2 Lite, Claude Haiku 4.5, Sonnet 4.5 and 4.6, Opus 4.5 to 4.8, GPT-5.4 and GPT-5.5) and Responses API models as generators, without separate announcements; no other new Bedrock Evaluations features announced since April 2025. Agent evaluation arrived separately in AgentCore Evaluations (generally available 31 March 2026).
  • 17 April 2025: custom metrics for model and RAG evaluation.
  • 20 March 2025: LLM-as-a-judge and RAG evaluation generally available, with bring-your-own responses and citation metrics.
  • 1 December 2024: LLM-as-a-judge and RAG evaluation preview; comparing jobs.
  • September to October 2024: evaluating inference profiles, custom models and imported models.
  • 23 April 2024: model evaluation (automatic and human) generally available, after a preview from November 2023.

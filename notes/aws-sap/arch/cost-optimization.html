Reference architecture

Cost optimization at scale

One cost programme for dozens of accounts: every dollar lands on a business unit's report, commitments are bought once and shared on purpose, waste is found by tools rather than by audits, and the expensive network and storage paths are designed out instead of explained afterwards.

Scenario and requirements

The company's bill grew with its account count. Finance gets one consolidated invoice from the management account of organization o-a1b2c3d4e5, but can't say what the retail or payments business unit costs, who owns the shared network, or whether the Savings Plans bought last year still fit. Task 3.3 already set the target: each business unit sees its own spend, including its share of the shared-network account 666666666666, enterprise support and the central GenAI account 222222222222, and the CFO wants warning before a team's spend jumps. Basics are in the SAA cost notes for storage, compute, databases and networks, and SOA-C03 Task 1.3 covers Compute Optimizer and EBS tuning. This page runs it as one programme.

The requirements:

  • Visibility first: every cost is allocated to a business unit, with line-item data to query.
  • Guardrails, not tickets: untagged resources are blocked at creation, and a dev OU that overspends is stopped automatically.
  • Commitments that fit: Savings Plans and RIs bought centrally for the steady baseline, with coverage and utilization tracked.
  • Less waste: rightsizing first, Graviton and Spot where possible, dev off at night.
  • Cheaper paths: data transfer and storage designed for cost (Task 3.2, Task 3.4).
  • Fair chargeback: amortized commitment cost and shared costs reach the units that benefit.

Reused from the SAP task pages: the tag keys CostCenter, BusinessUnit and Environment, the CostCenter values BU-RETAIL, BU-PAYMENTS and BU-PLATFORM, the tag policy and SCP, and the BusinessUnit cost category with its proportional split of SharedNetwork (Task 3.3); the web-tier Auto Scaling group, the image-workers service on Fargate Spot and the dev-web schedules (Task 3.1); the dev OU ou-ab12-dev00001; the retail development account 121212121212 (data-centre migration). New on this page:

  • Management account 999999999999 (the payer; not used elsewhere in the SAP notes), with the export bucket example-cur-999999999999 and the CUR 2.0 export org-cur2-daily.
  • Budget controls: budget dev-ou-monthly, the SCP p-denyruninst (deny new EC2 launches) and the role BudgetsActionRole in 999999999999.
  • Sharing groups: cost category CommitmentGroup, with groups Retail and Payments built from account lists.

Status changes to know. The legacy Cost and Usage Report is now a "legacy data export"; CUR 2.0 in AWS Data Exports is the recommended format, alongside FOCUS exports. Cost Anomaly Detection's custom monitors are now customer managed monitors, next to new AWS managed monitors, and ThresholdExpression replaces Threshold. New since the older blog posts: Database Savings Plans (December 2025), RI and Savings Plans group sharing (November 2025), account tags and IAM principal attribution for allocation, and billing views and billing transfer.

How it works

A month in the life of the programme. Steps marked AWS happen automatically.

  1. Measure
  2. Tag at creationWorkload teamsStacks carry the tag set; the SCP and tag policy reject missing or misspelled tags.
  3. AllocateAWSActivated cost allocation tags, account tags and the BusinessUnit cost category label every line item; shared-network costs are split proportionally across the units.
  4. Export line itemsAWSData Exports writes CUR 2.0 as Parquet to example-cur-999999999999, with resource IDs and container split cost.
  5. Watch
  6. AlertAWSCost Anomaly Detection flags unexpected increases per account and per cost category value; budgets alert on forecast spend and, for the dev OU, attach a deny SCP when actual spend crosses the limit.
  7. Optimize
  8. Rightsize, then commitFinOps teamCompute Optimizer recommendations first; then Savings Plans and RIs in the management account for the new baseline.
  9. Redesign the expensive pathsPlatform teamGraviton, Spot and schedules for compute; endpoints and AZ-local paths for the network; Intelligent-Tiering, gp3 and the right Aurora configuration for storage.
  10. Charge back
  11. Report and billFinanceAthena queries over CUR 2.0 produce amortized cost per business unit; units see their own numbers in billing views and Cost Explorer.

Build it

Step 1

Accounts, tags and enforcement

Accounts are the strongest boundary. An account per workload and environment allocates everything in it exactly, including charges that can't be tagged, such as support and data transfer on shared paths. Account tags on the Organizations accounts (for example BusinessUnit=retail on 121212121212) now allocate all usage in the account, credits and refunds included (Task 3.3). Tags and cost categories fill the gaps: shared accounts and the shared EKS cluster.

A small, fixed tag set, activated once. Tags appear in billing only after the management account activates them as cost allocation tags, within 24 hours, and aren't retroactive unless a backfill (up to 12 months) is requested.

Management account 999999999999: activate the three keys for cost allocation (AWS CLI) 
aws ce update-cost-allocation-tags-status \
  --cost-allocation-tags-status \
    TagKey=CostCenter,Status=Active \
    TagKey=BusinessUnit,Status=Active \
    TagKey=Environment,Status=Active

Enforcement in layers (Task 3.3, Task 2.3):

  • Tag policy: one spelling of CostCenter and its allowed values, with enforced_for blocking noncompliant values on listed resource types. It doesn't require the tag to exist; report_required_tag_for marks keys as required for IaC checks.
  • SCP: deny ec2:RunInstances when aws:RequestTag/CostCenter is null. One required key per Deny statement, and only for actions that support tag-on-create.
  • Proactive and detective: CloudFormation Guard rules or hooks fail a deployment without the tags; AWS Config required-tags in a conformance pack finds existing resources missing up to six tags.
Tag policy attached at the organization root: one spelling, three allowed values, enforced on EC2 instances (from Task 3.3) 
{
  "tags": {
    "costcenter": {
      "tag_key": { "@@assign": "CostCenter" },
      "tag_value": { "@@assign": ["BU-RETAIL", "BU-PAYMENTS", "BU-PLATFORM"] },
      "enforced_for": { "@@assign": ["ec2:instance"] }
    }
  }
}

The organization-wide tag compliance report runs from the management account in us-east-1, refreshes every 48 hours, and doesn't list untagged resources as noncompliant. "Tag compliance looks fine but costs are unallocated" usually means resources without the key at all; the SCP and Config rule catch those, the tag policy doesn't.

Step 2

Cost categories and CUR 2.0

Cost categories map spend to business values. The BusinessUnit category from Task 3.3 puts the shared-network account 666666666666 into SharedNetwork, the CostCenter tag values into Retail and Payments, and everything else into Unallocated. Rules are first match, so shared-cost rules go at the top; categories are created only in the management account, apply within 24 hours from the start of the month, and can be backdated up to 12 months. The split charge rule spreads SharedNetwork proportionally, but its results appear only on the category's details page and CSV, not in CUR, Cost Explorer or Budgets.

CUR 2.0 for line items. CUR 2.0 has a fixed schema, and from the management account it covers every member account. Table configurations add resource IDs and split cost allocation data, which breaks shared EC2 instances behind ECS, EKS or Batch into per-task or per-pod cost (enable it in Cost Management preferences first; it isn't in Cost Explorer).

Management account 999999999999: a daily-granularity CUR 2.0 export in Parquet for Athena, with resource IDs and split cost data (aws bcm-data-exports create-export --export file://export.json) 
{
  "Name": "org-cur2-daily",
  "Description": "Organization line items for chargeback",
  "DataQuery": {
    "QueryStatement": "SELECT line_item_usage_account_id, line_item_usage_account_name, line_item_product_code, line_item_usage_type, line_item_line_item_type, line_item_resource_id, line_item_usage_start_date, line_item_unblended_cost, tags FROM COST_AND_USAGE_REPORT",
    "TableConfigurations": {
      "COST_AND_USAGE_REPORT": {
        "TIME_GRANULARITY": "DAILY",
        "INCLUDE_RESOURCES": "TRUE",
        "INCLUDE_SPLIT_COST_ALLOCATION_DATA": "TRUE"
      }
    }
  },
  "DestinationConfigurations": {
    "S3Destination": {
      "S3Bucket": "example-cur-999999999999",
      "S3Prefix": "cur2",
      "S3Region": "us-east-1",
      "S3OutputConfigurations": {
        "OutputType": "CUSTOM",
        "Format": "PARQUET",
        "Compression": "PARQUET",
        "Overwrite": "OVERWRITE_REPORT"
      }
    }
  },
  "RefreshCadence": { "Frequency": "SYNCHRONOUS" }
}

How it reads: COST_AND_USAGE_REPORT is the SQL table name of CUR 2.0. INCLUDE_RESOURCES adds line_item_resource_id and multiplies the row count. The tags map carries resource tags, account tags, cost categories, user attributes and IAM principals under prefixes such as costCategory/ and accountTag/; add the split_line_item_ columns to the SELECT to export the container split. SYNCHRONOUS refreshes up to three times a day.

Who sees what. Units get no access to the management account: billing views show each unit only its own data in Cost Explorer and Budgets, and filtered exports (a WHERE on the unit's accounts) give it its own line items.

Step 3

Budgets, actions and anomalies

Budgets for planned limits. Each business unit gets a monthly cost budget filtered by its cost category value, alerting at 80% of forecast and 100% of actual to the unit's SNS topic. Budgets update up to three times a day, so they aren't real-time limits.

Budget actions for the dev OU. A budget action can apply an IAM policy or an SCP, or stop EC2 and RDS instances in the same account, automatically or after approval. Stopping instances only works in the budget's own account, so for dev accounts across the OU the management account attaches a deny SCP instead.

Management account 999999999999: when the dev OU's actual spend reaches 100% of its budget, attach an SCP that denies new instances, after manual approval (AWS CLI; the budget dev-ou-monthly exists already) 
aws budgets create-budget-action \
  --account-id 999999999999 \
  --budget-name dev-ou-monthly \
  --notification-type ACTUAL \
  --action-type APPLY_SCP_POLICY \
  --action-threshold ActionThresholdValue=100,ActionThresholdType=PERCENTAGE \
  --definition '{"ScpActionDefinition":{"PolicyId":"p-denyruninst","TargetIds":["ou-ab12-dev00001"]}}' \
  --execution-role-arn arn:aws:iam::999999999999:role/BudgetsActionRole \
  --approval-model MANUAL \
  --subscribers SubscriptionType=EMAIL,Address=finops@example.com

MANUAL means FinOps approves first; AUTOMATIC suits sandbox OUs. The execution role must be in the action's account. The SCP stops new launches, not running ones, so pair it with the Step 5 schedules.

Cost Anomaly Detection for surprises. AWS managed monitors on LINKED_ACCOUNT and on the BusinessUnit cost category watch each value separately (up to 5,000) and pick up new values automatically; only the management account can create them. Subscriptions use ThresholdExpression with absolute and percentage impact (the Task 3.3 example: at least $500 and 20%). Immediate alerts go to SNS only; email is daily or weekly. One threshold applies to every value, so route per unit with EventBridge or User Notifications filters. A new service needs about 10 days of history, alerts can lag up to 24 hours, and support and most Marketplace charges aren't monitored.

Billing data arrives hours late. For the central GenAI account, alarm on Bedrock token metrics in CloudWatch to catch a runaway agent loop quickly; the attribution pattern (application inference profiles, IAM principal data in CUR 2.0) is on the GenAI platform page.

Step 4

Commitments bought once, shared on purpose

Which commitment for which spend (Task 3.1, Task 3.4):

  • Compute Savings Plans (up to 66% off) follow the workload across instance families, Regions, Fargate and Lambda.
  • EC2 Instance Savings Plans (up to 72%) lock to one family in one Region.
  • Database Savings Plans (up to 35%, 1-year No Upfront only) cover Aurora, RDS, DynamoDB, ElastiCache and others across engines, for newer generations and serverless usage; they don't stack with RIs on the same usage.
  • Reserved Instances and reserved nodes remain for RDS and Aurora 3-year terms and older generations, ElastiCache, OpenSearch and Redshift. RDS RIs are size-flexible within a family (not SQL Server or Oracle License Included).
  • Spot never receives Savings Plans discounts, so Spot-heavy fleets lower the baseline to commit to.

Buy centrally, from data. Commit to the lowest steady hourly spend, buy small amounts often, and buy in an account with no usage, such as the management account, so the discount flows to wherever usage is. The PAYER account scope gives the organization-level recommendation, which differs from member-account views.

Management account: 1-year Compute Savings Plan recommendation for the whole organization (from Task 3.1) 
aws ce get-savings-plans-purchase-recommendation \
  --savings-plans-type COMPUTE_SP \
  --term-in-years ONE_YEAR \
  --payment-option NO_UPFRONT \
  --lookback-period-in-days THIRTY_DAYS \
  --account-scope PAYER
# The recommended commitment is the Savings Plans hourly rate, not On-Demand spend

Coverage and utilization targets. Utilization (commitment used) should stay near full, since unused hours are paid anyway. Coverage (eligible spend covered) stays deliberately below 100%, leaving peaks and workloads about to change uncommitted. Model purchases against a coverage target in the Savings Plans Purchase Analyzer, and track both in budgets.

Management account: last month's Savings Plans coverage per member account (AWS CLI; granularity can't be combined with group-by) 
aws ce get-savings-plans-coverage \
  --time-period Start=2026-10-01,End=2026-11-01 \
  --group-by Type=DIMENSION,Key=LINKED_ACCOUNT

Discount sharing preferences. By default commitments benefit the owner account first and then any account in the organization. The management account can deactivate sharing per account, or since November 2025 use group sharing with a cost category built only from the Accounts dimension:

  • Prioritized: owner first, then its group, then the rest of the organization.
  • Restricted: shared only within the group, even if capacity is unused, so utilization can fall.

Group rules: each account belongs to one group, the payer account can't be in a group, both the purchasing and receiving accounts must have sharing active, and the final bill uses the preferences set at 23:59:59 UTC on the last day of the month. Central purchases stay in the management account; Prioritized groups Retail and Payments (category CommitmentGroup) cover commitments a unit buys itself.

"An account's bill rose after a security change in the management account" is often discount sharing switched off. Savings Plans can't be cancelled (small, recent ones can be returned within 7 days in the same month), and buying them for oversized instances locks in waste: rightsize first (Step 5).

Step 5

Rightsizing, Graviton and Spot

Compute Optimizer for the whole organization. It is opt-in and free at the standard level; enrolling from the management account (or a delegated administrator) covers the member accounts. It covers EC2, Auto Scaling groups, EBS, Lambda, ECS on Fargate, RDS and Aurora, DynamoDB and ElastiCache, and flags idle resources. Enhanced infrastructure metrics (paid) extend the lookback to 93 days; install the CloudWatch agent so memory is seen. Cost Optimization Hub gathers these with commitment recommendations.

Management account 999999999999: opt in this account and all member accounts (AWS CLI) 
aws compute-optimizer update-enrollment-status \
  --status Active \
  --include-member-accounts

Graviton. Instances cost up to 20% less than comparable x86 ones, Lambda and Fargate on Arm cost 20% less, and RDS, Aurora, ElastiCache and OpenSearch offer Graviton classes. Check every dependency has an Arm build; Compute Savings Plans follow the move, EC2 Instance Savings Plans don't.

Spot for fault-tolerant work. The web-tier Auto Scaling group from Task 3.1 keeps four On-Demand instances as a base, then runs 25% On-Demand and 75% Spot across several Graviton types with the price-capacity-optimized strategy. Nightly reporting and image processing run on Spot or Fargate Spot (up to 70% off, 2-minute warning, ECS only) behind a regular Fargate base.

ECS service in the reporting cluster: two tasks always on Fargate, extra tasks three-to-one on Fargate Spot (from Task 3.1) 
aws ecs create-service \
  --cluster reporting \
  --service-name image-workers \
  --task-definition image-worker:7 \
  --desired-count 8 \
  --capacity-provider-strategy \
    capacityProvider=FARGATE,base=2,weight=1 \
    capacityProvider=FARGATE_SPOT,weight=3 \
  --network-configuration "awsvpcConfiguration={subnets=[subnet-0a1b2c3d4e5f60001],securityGroups=[sg-0a1b2c3d4e5f60001]}"

Serverless sizing. Lambda suits bursty traffic; sustained high-volume APIs are usually cheaper on containers with Savings Plans or on Lambda Managed Instances (a 15% management fee, no scale to zero). Aurora Serverless v2 suits variable load and can auto-pause to zero for idle dev clusters, but not with RDS Proxy, Global Database, replication or zero-ETL attached, which rules it out for the production orders cluster.

Dev off at night. Dev Auto Scaling groups use their own scheduled actions (the dev-web group scales to zero at 19:00 and back to two at 08:00 on weekdays), because Instance Scheduler on AWS can't stop instances that belong to an Auto Scaling group. Instance Scheduler covers standalone EC2 and RDS instances across dev accounts (AWS claims up to 70% savings for business-hours-only instances).

Step 6

Data transfer and storage

Network paths (Task 3.2; designs on Task 1.4). In us-east-1, a NAT gateway adds $0.045 per GB processed, cross-AZ traffic $0.01 per GB each way, inter-Region about $0.02 per GB, and Transit Gateway $0.02 per GB processed. The programme cuts them:

  • Endpoints: free gateway endpoints for S3 and DynamoDB in every VPC; interface endpoints for ECR, CloudWatch and STS, cheaper than NAT once traffic is meaningful, and centralized in the shared-network account when that beats per-VPC endpoint hours.
  • AZ-local paths: one NAT gateway per AZ, chatty tiers routed within an AZ, replicas read in the same AZ, private IPs instead of public or Elastic IPs inside the Region.
  • Who pays: Transit Gateway metering policies charge processing to the source, the destination or the owner, so the network team in 666666666666 can show each unit its own share; a direct peering for a heavy VPC pair skips TGW processing.
  • Edge and replication: CloudFront in front of S3 and ALB (origin fetches from AWS are free) with small cache keys; only the prefixes DR needs replicate to us-west-2, as Standard-IA.
Workload VPC: free S3 gateway endpoint on the private route tables (from Task 3.2) 
aws ec2 create-vpc-endpoint \
  --vpc-id vpc-0a1b2c3d4e5f60001 \
  --vpc-endpoint-type Gateway \
  --service-name com.amazonaws.us-east-1.s3 \
  --route-table-ids rtb-0a1b2c3d4e5f60001 rtb-0a1b2c3d4e5f60002 rtb-0a1b2c3d4e5f60003

Storage and databases (Task 3.4):

  • S3: Intelligent-Tiering for unknown access, Lifecycle rules for known patterns, noncurrent-version expiry and aborted multipart uploads; objects under 128 KB don't tier.
  • EBS: gp3 everywhere ($0.08 versus $0.10 per GB-month for gp2, 3,000 IOPS and 125 MiB/s included), migrated in place with Elastic Volumes and no downtime.
  • Aurora: the orders cluster has a heavy, steady I/O load, so I/O-Optimized (no I/O charges, about 30% more per instance-hour and 2.25 times the storage price) beats Standard; low-I/O clusters stay on Standard.
  • DynamoDB: provisioned with auto scaling and reserved capacity for steady tables; the Standard-IA table class for the storage-heavy order-history table.
A gp2 volume found by Compute Optimizer: move it to gp3 in place, with the included baseline (from Task 3.4) 
aws ec2 modify-volume \
  --volume-id vol-0a1b2c3d4e5f60001 \
  --volume-type gp3 \
  --iops 3000 \
  --throughput 125
Step 7

Chargeback per business unit

Showback first, then chargeback. Units first see their numbers without being billed, which surfaces untagged spend before money moves. Then:

  • Amortized cost for commitments: upfront Savings Plans and RI fees are spread over the term, and the discount lands on the account whose usage it covered.
  • Shared costs: the shared-network account, support and the platform team. The split charge rule shows the split on the cost category page; a chargeback built on CUR must apply the same proportions itself, or use Billing Conductor.
  • Containers: split cost allocation data gives per-namespace cost on the shared EKS cluster; pods without resource requests get no split cost under the requests method, so the platform team enforces requests.
  • GenAI: the central GenAI account passes cost back through application inference profiles with cost allocation tags and IAM principal data (GenAI platform).
Athena over the org-cur2-daily export: last month's unblended cost per business unit and account (SQL; the table name comes from your Glue crawler or table definition) 
SELECT
  tags['costCategory/BusinessUnit'] AS business_unit,
  line_item_usage_account_id,
  SUM(line_item_unblended_cost) AS unblended_cost
FROM cur2_org
WHERE line_item_usage_start_date >= DATE '2026-10-01'
  AND line_item_usage_start_date < DATE '2026-11-01'
GROUP BY 1, 2
ORDER BY unblended_cost DESC;

Unblended cost shows what was charged where; for chargeback the query adds the effective-cost columns from the Savings Plans and Reservation column groups to get amortized cost, and nets out credits for what was actually paid. cur2_org is a new name for the Athena table.

Billing Conductor produces pro forma bills per billing group with your own pricing rules, credits and fees; it doesn't change the AWS invoice or discount sharing.

Design decisions

Commitments bought in the management account, not by each unit

Central purchases pool the organization's baseline, so one unit's dip is another's coverage and utilization stays high. The cost: units don't feel the commitment, so chargeback must pass the amortized discount to them.

Compute Savings Plans as the default, EC2 Instance Savings Plans only for the fixed core

The company is still migrating, moving to Graviton and running DR in us-west-2, so flexibility is worth the smaller discount. EC2 Instance Savings Plans go only on a baseline that has stayed in one family and Region for months.

Rightsize before committing, and coverage below 100%

A commitment fixed before rightsizing pays for waste for one or three years. Committing only to the lowest steady hourly spend leaves peaks on On-Demand, interruptible work on Spot, and room for workloads still to be retired.

Prevent with SCPs and tag policies, detect with Config, react with budgets

Prevention keeps unallocated spend out, but only for actions with tag-on-create; detection finds the rest; budget actions limit damage in dev. Document the rules: member accounts can't read their effective SCPs.

Alternatives

Billing Conductor pro forma billing

Billing groups with markups, discounts and credits give each unit a bill at internal rates, including a proportional share of commitments (the September 2026 blog pattern). Simpler showback uses billing views and CUR.

Restricted group sharing per business unit

Each unit's commitments serve only that unit, which makes ownership clear but can strand unused commitment. Use it when units are separate legal or budget entities; otherwise Prioritized keeps the organization's discount rate.

Testing it

  1. Prove the guardrailsIn 121212121212, launch an instance without CostCenter (denied by the SCP) and with CostCenter=bu-retail (rejected by the enforced tag policy); a stack without tags must fail its Guard hook.
  2. Reconcile the exportThe monthly CUR 2.0 total must match the invoice, and the Unallocated cost category value must stay under the agreed share; every new item there becomes a tagging fix.
  3. Fire the budget actionOn a staging budget with a tiny limit, confirm the manual approval request arrives, the SCP attaches to the target OU, new launches fail, and reversing the action detaches it.
  4. Trigger an anomalyRun an unusual but harmless workload in a sandbox account; the managed monitor must alert, and the EventBridge rule must route it to that unit's topic.
  5. Review commitments monthlyCheck Savings Plans utilization and coverage per account against the targets, and the group sharing preference before month end, since the final bill uses the last setting.
  6. Measure each optimizationBefore and after: NAT bytes and DataTransfer-Regional-Bytes after adding endpoints, Spot interruptions and job completion, Aurora cost over a full month on each configuration.

"A company with many accounts wants the largest discount on steady compute that keeps changing instance families and Regions, with the least management" is a Compute Savings Plan bought in the management account with discount sharing on.

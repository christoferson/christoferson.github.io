AWS Certified Solutions Architect – Associate

Designing architectures on AWS that are secure, resilient, high-performing and cost-optimized, following the AWS Well-Architected Framework: who can reach what, how data is protected, how systems scale and survive failures, which storage, compute, database and network options fit, and what they cost.

How to use this guide

The exam is for people in a solutions architect role with at least a year of hands-on experience designing solutions on AWS. Every question is a design choice: given requirements and constraints, pick the architecture that is secure, resilient, fast enough and no more expensive than it needs to be. Each task page explains every skill in the official exam guide, with one running example, configuration and code, exam tips and common traps.

  1. Start with Domain 1Almost a third of the exam: IAM and multi-account access, securing workloads and networks, and protecting data with encryption and backups.
  2. Then Domains 2 and 3Loose coupling and high availability, then choosing storage, compute, databases, networking and data ingestion that perform at scale.
  3. Then Domain 4The same building blocks seen through cost: pricing models, storage tiers, right-sizing and data transfer.

About the exam in 2026. SAA-C03 has 65 multiple-choice and multiple-response questions in 130 minutes; 50 are scored and 15 are unscored and not identified. The passing score is 720 on a scale of 100 to 1,000, the fee is US$150, and the certification is valid for three years. It's taken at a Pearson VUE test center or online. The current exam guide is version 1.1, published on 29 October 2026; AWS says guide updates appear on the exam about a month after publication.

Changed services. The guide's in-scope list still names some services that have changed: AWS Proton reached end of support on 7 October 2026, AWS Application Discovery Service closed to new customers on 7 November 2025 (AWS Transform is the suggested path), AWS Data Pipeline is in maintenance mode, Snowball Edge devices are no longer offered to new customers, and the original Amazon Glacier vault service is closed to new customers (the S3 Glacier storage classes are unaffected). Amazon QuickSight is now Amazon Quick, and Amazon SageMaker is now Amazon SageMaker AI. The task pages teach the current services and say where the guide's names differ.

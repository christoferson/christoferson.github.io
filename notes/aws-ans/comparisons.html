Often confused pairs

  • Hosted connection and hosted VIF: A whole Partner-provisioned connection in your account versus a VIF that another connection owner creates for your account to accept.
  • LAG and redundant connections: A LAG bundles same-speed connections on one AWS device for bandwidth; only separate devices and locations give resilience.
  • High and maximum resiliency: 99.9% needs one connection at each of two locations; 99.99% needs at least four connections, two in each of two locations, on separate devices.
  • Private IP VPN and VPN over a public VIF: IPsec over a transit VIF with private addresses and no public endpoints exposed versus the older method with public VPN endpoints reached over Direct Connect.
  • Static and BGP VPN routes on a Transit Gateway: A static route to the VPN attachment beats every propagated route, including Direct Connect; a BGP VPN loses to Direct Connect and works as a backup.
  • Transit Gateway association and propagation: Each attachment associates with exactly one route table, which decides where its traffic goes, but can propagate its routes into many.
  • Appliance mode and cross-zone load balancing: Appliance mode pins both directions of a flow to one AZ on the inspection VPC attachment; cross-zone spreads a load balancer's traffic across AZ targets.
  • Traffic dial and endpoint weight: Global Accelerator's percentage per Regional endpoint group versus the 0 to 255 share between endpoints inside one Region.
  • Client IP preservation and proxy protocol v2: The NLB keeps the source address on the packet versus prepending a header with it when preservation is impossible, as with PrivateLink or across a Transit Gateway.
  • Signed URLs and signed cookies: One file or clients without cookies versus many files at once without changing their URLs.
  • Alias and CNAME records: Free for AWS targets, allowed at the zone apex and follows the target's TTL versus a name-to-name pointer that is not allowed at the apex.
  • enableDnsSupport and enableDnsHostnames: Whether the VPC Resolver answers at all (default on) versus public DNS hostnames for instances (default off in new VPCs); private zones need both.
  • Public DNS query logs and Resolver query logs: Queries from the internet to a public zone, to CloudWatch Logs in us-east-1 only, versus queries from VPCs and endpoints, to CloudWatch Logs, S3 or Firehose in-Region.
  • DNSSEC and DNS over HTTPS: Integrity of answers through signatures versus confidentiality of queries between resolvers; Resolver endpoints support DoH but not DNS over TLS.
  • Removing a PrivateLink principal and rejecting the endpoint: Removing the principal stops new connections only; reject the endpoint connection to cut existing ones.
  • Security group references across peering and across a Transit Gateway: Same-Region peering supports references in either direction; a Transit Gateway supports them in inbound rules once enabled, but not across Transit Gateway peering or inspection.
  • Nitro automatic encryption and Transit Gateway traffic: Nitro encrypts between supported instances in the same or peered VPCs with nothing in between; a load balancer or Transit Gateway in the path ends that protection.
  • MTU 9001, 8500 and 1446: Within a VPC and same-Region peering versus Transit Gateway, inter-Region peering, endpoints and GWLB versus inside a VPN tunnel (with no Path MTU Discovery).

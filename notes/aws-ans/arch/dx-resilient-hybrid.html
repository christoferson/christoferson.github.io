Reference architecture

Resilient hybrid connectivity with Direct Connect

The travel company connects both data centers to all three Regions with the Direct Connect maximum resiliency model: four 10 Gbps dedicated connections per data center, at two locations, on separate AWS devices, all carrying transit VIFs to one Direct Connect gateway. A BGP VPN is the last resort and SiteLink joins the data centers.

Scenario and requirements

The travel company (example.com) runs the Transit Gateway hub and spoke in us-east-1, eu-central-1 and ap-northeast-1, joined as described on the multi-Region network page. Booking and payment systems still run partly in the two data centers, so a lost path means failed bookings. The business asks for the 99.99% Direct Connect SLA, at least 10 Gbps to each data center after a failure, failover in about a second, and no outage during AWS maintenance.

The numbers used on this page, consistent with the hub pages:

  • US data center: 10.100.0.0/16, AS 65010; connections dxcon-us1a and dxcon-us1b at US location 1, dxcon-us2a and dxcon-us2b at US location 2, both locations associated with us-east-1.
  • European data center: 10.200.0.0/16, AS 65020; connections dxcon-eu1a, dxcon-eu1b, dxcon-eu2a and dxcon-eu2b at two locations associated with eu-central-1.
  • Direct Connect gateway dxgw-travel-global, AS 64512, associated with tgw-use1 (ASN 64521), tgw-euc1 (64522) and tgw-apne1 (64523).
  • Allowed prefixes (the Region summaries): 10.40.0.0/14, 10.44.0.0/14 and 10.48.0.0/14.

Task 1.5 sketches one connection per location; this page doubles that, as the 99.99% SLA requires.

How it works

A booking pod in Virginia calls the US data center's card vault, through normal operation and two failures.

  1. Normal operation
  2. Into the hubAWSThe pod at 10.40.12.30 sends to 10.100.20.5. The VPC route table sends 10.0.0.0/8 to tgw-use1, where the prod table holds 10.100.0.0/16 propagated from the Direct Connect gateway attachment.
  3. Four equal pathsAWSAll four US transit VIFs advertise 10.100.0.0/16 with the same community and AS_PATH length, so AWS spreads flows across them with ECMP.
  4. Into the data centerNetwork teamThe packet arrives on one of the two US routers. Equal local preference on all four sessions makes traffic toward AWS use all four links too.
  5. One AWS device fails
  6. BFD detects itAWSWith BFD configured on the routers, the session on dxvif-us1a drops after about 900 ms instead of the 90-second hold time.
  7. Routes withdrawnAWSThe path through that VIF is removed and ECMP continues over the three survivors, still 30 Gbps of capacity.
  8. All US Direct Connect paths fail
  9. VPN route appearsAWSWhen the last Direct Connect gateway route for 10.100.0.0/16 is withdrawn, the BGP route from the VPN attachment becomes the best route in the prod table. It was hidden until then, because Direct Connect gateway routes beat VPN routes for the same prefix.
  10. Degraded pathAWSTraffic now crosses IPsec over the internet at a few Gbps, with an MTU of about 1400 bytes instead of 8500.
  11. Data center to data center
  12. Across SiteLinkAWSA packet from 10.100.40.8 to 10.200.40.8 enters on a US transit VIF and leaves on a European transit VIF across the AWS backbone, without entering any Region or Transit Gateway.

Build it

Step 1

Eight connections in the maximum resiliency model

The Resiliency Toolkit (the console's Connection wizard) with Maximum resiliency orders equal-speed connections at two locations and keeps them off shared AWS devices. The 99.99% SLA conditions:

  • At least four connections, across at least two locations, with at least two per location, each on its own AWS device (the console shows the device ID).
  • Enterprise Support and a completed Well-Architected Review with an AWS solutions architect.
  • Private endpoints span two or more Availability Zones.
  • Dedicated connections only. Hosted connections and hosted VIFs from partners are not covered by the SLA, and a LAG counts as a single connection.

Port hours begin when a port goes active or 90 days after its LOA-CFA is issued, so order shortly before the cross connects can be installed.

Order the four US connections; the location codes are placeholders for the two chosen Direct Connect locations (AWS CLI, network account, us-east-1) 
# location 1, two ports; the Resiliency Toolkit would also keep them on separate devices
aws directconnect create-connection --location LOC-US-1 \
  --bandwidth 10Gbps --connection-name us-dc1-loc1-a --request-mac-sec
aws directconnect create-connection --location LOC-US-1 \
  --bandwidth 10Gbps --connection-name us-dc1-loc1-b --request-mac-sec

# location 2, a different building and carrier path
aws directconnect create-connection --location LOC-US-2 \
  --bandwidth 10Gbps --connection-name us-dc1-loc2-a --request-mac-sec
aws directconnect create-connection --location LOC-US-2 \
  --bandwidth 10Gbps --connection-name us-dc1-loc2-b --request-mac-sec

# repeat for the European data center at its two locations

Check that the two connections at each location report different awsDeviceV2 values, or they are maintained and fail together. MACsec is requested because card data crosses these links and Direct Connect is not encrypted (Task 1.5).

Optional LAGs. If a path later needs more than 10 Gbps, each connection can grow into a LAG of up to four 10 Gbps members. All members sit on one device, so a LAG adds bandwidth, not resilience, and the SLA counts it as one connection. Minimum links defaults to 0, so a two-member LAG with one member down keeps its routes and squeezes its traffic onto 10 Gbps. With minimum links 2, the whole LAG goes down and ECMP moves the traffic to healthy paths.

A two-member LAG at US location 1 that goes down as a whole if a member fails (AWS CLI) 
# new LAG with two 10 Gbps members on one device
aws directconnect create-lag --location LOC-US-1 \
  --number-of-connections 2 --connections-bandwidth 10Gbps \
  --lag-name us-dc1-loc1-a-lag --request-mac-sec

# both members must be up, or the LAG is down and BGP fails over
aws directconnect update-lag --lag-id dxlag-us1a --minimum-links 2

Adding an existing connection to a new LAG (create-lag --connection-id) interrupts that connection while it is re-established as a member. Do it while other paths carry the traffic, and never set minimum links above the members currently up.

Step 2

Transit VIFs to one Direct Connect gateway, Region summaries as allowed prefixes

Each connection carries one transit VIF to dxgw-travel-global. One gateway is recommended: it is a distributed set of route reflectors outside the data path, not a single point of failure, and ECMP across transit VIFs needs them on the same gateway. Limits: four transit VIFs per dedicated connection, 30 VIFs and six Transit Gateways per Direct Connect gateway.

The fourth US transit VIF, at location 2 on the second device, with jumbo frames (AWS CLI; the router uses AS 65010) 
aws directconnect create-transit-virtual-interface \
  --connection-id dxcon-us2b \
  --new-transit-virtual-interface '{
    "virtualInterfaceName": "us-dc1-transit-d",
    "vlan": 104,
    "asn": 65010,
    "mtu": 8500,
    "authKey": "REPLACE_WITH_MD5_KEY",
    "addressFamily": "ipv4",
    "amazonAddress": "169.254.20.13/30",
    "customerAddress": "169.254.20.14/30",
    "directConnectGatewayId": "dxgw-travel-global"
  }'
# the other US VIFs: us-dc1-transit-a/b/c on dxcon-us1a/us1b/us2a, VLANs 101-103

The gateway is associated with all three Transit Gateways, as built on the hub page, Step 4. For a Transit Gateway association the allowed prefixes are advertised as they are, must not overlap between Transit Gateways, and stay far below the 200 prefixes per Transit Gateway a transit VIF carries toward on premises.

Associate the Tokyo Transit Gateway with its Region summary as the allowed prefix (AWS CLI, network account) 
# one summary per Region; adding a VPC in Tokyo changes nothing here
aws directconnect create-direct-connect-gateway-association \
  --direct-connect-gateway-id dxgw-travel-global \
  --gateway-id tgw-apne1 \
  --add-allowed-prefixes-to-direct-connect-gateway cidr=10.48.0.0/14

Jumbo frames (8500 on transit VIFs) need every device in the data center path to support them; an MTU change can interrupt all VIFs on the connection for up to 30 seconds.

Step 3

BGP: communities, AS_PATH, MED, BFD and prefix limits

Each direction is steered from a different side. Toward the data centers, AWS picks by longest prefix, then local preference (communities 7224:7100 low, 7224:7200 medium, 7224:7300 high), then shortest AS_PATH, then lowest MED, then ECMP across up to 16 paths. Toward AWS, the routers' own local preference decides (Task 1.5).

Active/active within a data center is the choice here. All four US VIFs advertise 10.100.0.0/16 with the same community and AS_PATH, so AWS load-shares across them. AWS already gives locations associated with the same Region medium preference; tagging 7224:7200 everywhere makes that explicit. Communities are evaluated before AS_PATH, so a stray prepend does not unbalance the links; MED is honored but not recommended.

Active/passive is the fallback if stateful firewalls drop traffic returning on the other router: tag location 1 with 7224:7300 and location 2 with 7224:7100, and mirror it with higher local preference on location 1.

us-dc1-r1: equal preference on both of its sessions, BFD, and no AWS routes advertised back (Cisco IOS-style; r1 holds VIF a at location 1 and VIF c at location 2, r2 holds b and d) 
ip prefix-list DC-SUMMARY permit 10.100.0.0/16
ip as-path access-list 10 permit ^$
!
route-map TO-AWS permit 10
 match ip address prefix-list DC-SUMMARY
 match as-path 10
 ! same community on all four US paths: ECMP at AWS
 set community 7224:7200
!
route-map FROM-AWS permit 10
 ! equal on every session: ECMP toward AWS too
 set local-preference 150
!
interface TenGigabitEthernet0/0/0.101
 encapsulation dot1Q 101
 ip address 169.254.20.2 255.255.255.252
 bfd interval 300 min_rx 300 multiplier 3
!
router bgp 65010
 neighbor 169.254.20.1 remote-as 64512
 neighbor 169.254.20.1 password REPLACE_WITH_MD5_KEY
 neighbor 169.254.20.1 fall-over bfd
 neighbor 169.254.20.9 remote-as 64512
 neighbor 169.254.20.9 password REPLACE_WITH_MD5_KEY
 neighbor 169.254.20.9 fall-over bfd
 address-family ipv4
  network 10.100.0.0 mask 255.255.0.0
  maximum-paths 2
  neighbor 169.254.20.1 send-community
  neighbor 169.254.20.1 route-map TO-AWS out
  neighbor 169.254.20.1 route-map FROM-AWS in
  neighbor 169.254.20.9 send-community
  neighbor 169.254.20.9 route-map TO-AWS out
  neighbor 169.254.20.9 route-map FROM-AWS in

The as-path ^$ filter advertises only locally originated routes. It stops a router re-advertising the European summary learned over SiteLink back to AWS.

  • Timers: defaults are hold 90 s and keepalive 30 s, set on your router. BFD runs on the AWS side at 300 ms × 3 but does nothing until the router is configured.
  • MD5 is always on; the key comes from the downloaded router configuration (Task 2.1).
  • Prefix limits: private and transit VIFs accept 100 prefixes per address family from on premises by default. Exceeding it puts the session into idle. Since August 2026, prefix controls allow up to 1,000 per VIF, from a pool per connection (5,000 per family on 10 Gbps ports) and 10,000 per Direct Connect gateway. One summary per data center needs only the default.
Allocate 300 IPv4 prefixes to one VIF from its connection's pool, before a migration that advertises more specifics (AWS CLI) 
aws directconnect update-virtual-interface-attributes \
  --virtual-interface-id dxvif-us1a \
  --prefix-pool-allocated-count-ipv4 300
Step 4

A BGP Site-to-Site VPN as the last resort

The VPN covers the case of all four paths of one data center lost at once, and costs little. Each data center terminates a VPN on its nearest Transit Gateway, the US on tgw-use1 and Europe on tgw-euc1, associated with the onprem table and propagated like the Direct Connect gateway attachment (hub page).

The European data center's backup VPN on the Frankfurt Transit Gateway, BGP and IKEv2 (AWS CLI, network account) 
aws ec2 create-vpn-connection --region eu-central-1 \
  --type ipsec.1 \
  --customer-gateway-id cgw-eu-dc1 \
  --transit-gateway-id tgw-euc1 \
  --options '{
    "StaticRoutesOnly": false,
    "TunnelOptions": [
      { "TunnelInsideCidr": "169.254.30.0/30",
        "IKEVersions": [{"Value": "ikev2"}],
        "StartupAction": "start",
        "DPDTimeoutAction": "restart" },
      { "TunnelInsideCidr": "169.254.30.4/30",
        "IKEVersions": [{"Value": "ikev2"}],
        "StartupAction": "start",
        "DPDTimeoutAction": "restart" }
    ]
  }'

Four rules keep it a backup:

  • BGP, never static. A static route to the VPN attachment beats every propagated route and pulls all traffic onto the tunnels. With BGP, the Direct Connect gateway route wins for the same prefix.
  • Same prefix, not a more specific one. A longer prefix over the VPN would win before attachment type is compared.
  • MTU. The path drops from 8500 bytes to at most 1446 inside the tunnel (MSS 1406, less with NAT traversal or CBC ciphers) with no Path MTU Discovery. Clamp TCP MSS on the routers, or large DF-bit packets are dropped after failover.
  • Capacity. A standard tunnel carries up to 1.25 Gbps; with ECMP on the Transit Gateway both tunnels are used, so about 2.5 Gbps against 40 Gbps of Direct Connect. The runbook sheds bulk replication; Large Bandwidth Tunnels (5 Gbps each) cost more per hour.

The VPN lands in one Region only. Transit Gateway peering carries static routes for VPC summaries only, so during a total US Direct Connect outage Frankfurt and Tokyo lose the US data center unless they get their own VPNs. The company accepts this rare case.

Step 5

SiteLink between the data centers

SiteLink sends traffic between Direct Connect locations over the AWS backbone by the shortest path, bypassing Regions. It needs VIFs at two or more locations on the same Direct Connect gateway, and works on transit VIFs and on private VIFs on a Direct Connect gateway, not on public VIFs or private VIFs attached to a virtual private gateway. It is off by default, can be toggled at any time, preserves DSCP markings without managed QoS, and is billed separately. It replaces the leased line between the data centers.

Turn SiteLink on for every transit VIF in both data centers (AWS CLI) 
# eight VIFs; SiteLink only carries traffic between VIFs that have it enabled
for vif in dxvif-us1a dxvif-us1b dxvif-us2a dxvif-us2b \
           dxvif-eu1a dxvif-eu1b dxvif-eu2a dxvif-eu2b; do
  aws directconnect update-virtual-interface-attributes \
    --virtual-interface-id "$vif" --enable-site-link
done

With SiteLink on, the US routers learn 10.200.0.0/16 from the Direct Connect gateway alongside the three Region summaries, and the European routers learn 10.100.0.0/16. Two effects need planning:

  • Path selection changes. With SiteLink on, AWS prefers the shortest AS_PATH from any location instead of locations associated with the same Region, so keep AS_PATH equal on all paths of a data center.
  • Loop prevention. Routes learned over SiteLink must never be re-advertised to AWS, which the ^$ filter from Step 3 enforces.

This traffic avoids Transit Gateway charges; the SiteLink rate was not confirmed for this page, so check the pricing page before comparing it with the leased line.

Step 6

Monitoring and maintenance handling

Direct Connect publishes AWS/DX metrics every five minutes. Layer 1 and BGP are separate signals: a connection can be up with BGP down.

  • ConnectionState (1 up, 0 down) per ConnectionId.
  • ConnectionLightLevelTx and ConnectionLightLevelRx in dBm, per ConnectionId and OpticalLaneNumber; alarm on a falling trend.
  • ConnectionErrorCount (Sum statistic) for MAC-level errors, including CRC.
  • VirtualInterfaceBgpStatus (1 up, 0 down), VirtualInterfaceBgpPrefixesAccepted and VirtualInterfaceBgpPrefixesAdvertised, with the IpAddressFamily dimension, since March 2026.
Alarm when a transit VIF's BGP session drops, and when the US routers send more than 80 prefixes (AWS CLI) 
# use the exact dimension set the metric is published with (shown in the CloudWatch console)
aws cloudwatch put-metric-alarm \
  --alarm-name dx-us-dc1-transit-a-bgp-down \
  --namespace AWS/DX --metric-name VirtualInterfaceBgpStatus \
  --dimensions Name=ConnectionId,Value=dxcon-us1a Name=VirtualInterfaceId,Value=dxvif-us1a Name=IpAddressFamily,Value=ipv4 \
  --statistic Minimum --period 300 --evaluation-periods 1 \
  --threshold 1 --comparison-operator LessThanThreshold \
  --alarm-actions arn:aws:sns:us-east-1:111111111111:network-oncall

# early warning well before the default allocation of 100
aws cloudwatch put-metric-alarm \
  --alarm-name dx-us-dc1-transit-a-prefixes-high \
  --namespace AWS/DX --metric-name VirtualInterfaceBgpPrefixesAccepted \
  --dimensions Name=ConnectionId,Value=dxcon-us1a Name=VirtualInterfaceId,Value=dxvif-us1a Name=IpAddressFamily,Value=ipv4 \
  --statistic Maximum --period 300 --evaluation-periods 1 \
  --threshold 80 --comparison-operator GreaterThanThreshold \
  --alarm-actions arn:aws:sns:us-east-1:111111111111:network-oncall

A third alarm watches VirtualInterfaceBgpPrefixesAdvertised: each US VIF should send four prefixes (three Region summaries plus the European data center over SiteLink). Zero with the session up is a silent withdrawal. The ListVirtualInterfaceRoutes API (July 2026) shows the routes with AS paths and communities.

What AWS accepted from the US data center on one VIF, and what it advertises back (AWS CLI) 
# expect 10.100.0.0/16 with community 7224:7200
aws directconnect list-virtual-interface-routes \
  --virtual-interface-id dxvif-us1a \
  --filters routeDirection=accepted,addressFamily=ipv4

# expect 10.40.0.0/14, 10.44.0.0/14, 10.48.0.0/14 and 10.200.0.0/16
aws directconnect list-virtual-interface-routes \
  --virtual-interface-id dxvif-us1a \
  --filters routeDirection=advertised,addressFamily=ipv4

Maintenance. Planned Direct Connect maintenance is announced 14 days ahead, with reminders 7 days and 1 day before, usually in a 4-hour low-traffic window. AWS drains the device and never schedules redundant connections together, but connections on one logical device are maintained together, hence the device check in Step 1. Emergency maintenance can start at short notice, typically in a 2-hour window, and neither can be postponed. Notices also go to AWS Health, so an EventBridge rule can open a change ticket.

The runbook for a window on dxcon-us1b: confirm the other three paths are healthy, drain the path early with 7224:7100 and lower local preference on that session, watch the metrics, and restore afterwards. Three paths remain, still 30 Gbps; high resiliency would be down to one link.

Design decisions

Maximum resiliency instead of high resiliency

High resiliency (one connection at each of two locations, 99.9%) leaves a single link during every maintenance window or device failure. Maximum resiliency doubles ports and cross connects and turns maintenance into a non-event; with Enterprise Support already in place, the extra cost is four ports. See Task 1.5.

Size every failure state, not the normal one

The requirement is 10 Gbps after a failure. Losing a location leaves 20 Gbps; losing a location during maintenance at the other still leaves 10 Gbps, against a planned US peak under 15 Gbps.

One Direct Connect gateway for both data centers and all Regions

One gateway gives ECMP across all VIFs, makes SiteLink possible and keeps one set of allowed prefixes. Every association receives both data center ranges, so segmentation lives in the Transit Gateway route tables (hub page).

Active/active with communities, not prepending

Equal communities use every paid port, and communities beat AS_PATH, so they are the reliable lever on transit VIFs. Asymmetry-sensitive firewalls would force active/passive.

Cost

Port hours for a 10 Gbps dedicated connection are $2.25 an hour, charged even with no traffic: 8 × $2.25 × 730 ≈ $13,140 a month. Data transfer out over Direct Connect is charged by source Region and location, for example $0.02 per GB from a US Region to a US location; data in is free. Sending 100 TB a month from us-east-1 to the US data center costs 102,400 GB × $0.02 ≈ $2,048. Traffic entering the Transit Gateway from a VPC attachment also pays $0.02 per GB processing. The two backup VPNs cost $0.05 per connection-hour each, about $73 a month together, and the Direct Connect gateway is free.

Flat-rate pricing (September 2026) offers a fixed hourly fee for 10 and 100 Gbps dedicated port pairs that includes data transfer out within the chosen tier (metro or continent), with the effective bandwidth of one port. It is converted with UpdateConnectionsBillingMode, location-wide per account; tier prices were not confirmed here, so compare against measured transfer first. See Task 3.3.

Alternatives

High resiliency with VPN backup

Two connections per data center at two locations (99.9%) plus a BGP VPN. Half the port cost, but during maintenance or a device failure the data center runs on one 10 Gbps link, and a second failure in that window drops it to the VPN.

Hosted connections from a Direct Connect Partner

Faster to deliver, 50 Mbps to 25 Gbps, but one VIF, no LAG or MACsec, and no SLA coverage.

AWS Interconnect – last mile

An AWS-managed connection from the premises to a Direct Connect gateway (GA April 2026, US only at launch), with BGP, VLANs and MACsec configured by AWS. Kept for future offices.

Direct Connect gateway attached to Cloud WAN

If the company moves to Cloud WAN, the same connections can carry transit VIFs to a Direct Connect gateway attached to the core network. Allowed prefixes disappear and routing policies take their place (multi-Region page, Step 6).

VPN only, with ECMP

Two customer gateways with a VPN each give four tunnels on the Transit Gateway, about 5 Gbps standard or 20 Gbps with Large Bandwidth Tunnels. Cheap and quick, but latency varies with the internet.

Testing it

  1. Check the routes before breaking anythingRun list-virtual-interface-routes on all eight VIFs: each US VIF must accept exactly 10.100.0.0/16 with 7224:7200 and advertise the four expected prefixes. In the prod table, 10.100.0.0/16 must point at the Direct Connect gateway attachment.
  2. Fail one VIF with the BGP failover testaws directconnect start-bgp-failover-test --virtual-interface-id dxvif-us1a --test-duration-in-minutes 60 puts the VIF in testing and its BGP peers down (default 180 minutes, maximum 72 hours, stoppable early, logged in CloudTrail). Expect the BGP alarm, traffic shifting to three VIFs in VirtualInterfaceBpsEgress, and no failed synthetic checks. Not during maintenance.
  3. Fail a whole locationRun the test on dxvif-us1a and dxvif-us1b together. Location 2 should carry all US traffic.
  4. Run it as an FIS experimentThe AWS FIS action aws:directconnect:virtual-interface-disconnect targets resources of type aws:directconnect:virtual-interface (selected by tag, for example all four US VIFs), with a duration from 10 minutes to 12 hours (ISO 8601, such as PT30M). FIS first checks that every targeted VIF is available with BGP up; the experiment role needs directconnect:StartBgpFailoverTest, StopBgpFailoverTest and related read permissions. A stop condition on a booking error-rate alarm ends the experiment if the VPN cannot cope.
  5. Prove the VPN takes overDuring the four-VIF experiment, the VPN-propagated route must appear in the prod table, TunnelDataIn and TunnelDataOut must rise, and a DF-bit ping of 1410 bytes from the data center must succeed while 1500 fails.
  6. Test SiteLink and the edges of the designFrom the US data center, trace to 10.200.40.8 over SiteLink. Reachability Analyzer from a booking instance to the Direct Connect gateway attachment, destination 10.100.20.5, checks the AWS side only.

"The company needs the highest Direct Connect availability and must survive the loss of a device or a whole Direct Connect location" is the maximum resiliency model: at least four dedicated connections, two at each of two locations, on separate devices, with Enterprise Support and a Well-Architected Review for the 99.99% SLA. If two connections at one location are offered, or a LAG of four, they fail a location loss. "Direct Connect primary, VPN backup, but all traffic is using the VPN" is a static route to the VPN attachment; use BGP on the VPN. "A BGP session went idle after the data center added routes" is the 100-prefix default on the VIF: summarize, or raise the allocation with prefix controls.

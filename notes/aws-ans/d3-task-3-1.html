Overview

Task 3.1

Maintain routing and connectivity on AWS and hybrid networks.

Domain 3 is about operating what Domains 1 and 2 built. Questions here describe a working network that hits a limit or a change: a Direct Connect maintenance notice, a BGP session dropping after a new prefix, an instance that cannot go above 5 Gbps, on-premises servers that cannot reach S3 through the VPC's endpoint, or a route table close to its quota. The answer is usually the limit or rule that explains the behavior, and the least disruptive fix.

The travel company's network team runs the Direct Connect links, Transit Gateways and shared endpoints built earlier, and handles maintenance, growth and new access requests from application teams and partners.

Operating BGP

Knowledge 3.1.1Industry-standard routing protocols that are used in AWS hybrid networks (for example, BGP over Direct Connect)

BGP runs on every Direct Connect VIF (eBGP with MD5, optional BFD) and on dynamic VPN tunnels; Transit Gateway Connect uses BGP inside GRE. Path selection, communities, MED and AS_PATH are covered on Task 1.5. For operations, three more rules matter:

  • Loop prevention: Direct Connect drops a route whose AS_PATH contains 7224 twice or more, which happens if you re-advertise routes learned from AWS on one VIF to AWS on another. Filter AWS-learned routes out of your advertisements.
  • Prefix limits: more prefixes than the VIF allows (100 per address family by default on private and transit VIFs, up to 1,000 with prefix controls; 1,000 on public VIFs) puts the session into idle. VGW VPNs accept 100 routes from the customer gateway, Transit Gateway VPNs 1,000.
  • Failure detection: BFD (300 ms by 3 on the AWS side, once you enable it on your router) instead of waiting for the 90-second hold timer.

Skill 3.1.1Managing routing protocols for AWS and hybrid connectivity options (for example, over a Direct Connect connection, VPN)

Direct Connect maintenance

  • Planned: announced 14 days ahead, with reminders at 7 and 1 days, usually in a 4-hour low-traffic window. AWS drains traffic from the device under maintenance so it moves to your redundant connections, and never schedules both redundant connections at once. Requests to postpone or cancel are not accepted.
  • Emergency: can start at short notice, typically a 2-hour window.
  • Notices go to the account owning the connection (and to the Partner for hosted connections) and to AWS Health, so EventBridge can route them.
  • Connections on the same AWS logical device are maintained together: only separate devices (and locations) give real redundancy.
  • To move traffic yourself before a window, re-tag the affected path's advertisements with a lower local preference community (7224:7100) and lower local preference on your routers.

VPN maintenance

AWS replaces tunnel endpoints one at a time; keep both tunnels up with equal weight and local preference so traffic follows AWS's MED changes. Tunnel endpoint lifecycle control lets you schedule replacements (Task 2.1).

Changing advertised prefixes safely

  1. Check headroomCount accepted prefixes (VirtualInterfaceBgpPrefixesAccepted) against the VIF allocation, and Transit Gateway route table space.
  2. Advertise on the backup path firstAdd the new or summarized prefix on the secondary connection, confirm it is accepted (ListVirtualInterfaceRoutes).
  3. Shift trafficMove traffic with communities or a BGP failover test while you change the primary.
  4. VerifyCheck route tables, flow logs and Network Synthetic Monitor probes, then restore normal preferences.
Before a maintenance window: take the primary VIF's BGP session down for 30 minutes to rehearse failover (AWS CLI) 
aws directconnect start-bgp-failover-test \
  --virtual-interface-id dxvif-us1a \
  --test-duration-in-minutes 30

Connectivity methods

Knowledge 3.1.2Connectivity methods for AWS and hybrid networks (for example, Direct Connect gateway, Transit Gateway, VIFs)

The pieces and how they chain (details on Task 1.5 and Task 1.6):

  • Private VIF to a VGW (one VPC) or to a Direct Connect gateway (up to 20 VGWs in any Region); transit VIF to a Direct Connect gateway associated with up to 6 Transit Gateways or Cloud WAN; public VIF to AWS public endpoints.
  • The Direct Connect gateway is global and free, carries no traffic between its associations (except the supernet case), and decides what AWS advertises through allowed prefixes.
  • Transit Gateway joins VPCs, VPNs, Direct Connect, Connect peers and peered Transit Gateways in a Region.
  • Site-to-Site VPN over the internet, over a public VIF, or as private IP VPN over a transit VIF.

Operational checks when something stops working: VIF and BGP state, allowed prefixes on the Direct Connect gateway association, Transit Gateway route table associations and propagations, VPC route tables pointing at the right gateway, then security groups and network ACLs.

Limits and quotas

Knowledge 3.1.3How limits and quotas affect AWS networking services (for example, bandwidth limits, route limits)

Bandwidth limits

EC2 instances

  • Single flow (one 5-tuple): 5 Gbps, 10 Gbps inside a cluster placement group, up to 25 Gbps with ENA Express (now also across AZs in a Region). Use several flows to go faster.
  • Through an internet gateway: 5 Gbps for instances with fewer than 32 vCPUs, 50% of instance bandwidth above that (newer network-optimized families get full baseline).
  • Smaller instances burst to their "up to" figure on credits and fall back to baseline.
  • Per-instance allowances for bandwidth, packets per second, tracked connections and link-local traffic (1,024 packets per second to DNS, metadata and Time Sync) drop packets silently; watch the ENA *_allowance_exceeded counters.

Network services

  • VPN tunnel: 1.25 Gbps and 140,000 packets per second (5 Gbps with Large Bandwidth Tunnels); scale with ECMP on a Transit Gateway.
  • Transit Gateway: up to 100 Gbps and 7.5 million packets per second per VPC attachment per AZ; Connect peers 5 Gbps each.
  • Direct Connect: the port speed; hosted connections and VIF rate limiters police at their rate.
  • NAT gateway: 5 Gbps scaling to 100 Gbps, 1 to 10 million packets per second, and 55,000 connections per IP address to one destination (add up to 8 addresses; ErrorPortAllocation shows exhaustion).
  • Interface endpoints: 10 Gbps per AZ, scaling to 100 Gbps. Resolver endpoints: about 10,000 queries per second per IP.

Route and rule limits

  • VPC route table: 500 non-propagated routes by default (up to 1,000), and at most 100 propagated routes, not adjustable.
  • Transit Gateway: 10,000 routes across all route tables; 200 prefixes advertised to on premises per Transit Gateway over a transit VIF.
  • Direct Connect and VPN: the BGP prefix limits above.
  • Prefix lists count their maximum entries (their "weight") against security group rules and route quotas: CloudFront origin-facing 55, Route 53 health checks 25, VPC Lattice 10, S3 and DynamoDB 1.
  • Security groups: 60 rules each direction, 5 groups per interface (up to 16).

"One file transfer between two instances never exceeds 5 Gbps, but parallel transfers do" is the single-flow limit, not a network problem. Fixes: multiple flows, a cluster placement group (10 Gbps), or ENA Express (25 Gbps).

Access to custom services

Knowledge 3.1.4Available private and public access methods for custom services (for example, PrivateLink, VPC peering)

Private

PrivateLink endpoint services (one way, overlap-proof, cross-Region), VPC Lattice services and resource configurations, VPC peering or Transit Gateway routing for two-way access, and VPC sharing within the organization (Task 1.6).

Public

  • Internet-facing ALB or NLB in public subnets.
  • Global Accelerator in front of ALBs, NLBs or instances, which can be internal or in private subnets; the VPC still needs an internet gateway attached (no public IPs or internet routes needed).
  • CloudFront VPC origins (November 2024, no extra charge): CloudFront reaches an ALB, NLB or instance in a private subnet; also needs an internet gateway on the VPC. Not for Gateway Load Balancers, NLBs with TLS listeners, or gRPC.
  • API Gateway private integrations: REST APIs now connect directly to an ALB through VPC links V2; V1 links to NLBs are legacy.

Skill 3.1.2Maintaining private access to custom services (for example, PrivateLink, VPC peering)

PrivateLink endpoint services

  • New AZ: adding an AZ to the NLB does not add it to the endpoint service; update the service, and consumers add subnets in that AZ (by AZ ID).
  • Consumers: manage allowed principals and accept or reject connections; removing a principal does not cut existing connections, so reject the endpoint too.
  • Private DNS: keep the TXT verification record; if verification fails, new connections are refused but existing ones continue.
  • Capacity: scale NLB targets; endpoints scale to 100 Gbps per AZ.
  • Client addresses: with proxy protocol v2 on, every target (and health check) must parse it.
  • Other Regions: add supported Regions rather than building per-Region copies, accepting that failover is between AZs only.
The fare API adds a second NLB covering a new AZ to its endpoint service, and removes a partner that ended its contract (AWS CLI) 
aws ec2 modify-vpc-endpoint-service-configuration \
  --service-id vpce-svc-0fares1234567890 \
  --add-network-load-balancer-arns arn:aws:elasticloadbalancing:us-east-1:444444444444:loadbalancer/net/fares-nlb-az4/7e1a2b3c4d5e6f70

aws ec2 modify-vpc-endpoint-service-permissions \
  --service-id vpce-svc-0fares1234567890 \
  --remove-allowed-principals arn:aws:iam::666666666666:root

# Removing permission does not drop existing endpoints; reject them explicitly
aws ec2 reject-vpc-endpoint-connections \
  --service-id vpce-svc-0fares1234567890 \
  --vpc-endpoint-ids vpce-0partner66endpoint

VPC peering

  • Adding a CIDR to a peered VPC works only if it does not overlap the peer; then add routes on both sides.
  • Deleting a peering or a referenced security group leaves stale rules; find them with describe-stale-security-groups.
  • Keep DNS resolution enabled for inter-Region peers.
  • Watch the 125-peer limit and route table size as the mesh grows; move to a Transit Gateway when it does.

Inter- and intra-Region patterns

Knowledge 3.1.5Available inter-Regional and intra-Regional communication patterns

Within a Region

VPC peering (lowest latency, free within an AZ), Transit Gateway, VPC sharing, PrivateLink and Lattice. Cross-AZ traffic costs $0.01 per GB in each direction, so keep chatty tiers in the same AZ (matched by AZ ID across accounts) where resilience allows.

Between Regions

  • Inter-Region VPC peering (MTU 8500, encrypted).
  • Transit Gateway peering with static routes.
  • Cloud WAN with automatic dynamic routing.
  • Cross-Region PrivateLink, for endpoint services since November 2024 and for some AWS services (S3, KMS, Lambda, ECR and others) since November 2025, with Regional DNS only.
  • Direct Connect gateway (a data center reaches VPCs in any Region from one location) and SiteLink (site to site across the backbone).
  • Global Accelerator for users entering AWS near them.

Inter-Region transfer is charged on the sending side (about $0.02 per GB between US Regions; varies by Region pair), plus Transit Gateway processing on the sending Transit Gateway. Direct Connect data out is charged by source Region and location (for example $0.02 per GB from a US Region to a US location); data in is free.

Directing traffic with route tables

Skill 3.1.3Using route tables to direct traffic appropriately (for example, automatic propagation, BGP)

  • VPC route tables: enable VGW route propagation so BGP routes from VPN and Direct Connect appear automatically (Direct Connect wins over VPN for the same prefix); add static routes for summaries, Transit Gateway targets, peering and appliances. Gateway endpoints add their own prefix list route.
  • Transit Gateway route tables: associate each attachment, enable propagation from VPC, VPN and Direct Connect gateway attachments into the tables that need them, and use static or blackhole routes to override. AWS recommends BGP VPNs (with ECMP) and propagation for Direct Connect gateway and VPN attachments.
  • Edge (gateway) route tables: send inbound internet or VGW traffic through an inspection endpoint.
  • Remember precedence: longest prefix first; then static over propagated; Transit Gateway prefers static, then VPC, Direct Connect gateway, Connect, VPN (Task 1.5).
Let a new private route table learn the data center routes from the VGW, and send S3 traffic through the gateway endpoint (AWS CLI) 
aws ec2 enable-vgw-route-propagation \
  --route-table-id rtb-analytics-private --gateway-id vgw-0analytics123

aws ec2 modify-vpc-endpoint --vpc-endpoint-id vpce-0s3gateway1234 \
  --add-route-table-ids rtb-analytics-private

A propagated route stays while the VPN reports UP, even if traffic behind it is broken, and a route table can hold only 100 propagated routes. When the data center advertises more, summarize on premises; the limit cannot be raised.

Access to AWS services

Skill 3.1.4Setting up private access or public access to AWS services (for example, Direct Connect, VPN)

From inside a VPC

  • Gateway endpoints (S3, DynamoDB): free, a prefix list route in chosen route tables, endpoint policies, same Region only. Use aws:VpcSourceIp or aws:SourceVpce in bucket policies, not aws:SourceIp. Creating one resets open connections to the service.
  • Interface endpoints for most other services (and S3 and DynamoDB too), with private DNS, security groups and an hourly plus per-GB charge.

From on premises

  • Gateway endpoints cannot be used from VPN, Direct Connect, peering or a Transit Gateway. Use an interface endpoint reached over a private or transit VIF or VPN.
  • The endpoint-specific names (for example bucket.vpce-...s3.us-east-1.vpce.amazonaws.com) resolve anywhere to private IPs; to use the normal service names, forward them to a Resolver inbound endpoint.
  • For S3, "private DNS only for inbound endpoints" sends on-premises queries to the interface endpoint while VPC clients keep using the free gateway endpoint. It requires a gateway endpoint in the VPC.
  • DynamoDB has had interface endpoints since March 2024; use the endpoint URL rather than a private hosted zone override.
  • A public VIF reaches every AWS public endpoint over Direct Connect using public addresses, with no VPC in the path; scope it with communities and filter on your routers.
An S3 interface endpoint for the data centers that leaves VPC traffic on the free gateway endpoint (AWS CLI) 
aws ec2 create-vpc-endpoint --vpc-endpoint-type Interface \
  --vpc-id vpc-shared-services \
  --service-name com.amazonaws.us-east-1.s3 \
  --subnet-ids subnet-shared-az1 subnet-shared-az2 \
  --security-group-ids sg-s3-endpoint \
  --private-dns-enabled \
  --dns-options PrivateDnsOnlyForInboundResolverEndpoint=true

"On-premises servers must reach S3 privately over the existing private VIF" points to an S3 interface endpoint (a gateway endpoint will not work). "Reach S3 and other public AWS endpoints over Direct Connect without VPCs" points to a public VIF.

Optimizing routing

Skill 3.1.5Optimizing routing over dynamic and static routing protocols (for example, summarizing routes, CIDR overlap)

  • Summarize in both directions: on premises, advertise one or a few aggregates per site; toward on premises, use Direct Connect gateway allowed prefixes for Transit Gateways (for example 10.40.0.0/14 per Region) instead of every VPC CIDR. This keeps BGP under prefix limits and route tables small, and lets VPCs come and go without BGP changes.
  • Plan addresses for summarization: allocate each Region and environment a contiguous block from IPAM, so summaries are possible and nothing overlaps.
  • Prefix lists keep large sets of routes and rules in one object: reference them from VPC and Transit Gateway route tables (each entry becomes a route) and from security groups.
  • Static compared with dynamic: static routes are predictable and never flap, but do not fail over; use them for summaries, blackholes and Transit Gateway peering, and BGP for anything that must fail over.
  • Longest prefix for steering: a more specific route over the preferred path overrides summaries elsewhere, for example a /24 over a VPN for one site while the /16 uses Direct Connect.
  • Overlap: summaries must not hide overlapping ranges; where overlap exists, use NAT or service-level access (Task 1.6).
One Transit Gateway route per data center range, maintained in a prefix list instead of many static routes (AWS CLI) 
aws ec2 create-managed-prefix-list --address-family IPv4 \
  --prefix-list-name onprem-us-dc1 --max-entries 10 \
  --entries Cidr=10.100.0.0/16,Description=dc1-core Cidr=10.101.0.0/16,Description=dc1-dmz

aws ec2 create-transit-gateway-prefix-list-reference \
  --transit-gateway-route-table-id tgw-rtb-spokes \
  --prefix-list-id pl-0onpremusdc1 \
  --transit-gateway-attachment-id tgw-attach-dxgw

Key takeaways

  • BGP operations: AS_PATH with 7224 twice is dropped (do not re-advertise AWS routes); stay under VIF and VPN prefix limits; use BFD; change prefixes on the backup path first and verify with route listing and failover tests.
  • Direct Connect maintenance: planned 14 days ahead in about 4 hours, emergency about 2 hours; AWS drains traffic and never maintains redundant connections together, but same-device connections fall together; it cannot be postponed.
  • Limits: single flow 5 Gbps (10 in a placement group, 25 with ENA Express); internet gateway 5 Gbps under 32 vCPUs; VPN 1.25 Gbps per tunnel; Transit Gateway 100 Gbps per attachment per AZ; NAT 55,000 connections per IP per destination; 100 propagated routes per VPC table; prefix list weights.
  • Custom services: maintain PrivateLink AZs, principals (reject to cut existing), DNS verification and proxy protocol; peering CIDR additions and stale rules. Public access via internet-facing load balancers, Global Accelerator or CloudFront VPC origins (both need an internet gateway on the VPC).
  • Regions: cross-AZ $0.01 per GB each way; inter-Region on the sending side; peering, Transit Gateway peering, Cloud WAN, cross-Region PrivateLink, Direct Connect gateway and SiteLink.
  • Route tables: VGW propagation, Transit Gateway associations and propagations, edge route tables; static beats propagated after longest prefix.
  • AWS services: gateway endpoints are free but VPC-only; on premises needs interface endpoints (or a public VIF); S3 private DNS only for inbound endpoints combines both.
  • Optimize: summarize both ways, allocate contiguous blocks, use prefix lists, BGP for failover and static for summaries, longest prefix for steering.

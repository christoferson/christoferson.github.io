Overview

Task 4.3

Implement and maintain confidentiality of data and communications of the network.

Encryption questions turn on two things: which hops are already encrypted by AWS (and under what conditions), and which control closes the remaining gap at the required strength and speed. Know where Nitro encryption stops, which links need MACsec or IPsec, and where TLS must be configured explicitly.

The travel company's PCI DSS scope requires every hop carrying card data to be encrypted: browser to CloudFront, CloudFront to the ALB, the ALB to the payment service, the service to the database, and the payment VPC to the data centers over Direct Connect.

Encryption options and responsibility

Knowledge 4.3.1Network encryption options that are available on AWS

Automatic, by AWS

  • Physical layer: all data crossing AWS Regions over the AWS global network, and all traffic between AZs, is encrypted before it leaves AWS secured facilities. Inter-Region VPC peering and Transit Gateway peering traffic is also bulk-encrypted as it leaves a Region.
  • Nitro automatic encryption between instances: AEAD with 256-bit keys, no performance cost, when both instances are supported types, in the same Region, in the same VPC or peered VPCs, and the traffic does not pass through a virtual network device or service such as a load balancer or Transit Gateway.

Configured by you

  • MACsec: layer 2 encryption on dedicated 10, 100 and 400 Gbps Direct Connect connections, at line rate.
  • IPsec: Site-to-Site VPN (internet, public VIF or private IP VPN over Direct Connect), or between your own EC2 appliances.
  • TLS: CloudFront, load balancers, API Gateway, databases, S3 and every AWS API (TLS 1.2 required, 1.3 recommended).
  • VPC Encryption Controls (November 2025): monitor or enforce that traffic in a VPC is encrypted.
Which instance types encrypt instance-to-instance traffic automatically (AWS CLI) 
aws ec2 describe-instance-types \
  --filters Name=network-info.encryption-in-transit-supported,Values=true \
  --query "InstanceTypes[].InstanceType" --output text

Knowledge 4.3.4Network encryption under the AWS shared responsibility model

AWS secures the infrastructure: physical links, the encryption it applies automatically, and its own service endpoints. The customer decides what must be encrypted and configures it: in AWS's words, "It is your responsibility to use an encryption protocol, such as Transport Layer Security (TLS), to encrypt sensitive data in transit between clients and your Amazon EC2 instances." That includes choosing TLS security policies, enforcing TLS on databases and buckets, managing certificates, turning on MACsec or VPNs for hybrid links, and accepting that a load balancer or Transit Gateway in the path ends Nitro's automatic protection.

Nitro encryption does not cover traffic that passes through a virtual network device or service such as a Transit Gateway or a load balancer. "Traffic between VPCs must be encrypted, through the Transit Gateway" therefore needs TLS in the application, IPsec, or Encryption Controls with Transit Gateway encryption support; it does not happen by default.

VPN over Direct Connect

Knowledge 4.3.2VPN connectivity over Direct Connect

Direct Connect is private but not encrypted. Ways to encrypt it (Task 1.5):

  • Private IP VPN: IPsec over a transit VIF to a Transit Gateway, with private outside addresses on both ends; no public VIF and no public IPs. Give the Transit Gateway a CIDR block and include it in the Direct Connect gateway's allowed prefixes.
  • VPN over a public VIF: the older method; the VPN endpoints are public addresses reached over Direct Connect instead of the internet, and the public VIF also exposes all AWS public endpoints to the data center.
  • Customer overlay: your own IPsec between on-premises routers and EC2 appliances over a private VIF.
  • MACsec instead, for line-rate layer 2 encryption on 10 Gbps and faster dedicated connections (not hosted connections or 1 Gbps).

Each VPN tunnel carries 1.25 Gbps (5 Gbps with Large Bandwidth Tunnels), so a 10 Gbps link encrypted with IPsec needs ECMP across several tunnels; MACsec avoids that limit.

Meeting compliance requirements

Knowledge 4.3.3Encryption methods for data in transit (for example, IPsec)

  • IPsec (layer 3): encrypts all IP traffic between two gateways; IKEv2 with AES-GCM and DH group 14 or higher; tunnel overhead lowers the MTU to about 1400.
  • TLS (layer 4 to 7): per connection, end to end between client and server, with certificates for authentication; mutual TLS also authenticates the client.
  • MACsec (layer 2): hop by hop on one link.
  • Application-level: CloudFront field-level encryption for selected form fields, or encrypting payloads before sending.

Skill 4.3.1Implementing network encryption methods to meet application compliance requirements (for example, IPsec, TLS)

  • Strong TLS: TLS 1.2 or 1.3 only, with forward secrecy; on load balancers and CloudFront choose policies such as ELBSecurityPolicy-TLS13-1-2-Res-PQ-2025-09 and TLSv1.2_2025 or TLSv1.3_2025.
  • FIPS 140: FIPS load balancer policies (...-FIPS-2023-04 or ...-FIPS-PQ-2025-09), FIPS service endpoints, Resolver DoH-FIPS inbound.
  • Post-quantum readiness: hybrid ML-KEM key exchange in the 2025 ELB policies and on CloudFront viewer connections (TLS 1.3).
  • Proof: VPC Encryption Controls in monitor mode adds an encryption-status field to flow logs (0 none, 1 Nitro, 2 application, 3 both); enforce mode blocks resources that allow unencrypted traffic (with a small list of exclusions, and not in VPCs with Gateway Load Balancer or Network Firewall).
  • Policy enforcement: SCPs or IAM conditions that limit which ELB security policies may be used; resource policies that require TLS.

Encrypting each service

Skill 4.3.2Implementing encryption solutions to secure data in transit (for example, CloudFront, Application Load Balancers and Network Load Balancers, VPN over Direct Connect, AWS managed databases, Amazon S3, custom solutions on Amazon EC2, Transit Gateway)

CloudFront

  • Viewer protocol policy per cache behavior: redirect-to-https (301 for GET and HEAD, 307 for other methods) or https-only (403 for HTTP).
  • Viewer certificate from ACM in us-east-1, with SNI (all security policies, HTTP/2 and HTTP/3) rather than a dedicated IP ($600 per month, legacy clients only).
  • Origin protocol policy https-only (or match viewer) with TLS 1.2 to the origin. The origin's certificate must match the origin domain and chain to a publicly trusted CA (Mozilla list); self-signed or private CA certificates cause 502 errors. An ALB origin can use an ACM certificate in its own Region.
  • Mutual TLS: viewer mTLS (required, optional or passthrough modes with a trust store), and origin mTLS (CloudFront presents a client certificate imported into ACM in us-east-1; it is not auto-renewed).
The parts of the booking distribution's configuration that enforce TLS end to end (CloudFront DistributionConfig, JSON excerpt)

Application and Network Load Balancers

  • Security policies: the console default is now ELBSecurityPolicy-TLS13-1-2-Res-PQ-2025-09 (TLS 1.3 and 1.2 with hybrid post-quantum key exchange), but the CLI, CloudFormation and CDK default is still ELBSecurityPolicy-2016-08, so set it explicitly in templates. The policy toward targets follows the listener's.
  • End to end: HTTPS target groups (ALB) or TLS target groups (NLB) re-encrypt to targets. Load balancers do not validate target certificates, so self-signed certificates on targets work.
  • Passthrough: an NLB TCP listener (or QUIC) leaves TLS to the targets, keeping the private key off the load balancer and allowing mTLS at the target.
  • ALB mutual TLS: verify mode with a trust store, or passthrough of the client certificate in headers.
  • Gateway Load Balancer has no TLS; it passes packets to appliances.
ALB listener with the recommended policy, re-encrypting to an HTTPS target group (AWS CLI) 
aws elbv2 create-target-group --name payment-https --protocol HTTPS --port 443 \
  --vpc-id vpc-0payment01 --target-type ip --health-check-protocol HTTPS

aws elbv2 create-listener \
  --load-balancer-arn arn:aws:elasticloadbalancing:us-east-1:444444444444:loadbalancer/app/payment-alb/50dc6c495c0c9188 \
  --protocol HTTPS --port 443 \
  --ssl-policy ELBSecurityPolicy-TLS13-1-2-Res-PQ-2025-09 \
  --certificates CertificateArn=arn:aws:acm:us-east-1:444444444444:certificate/1f2e3d4c-5555-6666-7777-888899990000 \
  --default-actions Type=forward,TargetGroupArn=arn:aws:elasticloadbalancing:us-east-1:444444444444:targetgroup/payment-https/6d0ecf831eec9f09

Managed databases and S3

  • RDS: require TLS with rds.force_ssl=1 (PostgreSQL, on by default from PostgreSQL 15; SQL Server, static so it needs a reboot) or require_secure_transport=ON (MySQL and MariaDB, dynamic). Clients trust the RDS root CA (default rds-ca-rsa2048-g1); RDS Proxy uses ACM certificates. Aurora uses the same parameters in its cluster parameter group.
  • DynamoDB: HTTPS only, TLS 1.2 or later.
  • ElastiCache: in-transit encryption with mode preferred then required for Valkey and Redis OSS (Memcached only at creation); no mTLS.
  • Redshift: require_SSL=true. DocumentDB: the tls cluster parameter (static).
  • S3: deny requests without TLS (aws:SecureTransport false) or below TLS 1.2 (s3:TlsVersion), adding aws:PrincipalIsAWSService false so AWS service calls are not blocked.
Refuse unencrypted or old-TLS access to the card-data bucket (S3 bucket policy, JSON) 
{
  "Version": "2012-10-17",
  "Statement": [{
    "Sid": "DenyInsecureTransport",
    "Effect": "Deny",
    "Principal": "*",
    "Action": "s3:*",
    "Resource": ["arn:aws:s3:::travel-card-data", "arn:aws:s3:::travel-card-data/*"],
    "Condition": {
      "Bool": {"aws:SecureTransport": "false", "aws:PrincipalIsAWSService": "false"}
    }
  }, {
    "Sid": "DenyOldTls",
    "Effect": "Deny",
    "Principal": "*",
    "Action": "s3:*",
    "Resource": ["arn:aws:s3:::travel-card-data", "arn:aws:s3:::travel-card-data/*"],
    "Condition": {
      "NumericLessThan": {"s3:TlsVersion": "1.2"},

}
Require TLS for the payment MySQL database (AWS CLI; dynamic, no reboot) 
aws rds modify-db-parameter-group --db-parameter-group-name payment-mysql8 \
  --parameters "ParameterName=require_secure_transport,ParameterValue=1,ApplyMethod=immediate"

Custom solutions on EC2

Terminate TLS on the instance (certificates from ACM private exports, Private CA, or your own CA), use mTLS between services (or VPC Lattice with TLS listeners), or run IPsec between instances or appliances when every protocol must be covered. Within a VPC, supported Nitro instances already encrypt traffic between them, but compliance usually still requires TLS for authentication.

Transit Gateway

Intra-Region Transit Gateway traffic is not encrypted by Nitro. Options: TLS in the applications, IPsec between appliances or as Site-to-Site VPN attachments, and Transit Gateway encryption support with VPC Encryption Controls (November 2025; not with Connect, peering, multicast and some other attachment types). GRE on Transit Gateway Connect is unencrypted. Cross-Region Transit Gateway peering is encrypted automatically.

Certificate management

Skill 4.3.3Implementing a certificate management solution by using a certificate authority (for example, ACM, AWS Private Certificate Authority [ACM PCA])

ACM public certificates

  • Free for integrated services (CloudFront, ELB, API Gateway and others); Regional, except that CloudFront needs us-east-1.
  • Validity is now 198 days (it was 395), heading to under 100 days in March 2027 and under 47 days in March 2029 under CA/Browser Forum rules; ACM renews them automatically 45 days before expiry.
  • DNS validation (a CNAME record that stays in place) allows unattended renewal. Email validation is being withdrawn: no new email-validated requests after 31 March 2027, no renewals after 30 September 2027.
  • Imported certificates are never renewed; monitor their expiry (AWS Health and EventBridge send notices at 30, 15, 7, 3 and 1 days).
  • Exportable public certificates ($7 per name, $79 per wildcard) can be installed on EC2 or on premises; ACM renews them but you redeploy.
  • ACME (2026) lets tools such as Certbot or cert-manager get 45-day certificates from ACM; these cannot be attached to load balancers or CloudFront.

AWS Private CA

  • Your own private hierarchy (root plus up to four subordinate levels) for internal TLS, mTLS client certificates, devices and code signing; Regional; RSA, ECDSA and ML-DSA (post-quantum) keys.
  • General-purpose mode: $400 per CA per month, any validity, revocation by CRL (published to S3) or OCSP.
  • Short-lived certificate mode: $50 per CA per month, certificates valid up to 7 days so revocation is unnecessary; ACM cannot issue from it.
  • Certificate charges are tiered ($0.75 each for the first 1,000 per month, falling sharply after); private certificates used only on integrated services, with keys you never see, are free.
  • ACM issues and renews private certificates from a Private CA for load balancers and CloudFront origins inside the organization, and they can be exported for instances.
  • Connectors issue certificates to Active Directory, SCEP devices and Kubernetes (cert-manager).
A private certificate for the internal payment API from the company's issuing CA, and a public one with DNS validation (AWS CLI) 
# Private: issued by AWS Private CA through ACM, renewed by ACM
aws acm request-certificate --domain-name payment-api.aws.example.com \
  --certificate-authority-arn arn:aws:acm-pca:us-east-1:111111111111:certificate-authority/0a1b2c3d-4e5f-6789-abcd-ef0123456789

# Public: free, validated by a CNAME record left in place for renewals
aws acm request-certificate --domain-name booking.example.com \
  --subject-alternative-names www.booking.example.com \
  --validation-method DNS --key-algorithm EC_prime256v1

CloudFront needs its viewer certificate from ACM in us-east-1, and its origins need publicly trusted certificates: a Private CA certificate on an ALB origin causes 502 errors through CloudFront. Load balancers, by contrast, accept any certificate on their targets.

Secure DNS

Knowledge 4.3.5Security methods for DNS communications (for example, DNSSEC)

  • DNSSEC protects integrity, not confidentiality: signed answers let resolvers detect forged or altered records. Route 53 signs public zones and the VPC Resolver can validate (Task 2.3).
  • DNS over HTTPS (DoH) encrypts queries between resolvers. Resolver inbound and outbound endpoints support DoH (and DoH-FIPS inbound only), but not DNS over TLS.
  • Route 53 Global Resolver accepts Do53, DoH and DNS over TLS from authorized branch and remote clients, with DNSSEC validation and filtering.
  • DNS Firewall blocks lookups of malicious domains and DNS tunneling; it works on queries through the VPC Resolver only.
  • Inside a VPC, queries to the Amazon DNS server do not leave AWS's network; hybrid paths are where DoH or an encrypted link matters.

Skill 4.3.4Implementing secure DNS communications

  1. Sign public zonesDNSSEC on example.com with the DS record at the registrar, and alarms on the DNSSEC metrics.
  2. ValidateTurn on DNSSEC validation for VPCs that resolve public names.
  3. Encrypt hybrid queriesResolver endpoints with DoH between the data center DNS servers and AWS (the data center servers must support DoH), or keep DNS on the encrypted Direct Connect or VPN path.
  4. FilterDNS Firewall rule groups through a shared Route 53 Profile, failing closed for regulated VPCs.
  5. LogResolver query logs to the log archive for investigations.
An inbound Resolver endpoint that accepts DNS over HTTPS from the data centers, alongside plain DNS during migration (AWS CLI) 
aws route53resolver create-resolver-endpoint --creator-request-id in-doh-1 \
  --name dns-inbound-doh --direction INBOUND \
  --security-group-ids sg-dns-inbound-doh \
  --ip-addresses SubnetId=subnet-shared-az1 SubnetId=subnet-shared-az2 \
  --protocols Do53 DoH

The endpoint's security group must allow TCP 443 for DoH as well as TCP and UDP 53. To move fully to DoH, enable both protocols, move clients, then remove Do53.

Key takeaways

  • AWS encrypts physically between Regions and AZs; Nitro encrypts instance-to-instance traffic only for supported types on both ends, same Region, same or peered VPC, with no load balancer, Transit Gateway or other virtual network device in between. Everything else is yours to configure.
  • Direct Connect is not encrypted: MACsec (10 Gbps and up, dedicated), private IP VPN over a transit VIF, VPN over a public VIF, or your own overlay; IPsec tunnels cap at 1.25 or 5 Gbps each.
  • CloudFront: redirect-to-https or https-only, SNI certificate in us-east-1, TLSv1.2_2025 or TLSv1.3_2025, https-only to origins with publicly trusted certificates; viewer and origin mTLS.
  • Load balancers: console default policy TLS13-1-2-Res-PQ-2025-09, but API default 2016-08; HTTPS or TLS target groups for end to end (target certificates not validated); NLB TCP passthrough for TLS at the target.
  • Databases and S3: rds.force_ssl or require_secure_transport, ElastiCache preferred then required, Redshift require_SSL; S3 deny on aws:SecureTransport and s3:TlsVersion.
  • Transit Gateway does not encrypt intra-Region traffic; use TLS, IPsec or Encryption Controls with Transit Gateway encryption support.
  • ACM: free public certificates, now 198 days, DNS validation (email ending 2027), automatic renewal 45 days before expiry; imported certificates are not renewed. Private CA: $400 general-purpose or $50 short-lived (7-day certificates).
  • DNS: DNSSEC for integrity; DoH on Resolver endpoints (no DoT; DoH-FIPS inbound only); DoT through Global Resolver; DNS Firewall for filtering.

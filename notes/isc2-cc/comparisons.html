CIA triad

Domain 1: Security Principles. Each property answers a different question about information. Covered in objective 1.1.

PropertyProtects againstTypical controls
ConfidentialityUnauthorized disclosureAccess control, encryption, classification
IntegrityUnauthorized modification or destructionHashing, digital signatures, change control
AvailabilityLoss of timely, reliable accessBackups, redundancy, denial-of-service protection

Identification to accounting

Every access decision passes through the same four steps. Covered in objective 1.1.

StepQuestion it answersStore example
IdentificationWho do you claim to be?Typing a username
AuthenticationCan you prove it?Password plus a phone prompt
AuthorizationWhat may you do?Refunds up to $500, no price changes
AccountingWhat did you do?Each refund logged with name and time

Ways to assess risk

NIST SP 800-30 describes three ways to express the size of a risk. Covered in objective 1.2.

ApproachHow it measuresStrength and weakness
QualitativeDescriptive levels, such as very low to very highQuick and easy to explain; different experts may disagree
Semi-quantitativeScores or bins, such as 1 to 10Easy to rank; numbers only mean something inside the assessment
QuantitativeMoney and frequencyBest for cost-benefit decisions; needs good data

Risk responses

What to do about a risk once it is assessed, guided by appetite and tolerance. Covered in objective 1.2.

ResponseWhat it meansRetailer example
AcceptDo nothing more because the risk is within tolerance; keep monitoringGuest Wi-Fi occasionally going down
AvoidStop or never start the activity that creates the riskStop storing card numbers at all
MitigateAdd controls to reduce likelihood or impactMFA, backups, segmentation
Transfer or shareMove part of the financial impact to another partyCyber insurance; a payment processor holding card data

Control categories

The first way to classify a control is by how it works. Covered in objective 1.4.

CategoryHow it worksExamples
AdministrativePolicies, procedures and decisions that manage peopleSeparation of duties, background checks, training
TechnicalEnforced by hardware and softwareMFA, firewall rules, encryption, logging
PhysicalProtects facilities, equipment and peopleLocks, badges, guards, cameras, fire suppression

Control functions

The second way to classify a control is by what it does; every control has a category and a function. Covered in objective 1.4.

FunctionWhat it doesExample
PreventiveStops the event even if someone triesA locked door, MFA
DeterrentDiscourages the attacker from tryingLogin warning banner, visible cameras
DetectiveWarns of an attempted or successful eventLog alerts, door alarm, audit
CorrectiveLimits the damage after the eventRemoving malware, restoring from backup
CompensatingStands in for a control that cannot be usedExtra monitoring of an old till that cannot run MFA
Recovery (industry term)Restores normal operationsBackups, disaster recovery sites

Governing documents

From broad intent to detailed steps. Covered in objective 1.3.

DocumentWhat it doesBinding?
Law or regulationSet by government; applies whether the organization likes it or notYes, legally
PolicyManagement's intent: the what and whyYes
Standard (internal)Specific required technologies or settingsYes
ProcedureStep-by-step how-to for a taskYes, for that task
GuidelineAdvice that can be followed in more than one wayNo, recommended
BaselineMinimum security settings for a type of systemYes, as a minimum

RoleJobAt the retailer

Continuity and response plans

NIST SP 800-34 describes several related plans that work together. Covered in objective 2.2 and objective 5.3.

PlanFocusTriggered by
Business continuity plan (BCP)Keeping business processes runningAny significant disruption
Disaster recovery plan (DRP)Restoring IT at an alternate site; tied to a siteLoss of a facility or major failure
Incident response plan (IRP)Handling a security incidentA declared cybersecurity incident
Continuity of operations (COOP)US government essential functions at an alternate site, up to 30 daysDisruption to a federal organization
Occupant emergency plan (OEP)People's safety in a building; carried out firstFire, threat or other emergency

MTD, RTO and RPO

The three numbers a business impact analysis sets for each process. Covered in objective 2.2.

TargetMeasuresRule to remember
Maximum tolerable downtime (MTD)How long the process can be down before serious harmA business decision by the process owner
Recovery time objective (RTO)How long systems can take to recoverMust be shorter than the MTD
Recovery point objective (RPO)How much recent data can be lostAbout data, not time offline; not part of MTD

Alternate sites

Cost against speed of recovery, from SP 800-34. Covered in objective 2.2.

SiteWhat is readyCost and speed
ColdSpace, power and cooling; no equipmentCheapest; slowest
WarmPartly equippedMedium; medium
HotFully operational, ready to take overMedium to high; short setup
MirroredFully redundant with real-time mirroringMost expensive; close to 100% availability
MobileSelf-contained, transportable unitsOften available within 24 hours

Backup types

The trade-off is between backup time and restore time. Covered in objective 2.2.

TypeWhat it copiesTo restore you need
FullEverything, every timeJust the last full backup
IncrementalChanges since the last backup of any typeLast full plus every incremental since
DifferentialChanges since the last full backupLast full plus the latest differential

From least to most disruptive, as listed in SP 800-53 CP-4. Covered in objective 2.2.

TestWhat happensRisk to operations
Checklist reviewTeams check their part of the plan is complete and currentNone
Walk-through or tabletopTalking through a scenario; no systems touchedNone
SimulationProcedures acted out while production keeps runningLow
ParallelAlternate site brought up alongside the primaryLow to moderate
Full interruptionPrimary shut down; business runs on the alternate siteHighest

Phishing variants

The variants differ in channel and target. Covered in objective 2.3.

VariantChannel or targetTypical lure
Spear phishingSpecific people or groups, using details about themA message that mentions a real project
WhalingSenior executivesA legal or board matter
VishingVoice calls and voicemail"IT support" asking for a code
SmishingText messages and chat apps"Delivery failed" link
Business email compromiseFinance staff, via compromised or look-alike emailChanged supplier bank details

KPI and KRI

Both are metrics; they answer different questions. Covered in objective 2.4.

IndicatorQuestion it answersExample
KPIAre we making progress toward intended results?Percentage of critical patches applied on time
KRIIs a risk approaching what we can accept?Critical vulnerabilities open past a set number of days
Leading indicatorIs something likely to happen?Open for 10 days against a 14-day tolerance
Lagging indicatorHas it already happened?Open for 15 days: tolerance breached

Least privilege, separation of duties and need to know

Three related principles that limit what any one identity can do. Covered in objective 3.2.

PrincipleLimitsExample
Least privilegeAccess to the minimum a task needs, for the minimum timeAdmin role granted for two hours, then removed
Separation of dutiesAny one person completing a sensitive task aloneOne person enters a payment, another approves it
Need to knowInformation to what duties requireA store manager sees their own store's sales only
Dual authorizationA critical step to two people acting togetherTwo-person control over production changes

Firewall types

From least to most inspection, per NIST SP 800-41. Covered in objective 4.1.

TypeWhat it inspectsNote
Packet filterAddresses, ports and protocol of each packetStateless; no memory of connections
Stateful inspectionPackets plus the state of each connectionAllows replies only to connections started inside
Application firewallThe application protocol itselfDeep packet inspection
Application proxyFull traffic; talks to each side separatelyNever allows a direct connection
Web application firewallWeb requests in front of a web serverBlocks attacks such as SQL injection
Host-basedTraffic to one computerProtects even from devices on the same network

IDS and IPS

Detection versus prevention, and the two detection methods from SP 800-94. Covered in objective 4.1.

OptionWhat it doesWeakness
IDSDetects and alertsSomeone must act on the alert
IPSDetects and tries to stop the activityA false positive can block legitimate traffic
Signature-basedMatches known attack patternsLargely misses new attacks
Anomaly-based (including AI)Compares activity with normal behaviourMore false positives; needs tuning

ProtocolStatusNote

WPAObsoleteA stopgap after WEP

WPA3Current; mandatory for new certified devicesSAE resists password guessing; protected management frames

Ways to separate networks

TechniqueHow it separatesLimit

Defense in depth and zero trust

Two complementary architecture ideas. Covered in objective 4.2.

IdeaCore principleIn practice
Defense in depthMultiple, diverse layers so no single control is the only barrierTraining, email filtering, MFA, endpoint, network, data, monitoring
Zero trustNo trust from network location; verify every requestPer-session, least-privilege access decided by a policy decision point
Perimeter modelTrust anything inside the networkOnce inside, lateral movement is unhindered

Masking and de-identification

Domain 5: Security Operations and Incident Response. Ways to hide or replace sensitive values. Covered in objective 5.1.

TechniqueWhat it doesReversible or linkable?
MaskingRemoves or replaces a value, such as asterisksNo; the value is not usable
TokenizationSwaps the value for a random token, real value kept in a vaultOnly via the token vault
PseudonymizationReplaces identifiers with pseudonymsRecords still linkable to each other
AnonymizationRemoves the association with the person completelyNot meant to be; re-identification can still happen
RedactionRemoves information from a document before releaseNo

Clear, purge, destroy

The three sanitization levels in NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 2. Covered in objective 5.1.

LevelProtects againstMedia reusable?
ClearSimple recovery through the normal interfaceYes; for internal reuse
PurgeState-of-the-art laboratory recovery (includes cryptographic erase)Yes
DestroyState-of-the-art laboratory recoveryNo; shredded, pulverized, melted or burned

Symmetric, asymmetric, hashing

Three building blocks with different jobs. Covered in objective 5.1.

TechniqueKeysUsed for
Symmetric (AES)One shared secret key; fastBulk data; key distribution is the problem
Asymmetric (RSA, ECC)Public and private key pair; slowKey exchange, digital signatures, certificates
Hashing (SHA-2, SHA-3)No key; one-way, fixed-length outputIntegrity checks; salted slow hashes for passwords

Overview

A quick lookup for the 273 key terms across the five CC domains. Meanings are short summaries in our own words, with the source named where a standard defines the term (for example NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3 or SP 800-145); the objective pages give the full detail and quotes. The exam outline itself is on isc2.org.

Core security concepts

ReferenceThe properties security protects and how users prove who they are.

Domain 1, objective 1.1.

TermMeaningCovered in
ConfidentialityKeeping information from people and systems not authorized to see it (FISMA definition, via NIST)1.1
IntegrityGuarding against improper modification or destruction of information, including non-repudiation and authenticity (FISMA)1.1
AvailabilityTimely and reliable access to and use of information and systems1.1
CIA triadConfidentiality, integrity and availability together; FIPS 199 rates the impact of losing each as low, moderate or high1.1
High water markA system takes the highest impact level of any of its information types1.1
IdentificationClaiming an identity, for example typing a username1.1
AuthenticationVerifying the claimed identity of a user, process or device1.1
AuthorizationDeciding what a verified identity is allowed to do1.1
AccountingRecording who did what and when, so actions can be traced1.1
Multi-factor authentication (MFA)Two or more distinct factor types: something you know, have or are1.1
Phishing-resistant MFAAuthentication that cannot be relayed to a fake site (FIDO/WebAuthn, smart cards); codes typed by the user never qualify1.1
Non-repudiationProtection against someone falsely denying they performed an action (SP 800-63-4); provided by digital signatures, not by MACs1.1
Digital signatureA value made with the signer's private key and checked with the public key, proving origin and integrity1.1
PrivacyPeople's control over information about themselves; broader than confidentiality1.1
Personally identifiable information (PII)Information that can identify a person, alone or combined with other data1.1

Risk management

ReferenceThe vocabulary for describing, assessing and treating risk.

Domain 1, objective 1.2.

TermMeaningCovered in
RiskThe combination of the likelihood that a threat exploits a vulnerability and the impact if it does1.2
ThreatAny circumstance or event that could harm operations, assets or people1.2, 5.2
VulnerabilityA weakness that a threat source could exploit or trigger1.2, 5.5
AssetSomething of value to the organization: data, systems, people, facilities, reputation1.2, 5.4
Likelihood and impactHow probable a harmful event is, and how bad its consequences would be1.2
Qualitative and quantitative assessmentRating risk with categories (high, medium, low) versus money values1.2
Single loss expectancy (SLE)Expected loss from one occurrence; commonly taught as asset value times exposure factor1.2
Annualized loss expectancy (ALE)Expected yearly loss; commonly taught as SLE times annualized rate of occurrence (ARO)1.2
Inherent and residual riskRisk before controls, and the risk that remains after them (never zero)1.2
Risk appetiteThe amount and type of risk an organization is willing to take to meet its objectives1.2, 2.4
Risk toleranceHow much variation from the appetite the organization will bear for a specific risk1.2, 2.4
Risk responsesAccept, avoid, mitigate, or transfer (share); transferring does not remove the harm1.2
Risk registerA record of identified risks with owner, rating, response and status1.2
Risk ownerThe person accountable for managing a particular risk1.2
Risk Management Framework (RMF)NIST's process for categorizing, selecting, implementing, assessing, authorizing and monitoring controls1.2
Continuous monitoring (ISCM)Maintaining ongoing awareness of security, vulnerabilities and threats to support risk decisions1.2, 5.2
AI Risk Management FrameworkNIST's voluntary framework for AI risk, with four functions: Govern, Map, Measure, Manage1.2

Business continuity plan (BCP)Instructions for keeping business processes going during and after a significant disruption (SP 800-34)2.2

Maximum tolerable downtime (MTD)How long a process can be down before the harm becomes serious2.2
Recovery time objective (RTO)How long systems can take to recover; must be shorter than the MTD2.2
Recovery point objective (RPO)The point in time to which data must be recovered; how much data loss is acceptable2.2
Hot, warm and cold sitesFully ready (fast, costly); partly equipped; space and power only (cheap, slow)2.2

Full, incremental, differential backupEverything; changes since the last backup of any type; changes since the last full backup2.2
3-2-1 ruleThree copies, two media types, one offsite; a rule of thumb2.2
Tabletop, parallel and full interruption testsDiscussion of a scenario; alternate site run alongside production; production actually shut down2.2

All five are delivered by Pearson VUE (online or at a test center), are valid for 12 months, have no formal prerequisites, and are booked through the Anthropic Partner Academy by people at Claude Partner Network organizations. The certifications page singles out only the Associate as not counting toward partner tier eligibility, and suggests starting at Foundations and moving to Professional where available. On 19 November 2026 Developer – Professional had its own Partner Academy page but wasn't yet on the page's public list.

Who each exam is for

The guides place their candidates on two lines. Along one, the Associate uses Claude for business work, the Developer builds with it, and the Architect designs whole systems. Along the other, Foundations proves you can do the work and Professional that you can own it in production or for an organization. The updated guides describe each audience by what holders can show and the setting they work in; unlike the earlier versions, they no longer list who an exam isn't meant for.

Associate – Foundations

For professionals who use Claude as a core productivity tool and build Claude Projects: business analysts, project managers and operations leads; marketing, communications, HR and education professionals; consultants and team leads supporting AI adoption; and knowledge workers. The guide calls them intentional operators rather than casual users, accountable for their AI-assisted work and guided by Anthropic's four AI Fluency competencies (Delegation, Description, Discernment and Diligence). Holders can show that they can:

  • turn business objectives into well-specified Claude interactions, break down complex work and diagnose weak prompts;
  • evaluate, verify and adapt Claude's output before using it;
  • choose the right capability (Projects, Artifacts, Skills, research, code-executed analysis, connectors, memory), model and context strategy for a task;
  • design, configure and maintain repeatable workflows for themselves and their teams.

Developer – Foundations

For technical professionals who build agents and workflows, integrate Claude into applications and ship Claude-powered products: software engineers, AI/ML engineers, full-stack and back-end developers, and solutions engineers. Candidates typically have one to five years of software engineering, at least six months with Claude, and Python or TypeScript. Holders can show that they can:

  • choose between workflow and agentic designs and build Agent SDK agents, harnesses and hooks that enforce required actions;
  • integrate Claude through the Messages API and SDKs, with tool use, streaming, structured output, caching, batches and resilient error handling;
  • operate and extend Claude Code: configuration precedence, permission modes, Skills, plugins, hooks and subagents;
  • build evaluations, including calibrated LLM-as-judge harnesses and golden sets, and diagnose failures from traces;
  • choose model tiers and thinking options and optimise cost and latency;
  • engineer prompts and context, harden output parsing and ground answers in supplied documents;
  • defend against prompt injection with layered, deterministic guardrails, least privilege and correct credential handling;
  • build custom tools and MCP servers, and choose among built-in tools, custom tools, Skills and MCP.

Developer – Professional

For engineers who own Claude systems in production: senior and staff software engineers, platform and site reliability engineers, and solutions engineers accountable for the reliability, quality, cost and safety of live applications, often in healthcare, financial services, government or enterprise SaaS. They answer for service level objectives, respond to incidents, control spend across tenants, and increasingly direct fleets of coding agents. The guide expects at least one tool-using agent already in production. Holders can show that they can:

  • instrument Claude systems with tracing, metrics and logging, and monitor quality, cost and drift;
  • diagnose complex failures across multi-turn and multi-agent systems and lead incident response;
  • engineer evaluation systems, including calibrated LLM-as-judge pipelines, regression suites and CI/CD evaluation gates;
  • design resilient, high-throughput integration layers with progressive delivery of prompt and model changes;
  • design context, memory and retrieval architectures for long-running, knowledge-grounded agents;
  • optimise cost and latency across a fleet with model routing, caching strategy and latency engineering;
  • implement layered runtime guardrails and defence in depth for tool-using agents;
  • orchestrate durable multi-agent systems, and operate fleets of coding agents with verification, AI code review and unattended runs.

Architect – Foundations

For a solution architect who designs and implements production applications with Claude, with six months or more of practice building with the API, Agent SDK, Claude Code and MCP and a grasp of what models can and can't do in production. Where the Developer guide stresses shipping code, this one stresses design decisions and their trade-offs inside agent systems. The guide expects hands-on experience with:

  • agent applications on the Agent SDK: multi-agent orchestration, delegating to subagents, tool integration and lifecycle hooks;
  • configuring Claude Code for a team with CLAUDE.md files, Agent Skills, MCP servers and plan mode;
  • designing MCP tools and resources that connect to back-end systems;
  • prompts that give reliable structured output, with JSON schemas, few-shot examples and extraction patterns;
  • managing context across long documents, multi-turn conversations and handoffs between agents;
  • Claude in CI/CD pipelines for code review, test generation and pull request feedback;
  • escalation and reliability: error handling, human-in-the-loop workflows and self-evaluation.

Its guide also lists topics that are out of scope, such as streaming, vision, caching details, rate limits, pricing and cloud providers, which the Developer exams cover.

Architect – Professional

For mid- to senior-level solution architects, AI/ML engineers, technical leads and senior software engineers who own or shape the whole life cycle of a Claude system, from discovery to deployment and operation. Candidates typically have three or more years in systems architecture or platform engineering and at least six months running Claude in production. They lead architecture decisions, advise clients or internal teams, engage security, legal and executive stakeholders on attack surfaces and risk, and review code, logs and architecture diagrams. Holders can show that they can:

  • design end-to-end enterprise architectures under regulatory, security and cost constraints and lead the decisions through to deployment;
  • choose models, architectures, deployment surfaces and API patterns for a use case;
  • apply prompt engineering and context strategies;
  • integrate Claude into production systems with APIs, orchestration tools and data pipelines;
  • use evaluation, monitoring and observability to keep quality up;
  • build security, compliance and governance into the design;
  • work with other teams and explain architecture decisions.

What sets each exam apart

Topics that one exam treats as a skill of its own, where the others touch them lightly or not at all.

The Associate

  • Claude's own app features: Projects, Artifacts, research, code-executed analysis, Skills, connectors, memory and incognito chats.
  • Deciding, step by step, which parts of a business workflow Claude may run, which a person keeps and which a person reviews, and recognising over-delegation.
  • Adapting output for an audience and choosing its format, and explaining what Claude can and can't do to colleagues with little AI experience.
  • The heaviest weights of any exam on judging output (20%) and on responsible use and data protection (17%).

Developer – Foundations

  • Messages API mechanics in code: streaming events, stop reasons, vision, thinking, caching and structured output.
  • Finding defects in integration code: retry and fallback logic that ignores error classes, mishandled stream events, and files sent in the wrong block type.
  • How Claude interprets instructions on each surface (claude.ai, the API, the Agent SDK, Claude Code) and what crosses the application boundary.

Developer – Professional

  • Telemetry, production monitoring and alerting, and trace analysis at scale.
  • Incident response and learning loops, and durable execution and state for long-running agents.
  • Online quality measurement, alongside offline evaluation suites.
  • Fleets of coding agents: parallel orchestration, AI code review, and unattended, event-driven runs.

Architect – Foundations

  • Scenario-framed questions throughout, from a published set of six production scenarios.
  • Deep agent orchestration: agentic loops, coordinators and subagents, hooks, sessions and task decomposition.
  • Claude Code for teams and CI in depth (a fifth of the exam), and reliability topics such as error propagation and information provenance.

Architect – Professional

  • Choosing deployment surfaces and agent runtimes, and value-based architecture decisions.
  • Compliance obligations turned into architectural controls, and identifying where bias enters a system.
  • Architecture decision records, unsupportable commitments in requirements and SLAs, and complete operational handoffs.

Is the Associate exam easier?

Not in any way the guides state. From 7 December 2026 the Associate exam has the same format and scaled pass mark as the others: 70 items (60 scored) in 120 minutes and 720 out of 1,000. Anthropic doesn't publish relative difficulty, and no guide calls one exam easier than another. What differs is the role and the kind of knowledge tested:

  • It needs no technical knowledge. No code, API, SDK, MCP servers or CI. That makes it more accessible to non-developers, and its fee is lower.
  • Its hard parts are judgement calls. Its largest domains ask whether an answer is fit for purpose, whether it contains fabricated or unsupported content, how much review a piece of work needs before it's used, and whether a use is appropriate under policy and data-protection rules. These can't be looked up as facts.
  • It's broad. Six domains span prompting, output review, capability and model choice, workflow design, configuration and responsible use.

So a strong developer who is weak at reviewing output or applying privacy and policy rules could find the Associate exam harder than the Developer one, and the reverse is just as likely. The real difference between the exams is which things you need to know, not how hard each one is.

Which to take

  • You use Claude daily in business work and don't write code: Associate. It covers what you already do, at the depth of a skilled user; note that it doesn't count toward Partner Network tiers.
  • You write the application code, call the API and ship features: Developer – Foundations.
  • You run Claude systems in production, own their reliability and spend, respond to incidents or supervise coding agents: Developer – Professional, typically after Foundations.
  • You design agent systems and decide how tools, Claude Code and context fit together: Architect – Foundations.
  • You lead architecture, own governance and compliance, and advise stakeholders: Architect – Professional, typically after Foundations.
  • You do several of these: Developer – Foundations and Architect – Foundations overlap heavily on agents, tools, Claude Code and prompting, and the two Professional exams share evaluation, integration, observability and security. The Associate overlaps with the others mainly on prompting, model choice and judging output.

In short

  • All five have 120 minutes and a pass mark of 720. From 7 December 2026 every exam except Architect – Foundations (60 items) has 70 items, 60 of them scored; fees are $99 (Associate), $125 (both Foundations technical exams) and $175 (both Professional exams).
  • Associate: business users of the Claude apps; heaviest on judging output (20%), workflows (18%) and responsible use (17%); no code.
  • Developer – Foundations: building with the API, agents, Claude Code, tools and prompts. Developer – Professional: operating those systems in production, from observability and incidents to cost and coding-agent fleets.
  • Architect – Foundations: agent systems, Claude Code, tools and prompting, all scenario-based. Architect – Professional: end-to-end design, integration and retrieval, evaluation, governance and stakeholders.
  • "Easier" isn't a stated difference: the Associate exam needs no technical background, but its judgement-based content is demanding in its own way.

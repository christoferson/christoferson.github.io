How to use this guide

CCDV-F is for developers who build Claude-powered systems. Its exam guide lists eight weighted domains, each made up of skills; these notes will give each skill its own page, explained from Anthropic's documentation with working code. Skill numbers (1.1, 1.2, ...) follow the order in the guide.

About the exam in 2026. From 7 December 2026, CCDV-F has 70 scenario-based multiple-choice items in 120 minutes; 60 are scored, and 10 are unscored items that you can't tell apart from the rest. Until then, the exam has 53 items. The passing score is 720 on a scale of 100 to 1,000, the fee is US$125 per attempt, and the credential is valid for 12 months. It's delivered by Pearson VUE, online or at a test center, and only people at organizations in the Claude Partner Network can register. Both guides are now version 1.1: the current guide (November 2026) changed only the registration and support sections, and the guide for 7 December 2026 revises the objectives and weights. The guide recommends one to five years of software engineering, at least six months with Claude or similar systems, and Python or TypeScript.

Content is changing. This exam gets new content on 7 December 2026, and Anthropic published the updated exam guide on 17 November 2026. The outline below is from the updated guide, and the sample pages for Domains 2 and 5 have been rewritten for it; the other pages will follow.

Architect or developer? Many skills here (agents, tools and MCP, Claude Code, prompting) overlap with the Claude Certified Architect – Foundations notes. The developer exam adds the API mechanics, engineering and operations topics the architect exam leaves out. See Associate, Developer or Architect? for a comparison of all five Claude exams.

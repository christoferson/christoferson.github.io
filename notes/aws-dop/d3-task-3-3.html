Overview

Task statement 3.3

Implement automated recovery processes to meet RTO and RPO requirements.

High availability handles everyday failures. Disaster recovery (DR) handles the big ones: a Region outage, a deleted database, ransomware, a bad deployment that corrupts data. This task tests whether you can pick the cheapest strategy that still meets the recovery targets, automate the recovery, and test it before you need it. Typical scenarios: "RPO 15 minutes and RTO 1 hour at the lowest cost", "backups must be copied to another Region and account and must not be deletable", or "prove the database failover works".

Disaster recovery concepts: RTO and RPO

Knowledge

Assume a database fails at 10:00. The last backup that can be restored was taken at 09:45, and service is back at 11:00.

  • RPO (recovery point objective): the maximum acceptable data loss, measured backwards from the failure. Here, 15 minutes of data (09:45 to 10:00) was lost. To lower RPO, replicate or back up more often.
  • RTO (recovery time objective): the maximum acceptable downtime, measured forwards from the failure. Here it took 1 hour (10:00 to 11:00). To lower RTO, keep more of the environment ready to run.

Lower RTO and RPO always cost more, so they come from a business impact analysis: what does an hour of downtime, or an hour of lost orders, actually cost?

Replication is not a backup

Replication (Multi-AZ, global tables, S3 replication) copies every change, including deletions, corruption and encryption by ransomware. It protects against infrastructure failure, not against bad data. You still need point-in-time backups, ideally immutable and in another account.

DisasterWhat protects you
Instance, disk or AZ failureHigh availability: Multi-AZ, Auto Scaling, health checks
Region outageCross-Region replication or backups plus a DR strategy
Accidental deletion or bad deployment that corrupts dataPoint-in-time recovery, versioning, backups
Ransomware or a compromised accountImmutable backups (Vault Lock, Object Lock) in a separate account

AWS Backup and recovery strategies

Knowledge

The four DR strategies

They range from cheap and slow to expensive and instant. Pick the cheapest one that meets both RTO and RPO.

StrategyRunning in the DR RegionRPORTOCost
Backup and restoreNothing; only backupsHours (time since the last backup)Hours (restore and rebuild)Lowest
Pilot lightData replicated live; other infrastructure defined but switched offSeconds to minutesTens of minutes (start and scale everything else)Low
Warm standbyA complete, working copy at reduced sizeSecondsMinutes (scale up and switch traffic)Medium
Multi-site active/activeFull production capacity, serving trafficNear zeroNear zeroHighest

Backup and restore

  • Data: AWS Backup copies to the DR Region; S3 replication or versioned copies.
  • Infrastructure: CloudFormation or CDK templates ready to deploy; AMIs and images copied.
  • Recovery: deploy the stacks, restore the data, switch DNS.

Pilot light

  • Data: replicated continuously (Aurora Global Database, an RDS cross-Region read replica, DynamoDB global tables).
  • Infrastructure: deployed but idle: Auto Scaling groups with a desired capacity of 0, load balancers and networking ready.
  • Recovery: promote the database, scale compute from zero, switch DNS.

Warm standby

  • Everything running, smaller: for example, 2 instances instead of 20, already passing health checks.
  • Recovery: promote the database, scale out to full size, switch traffic. Because it already works, you can test it continuously.

Multi-site active/active

  • Both Regions serve users, usually with DynamoDB global tables and Route 53 latency routing or Global Accelerator.
  • Recovery: stop sending traffic to the failed Region. Remaining Regions must have enough capacity (or scale fast enough) to take it all.
  • "Lowest cost" with an RTO of a day: backup and restore.
  • "Core database must be current, the rest can start within an hour": pilot light.
  • "Minutes of downtime, and the DR site must be tested regularly": warm standby.
  • "No downtime even if a Region fails": active/active.

In a pilot light or warm standby design, the DR Region's scale-up depends on the EC2 and Auto Scaling control plane working during the event. To be safe, check capacity and quotas in the DR Region in advance, and prefer warm standby when RTO is tight.

Knowledge

AWS Backup

AWS Backup centralizes backups for EC2, EBS, RDS, Aurora, DynamoDB, EFS, FSx, S3, Storage Gateway, DocumentDB, Neptune, Redshift, Timestream and more, across accounts and Regions.

ConceptMeaning
Backup planRules: schedule, start window, lifecycle (move to cold storage, then delete), and copy actions to vaults in other Regions or accounts
Resource assignmentWhich resources a plan covers, usually by tag (for example, backup: daily)
Backup vaultWhere recovery points are stored, encrypted with a KMS key, with an access policy
Vault LockWrite-once protection: in compliance mode, no one (not even the root user) can delete recovery points before their retention ends or change the lock
Logically air-gapped vaultA vault isolated from your account's own credentials, shareable to a recovery account for ransomware recovery
Continuous backupPoint-in-time recovery (to the second, within 35 days) for RDS, Aurora, S3 and SAP HANA

Restore testingScheduled automatic restores that check recovery points actually work, and measure restore time

Recovery points moved to cold storage must stay there for at least 90 days. A lifecycle rule must keep total retention at least 90 days longer than the time before the move to cold storage.

Knowledge

AWS Elastic Disaster Recovery

Elastic Disaster Recovery (AWS DRS) protects servers (on-premises, in other clouds, or EC2 in another Region) with continuous block-level replication:

  1. Install the replication agentOn each source server.
  2. Replicate to a staging areaData flows continuously to low-cost staging resources (small replication servers and EBS volumes) in the recovery Region.
  3. Drill without disruptionLaunch recovery instances for a test at any time, while replication continues.
  4. RecoverLaunch full-size recovery instances from the latest state (RPO of seconds) or a chosen earlier point, typically within minutes.
  5. Fail backReplicate the recovered instances back to the original location when it's healthy again.

"Lift-and-shift servers that can't be redesigned need an RPO of seconds and an RTO of minutes, at low standby cost" points to AWS Elastic Disaster Recovery.

Recovery procedures

Knowledge

A recovery procedure (runbook) must be written down, automated as far as possible, and practised. A complete one covers:

  1. Detect and declareAlarms and health checks detect the failure. A named person decides to invoke DR, so the team doesn't argue during an outage.
  2. Recover dataPromote replicas, fail over global databases, or restore from backup to a chosen point in time.
  3. Recover computeDeploy or scale up the stacks in the recovery location.
  4. Redirect trafficRoute 53 failover, ARC routing controls or Global Accelerator weights.
  5. ValidateSynthetic canaries, smoke tests and business checks confirm it works.
  6. Fail backResynchronize data to the original location and move traffic back in a planned window.
  7. ReviewRecord the real RTO and RPO achieved and fix whatever slowed recovery.
  • Automate it: as a Systems Manager Automation runbook or a Step Functions workflow, so recovery is fast and repeatable.
  • Keep configuration ready: DR-Region values in Parameter Store and Secrets Manager replicas.

Point-in-time recovery for bad data

ServiceRecovery from accidental change or deletion
RDS and AuroraPoint-in-time restore to any second within the backup retention (up to 35 days), as a new instance or cluster; the latest restorable time is usually within 5 minutes
Aurora MySQLBacktrack: rewind the existing cluster in place to an earlier time, in minutes, without a restore
DynamoDBPoint-in-time recovery to any second in the last 35 days (configurable), restored to a new table; on-demand backups for long-term retention
S3Versioning (restore a previous version or remove a delete marker); Object Lock to prevent deletion
EBSSnapshots; the Recycle Bin recovers deleted snapshots and AMIs within its retention period

RDS and DynamoDB point-in-time restores create a new resource with a new endpoint or table name. The recovery procedure must repoint the application (or rename), which adds to RTO.

Testing failover of Multi-AZ and multi-Region workloads

Skill

An untested DR plan is only a hope. Test each failover mechanism on its own, then run whole-workload game days, and measure the RTO and RPO you actually achieve.

What to testHow
RDS Multi-AZaws rds reboot-db-instance --db-instance-identifier orders --force-failover
Aurora (within a Region)aws rds failover-db-cluster --db-cluster-identifier orders --target-db-instance-identifier orders-reader-2
Aurora Global Databaseswitchover-global-cluster for a planned move with no data loss; failover-global-cluster to rehearse an unplanned failover
ElastiCacheThe test-failover API promotes a replica
Route 53 failoverMake the primary's health check fail: stop the test endpoint, block the health check path, or use the health check's invert setting temporarily. Confirm with dig after the TTL. With ARC, flip the routing control
CloudFront origin failoverIn a test distribution, make the primary origin return one of the failover status codes (for example, by denying access to the primary bucket) and check that responses come from the secondary
Whole AZ or RegionAWS Fault Injection Service scenarios, such as the AZ power interruption or cross-Region connectivity scenarios

Repeatable tests with AWS Fault Injection Service

FIS experiments make failover tests repeatable and safe. This template fails over the Aurora cluster tagged app: orders and stops automatically if the error-rate alarm fires:

fis-aurora-failover.yml 
AuroraFailoverExperiment:
  Type: AWS::FIS::ExperimentTemplate
  Properties:
    Description: Fail over the orders Aurora cluster and measure recovery
    RoleArn: !GetAtt FisExperimentRole.Arn            # allows rds:FailoverDBCluster
    Targets:
      OrdersCluster:
        ResourceType: aws:rds:cluster
        ResourceTags: { app: orders }
        SelectionMode: ALL
    Actions:
      FailoverWriter:
        ActionId: aws:rds:failover-db-cluster
        Targets: { Clusters: OrdersCluster }
    StopConditions:
      - Source: aws:cloudwatch:alarm                 # abort if customers are hurt
        Value: !GetAtt OrdersErrorRateAlarm.Arn
    Tags:
      Name: aurora-failover-test
  • Other useful actions: aws:rds:reboot-db-instances (with forced failover), aws:ec2:stop-instances, aws:ec2:terminate-instances, aws:ecs:stop-task, aws:eks:pod-delete, aws:network:disrupt-connectivity (cut a subnet off) and aws:ssm:send-command (CPU or memory stress inside an instance).
  • Measure during the test: application error rate, time until writes succeed again (RTO), and the last transaction preserved (RPO).

"Regularly verify that failover meets the RTO, with automatic abort if customers are affected" points to scheduled FIS experiments with CloudWatch alarm stop conditions, combined with Resilience Hub assessments.

Implementing cross-Region backup and recovery strategies

Skill

AWS Backup with cross-Region and cross-account copies

Assume production runs in eu-west-1 in account 222222222222. Backups must also be kept in eu-central-1, and in a separate backup account 555555555555 where production credentials can't delete them.

backup-plan.yml (production account) 
ProdBackupPlan:
  Type: AWS::Backup::BackupPlan
  Properties:
    BackupPlan:
      BackupPlanName: prod-daily
      BackupPlanRule:
        - RuleName: daily-with-copies
          TargetBackupVault: prod-vault
          ScheduleExpression: cron(0 3 * * ? *)              # 03:00 UTC daily
          StartWindowMinutes: 60
          EnableContinuousBackup: true                     # point-in-time recovery where supported
          Lifecycle:
            DeleteAfterDays: 35
          CopyActions:
            - DestinationBackupVaultArn: arn:aws:backup:eu-central-1:222222222222:backup-vault:dr-vault        # other Region
              Lifecycle: { DeleteAfterDays: 35 }
            - DestinationBackupVaultArn: arn:aws:backup:eu-west-1:555555555555:backup-vault:locked-vault    # other account
              Lifecycle: { MoveToColdStorageAfterDays: 30, DeleteAfterDays: 365 }

ProdBackupSelection:
  Type: AWS::Backup::BackupSelection
  Properties:
    BackupPlanId: !Ref ProdBackupPlan
    BackupSelection:
      SelectionName: tagged-resources
      IamRoleArn: !Sub arn:aws:iam::${AWS::AccountId}:role/service-role/AWSBackupDefaultServiceRole
      ListOfTags:
        - { ConditionType: STRINGEQUALS, ConditionKey: backup, ConditionValue: daily }
  • Cross-account copies: both accounts must be in the same organization with cross-account backup enabled. The destination vault's access policy must allow the source account to copy into it.
  • Encryption: copied recovery points are re-encrypted with the destination vault's KMS key.
  • Immutability: apply Vault Lock in compliance mode to locked-vault, so the backups can't be deleted early even if the backup account itself is compromised.
  • Organization-wide: apply the same plan to every account in an OU with an Organizations backup policy.

Systems Manager in recovery

  • Automation runbooks: run the recovery steps (promote, restore, scale, switch DNS) in the right order, with approvals and logging. Resilience Hub can generate standard operating procedure runbooks for an application.
  • Parameter Store: holds DR-Region configuration (endpoints, AMI IDs) that the recovered stack reads.
  • Run Command and State Manager: reconfigure restored instances (new endpoints, agents) after recovery.

"Backups must survive a compromise of the production account" points to AWS Backup copies to a separate account's vault with Vault Lock (compliance mode), or a logically air-gapped vault. Snapshots in the same account can be deleted by whoever compromises it.

Configuring a load balancer to recover from backend failure

Skill

A load balancer recovers from backend failure by noticing it quickly, routing around it, and letting something else replace it. What happens as a backend fails:

  1. The target fails its health checksAfter UnhealthyThresholdCount consecutive failures (for example, 2 × 10 seconds), it's marked unhealthy.
  2. Traffic goes to the healthy targetsThe load balancer stops routing new requests to it. In-flight requests finish within the deregistration delay.
  3. The target is replacedThe Auto Scaling group (with HealthCheckType: ELB) terminates it and launches a replacement; ECS and Kubernetes replace unhealthy tasks or pods the same way.
  4. The new target joinsAfter passing HealthyThresholdCount checks it gets traffic, ramped up gradually if slow start is on.
  5. If a whole AZ or the whole load balancer is unhealthyTarget group health thresholds trigger DNS failover away from the unhealthy AZ, and a Route 53 record with evaluate target health fails over to another Region.
resilient-target-group.yml 
OrdersTargetGroup:
  Type: AWS::ElasticLoadBalancingV2::TargetGroup
  Properties:
    VpcId: !Ref Vpc
    Protocol: HTTP
    Port: 8080
    HealthCheckPath: /health/deep                      # checks dependencies, not just "process is up"
    HealthCheckIntervalSeconds: 10
    HealthCheckTimeoutSeconds: 5
    UnhealthyThresholdCount: 2                          # unhealthy after about 20 seconds
    HealthyThresholdCount: 3
    TargetGroupAttributes:
      - { Key: deregistration_delay.timeout_seconds, Value: "30" }
      - { Key: load_balancing.algorithm.type, Value: weighted_random }
      - { Key: load_balancing.algorithm.anomaly_mitigation, Value: "on" }               # shift traffic away from targets returning errors
      - { Key: target_group_health.dns_failover.minimum_healthy_targets.count, Value: "2" }   # fewer healthy targets in an AZ: fail that AZ out of DNS
  • Automatic target weights: with the weighted_random algorithm and anomaly mitigation on, an ALB detects targets with unusually high error rates and sends them less traffic, even before they fail health checks.
  • Fail open: if every target is unhealthy, an ALB routes to all of them anyway, on the basis that some may still work. Route 53 failover (evaluate target health) is what moves traffic to another Region.
  • Graceful errors: a listener rule can return a fixed maintenance response, and CloudFront can serve a custom error page from S3 when the origin is down.
  • Shifting between backends: a listener rule with weighted target groups moves traffic between two backends (for example, a standby Auto Scaling group) in one change.

A health check that only returns "200 OK" when the web server process is running misses broken dependencies. A check that tests every dependency can take all targets out at once when one shared dependency fails. Check what the instance itself needs, and handle shared dependencies with graceful degradation.

Key takeaways

  • RPO = acceptable data loss (backwards from the failure); RTO = acceptable downtime (forwards). Lower targets cost more.
  • Replication copies deletions and corruption; you still need point-in-time, immutable backups in another account.
  • Backup and restore (hours, cheapest), pilot light (data live, compute off), warm standby (small working copy), multi-site active/active (near zero). Pick the cheapest that meets both targets.
  • AWS Backup: plans with copy actions to other Regions and accounts, tag-based selection, Vault Lock compliance mode, backup policies across the organization, restore testing, Audit Manager. Cold storage needs 90 days minimum.
  • Elastic Disaster Recovery: continuous block-level server replication, low-cost staging, recovery in minutes with RPO of seconds, non-disruptive drills.
  • Point-in-time restores (RDS, DynamoDB) create new resources; Aurora Backtrack rewinds in place; S3 versioning undoes deletes.
  • Test failover: --force-failover for RDS Multi-AZ, failover-db-cluster for Aurora, switchover or failover for global databases, failing health checks for Route 53, and FIS experiments with alarm stop conditions.
  • Load balancers recover with fast health checks, Auto Scaling ELB health checks, anomaly mitigation, target group health thresholds for DNS failover, and Route 53 evaluate target health across Regions.

Deployment strategies

How a new version replaces the old one. The exam usually gives a constraint (no downtime, fastest rollback, lowest cost, keep full capacity) and asks for the strategy.

StrategyHow it worksDowntimeRollbackExtra cost
All at once (in-place)Every target is updated at the same timeYesRedeploy the old version (slow)None
RollingTargets are updated in batches, in placeNo, but capacity drops during each batchRedeploy (slow)None
Rolling with additional batchLaunches an extra batch first, so capacity never dropsNoRedeploy (slow)One extra batch during the deployment
ImmutableNew instances in a temporary Auto Scaling group; moved in only when healthyNoFast: terminate the new instancesDouble capacity during the deployment
Blue/greenA complete new environment; traffic switches by load balancer, DNS or aliasNoFastest: switch traffic backDouble capacity until the old environment is removed
CanaryA small share of traffic first (10%), then the rest after a bake timeNoFast: shift traffic backLow
LinearTraffic shifts in equal steps at fixed intervals (10% every minute)NoFast: shift traffic backLow

Where each strategy is available

PlatformOptions
EC2 and on premises (CodeDeploy)In-place with OneAtATime, HalfAtATime, AllAtOnce or a custom minimum healthy hosts; blue/green by copying the Auto Scaling group behind the load balancer
Elastic BeanstalkAll at once, rolling, rolling with additional batch, immutable, traffic splitting (canary to new instances); blue/green by swapping environment URLs
ECSRolling (minimumHealthyPercent and maximumPercent) with the deployment circuit breaker; built-in blue/green, linear and canary; or CodeDeploy blue/green with canary or linear traffic shifting
LambdaCodeDeploy on an alias: Canary10Percent5Minutes, Linear10PercentEvery1Minute, AllAtOnce, with alarms and hooks (SAM DeploymentPreference)
API GatewayCanary release settings on a stage
CloudFrontContinuous deployment: a staging distribution receives a share of traffic or requests with a header
Any HTTP workloadALB weighted target groups, or Route 53 weighted records (subject to DNS caching)

"Fastest rollback" means blue/green. "Keep full capacity at the lowest cost" on Beanstalk means rolling with additional batch. "Safest, new instances only" on Beanstalk means immutable. "Test with a small share of real users" means canary.

Disaster recovery strategies

Ordered from cheapest and slowest to most expensive and fastest. Pick the cheapest strategy that still meets the stated RTO (how long to recover) and RPO (how much data can be lost).

StrategyRunning in the recovery RegionRPORTOCost
Backup and restoreNothing except backups; infrastructure is rebuilt from IaCHours (time since the last backup)HoursLowest
Pilot lightData replicated continuously; core services (databases) running; application servers off or at zeroSeconds to minutesTens of minutes: start and scale the application tierLow
Warm standbyA complete, smaller copy that serves (or can serve) trafficSecondsMinutes: scale outMedium
Multi-site active/activeFull capacity in every Region, all serving trafficNear zeroNear zeroHighest
Building blockTypical use
AWS Backup cross-Region and cross-account copiesBackup and restore
S3 Cross-Region Replication (with Replication Time Control)Any strategy, for object data
Aurora Global Database, RDS cross-Region read replicasPilot light and warm standby
DynamoDB global tablesMulti-site active/active
AWS Elastic Disaster RecoveryServers replicated at block level: RPO in seconds, RTO in minutes, at low standby cost
Route 53 failover records, Application Recovery ControllerMoving traffic to the recovery Region

Read the RTO and RPO numbers first. "RTO of hours, lowest cost" is backup and restore. "Database must be current but compute can start on demand" is pilot light. "Minutes, already running at reduced size" is warm standby.

A read replica is not a high-availability solution on its own: it doesn't fail over automatically. "Automatic failover within the Region" is Multi-AZ.

Permission policy types

PolicyAttached toGrants access?Limits
Identity-basedUser, group or roleYesWhat that identity can do
Resource-basedBucket, key, queue, function, secret, role trustYes, including to other accountsWho can use that resource
Permissions boundaryUser or roleNoThe maximum that identity's policies can grant
Session policyAn assumed-role or federated sessionNoThe maximum for that one session
SCPOrganization root, OU or accountNoWhat principals in member accounts can do
RCPOrganization root, OU or accountNoWhat anyone can do to resources in member accounts

Need to stop a whole account doing something: SCP. Need to stop developer-created roles exceeding a limit: permissions boundary. Need to stop outsiders reaching your buckets: RCP.

Backup and replication

OptionProtects againstNotes
AWS BackupDeletion, corruption, ransomware, Region lossPolicies by tag, cross-Region and cross-account copies, Vault Lock, restore testing
S3 versioningAccidental overwrite or deletionKeeps previous versions; combine with lifecycle rules
S3 replication (CRR, SRR)Region loss; copies to another accountNew objects only; use Batch Replication for existing ones; requires versioning
S3 Object LockDeletion or change, even by administrators (compliance mode)Governance mode can be bypassed with a permission; compliance mode can't
EBS snapshots with Data Lifecycle ManagerVolume loss; Region loss with copiesSchedules, retention, cross-Region copy
RDS automated backupsCorruption, mistakes (point-in-time restore)Up to 35 days; cross-Region backup replication
Elastic Disaster RecoveryServer or Region lossContinuous replication; fast recovery and failback

Replication copies mistakes too: a deletion or corruption replicates. Protecting against human error or ransomware needs versioning, point-in-time backups or Object Lock, not just replication.

Often confused pairs

Each pair shows the first item in green and the second in blue, in both columns.

PairDifference
ECS task rolevsECS task execution roleECS task roleWhat your application code can call (DynamoDB, S3, SQS).ECS task execution roleWhat the ECS agent needs to start the task: pull the image from ECR, read secrets, write logs.
Lambda reserved concurrencyvsLambda provisioned concurrencyLambda reserved concurrencyCaps the function and guarantees it that much concurrency. No extra cost.Lambda provisioned concurrencyKeeps environments initialized to remove cold starts. Paid.
DeletionPolicyvsUpdateReplacePolicyDeletionPolicyApplies when the resource is removed from the template or the stack is deleted.UpdateReplacePolicyApplies when an update replaces the resource with a new one.
Stack policyvsTermination protectionStack policyBlocks updates to chosen resources in the stack.Termination protectionBlocks deleting the whole stack.
Change setvsDrift detectionChange setPreviews what an update will change before you run it.Drift detectionShows what changed outside CloudFormation since the last deployment.
StackSets self-managed permissionsvsStackSets service-managed permissionsStackSets self-managed permissionsYou create the administration and execution roles in each account.StackSets service-managed permissionsOrganizations creates the roles; can deploy to new accounts in an OU automatically.
EC2 health checks (Auto Scaling)vsELB health checks (Auto Scaling)EC2 health checks (Auto Scaling)Replace an instance only when its EC2 status checks fail.ELB health checks (Auto Scaling)Also replace an instance whose application fails the load balancer health check.
X-Ray annotationsvsX-Ray metadataX-Ray annotationsIndexed key-value pairs you can filter traces by.X-Ray metadataExtra data stored with the trace but not searchable.
CloudWatchvsCloudTrailCloudWatchPerformance: metrics, alarms and logs.CloudTrailAudit: who made which API call, when and from where.
Parameter StorevsSecrets ManagerParameter StoreCheaper and simpler; no built-in rotation.Secrets ManagerBuilt-in rotation, cross-Region replication and resource policies.
RDS Multi-AZvsRDS read replicaRDS Multi-AZAvailability: automatic failover to a standby.RDS read replicaRead scaling: manual promotion, no automatic failover.
Object Lock governance modevsObject Lock compliance modeObject Lock governance modeUsers with the bypass permission can delete or shorten retention.Object Lock compliance modeNobody can, including the root user, until retention ends.
SQS visibility timeoutvsSQS delay queueSQS visibility timeoutHides a message after a consumer receives it.SQS delay queueHides a message after it is sent, before anyone can receive it.
Step Functions StandardvsStep Functions ExpressStep Functions StandardLong-running (up to 1 year), exactly once, full execution history.Step Functions ExpressHigh volume, short (up to 5 minutes), at least once, cheaper.
GuardDutyvsInspectorGuardDutyIs something attacking us right now?InspectorWhat is vulnerable to attack?
Config rulevsSCPConfig ruleDetects noncompliance after the fact, and can remediate.SCPPrevents the action from happening at all.

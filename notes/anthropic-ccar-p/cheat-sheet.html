The exam

CCAR-P at a glance
Version
Exam guide version 1.1, effective 7 December 2026 (published 17 November 2026). Before 7 December, the current guide's objectives apply.
Format
From 7 December: 70 scenario-based multiple-choice items in 120 minutes, 60 scored and 10 unscored (not identified); each item says how many responses to select.
Scoring
Scaled 100 to 1,000; pass at 720; percent-correct by domain on the score report.
Cost, delivery, validity
US$175 per attempt; proctored online or at a test center; valid for 12 months; Claude Partner Network organizations only; Foundations recommended but not required.
Domains
1 Solution Design and Architecture 18% · 2 Claude Models, Prompting, and Context Engineering 13% · 3 Integration 21% · 4 Evaluation, Testing, and Optimization 17% · 5 Governance, Safety, and Risk Management 16% · 6 Stakeholder Communication and Lifecycle Management 7% · 7 Developer Productivity and Operational Enablement 8%

Domain 1: Solution Design and Architecture

Task delegation (1.1)

ClueAnswer

Irreversible or accountable decisionCollaborative (Claude prepares, a person approves) or retained (no tool for it) (1.1)
Enforcing the splitIn code: a can_use_tool gate, fail-closed, audited; not a prompt rule (1.1)

End-to-end architecture (1.2)

ClueAnswer
Which component fails first under load?Compute utilisation per component at the stated load, including RPM, ITPM, OTPM and human queues (1.2)
Input-token limit bindingCache the repeated prefix: cache reads don’t count toward ITPM (1.2)
Untrusted input meets write access or shared keysThe first to fail under a trust condition (1.2)

ClueAnswer

ClueAnswer

ClueAnswer

ClueAnswer

Numbers worth knowing

  • Exam: 70 items (60 scored) in 120 minutes from 7 December 2026; pass 720 on 100 to 1,000; US$175; valid 12 months.
  • Prices per million tokens: Fable 5.2 $8/$40, Opus 5.5 $4/$20, Sonnet 5.5 $2/$10, Haiku 5.5 from $0.10/$0.50.
  • Caching: reads 0.05x on Opus and Sonnet 5.5; writes 1.25x (5 minutes) or 2x (1 hour); at least 512 tokens; four breakpoints; agent-loop median read share 84%.
  • Discounts and premiums: Batches 50% off; US-only inference 1.1x; fast mode at a premium.
  • Context: 1M tokens, 128K output on the current tiers; compaction default trigger 150,000 (minimum 50,000).
  • Retrieval: whole corpus under about 200,000 tokens; contextual retrieval with reranking cut top-20 failures by 67%.
  • Model lifecycle: at least 60 days’ notice before retirement.
  • Compliance: flagged content kept up to 2 years; Fable 5.x and hosted Managed Agents outside ZDR.
  • Subagents: spawn depth 3 and 20 concurrent by default.

Traps

  • A prompt instruction is not a control: authorisation, least privilege and must-never rules belong in tools, hooks and deny rules.
  • The biggest or cheapest model is rarely the answer: find the binding constraint, and remove waste before changing tier.
  • More of the same doesn’t close a gap: more test cases on one dimension, more AI reviewers, more gates.
  • Pinning a retiring model only delays the problem.
  • Retention and residency are separate obligations: ZDR doesn’t place data, and inference_geo doesn’t stop storage.
  • Rank risks by residual exposure, not by how frightening they sound.
  • Every claim about the model needs evidence: measured on a named set, with an interval.

How to use this guide

CCAR-P is the senior architect exam: it tests decisions about whole Claude systems and how to justify them, more than how to code them. Its exam guide lists seven weighted domains, each with a list of skills; these notes will give each skill its own page, explained from Anthropic's documentation. Skill numbers (1.1, 1.2, ...) follow the order in the guide.

About the exam in 2026. From 7 December 2026, CCAR-P has 70 scenario-based multiple-choice items in 120 minutes; 60 are scored, and 10 are unscored items that you can't tell apart from the rest. Until then, the exam has 63 items. The passing score is 720 on a scale of 100 to 1,000, the fee is US$175 per attempt, and the credential is valid for 12 months. It's delivered by Pearson VUE, online or at a test center, and only people at organizations in the Claude Partner Network can register. There are no mandatory prerequisites; Anthropic's certifications page suggests starting with Foundations and moving to Professional. The updated guide describes candidates with three or more years in systems architecture or platform engineering and at least six months running Claude in production. Both guides are now version 1.1: the current guide (November 2026) changed only the registration and support sections, and the guide for 7 December 2026 revises the objectives and weights.

Who this is for. The guide describes mid- to senior-level solution architects, AI/ML engineers, technical leads and senior software engineers who own a Claude system from discovery to deployment and operation, advise clients or internal teams, and lead architecture decisions. They engage security, legal and executive stakeholders on risk and review code, logs and architecture diagrams. The updated guide expects a grounding in software engineering practice, three or more years in systems architecture or platform engineering, and at least six months running Claude in production, with daily work on the Claude platform, Agent SDK, MCP and Managed Agents, including how the deployment surfaces differ. Much of it builds on the Architect – Foundations notes; see Associate, Developer or Architect? for a full comparison of all five Claude exams.

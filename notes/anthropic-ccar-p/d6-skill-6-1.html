6.1: Unsupportable Commitments in Requirements and SLAs

Reading discovery notes and draft service levels for promises a model-based system can't keep (always right, always the same, processed somewhere it can't be, never changing) and replacing each with a clarified requirement or a change to the architecture before anyone signs.

Overview

Skill 6.1

Unsupportable Commitments in Requirements and SLAs: "Identifying a commitment in discovery notes or a draft SLA that the proposed Claude architecture cannot meet, and specifying the requirement clarification or architectural change that resolves it."

Exam items quote discovery notes or a draft SLA and ask which commitment can't be met and how to resolve it. Explaining capability to non-technical stakeholders is in the Associate notes (CCAO-F 4.4); recording the decision is skill 6.2.

Sources: Claude documentation on hallucinations, model deprecations, data residency and data retention, as used on earlier pages, and paraphrased. The proposal, SLA and code are my own; the review is tested. Availability figures aren't stated here because I haven't verified a published number.

Running example. A law firm proposes a contract-review assistant for its clients on Opus 5.5 through the Claude API, with retrieval over each client's documents and a lawyer checking every output. Sales has drafted the client SLA.

Commitments a Claude system can't make

Skill 6.1The usual offenders, and the fact behind each.

  • Perfect accuracy ("100%", "never misses", "no hallucinations"): Anthropic's guidance describes techniques that reduce hallucinations but don't eliminate them.
  • Identical output every time: generation varies between runs, and sampling controls such as temperature are rejected under API version 2026-10-21.
  • Regions the surface doesn't offer: the Claude API's inference geography is US or global; other regions mean a different surface and its trade-offs (skill 1.6).
  • A frozen model for years: models are deprecated and retired, with at least 60 days' notice, and retired models' requests fail.
  • Absolute data claims: zero data retention covers eligible features only, some models require 30-day retention, and flagged content may be kept up to 2 years (skill 5.5).
  • Unbounded throughput or spend: rate limits and monthly spend caps apply by tier (skill 1.2).

Reviewing a draft SLA

Skill 6.1Each clause against the architecture.

sla_check.py (sla_check.py)Optional 
"""Find the clauses in a draft SLA that the proposed Claude architecture can't support, and propose a fix.

Proposal: a contract-review assistant for a law firm's clients, on Opus 5.5 through the Claude API,
with retrieval over each client's documents and a lawyer reviewing every output."""

ARCHITECTURE = {"model": "claude-opus-5-5", "surface": "claude api", "inference_geo": ["us", "global"],
                "reviewed_by_lawyer": True, "zdr": True}

DRAFT_SLA = [
    "C1 The assistant will identify 100% of non-standard clauses.",
    "C2 The same contract will always produce exactly the same review.",
    "C3 All processing will take place in the European Union.",
    "C4 First draft of a review within 10 minutes for contracts up to 80 pages.",
    "C5 The underlying model will not change for the 3-year term.",
    "C6 No client content is stored by any AI provider.",
    "C7 Every review is checked by a qualified lawyer before it reaches the client.",
]

CHECKS = [
    ("100%", "no model guarantees perfect recall; Anthropic's guidance says hallucination can be reduced, not eliminated",
     "commit to a measured recall on a reviewed test set (e.g. >= 95% of non-standard clauses), plus lawyer review"),
    ("exactly the same", "outputs vary between runs; sampling controls such as temperature are rejected under API version 2026-10-21",
     "commit to consistent structure (schema, checklist) and to the same findings within a tolerance, not identical text"),
    ("European Union", "the Claude API offers inference in the US or globally, not the EU",
     "clarify the obligation; if EU-only is required, change the surface (a cloud provider's EU regional endpoint) and accept its feature limits"),
    ("will not change", "models are deprecated and retired, with at least 60 days' notice",
     "commit to notice, re-validation on the evaluation set before any model change, and a migration window"),
    ("No client content is stored", "holds under ZDR for eligible features only; flagged content may be retained up to 2 years",
     "state the ZDR arrangement, the eligible-feature restriction and the flagged-content exception"),
]


def review(sla=DRAFT_SLA, arch=ARCHITECTURE):
    findings = []
    for clause in sla:
        for trigger, why, fix in CHECKS:
            if trigger.lower() in clause.lower():
                if trigger == "No client content is stored" and arch["zdr"]:
                    findings.append((clause, "supportable with caveats", why, fix))
                else:
                    findings.append((clause, "not supportable", why, fix))
    return findings


if __name__ == "__main__":
    for clause, verdict, why, fix in review():
        print(f"{clause}\n  {verdict}: {why}\n  revise: {fix}")

Clarify, or change the architecture

Skill 6.1Two kinds of resolution.

  • Clarify the requirement when the stakeholder's real need is something the system can meet: "100%" usually means "we can't afford to miss important clauses", which becomes a measured recall target plus human review; "exactly the same" usually means "consistent", which becomes a fixed structure and stable findings.
  • Change the architecture when the requirement is genuine and binding: if EU-only processing is a legal obligation, move to a surface with an EU regional endpoint and accept its feature limits; if no retention is required, use ZDR and avoid models and features it doesn't cover.
  • Ask before assuming: the same words can be a hard legal constraint or a sales flourish; the resolution depends on which.

"Agree to the clause and manage it later" creates a breach on day one. "Remove the clause" drops the need behind it. The right move rewrites it as something measurable that the architecture can deliver, or changes the architecture.

Writing commitments that hold

Skill 6.1The shape of a supportable clause (my guidance).

  • Measured, on a named set: "at least 95% of non-standard clauses on the agreed review set, re-measured quarterly".
  • About the process, not just the model: "every review checked by a qualified lawyer before release".
  • Change-aware: "model changes announced at least 30 days ahead and re-validated on the review set first".
  • Precise about data: name the arrangement and its exceptions rather than "no data stored, ever".
  • Owned: each commitment has someone accountable for measuring and reporting it.

Key takeaways

  • Unsupportable: perfect accuracy, identical outputs, regions the surface doesn't offer, a frozen model, absolute retention claims, unlimited throughput.
  • Check each clause against documented facts: hallucinations can be reduced, not eliminated; sampling controls are gone; Claude API inference is US or global; models retire with 60 days' notice; ZDR has scope and exceptions.
  • Resolve by clarifying the underlying need into a measurable commitment, or by changing the architecture when the requirement is binding.
  • Supportable commitments are measured on a named set, include the human process, anticipate model change and name data arrangements precisely.
  • Don't sign what can't be met, and don't delete the need behind it.

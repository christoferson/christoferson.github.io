Overview

Skill 6.1

Unsupportable Commitments in Requirements and SLAs: "Identifying a commitment in discovery notes or a draft SLA that the proposed Claude architecture cannot meet, and specifying the requirement clarification or architectural change that resolves it."

Exam items quote discovery notes or a draft SLA and ask which commitment can't be met and how to resolve it. Explaining capability to non-technical stakeholders is in the Associate notes (CCAO-F 4.4); recording the decision is skill 6.2.

Find the commitment the design can’t keep, then fix the requirement or the design

Skill 6.1What this skill asks, and a draft SLA to review.

Questions on this skill give discovery notes or a draft service-level agreement and ask which commitment the proposed Claude architecture can’t meet, and how to resolve it: by clarifying what the stakeholder really needs, or by changing the architecture.

Take a law firm proposing a contract-review assistant for its clients, running on Opus 5.5 through the Claude API, with retrieval over each client’s documents and a lawyer checking every output. Sales has drafted the client SLA. Two of its clauses hold: a first draft within 10 minutes, and review of every output by a lawyer. The sections below take the others.

Perfect accuracy can’t be promised

Skill 6.1“Identifies 100% of non-standard clauses”, “never misses”, “no hallucinations”.

Why it fails. Anthropic’s guidance describes techniques that reduce hallucinations; none eliminates them, and no model guarantees perfect recall.

What to commit to instead. A measured recall on a reviewed test set, such as at least 95% of non-standard clauses, re-measured each quarter, together with lawyer review of every output.

Identical output every time can’t be promised

Skill 6.1“The same contract will always produce exactly the same review.”

Why it fails. Outputs vary between runs, and the sampling controls that used to reduce variation, such as temperature, are rejected under API version 2026-10-21.

What to commit to instead. A consistent structure (a schema or checklist) and the same findings within a tolerance, rather than identical text.

A region the surface doesn’t offer can’t be promised

Skill 6.1“All processing will take place in the European Union.”

Why it fails. The Claude API offers inference in the US or globally, not in the EU.

What to do. Ask whether EU-only processing is a legal obligation or a preference. If it’s an obligation, change the architecture: a cloud provider’s EU regional endpoint, accepting its feature limits.

An unchanging model for years can’t be promised

Skill 6.1“The underlying model will not change for the three-year term.”

Why it fails. Models are deprecated and retired, with at least 60 days’ notice, and a retired model’s requests fail.

What to commit to instead. Advance notice of any model change, re-validation on the evaluation set before the change, and a migration window.

Absolute claims about data need their exceptions stated

Skill 6.1“No client content is stored by any AI provider.”

Why it’s only partly true. It holds under a zero data retention arrangement, but only for eligible features; some models require 30-day retention; and content flagged by Anthropic’s safety systems may be kept for up to 2 years. Without a ZDR arrangement, the clause fails outright.

What to commit to instead. Name the arrangement, the restriction to eligible features, and the flagged-content exception.

Unlimited throughput or spend can’t be promised

Skill 6.1“Any volume, at any time.”

Why it fails. Rate limits and monthly spend caps apply by usage tier.

What to commit to instead. A stated capacity, with the tier and the plan for raising it.

The code below checks each clause of the draft SLA against these facts and proposes a revision for those that fail:

sla_check.py (sla_check.py)Optional 
"""Find the clauses in a draft SLA that the proposed Claude architecture can't support, and propose a fix.

Proposal: a contract-review assistant for a law firm's clients, on Opus 5.5 through the Claude API,
with retrieval over each client's documents and a lawyer reviewing every output."""

ARCHITECTURE = {"model": "claude-opus-5-5", "surface": "claude api", "inference_geo": ["us", "global"],
                "reviewed_by_lawyer": True, "zdr": True}

DRAFT_SLA = [
    "C1 The assistant will identify 100% of non-standard clauses.",
    "C2 The same contract will always produce exactly the same review.",
    "C3 All processing will take place in the European Union.",
    "C4 First draft of a review within 10 minutes for contracts up to 80 pages.",
    "C5 The underlying model will not change for the 3-year term.",
    "C6 No client content is stored by any AI provider.",
    "C7 Every review is checked by a qualified lawyer before it reaches the client.",
]

CHECKS = [
    ("100%", "no model guarantees perfect recall; Anthropic's guidance says hallucination can be reduced, not eliminated",
     "commit to a measured recall on a reviewed test set (e.g. >= 95% of non-standard clauses), plus lawyer review"),
    ("exactly the same", "outputs vary between runs; sampling controls such as temperature are rejected under API version 2026-10-21",
     "commit to consistent structure (schema, checklist) and to the same findings within a tolerance, not identical text"),
    ("European Union", "the Claude API offers inference in the US or globally, not the EU",
     "clarify the obligation; if EU-only is required, change the surface (a cloud provider's EU regional endpoint) and accept its feature limits"),
    ("will not change", "models are deprecated and retired, with at least 60 days' notice",
     "commit to notice, re-validation on the evaluation set before any model change, and a migration window"),
    ("No client content is stored", "holds under ZDR for eligible features only; flagged content may be retained up to 2 years",
     "state the ZDR arrangement, the eligible-feature restriction and the flagged-content exception"),
]


def review(sla=DRAFT_SLA, arch=ARCHITECTURE):
    findings = []
    for clause in sla:
        for trigger, why, fix in CHECKS:
            if trigger.lower() in clause.lower():
                if trigger == "No client content is stored" and arch["zdr"]:
                    findings.append((clause, "supportable with caveats", why, fix))
                else:
                    findings.append((clause, "not supportable", why, fix))
    return findings


if __name__ == "__main__":
    for clause, verdict, why, fix in review():
        print(f"{clause}\n  {verdict}: {why}\n  revise: {fix}")

Clarify the real need, or change the architecture

Skill 6.1The two ways to resolve an unsupportable commitment.

  • Clarify the requirement when the real need is something the system can meet. “100%” usually means “we can’t afford to miss important clauses”, which becomes a measured recall target plus human review; “exactly the same review” usually means a consistent structure.
  • Change the architecture when the requirement is real and binding: if EU-only processing is a legal obligation, move to a surface with an EU regional endpoint and accept its feature limits; if no retention is required, use ZDR and only eligible features.
  • Ask before assuming. The same words can be a hard legal constraint or a sales flourish, and the resolution depends on which.

“Agree to the clause and manage it later” creates a breach on day one. “Remove the clause” drops the need behind it. The right move rewrites the clause as something measurable the architecture can deliver, or changes the architecture.

Write commitments that will hold

Skill 6.1The shape of a clause the system can keep.

  • Measured, on a named set: “at least 95% of non-standard clauses on the agreed review set, re-measured quarterly”.
  • About the process, not only the model: “every review checked by a qualified lawyer before release”.
  • Aware of change: “model changes announced at least 30 days ahead and re-validated on the review set first”.
  • Precise about data: name the arrangement and its exceptions rather than “no data stored, ever”.
  • Owned: each commitment has someone accountable for measuring and reporting it.

Practice questions

Exam-style questions written for these notes. Decide your answer before reading the explanation.

Question 1
Scenario
A draft SLA for a claims-triage assistant promises “100% correct classification”. What should replace it?
  1. A. Keep it and add more testing
  2. B. A measured accuracy target on a reviewed test set, re-measured regularly, plus human review of low-confidence cases
  3. C. Remove the clause entirely
  4. D. “Best-effort accuracy”
Answer
B
Why
No model guarantees perfect accuracy. The underlying need becomes a measurable commitment on a named set, with people covering the risk. Removing the clause drops the need; “best effort” isn’t measurable.
Question 2
Scenario
A client requires all processing in Japan, as a legal obligation, and the design uses the Claude API. What is the right resolution?
  1. A. Promise Japan-only processing anyway
  2. B. Change the architecture to a surface with a Japanese regional endpoint and accept its feature limits
  3. C. Set inference_geo to us
  4. D. Ask the client to accept global processing
Answer
B
Why
The Claude API offers US or global inference. A binding residency obligation means changing the surface; persuading the client to drop a legal obligation, or promising what the design can’t do, both fail.
Question 3
Scenario
A contract says “the model will not change during the five-year term”. Why can’t it be supported, and what holds instead?
  1. A. Models never change
  2. B. Models are retired with at least 60 days’ notice; commit to notice, re-validation and a migration window
  3. C. Pinning a model ID keeps it forever
  4. D. Only Bedrock retires models
Answer
B
Why
Pinned snapshots retire, after which requests fail. A clause about notice and re-validation before any change is something the architecture can deliver.
Question 4
Scenario
The sales team suggests signing a clause promising identical output for identical input, then “managing it later”. What is wrong with that?
  1. A. Nothing; it can be fixed with temperature
  2. B. It creates a breach from day one; outputs vary, and sampling controls such as temperature are rejected under API version 2026-10-21
  3. C. It only matters for long documents
  4. D. It is fine if a lawyer reviews
Answer
B
Why
The commitment is false from the start. Rewrite it as consistent structure and findings within a tolerance, which the system can meet.

Key takeaways

  • Unsupportable: perfect accuracy, identical outputs, regions the surface doesn’t offer, a model frozen for years, absolute retention claims, unlimited throughput.
  • Check each clause against documented facts: hallucinations can be reduced, not eliminated; sampling controls are gone; Claude API inference is US or global; models retire with at least 60 days’ notice; ZDR has a scope and exceptions.
  • Resolve by clarifying the real need into a measurable commitment, or by changing the architecture when the requirement is binding.
  • Write clauses that are measured on a named set, cover the process, anticipate change, are precise about data, and have an owner.

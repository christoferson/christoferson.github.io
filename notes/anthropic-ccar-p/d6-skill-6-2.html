6.2: Architecture Decision Records

Checking a written design decision against what stakeholders said they need, finding the constraint it quietly skipped, and rewriting it so the reader can see what was traded away and what risk remains, stated with the confidence the evidence supports and no more.

Overview

Skill 6.2

Architecture Decision Records: "Evaluating a draft architecture decision record against a stakeholder constraint it fails to address, and selecting the revision that makes the trade-off and its residual risk explicit without overstating certainty about model behavior."

Exam items show a draft ADR and a stakeholder constraint, and offer revisions. The right one addresses the constraint, names the trade-off and the remaining risk, and describes model behaviour with evidence rather than promises. Commitments in SLAs are skill 6.1; the evidence itself comes from skill 4.4.

Sources: Claude documentation on hallucinations, citations and evaluation statistics, as used on earlier pages, and paraphrased. The ADR sections are common practice rather than an Anthropic format; the ADRs and linter are my own, and the linter is tested.

Running example. An internal policy assistant answers about 3,000 staff questions a month on HR, travel and IT policies. Compliance requires every answer to be traceable to the policy text behind it.

What a draft gets wrong

Skill 6.2Three common faults (my list).

  • A constraint stated in the context and never addressed: the compliance audit requirement appears in the first paragraph and nowhere in the decision.
  • No trade-off: the decision reads as free; the options not chosen, and what they would have offered, are missing.
  • Certainty the model can't provide: "will always answer accurately", "will not hallucinate", "no review is needed". Anthropic's own guidance is that hallucinations can be reduced, not eliminated.

# ADR-007: Answer policy questions with Claude over the policy library

Status: proposed

## Context
Staff ask about 3,000 questions a month on HR, travel and IT policies. The compliance team requires
that every answer can be traced to the policy text it relied on, for audit.

## Decision
Use Sonnet 5.5 with the whole 150,000-token policy library in the prompt, cached.

## Consequences
Claude will always answer accurately from the policies and will not hallucinate.
Accuracy is 98%, so no review is needed.
adr-007-revised.md (adr-007-revised.md) 
# ADR-007: Answer policy questions with Claude over the policy library

Status: proposed (revision 2)

## Context
Staff ask about 3,000 questions a month on HR, travel and IT policies.
Constraints:
- C1 Compliance: every answer must be traceable to the policy text it relied on, for audit.
- C2 Answers within 5 seconds; budget $1,500 a month.

## Options considered
1. Whole library in the prompt (150,000 tokens, cached), citations on.
2. Retrieval over chunked policies with citations.
3. Agentic search over the policy site.

## Decision
Option 1, with citations enabled so each answer cites the passages it used (addresses C1), and the
question, answer, cited passages and policy version logged for each request (audit trail for C1).

## Evidence
Evaluation on 300 real questions labelled by the policy team: 94% correct (95% CI 91-97%), 99% of
claims supported by a cited passage; p95 3.1 s; projected cost $900 a month with caching.

## Trade-offs
- Simpler than retrieval (nothing to index or tune), at the cost of sending the whole library each call;
  caching keeps cost and latency inside C2.
- If the library grows past about 200,000 tokens, option 2 becomes the better fit.

## Residual risk
- About 1 in 17 answers in the evaluation was wrong; answers can be wrong while citing real text.
  Mitigation: answers show their citations, staff are told to check them for decisions that matter,
  and HR-eligibility questions go to a person.
- Model behaviour can change at a model update; re-run the evaluation before any model change.

## Revisit when
The library exceeds 200,000 tokens, accuracy on the monthly sample falls below 92%, or a model
version is deprecated.
  • The constraint is addressed explicitly: citations on every answer, plus logging of the question, answer, cited passages and policy version, which is what an auditor will ask for.
  • Options and trade-off: retrieval and agentic search were considered; the whole-library approach is simpler and fits inside the latency and budget, and the ADR says when that stops being true (a library past about 200,000 tokens).
  • Evidence with uncertainty: 94% correct with a 95% interval of 91–97% on 300 labelled questions, instead of a bare "98%".
  • Residual risk and mitigation: about one answer in seventeen was wrong in testing; citations, a check-before-acting instruction and routing of eligibility questions to people handle it.
  • Revisit triggers: library size, accuracy on the monthly sample, and model deprecation.

Stating model behaviour honestly

Skill 6.2Language that matches the evidence.

  • Measured, on a named set: "94% correct (95% CI 91–97%) on 300 real questions", not "accurate".
  • Behaviour as observed, not guaranteed: "in the evaluation", "on this model version".
  • Change acknowledged: a new model version can behave differently; the ADR says the evaluation reruns first.
  • Controls named for what they do: citations make answers checkable; they don't make them correct, since an answer can cite real text and still misread it.

A revision that adds "Claude is highly reliable" sounds more cautious than "always accurate" but is still an unsupported claim. The correct revision replaces adjectives with measured results, intervals and mitigations.

Checking an ADR

Skill 6.2A linter for the mechanical part of the review.

adr_lint.py (adr_lint.py) 
"""Lint an ADR for the two failures that matter most: an unaddressed stated constraint, and false certainty
about model behaviour. It can't judge a decision; it flags drafts that aren't ready for review."""
import re
import sys

REQUIRED = ["Context", "Options considered", "Decision", "Evidence", "Trade-offs", "Residual risk", "Revisit when"]
OVERCONFIDENT = [r"\balways\b", r"\bnever\b", r"will not hallucinate", r"\bguarantee", r"no review is needed", r"100%"]


def lint(text: str) -> list[str]:
    issues = [f"missing section: {s}" for s in REQUIRED if f"## {s}" not in text]
    body = text.split("## Context", 1)[-1]
    for pat in OVERCONFIDENT:
        for m in re.finditer(pat, body, re.I):
            line = body[:m.start()].count("\n")
            issues.append(f"overstated certainty: '{m.group(0)}' (line {line + 1} after Context)")
    for cid in re.findall(r"\b(C\d+)\b", text.split("## Decision")[0]):
        rest = text.split("## Decision", 1)[-1] if "## Decision" in text else ""
        if cid not in rest:
            issues.append(f"constraint {cid} stated but not addressed after the decision")
    if "audit" in text.lower() and "## Decision" in text and "audit" not in text.split("## Decision", 1)[1].lower():
        issues.append("audit requirement in context but not addressed in the decision or its consequences")
    return sorted(set(issues))


if __name__ == "__main__":
    for path in sys.argv[1:]:
        found = lint(open(path, encoding="utf-8").read())
        print(f"{path.split('/')[-1]}: {len(found)} issue(s)")
        for i in found:
            print("  -", i)

The linter catches missing sections, absolute language and constraints that aren't mentioned after the decision. It can't judge whether the decision is right; that's the reviewer's job, now with a draft that gives them what they need.

Key takeaways

  • Check every stated constraint is addressed after the decision, with the specific mechanism (citations and an audit log for traceability).
  • Record options considered and the trade-off made, including when the decision should be revisited.
  • Describe model behaviour with measured results and intervals on a named set, never as guarantees.
  • Make residual risk explicit with its mitigation, and note that model updates require re-evaluation.
  • Replace adjectives ("reliable", "accurate") with evidence; a softer adjective is still an unsupported claim.

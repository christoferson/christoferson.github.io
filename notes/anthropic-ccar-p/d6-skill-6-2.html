Overview

Skill 6.2

Architecture Decision Records: "Evaluating a draft architecture decision record against a stakeholder constraint it fails to address, and selecting the revision that makes the trade-off and its residual risk explicit without overstating certainty about model behavior."

Exam items show a draft ADR and a stakeholder constraint, and offer revisions. The right one addresses the constraint, names the trade-off and the remaining risk, and describes model behaviour with evidence rather than promises. Commitments in SLAs are skill 6.1; the evidence itself comes from skill 4.4.

An architecture decision record must answer every constraint, honestly

Skill 6.2What this skill asks, and a draft record to fix.

An architecture decision record (ADR) is a short document that records a design decision, why it was made, and what it costs. Questions on this skill give a draft ADR and a stakeholder constraint it fails to address, and ask which revision makes the trade-off and the remaining risk explicit without claiming more than the evidence supports.

Take an internal policy assistant that answers about 3,000 staff questions a month on HR, travel and IT policies. Compliance requires every answer to be traceable to the policy text behind it. The draft ADR chooses to load the whole policy library into the prompt, and says the assistant “will always answer accurately”.

Address every constraint stated in the context

Skill 6.2The most common fault: a requirement mentioned once and never answered.

What goes wrong. The compliance requirement appears in the first paragraph and nowhere in the decision.

What fixes it. Name the mechanism that meets it: citations on every answer, plus a log of the question, the answer, the cited passages and the policy version, which is what an auditor will ask for.

Record the options considered and what was given up

Skill 6.2A decision with no trade-off reads as free.

What goes wrong. The draft gives one option and no alternatives, so nobody can see what was traded away.

What fixes it. List the options and why each lost. Here, retrieval and agentic search were considered; loading the whole library is simpler and fits within the latency limit and the budget. Say when that stops being true: once the library passes about 200,000 tokens.

Describe the model’s behaviour with evidence, not certainty

Skill 6.2Language that matches what was measured.

What goes wrong. “Will always answer accurately”, “will not hallucinate”, “no review is needed”. Anthropic’s own guidance is that hallucinations can be reduced, not eliminated.

What fixes it.

  • Measured, on a named set: “94% correct (95% interval 91 to 97%) on 300 real questions”, not “accurate”.
  • Observed, not guaranteed: “in the evaluation”, “on this model version”.
  • Change acknowledged: a new model version can behave differently, so the evaluation is rerun first.
  • Controls described for what they do: citations make answers checkable; they don’t make them correct, because an answer can cite real text and still misread it.

State the remaining risk and how it’s handled

Skill 6.2Residual risk with its mitigation.

About one answer in seventeen was wrong in testing. The revision says so, and names the mitigations: citations so staff can check, an instruction to check before acting, and routing of questions about eligibility to people.

Say when the decision should be revisited

Skill 6.2The conditions that would reopen it.

The size of the policy library, accuracy on the monthly sample of real questions, and the model being deprecated. Any one of them crossing its line reopens the decision.

# ADR-007: Answer policy questions with Claude over the policy library

Status: proposed (revision 2)

## Context
Staff ask about 3,000 questions a month on HR, travel and IT policies.
Constraints:
- C1 Compliance: every answer must be traceable to the policy text it relied on, for audit.
- C2 Answers within 5 seconds; budget $1,500 a month.

## Options considered
1. Whole library in the prompt (150,000 tokens, cached), citations on.
2. Retrieval over chunked policies with citations.
3. Agentic search over the policy site.

## Decision
Option 1, with citations enabled so each answer cites the passages it used (addresses C1), and the
question, answer, cited passages and policy version logged for each request (audit trail for C1).

## Evidence
Evaluation on 300 real questions labelled by the policy team: 94% correct (95% CI 91-97%), 99% of
claims supported by a cited passage; p95 3.1 s; projected cost $900 a month with caching.

## Trade-offs
- Simpler than retrieval (nothing to index or tune), at the cost of sending the whole library each call;
  caching keeps cost and latency inside C2.
- If the library grows past about 200,000 tokens, option 2 becomes the better fit.

## Residual risk
- About 1 in 17 answers in the evaluation was wrong; answers can be wrong while citing real text.
  Mitigation: answers show their citations, staff are told to check them for decisions that matter,
  and HR-eligibility questions go to a person.
- Model behaviour can change at a model update; re-run the evaluation before any model change.

## Revisit when
The library exceeds 200,000 tokens, accuracy on the monthly sample falls below 92%, or a model
version is deprecated.

A revision that adds “Claude is highly reliable” sounds more cautious than “always accurate”, but it’s still an unsupported claim. The correct revision replaces adjectives with measured results, intervals and mitigations.

A linter can check the mechanical part of the review

Skill 6.2Missing sections, absolute language and constraints left unanswered.

The code below finds the mechanical faults: missing sections, absolute words such as “always”, and constraints in the context that are never mentioned after the decision. It finds nine issues in the draft and none in the revision. It can’t judge whether the decision is right; that’s the reviewer’s job, now with a record that gives them what they need.

adr_lint.py (adr_lint.py)Optional 
"""Lint an ADR for the two failures that matter most: an unaddressed stated constraint, and false certainty
about model behaviour. It can't judge a decision; it flags drafts that aren't ready for review."""
import re
import sys

REQUIRED = ["Context", "Options considered", "Decision", "Evidence", "Trade-offs", "Residual risk", "Revisit when"]
OVERCONFIDENT = [r"\balways\b", r"\bnever\b", r"will not hallucinate", r"\bguarantee", r"no review is needed", r"100%"]


def lint(text: str) -> list[str]:
    issues = [f"missing section: {s}" for s in REQUIRED if f"## {s}" not in text]
    body = text.split("## Context", 1)[-1]
    for pat in OVERCONFIDENT:
        for m in re.finditer(pat, body, re.I):
            line = body[:m.start()].count("\n")
            issues.append(f"overstated certainty: '{m.group(0)}' (line {line + 1} after Context)")
    for cid in re.findall(r"\b(C\d+)\b", text.split("## Decision")[0]):
        rest = text.split("## Decision", 1)[-1] if "## Decision" in text else ""
        if cid not in rest:
            issues.append(f"constraint {cid} stated but not addressed after the decision")
    if "audit" in text.lower() and "## Decision" in text and "audit" not in text.split("## Decision", 1)[1].lower():
        issues.append("audit requirement in context but not addressed in the decision or its consequences")
    return sorted(set(issues))


if __name__ == "__main__":
    for path in sys.argv[1:]:
        found = lint(open(path, encoding="utf-8").read())
        print(f"{path.split('/')[-1]}: {len(found)} issue(s)")
        for i in found:
            print("  -", i)

Practice questions

Exam-style questions written for these notes. Decide your answer before reading the explanation.

Question 1
Scenario
A draft ADR states in its context that “all answers must be auditable”, then never mentions auditing again. What revision fixes it?
  1. A. Add “the system is auditable” to the conclusion
  2. B. Name the mechanism: citations on every answer and a log of question, answer, cited passages and policy version
  3. C. Delete the requirement from the context
  4. D. Add more detail about the model
Answer
B
Why
Every stated constraint must be answered after the decision with the specific mechanism that meets it. A bare claim isn’t a mechanism, and deleting the requirement hides it.
Question 2
Scenario
Which statement belongs in an honest ADR?
  1. A. “Claude is highly reliable”
  2. B. “94% correct (95% interval 91 to 97%) on 300 real questions, on this model version”
  3. C. “The assistant will not hallucinate”
  4. D. “No review is needed”
Answer
B
Why
Measured results with an interval, on a named set and model version, match the evidence. The others claim certainty the evidence doesn’t support; “highly reliable” is softer but still unsupported.
Question 3
Scenario
An ADR chooses to load the whole policy library into the prompt. What should it say about when to revisit the decision?
  1. A. Nothing; decisions are final
  2. B. Revisit when the library grows past about 200,000 tokens, accuracy on the monthly sample drops, or the model is deprecated
  3. C. Revisit every week
  4. D. Only when users complain
Answer
B
Why
Revisit triggers tie the decision to the conditions that made it right. Naming them makes the trade-off explicit and gives a clear point to reopen it.
Question 4
Scenario
A reviewer says “citations make the answers correct”. How should the ADR describe citations?
  1. A. As a guarantee of correctness
  2. B. As making answers checkable, not correct: an answer can cite real text and still misread it
  3. C. As unnecessary
  4. D. As a replacement for evaluation
Answer
B
Why
Describe each control for what it actually does. Citations let people check the source; correctness still has to be measured and the remaining risk stated.

Key takeaways

  • Check every constraint stated in the context is answered after the decision, with the mechanism that meets it (citations and an audit log for traceability).
  • Record the options considered and the trade-off made, including when the decision should be revisited.
  • Describe model behaviour with measured results and intervals on a named set, never as guarantees.
  • State the remaining risk with its mitigation, and note that a model update requires re-evaluation.
  • Replace adjectives (“reliable”, “accurate”) with evidence; a softer adjective is still an unsupported claim.

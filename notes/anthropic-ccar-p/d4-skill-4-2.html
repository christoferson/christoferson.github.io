4.2: Deriving Evaluations from Business Processes

Starting from the business process rather than from a benchmark: finding the step Claude actually changes, turning the business outcome into a pass mark for that step, and choosing a mix of tests that looks like real traffic, different for a prompt tweak, a model swap and a release.

Overview

Skill 4.2

Deriving Evaluations from Business Processes: "Determining which portion of a business process constitutes a valid evaluation for a decision it must support (model change, prompt change, release gate), deriving the success criterion from the business outcome, and selecting the test-methodology mix that covers the deployed distribution."

Exam items describe a business process and a decision, and ask what to measure, against what, and with which tests. Finding a missing dimension after an incident is skill 4.1; designing a fair comparison between two configurations is skill 4.4.

Sources: Anthropic's documentation on developing tests and defining success criteria, and its model-selection guidance, checked on 19 and 20 November 2026 and paraphrased. The process and code are my own; the code is tested.

Running example. An industrial distributor receives requests for quotation by email. Claude extracts the line items and drafts a quote; a sales rep corrects and sends it; some customers order weeks later. The team wants to change the prompt, is considering a model change, and needs a release gate.

Which part of the process is a valid evaluation

Skill 4.2Tests for a step's fitness as an evaluation (my criteria).

  • Attributable: Claude's change shows up there. The draft quote is Claude's work; whether the customer orders depends on price, stock and the rep as much as on the draft.
  • Labelled by the process itself: the quote the rep actually sent is a ready-made correct answer for each historical request, produced without extra labelling work.
  • Fast enough for the decision: an order that arrives weeks later can't judge a prompt change this afternoon; a comparison against sent quotes can.
  • Tied to the outcome: drafts the rep sends unchanged save time and avoid wrong prices, which is why the feature exists.

Downstream outcomes like conversion still matter: they belong in a slower, online check of the whole system, not in the gate for a prompt change.

From business outcome to criterion

Skill 4.2Turning "save reps time, never send a wrong price" into a pass mark.

  • Metric: the share of drafts sent with no line-item change (SKU, quantity, unit price).
  • Gate: at least 85% overall, at least 75% in every segment, and no wrong-currency quote at all, because one of those is costlier than many small edits.
  • Why the shape: Anthropic's guidance asks for criteria that are specific, measurable, achievable and relevant to the application's purpose, and for several dimensions rather than one; a hard zero for the costly error is one way to express that some mistakes are worse than others.

Covering the deployed distribution

Skill 4.2Sample like production, and gate per segment.

derive_eval.py (derive_eval.py) 
"""Derive an evaluation from a distributor's quote-to-order process.

Process: RFQ email arrives -> Claude extracts line items and drafts a quote -> a sales rep corrects and
sends it -> the customer orders (or doesn't) weeks later.
Claude affects only the draft. Conversion depends on price, stock and the rep, so it can't judge a
prompt change; the rep's corrections can. Label = the quote the rep actually sent."""
import random
from collections import Counter

PRODUCTION_MIX = {"standard catalogue": 0.62, "custom cut lengths": 0.18, "mixed currencies": 0.08,
                  "scanned PDF attachments": 0.07, "follow-up revisions": 0.05}

CRITERION = {
    "metric": "share of drafts the rep sent with no line-item change (SKU, quantity, unit price)",
    "why": "a no-change draft is the business outcome the feature exists for: rep time saved, no wrong price sent",
    "release_gate": ">= 0.85 overall and >= 0.75 in every segment; no wrong-currency quote",
}

METHODS = {   # which decision needs which mix of tests
    "prompt change": ["replay 600 historical RFQs, stratified by segment", "edge-case set"],
    "model change": ["replay 600 historical RFQs, stratified by segment", "edge-case set",
                     "shadow mode on live RFQs for 1 week (drafts not shown to reps)"],
    "release gate": ["replay set at the gate thresholds", "edge-case set", "red-team set (injected instructions in emails)"],
}


def sample(history: list[dict], n: int, seed: int = 7) -> list[dict]:
    """Stratified by the production mix, so rare-but-real segments are present in proportion."""
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    by_seg = {}
    for r in history:
        by_seg.setdefault(r["segment"], []).append(r)
    picked = []
    for seg, share in PRODUCTION_MIX.items():
        k = max(1, round(n * share))                 # at least one case per segment
        picked += rng.sample(by_seg[seg], min(k, len(by_seg[seg])))
    return picked


def gate(results: list[dict]) -> tuple[bool, dict]:
    seg_total, seg_ok = Counter(), Counter()
    for r in results:
        seg_total[r["segment"]] += 1
        seg_ok[r["segment"]] += r["no_change"]
    overall = sum(seg_ok.values()) / len(results)
    per_seg = {s: seg_ok[s] / seg_total[s] for s in seg_total}
    wrong_currency = any(r.get("wrong_currency") for r in results)
    ok = overall >= 0.85 and min(per_seg.values()) >= 0.75 and not wrong_currency
    return ok, {"overall": round(overall, 3), **{s: round(v, 2) for s, v in per_seg.items()}}
  • Stratified sampling: 600 historical requests split by the production mix, so scanned PDFs (7%) and revisions (5%) appear in proportion. Anthropic's test guidance says to mirror the real-world task distribution and to include edge cases.
  • Per-segment gate: the test shows a release with 90% overall but 0% on scanned PDFs failing the gate; an overall average alone would have passed it.
  • Edge cases beyond history: empty or irrelevant emails, very long RFQs, ambiguous quantities, hostile input; the guidance lists these kinds of cases explicitly.
  • Volume and automation: the guidance favours many automatically graded cases over a few hand-graded ones; field-level comparison against sent quotes is code-gradable.

A methodology mix per decision

Skill 4.2The same process, three decisions (my mapping).

  • Prompt change: replay the stratified historical set and the edge-case set; fast, cheap, and enough to catch regressions in the step that changed.
  • Model change: the same, plus a shadow period on live requests (drafts produced but not shown to reps), because a new model can behave differently on inputs the history doesn't capture. Anthropic's guidance is to rerun your evaluations on your own prompts and data before switching.
  • Release gate: the replay set at the gate thresholds, the edge cases, and a red-team set (instructions injected into emails), since a release faces every kind of input.
  • Afterwards: online monitoring of conversion and rep edit rates, the slow outcome measures the gate couldn't use.

"Use order conversion as the release metric" sounds closest to the business, but it's slow and confounded by things Claude doesn't control. "Use a public benchmark" isn't the deployed distribution at all.

Key takeaways

  • A valid evaluation sits at the step Claude changes, is labelled by the process (what people actually did), is fast enough for the decision, and is tied to the business outcome.
  • Derive the criterion from the outcome: a measurable pass mark, per-segment floors, and hard limits for the costly errors.
  • Cover the deployed distribution: stratify by the production mix, add edge cases, automate grading and favour volume.
  • Match methods to the decision: replay for prompt changes; replay plus shadow traffic for model changes; replay, edge cases and red-team sets for release gates; online monitoring for slow outcomes.
  • Lagging, confounded business metrics and generic benchmarks make poor gates.

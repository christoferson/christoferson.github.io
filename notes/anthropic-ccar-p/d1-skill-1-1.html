Overview

Skill 1.1

Task Delegation Between Claude and Humans: "Determining which components of a described business process should be delegated to Claude, retained by humans, or handled collaboratively, and justifying each assignment against reversibility, stakes, and accountability."

Exam items describe a process and a proposed split, and ask which assignment is wrong or which revision fixes it, including splits that are too cautious. The guide's own sample question for this skill isn't reproduced here. The business-user view of the same idea is in the Associate notes (CCAO-F 4.2); this page adds the architect's job of making the split enforceable.

Sources: Anthropic's Usage Policy (effective 12 November 2026) and the Agent SDK permissions documentation, checked on 20 November 2026 and paraphrased. The claims process and the code are my own; the code is tested against the SDK's types with a mocked approval queue.

Running example. A home insurer wants a Claude agent in its claims process: about 3,000 claims a month, from a burst pipe to a fire, each with photos, invoices and a policy to check.

The three criteria

Skill 1.1What the guide asks you to justify each assignment against (my reading of each).

  • Reversibility: can the effect be undone cheaply and completely? Extracted fields can be corrected; a payment, a sent letter or a denial that reached the customer can't simply be withdrawn.
  • Stakes: what does a wrong result cost, and to whom: money, a customer's housing or livelihood, legal exposure, regulatory findings? Stakes depend on the case, not only the step: a 300 payout and a 300,000 payout are the same tool.
  • Accountability: who must answer for the outcome by law, regulation or role? If a licensed or named person is accountable, that person makes or approves the decision; an agent can't hold accountability.

In regulated areas the Usage Policy turns these into requirements. Insurance is one of its high-risk areas: an AI recommendation that affects a person must be meaningfully reviewed by a qualified person, who can change it before it's acted on and stays responsible, and the person affected must be told AI was used. The policy allows one exception: some outcomes entirely in the individual's favour, such as paying an insurance claim, can skip review where the law permits.

Delegated, collaborative, retained

Skill 1.1The three outcomes, and what each means in a system.

  • Delegated to Claude: the agent acts and the result is used without a per-item check. Suits read-only, reversible, low-stakes steps, or steps whose output is checked downstream anyway. Monitor with sampling and metrics.
  • Collaborative: Claude prepares and a person approves, edits or rejects before the effect happens; or a person decides and Claude supplies the evidence. Suits irreversible or higher-stakes steps where most of the work can still be automated.
  • Retained by humans: the decision or action is the person's; the agent may assemble a summary but has no tool that performs the step. Suits accountable, regulated or judgment-heavy decisions.

The distinction an architect adds: "retained" should mean the agent can't do it, not that it's told not to.

A claims process, step by step

Skill 1.1Each component, its assignment, and the reason.

  • Read documents, extract fields (dates, amounts, damage type): delegated. Read-only, correctable, and the adjuster sees the fields before any decision.
  • Coverage check: delegated to a rules engine, not to Claude. Policy terms are deterministic; Claude summarises the result.
  • Fraud signals: delegated as flags only. A flag routes the claim to an investigator; it decides nothing.
  • Draft letters: delegated; send letters: collaborative. Drafting is reversible; sending isn't.
  • Payments: collaborative, with one delegated case: small, covered, unflagged payouts of the full claim, within the policy's favourable-outcome exception and the insurer's own risk appetite.
  • Denials and part-payments: retained. Adverse to the customer, in a high-risk area, and a licensed adjuster is accountable.

"The agent is accurate enough, so let it deny clearly invalid claims" confuses accuracy with accountability. A denial is an adverse high-risk decision that needs qualified review whatever the model's error rate.

Enforcing the split in code

Skill 1.1The delegation matrix as an Agent SDK permission gate.

claims_delegation.py (claims_delegation.py) 
"""Delegation for a home-insurance claims agent, enforced outside the model.

Each tool the agent can call is assigned AI-executed, collaborative (a person approves) or
human-retained (the agent can only hand over). The gate runs as the Agent SDK's can_use_tool
callback, so the assignment holds whatever the model decides to do."""
from dataclasses import dataclass

from claude_agent_sdk import ClaudeAgentOptions, PermissionResultAllow, PermissionResultDeny

AUTO_PAY_LIMIT = 1_500          # policy: small, fully covered, favourable payouts need no review


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Rule:
    assignment: str             # "ai" | "collaborative" | "human"
    why: str                    # reversibility, stakes, accountability: kept for the audit trail


MATRIX = {
    "mcp__claims__read_documents":  Rule("ai", "read-only; nothing to reverse"),
    "mcp__claims__extract_fields":  Rule("ai", "draft data; an adjuster sees it before any decision"),
    "mcp__claims__flag_fraud_signal": Rule("ai", "a flag only routes the claim to an investigator"),
    "mcp__claims__draft_letter":    Rule("ai", "draft only; sending is a separate step"),
    "mcp__claims__send_letter":     Rule("collaborative", "external and can't be unsent"),
    "mcp__claims__issue_payment":   Rule("collaborative", "money leaves; small favourable payouts excepted"),
    "mcp__claims__deny_claim":      Rule("human", "high-risk decision: a licensed adjuster decides and is accountable"),
}


def auto_payable(args: dict) -> bool:
    """The only payment the agent may make alone: small, covered by a rules check, wholly in the customer's favour."""
    return (args.get("amount", 0) <= AUTO_PAY_LIMIT and args.get("coverage_check") == "passed"
            and not args.get("fraud_flag") and args.get("pays_full_claim") is True)


def gate(approve, audit):
    async def can_use_tool(tool_name, tool_input, ctx):
        rule = MATRIX.get(tool_name)
        if rule is None:
            audit(tool_name, "denied", "not in the delegation matrix")
            return PermissionResultDeny(message=f"{tool_name} isn't assigned to this agent.")
        if rule.assignment == "ai" or (tool_name.endswith("issue_payment") and auto_payable(tool_input)):
            audit(tool_name, "allowed", rule.why)
            return PermissionResultAllow()
        if rule.assignment == "human":
            audit(tool_name, "handed over", rule.why)
            return PermissionResultDeny(message="This decision belongs to a licensed adjuster. Summarise the claim "
                                        "and your findings with mcp__claims__draft_letter, then stop.")
        approved = await approve(tool_name, tool_input, rule.why)      # e.g. an adjuster's work queue
        audit(tool_name, "approved" if approved else "declined", rule.why)
        return PermissionResultAllow() if approved else PermissionResultDeny(message="The adjuster declined this step.")
    return can_use_tool


def options(approve, audit) -> ClaudeAgentOptions:
    return ClaudeAgentOptions(
        allowed_tools=[],                     # nothing pre-approved: every call reaches the gate
        disallowed_tools=["Bash", "Write", "Edit", "WebFetch", "WebSearch"],
        can_use_tool=gate(approve, audit),
        permission_mode="default",
        max_turns=25,
    )
  • Why a callback: in the Agent SDK, permission checks run in order: hooks, deny rules, ask rules, the permission mode, allow rules, and finally the can_use_tool callback for anything not yet decided. With nothing pre-approved in allowed_tools, every call reaches the gate.
  • Retained means no path: deny_claim is denied with a message that tells the agent to hand over, so the model can't argue its way past it.
  • Collaborative means a queue: the approve function is the adjuster's work queue; the agent waits for the result.
  • The exception is a rule, not a judgment: auto_payable checks amount, coverage result, fraud flag and full payment; the model's confidence isn't an input.
  • Unknown tools fail closed, and every outcome is written to the audit trail with its reason.

Where gates go, and where they don't

Skill 1.1Over- and under-delegation are both design faults.

  • Gate the effect, not the activity: approval belongs immediately before the irreversible or high-stakes action (send, pay, deny), not before reads, lookups or drafts.
  • Too many gates build queues, slow the process, and train reviewers to approve without reading, which is worse than an honest automated step with monitoring.
  • Too few gates let irreversible effects happen on the model's say-so, or put an accountable decision with no accountable person.
  • Make reversible steps reversible in fact: versioned records, an audit log and an undo path let you delegate more safely.
  • Thresholds by case: route by amount, flags and customer impact, so most claims flow and the risky ones stop.

Justifying an assignment

Skill 1.1What a good justification contains.

  • The step and its effect: what changes in the world when it runs.
  • Each criterion: can it be undone, what a wrong result costs, who is accountable.
  • Constraints: policy and regulation (such as high-risk review), the organisation's risk appetite.
  • The control that implements it: no tool, a gate, a threshold, a queue, sampling.
  • What would change it: evidence from monitoring that would move a step up or down a level.

Key takeaways

  • Justify every assignment against reversibility, stakes (per case, not just per step) and accountability.
  • Delegated: reversible, low-stakes or checked downstream; collaborative: Claude prepares, a person approves before the effect; retained: the agent has no tool that performs the step.
  • High-risk areas such as insurance need qualified human review and disclosure; outcomes entirely in the person's favour may be exempt where law permits.
  • Enforce the split outside the model: an Agent SDK can_use_tool gate with nothing pre-approved, rules for exceptions, fail-closed defaults and an audit trail.
  • Gate the irreversible effect, not every activity; too many gates create rubber-stamp review, too few let the model act alone on accountable decisions.

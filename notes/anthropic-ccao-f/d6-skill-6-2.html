6.2: Data Protection in Practice

Working with personal, confidential or regulated data without exposing it: knowing how sensitive it is, stripping out what Claude doesn't need before it's uploaded, and using the right account and memory settings, as your organisation's policy requires.

Overview

Skill 6.2

Data Protection in Practice: "Applying data-protection practice to work involving sensitive, confidential, or regulated information, including classification, redaction and anonymization, upload judgment for code-executed analysis, and memory and incognito controls, consistent with organizational policy."

Exam items describe data and a policy and ask which way of using Claude complies. The guide's sample question for Domain 6 is of this kind and isn't reproduced here. Whether a use is appropriate at all is skill 6.1; this skill is how to handle the data once it is.

Sources: Anthropic's privacy articles on model training (consumer and commercial) and data retention, and the Claude Help Center articles on memory and incognito chats, creating files and connectors, checked on 20 November 2026 and paraphrased. The preparation steps are my own.

Running example. The outdoor-gear shop's returns of hiking boots doubled this autumn. An analyst wants Claude to analyse the returns export, which holds customer names, contact details, order numbers and free-text notes.

Classification first

Skill 6.2Know what the data is before deciding what to do with it.

  • Use your organisation's scheme: many use levels such as public, internal, confidential and restricted, each with rules on where data may go. The policy, not convenience, decides what can go into an AI tool and in which account.
  • Look for the sensitive kinds: personal data about customers or staff; special categories such as health; financial and payment data; regulated data with legal duties attached; and confidential business data such as contracts and prices.
  • Classify the whole file by its most sensitive part: one column of health notes makes a returns export sensitive.
  • Check the account: a work Team or Enterprise account is usually where policy allows business data; a personal account usually isn't.

Redaction and de-identification

Skill 6.2Removing what Claude doesn't need (my summary of standard practice).

  • Delete: fields the task doesn't need (names, contact details, card numbers). The safest data is data that's never uploaded.
  • Replace: identifiers that the analysis needs to link rows get codes; the key that maps codes back to people stays out of the upload.
  • Generalise: exact values become bands or areas (age band instead of date of birth, postcode area instead of full postcode), so patterns survive but individuals don't stand out.
  • Screen free text: notes and comments often hide names, phone numbers, relatives and health details. Read or filter them; they're the easiest place to miss identifying information.
  • Test re-identification: could someone who knows the business work out who a row is? Rare combinations (one store, one day, one unusual product) can identify a person even with names removed.

Redacting or de-identifying must happen before upload. Asking Claude to anonymise a file after uploading it has already shared the original.

Preparing a file for analysis

Skill 6.2Upload judgment for code-executed analysis.

returns_prep.txt (Returns export preparation) 
Task: find why returns of hiking boots doubled this autumn, using code-executed
analysis on the returns export. Shop policy: customer personal data is
Confidential; it may go into an AI tool only after de-identification, and only
in a Team workspace (not a personal account).

Export columns and what happens to each (done in the spreadsheet BEFORE upload)

  customer_name, email, phone, address   delete
  order_number                           replace with a code (R0001, R0002...);
                                         the code-to-order key stays in the
                                         shop's system, never uploaded
  card_last4                             delete (not needed for the question)
  postcode                               keep only the first part (area), so
                                         region can still be analysed
  date_of_birth                          replace with an age band (18-29, 30-44...)
  product, size, store, return_date,     keep: these answer the question
   return_reason_code
  staff_notes, customer_comment          read through, then edit or remove:
   (free text)                           names, phone numbers, "my wife Jan",
                                         health details ("knee surgery"), and
                                         anything else that identifies a
                                         person; keep the product feedback

Checks before upload
  - Filter the free-text columns for @, digits and capitalised words; review hits.
  - Ask: could someone in the shop work out who a row is? If yes, generalise more.
  - Upload only the columns the question needs.

In Claude
  - Team workspace, in a chat with memory turned off (or a Project with
    "Use account memory" off), so details don't carry into other work.
  - Prompt: "Using Python, compare return rates by size, store and month for
    the boot models in returns_deid.csv. Show the code."
  - Delete the chat when the analysis is saved, if policy says so.
  • Start from the question: "why did boot returns double?" needs product, size, store, date and reason, not who the customers are.
  • Upload the minimum: only the columns and rows the analysis needs.
  • Code execution handles files you upload: files up to 30 MB, processed in Claude's environment, which also brings the risk of hidden instructions in untrusted files (skill 2.4).

What the settings do and don't do

Skill 6.2Memory, incognito and Projects control different things.

  • Memory off for a chat, or "Use account memory" off for a Project: stops details carrying into other conversations (skill 3.3).
  • Incognito chats: not saved to chat history or memory, and never used to train models, even with model improvement on. On Team and Enterprise they're still included in data exports and kept for at least 30 days.
  • A restricted Project: controls which colleagues can see the material and results.
  • Memory's own limits: sensitive topics aren't saved to memory unless you opt in, and some data (such as government ID and financial account numbers) is never stored.

Settings don't replace de-identification. Incognito, memory off and restricted Projects change what's kept and who sees it; none of them changes what you've disclosed by uploading. If policy says de-identify first, a setting can't make an identifiable upload compliant.

Training and retention

Skill 6.2What Anthropic does with the data, per its privacy articles.

  • Commercial products (Claude for Work, i.e. Team and Enterprise, and the API): by default, inputs and outputs aren't used to train models. Exceptions are explicit feedback (thumbs up or down) and choosing to opt in. Team and Enterprise owners can turn off feedback submission.
  • Consumer plans (Free, Pro, Max): training use depends on the Model Improvement setting in Privacy Settings. Raw connector content is excluded, though content you paste in isn't.
  • Retention (consumer): deleted chats leave your history at once and Anthropic's back-end within 30 days; with model improvement on, de-identified data may be kept for up to 5 years for training; feedback is kept for 5 years; safety-flagged content is kept longer.
  • Connectors: Anthropic says it doesn't train on Gmail, Drive or Calendar connector data.

Key takeaways

  • Classify data with your organisation's scheme, by its most sensitive part, and use the account the policy allows.
  • Before upload: delete unneeded fields, replace identifiers with codes (key kept back), generalise exact values, screen free text, and test for re-identification.
  • Upload only what the question needs; anonymising after upload is too late.
  • Memory controls, incognito and restricted Projects limit retention and visibility, not disclosure; they don't substitute for de-identification.
  • Commercial plans don't train on your data by default; consumer plans follow the Model Improvement setting; incognito chats are never used for training; deleted consumer chats leave the back-end within 30 days.

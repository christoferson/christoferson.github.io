5.2: Connector and Knowledge Management

Getting the right sources in front of Claude: connecting Google Drive and Gmail, deciding whether to upload a file or connect to it, and pruning what's there so Claude works from current, relevant material rather than old drafts.

Overview

Skill 5.2

Connector and Knowledge Management: "Applying connector and knowledge management to the sources a workflow needs, including connecting sources such as Google Drive and Gmail, upload-versus-connector trade-offs, curating uploads, and keeping connected knowledge current, relevant, and free of noise."

Exam items describe a workflow's sources and ask whether to upload or connect, or why outputs are using stale or wrong material. What connectors are in general, and their permission settings, is in skill 3.1; where knowledge sits in a Project is skill 5.1.

Google Workspace connectors read with your permissions and ask before risky actions

Skill 5.2Drive, Gmail and Calendar, per the Help Center.

  • Setting up: available to all users in Claude and Claude Desktop. You sign in with your Google account; on Team and Enterprise an owner enables the connectors first. Turn individual ones on or off from "+" > Connectors. Docs, Sheets and Slides are separate connectors from Drive.
  • Drive: search and read Docs, Sheets, Slides, PDFs, images and Office files. Claude reads text only; images inside documents aren't processed. It can save files Claude creates to Drive. Sharing, moving or deleting files needs your approval by default.
  • Gmail: search and read mail, draft replies, and manage labels. Attachments up to about 12 MB can be opened, which needs code execution. Sending, replying and forwarding need your approval each time unless you're in Auto mode, and the Help Center warns that Auto's check can miss risky actions.
  • Calendar: view events, find shared availability, and create or change events.
  • Permissions: Claude mirrors your Google permissions; it can only open what your account can.
  • Privacy: data is fetched only when you ask, and kept with the chat (deleted when the chat is). Anthropic says it doesn't train its models on Gmail, Drive or Calendar connector data.

Upload stable, shared material; connect material that changes or depends on access

Skill 5.2The trade-offs.

  • Upload when the material is stable (a policy, a season's spec sheets, approved examples), should be the same for everyone in the Project, and you want a fixed, reviewable version. The cost: an uploaded file doesn't update itself, so someone must replace it when the source changes.
  • Connect when the material changes often (email, a live product list, meeting notes), is too large to upload, or must respect each person's access. The cost: results depend on what each person can see, and connected tools use context, so turn off ones you don't need.
  • Drive files in chats and Projects: Google Docs added from Drive stay synced to the latest version. In Projects, adding from Drive works only in private Projects; it's turned off for shared ones. A shared Project therefore needs uploaded copies, replaced when the source changes, or a connector each person uses in their chats.

"Upload this week's supplier emails to the Project" freezes material that changes daily and copies it out of each person's own access controls. "Connect the whole Drive" to a task that needs three documents adds noise and depends on whoever is asking. Match the method to how the source behaves.

Review knowledge to remove outdated files, drafts and old examples

Skill 5.2A quarterly review of the Project.

Take a shared product-descriptions Project with three members that has grown to 22 files after two seasons, where pages have started quoting last year’s specs.

Next review: first Monday of the next quarter, or whenever a season changes.
  • Remove what's out of date: Claude can't tell a retired spec sheet from a current one if both are in knowledge.
  • Remove drafts and noise: brainstorms, rejected versions and duplicates compete with the real guidance.
  • Fix examples: examples teach strongly, so an outdated example teaches the old way.
  • Date or version every file: "Style guide - 2026-11" makes the current version obvious to people and to Claude.

Give each source an owner, replace rather than add, and prune connectors

Skill 5.2Habits that stop drift.

  • An owner for each source: someone replaces the file when the original changes.
  • A trigger and a schedule: review when the season or policy changes, and on a regular date.
  • Replace, don't add: upload the new version and delete the old one in the same step.
  • Watch the symptoms: outputs quoting retired figures or another team's terms usually mean stale or mixed knowledge (or memory), not bad instructions. Check the sources first.
  • Prune connectors too: turn off connectors a workflow doesn't need, and disconnect services you no longer use.

  2. B. Remove the outdated files and date or version the current ones

B

Key takeaways

  • Google Workspace connectors: sign in with Google (owners enable on Team and Enterprise); Drive reads text from Docs, Sheets, Slides, PDFs and Office files; Gmail sending needs approval outside Auto mode; Claude mirrors your Google permissions.
  • Upload stable material that should be the same for everyone; connect material that changes often, is large, or must respect each person's access.
  • Google Docs added from Drive stay synced; adding Drive files to Project knowledge works only in private Projects, so shared Projects need replaced uploads or per-person connectors.
  • Curate: remove out-of-date files, drafts and duplicates; fix outdated examples; date every file.
  • Keep it current with an owner per source, a review trigger, replace-not-add, and pruned connectors; stale outputs usually point to stale sources.

6.3: Policy, Ethics, and Diligence

Looking at how people really use Claude, comparing it with the organisation's AI policy, and working out what kind of gap each problem is, because a missing rule, an unknown rule and an ignored rule each need a different fix.

Overview

Skill 6.3

Policy, Ethics, and Diligence: "Analyzing observed usage patterns against organizational AI policies, including compliance gaps, ethical risks such as bias and transparency failures, Diligence lapses, and the distinction between a policy gap and a training gap, and determining the corrective action that closes the gap."

Exam items describe what people have been doing with Claude and ask what kind of problem it is, or which corrective action fixes it. Approving a use in the first place is skill 6.1; handling data correctly is skill 6.2. This skill reviews practice after the fact.

Diligence is the AI Fluency competency about taking responsibility for how AI is used. The course page names its parts as Creation Diligence, Transparency Diligence and Deployment Diligence. In practice: being careful about how work is made with AI, being open about AI's role, and taking responsibility for what's released.

Name the gap: compliance, policy, training, Diligence or ethical risk

Skill 6.3What each gap looks like and what closes it.

  • Compliance gap: practice breaks a rule, whether the organisation's policy, a law or Anthropic's Usage Policy. First stop the harm (restore, correct, remove), then find out why it happened, which leads to one of the kinds below.
  • Policy gap: no rule covers the situation, or the rule is unclear. Fix: write or clarify the rule, then tell people.
  • Training gap: a clear rule exists, but people don't know it or don't know how to follow it. Fix: brief, train, add it to onboarding, and make the right way easy.
  • Diligence lapse: people know the rule and can follow it, but didn't, often under time pressure. Fix: build the check into the process so it can't be skipped, and make accountability clear.
  • Ethical risk: the output is unfair or misleading even if no rule names it, such as bias or a failure to be open about AI's role. Fix: correct the output, then usually a policy change too.

No clear rule is a policy gap; a clear rule people didn't know is a training gap

Skill 6.3The distinction the guide names explicitly.

  • Ask first: is there a written rule that clearly covers this? If no, it's a policy gap; training people on a rule that doesn't exist can't help.
  • If yes: did the people involved know it and understand it? If no, it's a training gap; rewriting a rule that's already clear won't reach them.
  • If they knew and understood it: it's neither; it's a Diligence lapse (or a process that makes the rule hard to follow).
  • Evidence: read the policy text, and ask the people involved. Several people making the same mistake independently suggests a training or process problem, not individual carelessness.

"Send everyone a reminder" is the default fix, and it only closes a training gap. It doesn't create a missing rule, and it doesn't stop a known rule being skipped under deadline pressure.

Review for bias, missing disclosure and over-trust even where no rule names them

Skill 6.3Problems a rulebook may not mention.

  • Bias: Claude can reproduce patterns in the data or the request. Plans built on "who responded before" can exclude groups who were never reached; drafts can use stereotyped language. Review who is helped, harmed or left out, especially in decisions about people.
  • Transparency failures: people aren't told AI was involved when they should be. The Usage Policy requires public-facing chatbots to say they're AI, and people affected by high-risk AI recommendations to be told; organisations often add their own disclosure rules.
  • Over-trust: AI output passed on as checked fact, with no one accountable for it (skill 2.3).

Fix the immediate harm and the cause, in proportion

Skill 6.3Four observations, four different gaps.

Take a company’s quarterly AI usage review that turns up four problems, one of each kind.

usage_review.txt (Quarterly AI usage review) 
Quarterly AI usage review: outdoor-gear shop (operations lead, with the owners)
Shop AI policy (v2, June): work accounts only for customer data; a person
checks every AI-assisted text before customers see it; label AI chat to
customers; de-identify personal data before analysis.

1. Observed: three store staff paste customer emails into personal
   Claude accounts to draft replies.
   Policy says: work accounts only. Staff asked say they didn't know.
   Gap type: TRAINING GAP (rule exists and is clear; not known)
   Action: 15-minute briefing for store staff; add the rule to the induction
   checklist; check again next quarter.

2. Observed: marketing used Claude to plan postcode-targeted discounts;
   the plan left out two lower-income areas because "past response was low".
   Policy says: nothing about fairness in targeting.
   Gap type: POLICY GAP + ethical risk (bias reproduced from past data)
   Action: add a rule that targeting plans are reviewed for excluded groups
   before launch; marketing lead re-runs this plan with the areas included.

3. Observed: product pages published with AI-written claims ("waterproof to
   10,000 mm") that weren't in the supplier specs; nobody checked them.
   Policy says: a person checks every AI-assisted text. Writers knew.
   Gap type: DILIGENCE LAPSE (rule known, not followed under deadline)
   Action: correct the pages; add a spec check to the publishing step so it
   can't be skipped; the online lead signs off each batch.

4. Observed: the website chat assistant's new greeting dropped the line
   saying it's an AI.
   Policy says: label AI chat to customers (also required by the Usage Policy).
   Gap type: COMPLIANCE GAP / transparency failure (a change broke a rule)
   Action: restore the line today; add "AI label present" to the change
   checklist for the assistant.

Summary for the owners: 1 policy change, 1 briefing, 2 process fixes.
  • Same symptom, different causes: unchecked AI text could be a training gap (nobody knew the rule) or a Diligence lapse (everyone knew). Asking the people involved decides which.
  • Fix the immediate harm and the cause: correct the pages, restore the label, and also change the process, rule or training that let it happen.
  • Proportionate actions: a briefing, a policy line, a checklist item; not a ban on using Claude.

Find usage patterns in audit logs, admin settings and spot checks

Skill 6.3Where the observations come from.

  • Audit logs (Enterprise): Owners and Primary Owners can export 180 days of events, such as sign-ins, Projects created or shared, knowledge added, conversations created and files uploaded. Logs show IDs, not titles or content; chat content comes through separate data exports or the Compliance API.
  • Admin settings as evidence: which models, connectors, Skills and sharing options are enabled shows what people can do (skill 3.2).
  • People and outputs: spot checks of published work, staff surveys, customer complaints and team leads' reports catch what logs can't.

Practice questions

Exam-style questions written for these notes. Decide your answer before reading the explanation.

Question 1
Scenario
Staff paste customer data into a personal account. No company rule covers personal accounts. What kind of gap is it?
  1. A. Training gap
  2. B. Policy gap: write or clarify the rule, then tell people
  3. C. Diligence lapse
  4. D. No gap
Answer
B
Why
Training can’t teach a rule that doesn’t exist.
Question 2
Scenario
A clear rule says AI-drafted pages must be checked, but new staff didn’t know it. What closes the gap?
  1. A. Rewrite the rule
  2. B. Brief and train them, and add it to onboarding
  3. C. Ban Claude
  4. D. Nothing
Answer
B
Why
A clear rule people don’t know is a training gap.
Question 3
Scenario
Everyone knows the checking rule, but it is skipped under deadline pressure. What is the fix?
  1. A. A reminder email
  2. B. Build the check into the process so it can’t be skipped, with clear accountability
  3. C. A new policy
  4. D. More training
Answer
B
Why
That is a Diligence lapse; reminders only close training gaps.
Question 4
Scenario
A plan built on “who responded before” leaves out groups who were never reached. What kind of risk is it?
  1. A. A compliance gap
  2. B. An ethical risk (bias): correct the output and usually add a policy
  3. C. A training gap
  4. D. None
Answer
B
Why
Bias can matter even where no rule names it.

Key takeaways

  • Review actual usage against the policy: compliance gaps (a rule broken), policy gaps (no clear rule), training gaps (a clear rule not known), Diligence lapses (a known rule not followed), and ethical risks such as bias and transparency failures.
  • Policy gap versus training gap: is there a clear written rule? If not, write one; if so but people didn't know it, train them.
  • A reminder only fixes a training gap; Diligence lapses need checks built into the process and clear accountability.
  • Fix the immediate harm and the cause, with proportionate actions.
  • Evidence comes from audit logs (Enterprise, 180 days, IDs not content), admin settings, spot checks and people. Diligence covers creation, transparency and deployment (names from AI Fluency).

6.1: Use-Case Appropriateness

Deciding whether a proposed use of Claude should go ahead as it is, go ahead only with safeguards, or not go ahead at all, and writing down why, using the task itself, the limits of delegation, the trustworthiness of add-ons, and Anthropic's Usage Policy.

Overview

Skill 6.1

Use-Case Appropriateness: "Evaluating a proposed Claude use case for appropriateness, including task suitability, delegation limits, trust in third-party Skills and connectors, and the Anthropic Usage Policy, and classifying it as appropriate, appropriate with safeguards, or inappropriate with documented rationale."

Exam items describe a proposed use and ask how to classify it, or which safeguard it needs. Assigning steps within an approved workflow is skill 4.2; review depth for one piece of output is skill 2.3. This skill is the yes, yes-if or no for a whole use case.

Sources: Anthropic's Usage Policy (effective 12 November 2026) and the Claude Help Center articles on using Skills and on connectors, checked on 20 November 2026 and paraphrased. The register is my own.

Running example. Staff at the outdoor-gear shop have proposed five new uses of Claude. The operations lead has to classify each one before anyone starts.

The Usage Policy

Skill 6.1What it covers and who it binds.

  • Who: anyone who submits inputs to Anthropic's products, including claude.ai and Claude Code users, API customers and end users of products built on Claude. It's about how Claude is used and doesn't bar whole industries.
  • Universal Usage Standards: fifteen prohibitions that apply to everyone, for example breaking the law, compromising computer systems without authorisation, weapons, inciting violence or hate, compromising privacy or identity rights, disinformation, deceptive campaigns or artificial activity, fraud and predatory practices, and sexually explicit content. Nothing makes these uses acceptable.
  • High-risk use cases: in eleven areas, including legal, medical, financial, credit, insurance, housing, employment and education, an AI recommendation that affects people must be meaningfully reviewed by a qualified person, and the person affected must be told AI was used. Internal drafting and general educational content are excluded.
  • Chatbots and agents: a chatbot or agent talking to external users must say it's AI, at least at the start of each chat or in the interface; it needn't name Claude. Users are accountable for what their agents do through tools, browsers and connected systems.
  • Enforcement: Anthropic may warn, throttle, suspend or end access. Problem outputs can be reported with the thumbs-down button.

Four questions for any proposal

Skill 6.1The guide's four factors, as questions.

  • Task suitability: is this something Claude does well and reliably enough (drafting, summarising, analysis with code), or does it depend on facts Claude can't know, perfect accuracy, or a judgment that must be human?
  • Delegation limits: how much would Claude decide or do alone? Check reversibility, stakes, need for empathy and accountability (skill 4.2).
  • Trust in third-party Skills and connectors: where does each add-on come from, what can it reach, and who reviewed it? Skills can run code and install packages, and the Help Center warns of prompt injection and data exfiltration: install only from trusted sources and read a Skill's files first. Connect only services you trust and need.
  • Usage Policy: is it prohibited outright? Is it in a high-risk area, needing qualified review and disclosure? Does it face the public, needing AI disclosure?

Three classifications

Skill 6.1And what each one needs written down.

  • Appropriate: suitable task, low stakes, no policy concerns, normal review. Record why it's low-risk.
  • Appropriate with safeguards: acceptable only with named controls, such as human review, disclosure, restricted sources, limited data, or monitoring. Record each safeguard and who owns it; without them, the classification falls to inappropriate.
  • Inappropriate: prohibited by the Usage Policy, or the risks can't be brought down to an acceptable level. Record the reason and, where possible, an acceptable alternative.

"Add a disclaimer and it's fine" doesn't rescue a prohibited use, and "it's high-risk, so ban it" ignores that the policy allows high-risk uses with qualified human review and disclosure. Check which kind of rule applies before classifying.

A use-case register

Skill 6.1Five proposals, classified with documented rationale.

use_case_register.txt (Use-case register) 
AI use-case register: outdoor-gear shop (reviewed by the operations lead, 20 Nov)

1. Draft internal training notes on new tents from the spec sheets
   Classification: APPROPRIATE
   Rationale: internal drafting from our own documents; errors are cheap and
   caught in normal review; no personal data.

2. Website chat assistant answering product and delivery questions
   Classification: APPROPRIATE WITH SAFEGUARDS
   Safeguards: tells customers at the start of each chat that they're talking
   to an AI; answers only from the product and delivery pages; hands refunds,
   complaints and safety questions to staff; weekly review of transcripts.
   Rationale: Usage Policy requires AI disclosure for chatbots facing external
   users; we're accountable for what it says and does.

3. Recommend approve/decline for "pay in 3 instalments" applications
   Classification: APPROPRIATE WITH SAFEGUARDS (or don't proceed)
   Safeguards: credit is a high-risk area, so a qualified staff member
   meaningfully reviews every recommendation and can change it before it's
   acted on; applicants are told AI was used; the credit partner's rules,
   not Claude, set the criteria.
   Rationale: affects individuals' access to credit; human review and
   disclosure are policy requirements, not options.

4. Generate 200 five-star "customer" reviews for new products
   Classification: INAPPROPRIATE
   Rationale: fake reviews are deceptive artificial activity, prohibited by
   the Usage Policy's Universal Usage Standards; no safeguard makes it
   acceptable. Alternative: email real buyers asking for reviews.

5. Install a free "competitor price scraper" Skill found on a forum
   Classification: INAPPROPRIATE (as proposed)
   Rationale: unknown source; Skills can run code and send data out, and the
   Help Center says to install Skills only from trusted sources. May be
   reconsidered if IT reviews the files and the target sites' terms allow it.

Next review: when a use changes, or in six months.
  • The rationale is the point: it lets someone else check the decision later and shows what would change it.
  • Safeguards are specific: who reviews, what's disclosed, which sources are allowed, how it's monitored.
  • Revisit: a change in scope (the chat assistant starting to issue refunds, say) means a new review.

Key takeaways

  • The Usage Policy binds all users: Universal Usage Standards are absolute prohibitions; high-risk areas need qualified human review and disclosure; public-facing chatbots must say they're AI; you're accountable for your agents.
  • Assess every proposal on task suitability, delegation limits, trust in third-party Skills and connectors, and the Usage Policy.
  • Classify as appropriate, appropriate with safeguards (named controls and owners), or inappropriate (with the reason and an alternative).
  • A disclaimer can't rescue a prohibited use; a high-risk use isn't automatically banned.
  • Keep a register with the rationale, and re-review when the scope changes.

AI and ML services in scope

The exam guide lists Amazon Bedrock, Amazon SageMaker AI and Amazon Quick for basic, strategic-level application.

ServiceWhat it isKnow for the exam
Amazon BedrockManaged, serverless access to foundation models from Amazon and other providers, with tools to build generative AI applicationsThe default way to build with foundation models on AWS. Prompts and outputs aren't used to train models or shared with model providers. Charged mainly per token.
Amazon Bedrock AgentCoreA platform to build, deploy and operate AI agents with any framework and modelRuntime and Harness, Gateway (APIs as MCP tools), Memory, Identity, Policy (deterministic tool-call rules), Observability, Evaluations, Registry. Supports MCP and A2A. Recommended for new agents.
Amazon SageMaker AIA service to build, train, tune and deploy custom ML modelsThe "build" option for custom ML on proprietary data, such as a fraud model. Needs ML skills. SageMaker AI MLflow tracks experiments and models.
Amazon QuickAn AI-powered workspace for business users, used through natural language chatQuick Sight (dashboards and BI), Quick Flows and Quick Automate (automation), Quick Index (answers grounded in company documents), Quick Research (cited reports). Fully managed, no ML skills needed, per-user subscriptions: the "buy" option for business users. Evolved from Amazon QuickSight, which continues as Quick Sight.

Amazon Bedrock features

FeatureWhat it doesTypical exam trigger
Knowledge BasesManaged RAG: connects company documents (S3, SharePoint, Confluence and others), retrieves relevant passages, adds citations; document-level permission filtering in the managed option"Answers must use our latest documents and show sources"
GuardrailsContent filters (hate, insults, sexual, violence, misconduct, prompt attacks) for text and images; denied topics; word filters; sensitive information filters; contextual grounding checks; Automated Reasoning checks. Usable without a Bedrock model through the ApplyGuardrail API."Block harmful content", "mask personal data", "stop off-topic advice", "detect hallucinations"
Contextual grounding checkScores each answer for grounding (supported by the source) and relevance (answers the question); blocks below a threshold"Reduce hallucinations in a RAG application"
Automated Reasoning checksValidates answers against logical rules; detects errors and unstated assumptions"Answers must respect policy rules exactly"
EvaluationsAutomatic, human and judge-model evaluation of models; RAG evaluation of retrieval and answers"Compare models", "test before switching models"
Model customizationSupervised fine-tuning (labeled examples), reinforcement fine-tuning (reward functions), distillation (large teacher to small student)"Consistent house style or format" (fine-tune); "same quality, lower cost at volume" (distill)
Prompt management and optimizationStores and versions prompts; suggests optimized rewrites"Govern and reuse approved prompts"
Prompt cachingReuses long, repeated prompt prefixes at a lower token rate and with faster responses"The same long instructions or document in every request"
Intelligent prompt routingRoutes each request between two models in a family for the best quality for the cost"Cut cost without losing quality on easy requests"
Application inference profilesPer-application profiles with tags, to track model usage and cost by application or team"Show each department its AI spend"
Cross-Region inferenceSpreads requests across Regions within a geography"Higher availability and throughput"
Data AutomationTurns documents, images, audio and video into structured output, with confidence scores"Staff retype thousands of forms"
Converse APIOne consistent interface across Bedrock models"Switch models without rewriting the application"
Model lifecycleEach model is Active, Legacy or End-of-Life. Most Legacy periods are six months. Requests fail after end-of-life, and migration isn't automatic."The model is being retired; what should the business plan?"

AWS frameworks and guidance

FrameworkWhat it isRemember
AWS Cloud Adoption Framework (CAF)AWS's framework for transformation readiness and planning; CAF for AI enriches it for AISix perspectives: Business, People, Governance, Platform, Security, Operations. Four phases: Envision, Align (find capability gaps), Launch, Scale. People capabilities include transformational leadership, culture evolution, change acceleration, workforce transformation.
AWS shared responsibility modelHow security duties split between AWS and the customerAWS secures the infrastructure and managed services; the customer secures its content, access, configuration, guardrails, agent permissions and lawful use
Responsible AI dimensionsAWS's definition of responsible AIFairness, explainability, privacy and security, safety, controllability, veracity and robustness, governance, transparency (the Responsible AI Lens lists ten by separating privacy, security, veracity and robustness)
Well-Architected Responsible AI LensBest practices for building AI responsibly (November 2025)Eight focus areas: use case, benefits and risks, release criteria, dataset planning, system planning, evaluate and release, user guidance, monitoring. Release criteria are met by baking, filtering or guiding. Not a compliance checklist.
Well-Architected Generative AI LensBest practices for generative AI workloads across the six pillarsExcessive agency: least privilege and user confirmation for agents (GENSEC05). Cost: right-size models, choose pricing models, shorten prompts and responses, cache, set agent stopping conditions (GENCOST01 to 05).
Well-Architected Machine Learning LensBest practices for ML workloadsBusiness goal identification is the most important phase; first decide whether ML is the right approach
Generative AI Security Scoping MatrixClassifies generative AI uses by ownership to decide security controls1 consumer app, 2 enterprise app, 3 pre-trained model, 4 fine-tuned model, 5 self-trained model. Buy (1 to 2) or build (3 to 5)?
AWS AI Service CardsDocumentation for AWS AI servicesIntended uses, limitations, responsible AI design choices, best practices
AWS Generative AI Innovation CenterAWS scientists and strategists working with customersThe "partner" route: advisory, hands-on builds, embedded teams

Pricing and cost management

ItemWhat it isRemember
Consumption-based pricingPay per use: tokens, requests, pagesBedrock on-demand: per input and output token; output usually costs more. Grows with volume and request length.
Instance-based pricingPay per hour for reserved capacityDedicated hosting in SageMaker AI; Bedrock Provisioned Throughput (no-commitment, 1-month or 6-month terms). Paid whether used or not; suits steady, high volume.
Seat-based pricingPay per user per monthAmazon Quick; most office AI assistants. Grows with headcount, not usage.
Bedrock batch inferenceAsynchronous processing of large request sets50% lower price than on-demand for supported models; for work nobody is waiting on
AWS Pricing CalculatorFree tool to estimate costs before buildingShows the calculations; groups estimates; exports CSV or PDF; state assumptions and unit costs
AWS Cost ExplorerAnalyzes actual costs and usageUp to 13 months of history and an 18-month forecast; group by service or tag; natural-language questions
AWS BudgetsSpending and usage limits with alertsAlerts on actual or forecasted spend; budget actions can respond automatically; alerts can lag charges
Cost allocation tagsLabels that group costs by application, team or cost centerCombine with Bedrock application inference profiles for per-application model costs; enables showback and chargeback
AWS MarketplaceCurated catalog of third-party software, data and servicesCharges on the AWS bill; private offers with custom pricing and terms; a route to buy AI tools or engage partners, or to sell agentic solutions

High-risk examples: recruitment, credit scoring (except fraud detection), life and health insurance pricing. Dates: prohibitions February 2025; general-purpose AI August 2025; transparency August 2026; Annex III high-risk December 2027; product-embedded high-risk August 2028 (as amended in 2026).

NIST AI Risk Management FrameworkVoluntary US framework (January 2023)Govern, Map, Measure, Manage. Generative AI Profile: NIST AI 600-1 (July 2024).

Formulas and numbers

Formulas
ROI
(total benefits − total costs) ÷ total costs × 100%
Payback period
The time until cumulative benefits equal cumulative costs
Weighted score
Σ (criterion score × criterion weight)
Recall
Frauds caught ÷ all actual frauds
Precision
True frauds flagged ÷ all items flagged
AI effect
Change in the pilot group − change in the comparison group
Unit cost
Daily AI cost ÷ daily transactions
Agent success
(Step success rate) to the power of (number of steps); for example, 0.9510 ≈ 0.60
  • Prediction outcomes: true positive, false positive (false alarm), false negative (miss), true negative. Accuracy misleads for rare events.
  • Time savings: capacity, not cash, unless they reduce spending or produce valuable output.
  • Tokens: the context window counts input plus output; conversation history is resent on every turn.
  • Innovation portfolio: a common starting split is 70% core, 20% adjacent, 10% transformational.

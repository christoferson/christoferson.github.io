Templates

Fill-in templates for the documents an AI business strategist produces, from the first use case brief to the production readiness review. Each shows what to capture, with placeholders in {braces} and short notes on the fields that matter most. They also make a good revision tool: if you can fill one in for a scenario, you understand that part of the exam.

Strategy and value

Use case brief

UseAt the envision stage, to propose a use case on one page so it can be compared with others.

use-case-brief 
Use case:
  {short name}
Business owner:
  {name, role}
  accountable for the outcome
Objective served:
  {which stated business objective}
Problem today:
  {what is slow, costly or risky, with
  numbers}
Proposed solution:
  {what the AI will do: rules, ML,
  generative AI or agent; what stays with
  people}
Users:
  {who uses it; internal or
  customer-facing}
Value hypothesis:
  {e.g. save 90 seconds on 24,000 calls a
  day}
Measures:
  {primary KPI, guardrail metrics}
Data needed:
  {sources; available? permitted for this
  use?}
Risk tier (draft):
  {low / medium / high, and why}
Sourcing (draft):
  {build / buy / partner}
Not AI?
  {could rules, a process change or a
  dashboard solve it instead?}
Next step:
  {e.g. 6-week proof of concept}

WatchA brief without a business owner, a baseline number or a "could this be solved without AI?" answer isn't ready to compare.

How to fill it

FieldHow to fill itCommon mistake
Use caseA short name for the task the AI will do, in business language: "after-call summaries", not "LLM pilot"Naming a technology instead of a task
Business ownerOne named business leader who will answer for the result and has authority over the process that changes"The AI team", or two co-owners
Objective servedQuote the stated business objective it moves. If it doesn't move one, say so; that is useful information.Vague goals such as "innovation" or "being AI-first"
Problem todayWhat is slow, costly or risky now, with measured numbers and their source. If you have no numbers, getting them is the first step.Estimates from memory ("it takes ages")
Proposed solutionWhat the AI will do, the solution type (rules, ML, generative AI, agent), and what stays with peopleChoosing the most advanced type by default
UsersWho uses it, how many, and whether it is internal or customer-facing. The audience strongly affects the risk tier.Forgetting the people affected by its output, not only those who use it
Value hypothesisA testable statement: effect per case × volume × value, with the assumption labeled as an estimate to be testedCounting all time saved as cash
MeasuresOne primary business KPI, one or two guardrail metrics, and a leading indicator for the pilotModel accuracy as the only measure
Data neededEach source, whether it exists digitally, its quality, who owns it, and whether its use for this purpose is permittedAssuming data collected for one purpose can be used for another
Risk tier (draft)A first view using impact on people, autonomy, data sensitivity, audience, scale, reversibility and regulation. Governance confirms it.Classifying by technology rather than use
Sourcing (draft)Build, buy or partner, with one line of reasoning (differentiation, timeline, skills, data, compliance)Deciding before checking whether a product already does this
Not AI?An honest answer: could a rule, a process change or a dashboard solve most of the problem?Leaving it blank
Next stepThe smallest step that tests the riskiest assumption, with a duration and a decision at the end"Build it", with no end point

Filled example

use-case-brief: after-call summaries (Harbor Mutual) 
Use case:
  After-call summaries for contact-center
  agents

Business owner:
  Head of Contact Center Operations

Objective served:
  Reduce contact-center cost per call by
  15% without lowering customer
  satisfaction

Problem today:
  After-call work averages 6.2 minutes per
  call (4-week measurement from the
  contact-center system). 24,000 calls a
  day. Note quality varies; 18% of notes
  fail the monthly quality audit.

Proposed solution:
  Generative AI drafts a structured
  summary from the call transcript. The
  agent reviews and edits every draft
  before saving. Nothing is saved without
  the agent.

Users:
  400 agents (internal). Customers are
  affected indirectly through the notes
  used on their next call.

Value hypothesis (estimate to be tested):
  Save about 90 seconds per call x 24,000
  calls a day = 600 agent-hours a day.
  Cash benefit only if freed time avoids
  planned hiring (40 agents for 10% volume
  growth).

Measures:
  Primary:
    after-call work time per call
  Guardrails:
    CSAT stays at or above 82%; note audit
    pass rate at or above 90%
  Leading:
    share of drafts accepted without edits

Data needed:
  Call transcripts (available; owner: Head
  of Contact Center). Privacy officer to
  confirm use for this purpose is covered
  by the customer notice.

Risk tier (draft):
  Medium. Internal, human review of every
  draft, reversible; but it processes
  personal data at scale.

Sourcing (draft):
  Build on Amazon Bedrock with a partner,
  with handover to two internal engineers.
  No product fits our note format and
  systems, and we need the skills for
  later use cases.

Not AI?
  Partly. A shorter note template would
  save about 20 seconds; we will adopt it
  anyway. It doesn't remove the typing.

Next step:
  4-week baseline, then an 8-week pilot
  with 40 agents and a comparison group of
  40. Decision on rollout at the end of
  the pilot.

Notice what makes this brief strong:

  • Every number has a source.
  • The value is stated as a hypothesis, separating capacity from cash.
  • The "Not AI?" answer is honest and leads to an extra quick fix.
  • The next step ends in a decision.

Use case scoring matrix

UseTo prioritize several candidates consistently. Weights are agreed by the steering committee before scoring.

scoring-matrix 
Criteria and weights (must total 100%)
  Business value       {35}%
    savings, revenue, risk, experience
  Feasibility          {25}%
    data, complexity, skills, integration
  Sustainability       {15}%
    running cost, maintenance, lasting
    value
  Strategic alignment  {25}%
    fit with objectives and risk appetite

Score each 1 (low) to 5 (high), with a
one-line reason.

Candidate: {name}
  Value {4}   Feasibility {5}
  Sustainability {4}   Alignment {4}
  Weighted {4.25}   Group {quick win}

Candidate: {name}
  Value { }   Feasibility { }
  Sustainability { }   Alignment { }
  Weighted {    }   Group {    }

Weighted = Σ (score × weight)
Groups: quick win / strategic bet /
        nice to have / avoid

WatchScores support judgment; a mid-scoring idea with one fixable blocker may still be the right next step.

How to fill it

FieldHow to fill itCommon mistake
Criteria and weightsAgree the four criteria and their weights with the steering committee before anyone scores, so weights reflect strategy, not favorite projectsChanging weights after seeing the scores
Score definitionsWrite what 1, 3 and 5 mean for each criterion (for example, feasibility 5 = data available and joined, no new integration)Scores that mean different things to different people
ScoresScore as a group with business, technical and risk views, and write one line of reasoning per scoreOne person scoring alone, or scores with no reasons
Weighted scoreMultiply each score by its weight and add them upSimple averages that ignore the agreed weights
GroupPlace each candidate as a quick win, strategic bet, nice to have or avoid, based on value and feasibilityRanking only by the total and ignoring the mix of risk and timing
Judgment checkNote any fixable blocker that explains a low score, and whether a smaller version would score betterTreating the score as the decision

ROI worksheet

UseTo build the business case, then to check it against measured results after launch.

roi-worksheet 
Measured effect:
  {e.g. 1.3 min saved per call, pilot vs
  comparison group}
Volume:
  {e.g. 24,000 calls/day × 220 days}

Benefits per year
Time savings realized (cash only)
  Y1 {   }  Y2 {   }  Y3 {   }
Cost reduction
  Y1 {   }  Y2 {   }  Y3 {   }
Revenue (at margin)
  Y1 {   }  Y2 {   }  Y3 {   }
Losses avoided
  Y1 {   }  Y2 {   }  Y3 {   }

Costs per year
Build, partner or licences
  Y1 {   }  Y2 {   }  Y3 {   }
Usage (tokens, hosting)
  Y1 {   }  Y2 {   }  Y3 {   }
Data preparation
  Y1 {   }  Y2 {   }  Y3 {   }
Run team
  Y1 {   }  Y2 {   }  Y3 {   }
Training and change management
  Y1 {   }  Y2 {   }  Y3 {   }
Governance and monitoring
  Y1 {   }  Y2 {   }  Y3 {   }
Model upgrades and retesting
  Y1 {   }  Y2 {   }  Y3 {   }

Results
Total benefits (3 years):
  {   }
Total costs (3 years):
  {   }
ROI = (benefits − costs) ÷ costs
  {   }
Payback (cumulative benefits pass costs):
  {   }
ROI if benefits are 50% lower:
  {   }
Intangible benefits (reported, not
priced):
  {   }

WatchCount saved time as cash only when it reduces spending or produces valuable output; otherwise it is capacity.

How to fill it

FieldHow to fill itCommon mistake
Measured effectUse the effect measured against a comparison group in the pilot, not the vendor's claim or a before-and-after figureUsing the most optimistic number available
VolumeReal volumes from systems, with working days and expected growthPeak volumes presented as normal
Time savings realizedOnly the part of saved time that turns into cash: avoided hiring, less overtime or outsourcing, or extra output that is soldCounting all saved hours at full salary
Other benefitsCost reduction, revenue at profit margin, and losses avoided, each with its sourceUsing revenue instead of margin
CostsEvery category, every year: build, usage, data, run team, training, governance, upgradesLeaving out running and people costs after year 1
Ramp-upReduce year 1 benefits for the time adoption takesAssuming full benefit from day one
SensitivityShow ROI if benefits are much lower, for example 50%Presenting a single number as certain
IntangiblesList them separately, with how they are measuredForcing them into dollar values

Filled example

roi-worksheet: after-call summaries 
Measured effect:
  1.3 min saved per call (pilot vs
  comparison group)
Volume:
  24,000 calls/day × 220 days
Time freed:
  about 114,000 agent-hours a year (gross
  value about $4.0M: capacity, not cash)

Benefits per year (cash only)
Avoided hiring of 40 agents for growth
  Y1 $1,200,000 (50% ramp-up)
  Y2 $2,400,000
  Y3 $2,400,000

Costs per year
Build (partner project)
  Y1 $300,000   Y2 -          Y3 -
Usage (tokens)
  Y1 $85,000    Y2 $85,000    Y3 $85,000
Run team (2 people)
  Y1 $280,000   Y2 $280,000   Y3 $280,000
Training and change management
  Y1 $50,000    Y2 -          Y3 -
Governance and monitoring
  Y1 $40,000    Y2 $40,000    Y3 $40,000

Results
Total benefits (3 years):
  $6,000,000
Total costs (3 years):
  $1,565,000
ROI:
  (6,000,000 − 1,565,000) ÷ 1,565,000
  = about 283%
Payback:
  within year 1
ROI if benefits are 50% lower:
  about 92%
Intangible benefits:
  more time with complex callers; note
  quality (audit pass rate)

What makes this example strong:

  • It uses the measured 1.3 minutes, not the 1.6 a before-and-after comparison suggested.
  • Only avoided hiring is counted as cash; the $4.0 million gross time value is shown but not used.
  • All cost categories run for all three years, and the 50% test shows the decision doesn't depend on optimism.

KPI definition

UseFor every KPI in a business case, so before and after are measured the same way.

kpi-definition 
KPI:
  {name}
Definition:
  {exact calculation and scope}
Type:
  {tangible / intangible (proxy)}
  {leading / lagging}
Owner:
  {name}
Baseline:
  {value, period measured, seasonality
  noted}
Target:
  {value} by {date}
Data source:
  {system}, {frequency}
Guardrails:
  {metrics that must not get worse, e.g.
  CSAT}
Attribution:
  {comparison group / A-B test /
  before-after}

How to fill it

FieldHow to fill itCommon mistake
KPI and definitionThe exact calculation and scope, precise enough that two people would get the same numberDefinitions like "handling efficiency"
TypeTangible or intangible (with its proxy), leading or laggingOnly lagging measures, so problems show up too late
OwnerOne named person who reports it and acts on itA team or a system as the owner
BaselineThe measured value before any change, the period it covers, and any seasonalityA baseline captured after the pilot started
Target and dateA realistic target with a date, based on the pilotA target with no date, or copied from a vendor claim
Data sourceThe system and how often it is refreshedManual spreadsheets nobody maintains
GuardrailsMetrics that must not get worse while this one improvesNo guardrails, so gains hide losses
AttributionHow the AI's effect will be separated from other changesCrediting the AI with every improvement

Filled example

kpi-definition: after-call work time 
KPI:
  Average after-call work time per call
Definition:
  Minutes from call end to "wrap-up
  complete" in the contact-center system,
  all inbound service calls, excluding
  calls transferred out
Type:
  Tangible; lagging (operational)
Owner:
  Head of Contact Center Operations
Baseline:
  6.2 minutes (4 weeks, February; no storm
  events)
Target:
  4.9 minutes by end of Q3 (all agents)
Data source:
  Contact-center system timestamps, daily
Guardrails:
  CSAT at or above 82%; note audit pass
  rate at or above 90%
Attribution:
  Pilot group vs comparison group of 40
  agents

Governance and risk

AI policy (outline)

UseAs the organization's top-level statement on AI, approved by the steering committee or board.

ai-policy-outline 
1. Purpose and scope
   {which AI, which people, which
   entities}
2. Principles
   {fairness, explainability, privacy and
   security, safety, controllability,
   veracity and robustness, governance,
   transparency}
3. Prohibited uses
   {e.g. emotion recognition of staff;
   fully automated claim denials}
4. Risk appetite and tiers
   {definition of low / medium / high and
   the review each needs}
5. Roles
   {steering committee, review board, CoE,
   business owners, data owners}
6. Approved tools
   {link to the AI tool register}
7. Data rules
   {what data may be used in which tools}
8. Human oversight
   {where people must decide or review}
9. Transparency
   {disclosure to customers and staff}
10. Monitoring and incidents
   {requirements and reporting}
11. Training
   {AI literacy requirements by role}
12. Review
   {policy owner; review at least yearly}

How to fill it

FieldHow to fill itCommon mistake
Purpose and scopeWhich AI it covers (built, bought and embedded features), which people (staff, contractors) and which entitiesCovering only systems built in-house
PrinciplesThe responsible AI dimensions the organization commits to, in plain languagePrinciples with no link to any process
Prohibited usesSpecific uses that are never allowed, including legal prohibitionsLeaving this empty or vague
Risk tiersDefinitions of each tier and the review, approval and monitoring each needsOne process for every use
RolesWho decides what: steering committee, review board, CoE, ownersUnclear decision rights
Data rulesWhich data classifications may be used in which kinds of toolsReferring to a data policy staff can't find
Oversight and transparencyWhere people must decide or review; when customers and staff are told AI is involvedPromising human review that isn't resourced
ReviewAn owner and a review cycle of at least once a yearA policy that never changes while AI does

ai-tool-register-entry

  {1 consumer / 2 enterprise / 3
  pre-trained / 4 fine-tuned / 5
  self-trained}
Business owner:
  {name}
Allowed data:
  {public / internal / confidential /
  personal}
Allowed uses:
  {   }
Not allowed:
  {   }
Decision basis:
  {terms on data use, opt-out, data
  location, security review, cost, value}
Alternative:
  {approved tool to use instead, if
  blocked}

  {if under evaluation, who and until
  when}

  {date}

How to fill it

FieldHow to fill itCommon mistake
Tool and statusThe exact product and version; one status onlyListing a vendor without naming the product
Scoping MatrixConsumer app, enterprise app, pre-trained model, fine-tuned or self-trained; decides the depth of reviewTreating a consumer app like an enterprise contract
Allowed data and usesData classifications permitted and specific allowed usesApproving a tool without stating what data may go into it
Decision basisThe facts behind the decision: data-use terms, opt-out, location, security, cost, valueDecisions with no stated reason
AlternativeFor blocked tools, the approved tool to use insteadBlocking with no alternative
Pilot groupFor tools under evaluation, who may use them and until whenOpen-ended evaluations
Next reviewA date; tools and their terms changeEntries never revisited

  trade-offs}

  {executive sponsor}

  {business unit leaders, CIO/CTO, chief
  risk officer, general counsel, privacy
  officer, CISO, HR, customer experience}
Decides:
  {AI strategy and investment priorities;
  AI policy; high-risk approvals; risk
  appetite}
Delegates:
  {medium risk to the review board; low
  risk to business owners with
  registration}
Inputs:
  {portfolio dashboard, risk reports,
  incidents}
Outputs:
  {decision records with conditions and
  owners}
Cadence:
  {monthly}
Reports to:
  {board risk committee}, {quarterly}
Quorum and escalation:
  {   }

How to fill it

FieldHow to fill itCommon mistake
PurposeWhat the committee directs and decides, in one or two linesA purpose so broad it overlaps every other body
Chair and membersThe executive sponsor as chair; every function with a stake, including legal, risk, privacy, security and HROnly technology leaders
Decides and delegatesWhat it decides itself, and what it delegates to the review board and ownersApproving every AI use itself, which creates a bottleneck
Inputs and outputsThe reports it receives; the decision records it producesMeetings with no recorded decisions
Cadence and reportingHow often it meets and how it reports to the boardNo line to the board
EscalationHow disputed trade-offs reach it, and how fastNo defined route, so disputes stall projects

Risk classification questionnaire

UseAt the idea stage, and again whenever the system changes. The highest-risk answer usually sets the tier.

risk-classification 
1. Effect on people Could output affect
   someone's finances, health, job, rights
   or safety?
   {no / some / significant}
2. Autonomy Does the AI suggest, act with
   approval, or act alone?
   {suggest / approve / act}
3. Data Public, internal, personal or
   special-category?
   {   }
4. Audience Internal staff, or customers
   and public?
   {   }
5. Scale Decisions or interactions per
   day?
   {   }
6. Reversibility Are errors easy to spot
   and undo?
   {yes / no}
7. Regulation Named in AI-specific law
   (e.g. EU AI Act high-risk) or a closely
   regulated sector?
   {   }
8. Prohibited Does it match any prohibited
   use?
   {yes / no}

Tier:
  {prohibited / high / medium / low}
Review path:
  {   }

  {new data, customer-facing, more
  autonomy, new country, new law}

How to fill it

FieldHow to fill itCommon mistake
Effect on peopleConsider everyone affected by the output, not just usersAnswering only for the user
AutonomyWhat the system can do without a person approvingIgnoring planned increases in autonomy
Data and audienceThe most sensitive data involved; internal or publicAssuming internal tools carry no data risk
Scale and reversibilityVolume of decisions, and how easily errors are found and undoneIgnoring scale when each error seems small
RegulationCheck AI-specific law (for example, EU AI Act high-risk areas) and sector rulesAssuming no AI law means no regulation
TierThe highest-risk answer usually sets the tier; record the reasoningAveraging answers to get a lower tier
Re-classification triggersThe changes that would move the tierClassifying once and never again

Filled example

   payment of honest customers' claims
2. Autonomy suggest; investigators decide
3. Data personal and financial data
4. Audience internal, but affects
   customers
5. Scale about 10,000 claims a month
6. Reversibility partly; delays can't be
   undone
7. Regulation insurance and
   anti-discrimination rules; the EU AI
   Act credit-scoring category excludes
   fraud detection
8. Prohibited no

  HIGH
Reason:
  significant effect on customers at
  scale, with fairness risk across regions

  full review, impact assessment, steering

  new data sources, any automatic denial,

What makes this example strong:

  • It considers customers affected by the output, not just the investigators who use it.
  • It records why the EU AI Act category doesn't apply.
  • It still classifies as high on impact and scale.

  {   }

What stays humanThe decisions and reviews people keepLeaving people to assume the worst

Templates

Fill-in templates for the documents an AI business strategist produces, from the first use case brief to the production readiness review. Each shows what to capture, with placeholders in {braces} and short notes on the fields that matter most. They also make a good revision tool: if you can fill one in for a scenario, you understand that part of the exam.

Strategy and value

Use case brief

UseAt the envision stage, to propose a use case on one page so it can be compared with others.

use-case-brief 
Use case:          {short name}
Business owner:    {name, role}  accountable for the outcome
Objective served:  {which stated business objective}
Problem today:     {what is slow, costly or risky, with numbers}
Proposed solution: {what the AI will do; rules, ML, generative AI
                   or agent}
Users:             {who uses it; internal or customer-facing}
Value hypothesis:  {e.g. save 90 seconds on 24,000 calls a day}
Measures:          {primary KPI, guardrail metrics}
Data needed:       {sources; available? permitted for this use?}
Risk tier (draft): {low / medium / high, and why}
Sourcing (draft):  {build / buy / partner}
Not AI?            {could rules, a process change or a dashboard
                   solve it instead?}
Next step:         {e.g. 6-week proof of concept}

WatchA brief without a business owner, a baseline number or a "could this be solved without AI?" answer isn't ready to compare.

How to fill it

FieldHow to fill itCommon mistake
Use caseA short name for the task the AI will do, in business language: "after-call summaries", not "LLM pilot"Naming a technology instead of a task
Business ownerOne named business leader who will answer for the result and has authority over the process that changes"The AI team", or two co-owners
Objective servedQuote the stated business objective it moves. If it doesn't move one, say so; that is useful information.Vague goals such as "innovation" or "being AI-first"
Problem todayWhat is slow, costly or risky now, with measured numbers and their source. If you have no numbers, getting them is the first step.Estimates from memory ("it takes ages")
Proposed solutionWhat the AI will do, the solution type (rules, ML, generative AI, agent), and what stays with peopleChoosing the most advanced type by default
UsersWho uses it, how many, and whether it is internal or customer-facing. The audience strongly affects the risk tier.Forgetting the people affected by its output, not only those who use it
Value hypothesisA testable statement: effect per case × volume × value, with the assumption labeled as an estimate to be testedCounting all time saved as cash
MeasuresOne primary business KPI, one or two guardrail metrics, and a leading indicator for the pilotModel accuracy as the only measure
Data neededEach source, whether it exists digitally, its quality, who owns it, and whether its use for this purpose is permittedAssuming data collected for one purpose can be used for another
Risk tier (draft)A first view using impact on people, autonomy, data sensitivity, audience, scale, reversibility and regulation. Governance confirms it.Classifying by technology rather than use
Sourcing (draft)Build, buy or partner, with one line of reasoning (differentiation, timeline, skills, data, compliance)Deciding before checking whether a product already does this
Not AI?An honest answer: could a rule, a process change or a dashboard solve most of the problem?Leaving it blank
Next stepThe smallest step that tests the riskiest assumption, with a duration and a decision at the end"Build it", with no end point

Filled example

use-case-brief: after-call summaries (Harbor Mutual) 
Use case:
  After-call summaries for contact-center agents

Business owner:
  Head of Contact Center Operations

Objective served:
  Reduce contact-center cost per call by 15% without
  lowering customer satisfaction

Problem today:
  After-call work averages 6.2 minutes per call
  (4-week measurement from the contact-center system).
  24,000 calls a day. Note quality varies; 18% of notes
  fail the monthly quality audit.

Proposed solution:
  Generative AI drafts a structured summary from the call
  transcript. The agent reviews and edits every draft
  before saving. Nothing is saved without the agent.

Users:
  400 agents (internal). Customers are affected indirectly
  through the notes used on their next call.

Value hypothesis (estimate to be tested):
  Save about 90 seconds per call
  x 24,000 calls a day = 600 agent-hours a day.
  Cash benefit only if freed time avoids planned hiring
  (40 agents for 10% volume growth).

Measures:
  Primary:   after-call work time per call
  Guardrail: CSAT stays at or above 82%;
             note audit pass rate at or above 90%
  Leading:   share of drafts accepted without edits

Data needed:
  Call transcripts (available; owner: Head of Contact
  Center). Privacy officer to confirm use for this purpose
  is covered by the customer notice.

Risk tier (draft):
  Medium. Internal, human review of every draft,
  reversible; but it processes personal data at scale.

Sourcing (draft):
  Build on Amazon Bedrock with a partner, with handover to
  two internal engineers. No product fits our note format
  and systems, and we need the skills for later use cases.

Not AI?
  Partly. A shorter note template would save about
  20 seconds; we will adopt it anyway. It doesn't remove
  the typing.

Next step:
  4-week baseline, then an 8-week pilot with 40 agents
  and a comparison group of 40. Decision on rollout at
  the end of the pilot.

Notice what makes this brief strong:

  • Every number has a source.
  • The value is stated as a hypothesis, separating capacity from cash.
  • The "Not AI?" answer is honest and leads to an extra quick fix.
  • The next step ends in a decision.

Use case scoring matrix

UseTo prioritize several candidates consistently. Weights are agreed by the steering committee before scoring.

scoring-matrix 
Criteria and weights (must total 100%)
  Business value       {35}%
    savings, revenue, risk, experience
  Feasibility          {25}%
    data, complexity, skills, integration
  Sustainability       {15}%
    running cost, maintenance, lasting value
  Strategic alignment  {25}%
    fit with objectives and risk appetite

Score each 1 (low) to 5 (high), with a one-line reason.

Candidate: {name}
  Value {4}   Feasibility {5}
  Sustainability {4}   Alignment {4}
  Weighted {4.25}   Group {quick win}

Candidate: {name}
  Value { }   Feasibility { }
  Sustainability { }   Alignment { }
  Weighted {    }   Group {    }

Weighted = Σ (score × weight)
Groups: quick win / strategic bet /
        nice to have / avoid

WatchScores support judgment; a mid-scoring idea with one fixable blocker may still be the right next step.

Governance and risk

AI policy (outline)

UseAs the organization's top-level statement on AI, approved by the steering committee or board.

ai-policy-outline 
1. Purpose and scope       {which AI, which people, which entities}
2. Principles              {fairness, explainability, privacy and
                           security, safety, controllability,
                           veracity and robustness, governance,
                           transparency}
3. Prohibited uses         {e.g. emotion recognition of staff;
                           fully automated claim denials}
4. Risk appetite and tiers {definition of low / medium / high and
                           the review each needs}
5. Roles                   {steering committee, review board, CoE,
                           business owners, data owners}
6. Approved tools          {link to the AI tool register}
7. Data rules              {what data may be used in which tools}
8. Human oversight         {where people must decide or review}
9. Transparency            {disclosure to customers and staff}
10. Monitoring and incidents {requirements and reporting}
11. Training               {AI literacy requirements by role}
12. Review                 {policy owner; review at least yearly}

ai-tool-register-entry

                   4 fine-tuned / 5 self-trained}

Allowed data:      {public / internal / confidential / personal}
Allowed uses:      {   }
Not allowed:       {   }

Risk classification questionnaire

UseAt the idea stage, and again whenever the system changes. The highest-risk answer usually sets the tier.

risk-classification 
1. Effect on people: could output affect someone's finances,
   health, job, rights or safety?            {no / some / significant}
2. Autonomy: does the AI suggest, act with approval, or act
   alone?                                    {suggest / approve / act}
3. Data: public, internal, personal, special-category?   {   }
4. Audience: internal staff or customers and public?     {   }
5. Scale: decisions or interactions per day?             {   }
6. Reversibility: are errors easy to spot and undo?      {yes / no}
7. Regulation: named in AI-specific law (e.g. EU AI Act
   high-risk) or closely regulated sector?               {   }
8. Prohibited: does it match any prohibited use?         {yes / no}

Tier: {prohibited / high / medium / low}
Review path: {   }   Re-classify if: {new data, customer-facing,
more autonomy, new country, new law}

Tier: {   }   Approver: {   }

Veracity
  {correct answers on test set}  ≥ {99%}
Robustness
  {accuracy on unusual inputs}  ≥ {   }
Fairness
  {gap between groups or languages}  < {2 points}
Privacy
  {data disclosures in attack tests}  = {0}
Safety
  {harmful outputs in red-team tests}  = {0}
Explainability
  {answers with sources}  = {100%}
Controllability
  {time to switch off}  < {15 min}
Transparency
  {AI disclosure shown}  = {100%}

If a criterion isn't met:
  {narrow scope / delay / add review}

Vendor evaluation questionnaire

UseBefore buying an AI product or engaging an AI partner.

vendor-questionnaire 
Evidence
  Tested on our data? Results we can verify?
Data use
  Is our data used to train or improve models?
  Can we opt out? Shared with third parties?
  Where is it stored and processed?
Models
  Which models does it use?
  What happens when they change or are retired?
Compliance
  Certifications and independent audit reports
  (e.g. ISO/IEC 42001, ISO/IEC 27001)?
  Support for our duties as deployer?
Intellectual property
  Who owns outputs and custom models?
  Is there IP indemnity?
Security
  Access controls, encryption, logging,
  incident notification?
Responsible AI
  Fairness testing? Explanations? Guardrails?
Cost
  Pricing model? Cost at 3× today's volume?
Exit
  Export of data, prompts and configuration?
  Notice periods and transition support?

Delivery and operations

Monitoring plan entry

UseFor each metric that must be watched in production. A metric without an owner and response isn't a control.

monitoring-plan-entry 
Metric:      {e.g. answers blocked by grounding check}
Area:        {quality / safety / fairness / security / operations /
             cost / customer}
Threshold:   {e.g. above 3% of answers in a day}
Frequency:   {real-time / daily / weekly / monthly}
Source:      {logs, dashboard, sampled review}
Owner:       {name}
Response:    {what happens when the threshold is crossed}

UsePrepared and rehearsed before go-live, for each production AI system.

incident-runbook

Business owner:  {   }
On call:         {   }

1. Detect
   Sources: {monitoring alerts, staff, customers}
2. Contain
   Switch-off: {how; who may decide; < 15 min}
   Fallback: {manual process / previous version}
3. Assess
   Who was affected, how, since when?
   Logs: {where; who can access}

   Inform: {review board, sponsor, communications}
5. Remediate

   Blameless review within {5} days

Escalation criteria

UseFor any assistant or agent: when the AI must hand over to a person.

escalation-criteria 
Hand over to a person when:
  - the user asks for a person
  - the topic is {decisions reserved for people, e.g. denials}
  - safeguards block an answer {2} times in one conversation
  - confidence is below {threshold}
  - the user shows signs of vulnerability or makes a complaint
  - identity can't be verified
  - an action exceeds {limit, e.g. $40 a day}
  - there is no resolution after {10} turns
Hand over with: {summary, verified identity, documents}
Target time to human: {   }

People and data

Change communication message

UseTo announce an AI change to affected staff, delivered by their own managers, well before go-live.

change-message 
What is changing:  {tool, who, from when}
What stays human:  {decisions and reviews people keep}
Why:               {the problem and realistic expected outcome}
Your job:          {honest statement of effects on roles and the
                   commitments the company makes}
Monitoring:        {what is measured; what isn't used to rate people}
Support:           {training dates, champion, help channel}
Your voice:        {how to give feedback; Q&A sessions}
What happens next: {when results will be shared; decision points}

WatchOnly make commitments leadership can keep; a broken promise undermines every later change.

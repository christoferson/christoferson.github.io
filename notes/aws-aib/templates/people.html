People and data

Change communication message

The change message is how staff first hear that AI is coming to their work. It answers the questions people ask whether or not leaders address them: will I lose my job, what changes for me and when, will it monitor me, what stays human, and how will I get help.

Rumors fill any gap the message leaves, and they are usually worse than the truth. An honest, specific message, with commitments leadership can actually keep, is one of the cheapest and most effective adoption tools there is; a broken promise undermines every later change.

Who owns itThe executive sponsor for the overall program; the affected team's own manager for each rollout.

WhenBefore the pilot starts, not when the tool appears on screens; repeated at each stage.

RelatedTask 4.3: transparent communication · Task 4.3: cultural barriers

UseTo announce an AI change to affected staff, delivered by their own managers, well before go-live.

Filled example

Change message: after-call summary pilot
What is changing
From 3 March, 40 agents in two teams will pilot a tool that drafts after-call notes.
What stays human
You review and edit every draft. Nothing is saved without you.
Why
Wrap-up takes about 6 minutes per call. We expect to save around a minute, time we want you to spend on customers who need more help.
Your job
No agent will lose their job because of this tool. As call volumes grow, we expect to hire fewer new agents than we otherwise would.
Monitoring
We measure how the tool performs. We won't rate individual agents on how often they edit drafts.
Support
Two-hour training before the pilot; your team champion; help channel #agent-assist.
Your voice
Q&A every Thursday; a feedback button on each draft.
What happens next
Results shared with everyone in May, before any decision to expand.

Data product description

A data product description treats a key data set like a product: it has an owner, documented contents, a refresh schedule, a quality commitment, permitted uses and a clear way to request access. It replaces "ask around until someone sends you a spreadsheet".

Data products are how organizations stop rebuilding the same data for every AI project. When the forecasting team, the analytics team and the fraud team all reuse one governed data set, quality improves once for everyone, and permitted uses are decided once, by the person who understands the data.

Who owns itThe business data owner, with a data steward handling day-to-day quality.

WhenWhen a data set becomes shared across teams; updated as contents or quality change.

UseFor each key data set shared for AI and analytics, published in the data catalog.

How to fill it

Owner and steward

How to fill itA business leader accountable for it, and the person who maintains it day to day.

Common mistakeIT as the owner.

Contents and refresh

How to fill itWhat is included, since when, and how often it updates.

Common mistakeNo refresh commitment.

Quality

How to fill itMeasured quality figures, not adjectives.

Common mistake"High quality" with no numbers.

Classification

How to fill itThe sensitivity level, which drives access rules.

Common mistakeUnclassified data with personal details.

Permitted uses

How to fill itAllowed and not-allowed purposes, matching the privacy notice.

Common mistakeSilence, so any use seems allowed.

Access

How to fill itHow to request, who approves, and masking rules.

Common mistakeAccess by personal favour.

Data sharing agreement (outline)

A data sharing agreement governs data combined across organizations: the purpose, exactly which data, permitted and prohibited uses, how it is shared (ideally without exchanging raw data, for example in a clean room), security, retention, ownership of results, compliance and what happens at the end.

Some of the most valuable AI uses need data no single company holds, such as measuring advertising across a manufacturer and a retailer or spotting fraud across institutions. The agreement is what makes that possible without turning a partnership into a privacy or competition problem.

Who owns itLegal, with the business owners and data owners on each side.

WhenBefore any data is exchanged or a joint analysis is set up; reviewed when the purpose changes.

UseBefore sharing data with a partner or combining data across organizations.

Data sharing agreement (outline)
1. Parties and purpose
One specific purpose only
2. Data in scope
Exact fields; anonymized or aggregated where possible
3. Permitted and prohibited uses
Including whether data may train models
4. Method
For example: a clean room with analysis rules; no raw data exchanged
5. Security requirements
Encryption, access, logging
6. Retention and deletion
How long, and how it is deleted
7. Ownership of results
Insights and models
8. Compliance
Lawful basis, data residency
9. Audit rights and logs
Who can audit, and how
10. Termination
What happens to data and results

How to fill it

Purpose

How to fill itOne specific purpose; anything else needs a new agreement.

Common mistakeBroad purposes such as "analytics"

Data in scope

How to fill itExact fields, minimized, anonymized or aggregated where possible.

Common mistakeSharing whole tables "in case"

Method

How to fill itHow sharing happens; prefer methods where raw data isn't exchanged.

Common mistakeEmailing extracts.

Uses

How to fill itPermitted and prohibited, including whether data may train models.

Common mistakeSilence on model training.

Ownership

How to fill itWho owns results, insights and any models.

Common mistakeDisputes discovered later.

Compliance and audit

How to fill itLawful basis, residency, logs and audit rights.

Common mistakeNo way to check the partner.

Termination

How to fill itWhat happens to data and results at the end.

Common mistakeData kept forever.

Filled example

Data sharing agreement: retailer and manufacturer campaign measurement
1. Parties and purpose
The retailer and a consumer goods manufacturer; measuring whether the manufacturer's online ads led to purchases in the retailer's stores
2. Data in scope
Manufacturer: hashed email addresses of people shown the ads, campaign ID. Retailer: hashed email addresses of loyalty members who bought the product, purchase date. No names.
3. Permitted and prohibited uses
Campaign measurement only; no targeting of individuals, and no model training on the other party's data
4. Method
AWS Clean Rooms collaboration; aggregation analysis rules allow only the agreed overlap query, with a minimum group size of 100
5. Security
Encryption; access limited to named analysts on each side
6. Retention and deletion
Aggregated results kept 2 years; contributed tables removed 90 days after the campaign ends
7. Ownership of results
Both parties may use the aggregated results
8. Compliance
Covered by both privacy notices; processing in EU Regions
9. Audit
Analysis logs reviewed quarterly
10. Termination
A leaving party's tables are removed within 30 days

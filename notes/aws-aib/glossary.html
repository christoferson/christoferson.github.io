A

TermDefinition
A/B testA test that randomly assigns cases or people to the AI or to the current approach, giving the strongest evidence of the AI's effect.
Accountable ownerThe one named person, usually a business leader, who answers for an AI system's outcomes.
Agent (AI agent)Software that pursues a goal set by people by planning steps, using tools and taking actions in other systems, within limits people set.
Agent2Agent (A2A)An open protocol for agents to communicate with other agents, including agents built by other teams or organizations.
AgentCore (Amazon Bedrock AgentCore)AWS's platform for building, deploying and operating agents with any framework and model, including Runtime, Gateway, Memory, Identity, Policy, Observability, Evaluations and Registry.
AI (artificial intelligence)The broad field of machines performing tasks that normally need human intelligence; includes rules, search and machine learning.
AI center of excellence (CoE)A small cross-functional team that provides platforms, patterns, standards, training and support so the whole organization can adopt AI well.
AI impact assessmentAn assessment of an AI system's societal, ethical and legal effects on people, used for high-risk uses alongside risk assessment.
AI inventoryA register of every AI system and tool in use, with owner, risk tier and status.
AI literacyThe ability to understand what AI can and can't do, use it effectively and responsibly, and recognize its risks.
AI management system (AIMS)The policies, roles and repeatable processes an organization uses to govern AI; the subject of ISO/IEC 42001.
AI Service CardsAWS documentation describing intended uses, limitations, responsible AI design choices and best practices for AWS AI services.
AlgorithmThe method a system uses to learn patterns from data; the recipe that produces a model.
Amazon BedrockAWS's managed service for accessing foundation models and building generative AI applications.
Amazon QuickAWS's AI-powered workspace for business users: dashboards, research, automation and answers grounded in company documents, sold per user.
Amazon SageMaker AIAWS's service for building, training and deploying custom machine learning models.
Application inference profileAn Amazon Bedrock resource that lets an application call models through its own tagged profile, so its usage and cost can be tracked.
Automated Reasoning checksAn Amazon Bedrock Guardrails safeguard that validates responses against logical rules to detect errors and unstated assumptions.
Automation biasThe tendency of people to accept AI output without real scrutiny.
AWS Cloud Adoption Framework (CAF)AWS's framework for transformation readiness: six perspectives (Business, People, Governance, Platform, Security, Operations) and four phases (Envision, Align, Launch, Scale).

B

TermDefinition
BaselineThe measured value of a KPI before the AI is introduced, used to show what changed.
Batch inferenceProcessing large sets of requests on a schedule rather than one at a time; on Bedrock, priced 50% lower than on-demand for supported models.
Bias driftA change in a model's fairness across groups after deployment.
Build, buy, partnerThe three ways to source an AI capability: create your own, use a ready-made product, or work with an outside expert.

C

TermDefinition
Chain of thoughtA prompting technique that asks the model to reason step by step before answering.
Champion (AI champion)A respected employee who adopts AI early, helps colleagues, collects feedback and spots new use cases.
Chatbot (assistant)An AI that replies to each request with an answer or draft, leaving a person to act on it.
Clean room (AWS Clean Rooms)A service where partners analyze combined data without revealing their underlying data to each other.
Comparison groupA similar group that doesn't use the AI during a pilot, so the AI's effect can be separated from other changes.
Concept driftA change in the relationship between inputs and the right answer, so patterns the model learned stop holding.
Consumption-based pricingPricing per unit of use, such as tokens or requests.
Content filtersAn Amazon Bedrock Guardrails safeguard that detects harmful text and images (hate, insults, sexual, violence, misconduct) and prompt attacks.
Context windowThe maximum number of tokens a model can consider in one request, counting input and output.
Contextual grounding checkAn Amazon Bedrock Guardrails safeguard that blocks answers not supported by the source or not relevant to the question.
ControllabilityThe responsible AI dimension of having mechanisms to monitor and steer AI system behavior.
Converse APIAn Amazon Bedrock interface that works the same way across models, so code can switch models without rewriting.
Cross-Region inferenceAmazon Bedrock routing of requests across AWS Regions within a geography to improve availability and throughput.

D

TermDefinition
Data custodianThe technical role responsible for storing, securing and backing up data and implementing access.
Data driftA change in the inputs a model sees compared with its training data.
Data flywheelA self-reinforcing loop where use of an AI system generates data that improves it, widening an advantage over time.
Data leakageTraining data that contains information that wouldn't be available at prediction time, making test results look better than reality.
Data ownerThe business leader accountable for a data domain's definitions, quality, permitted uses and access decisions.
Data productA data set packaged for reuse, with an owner, documentation, quality commitments and a defined way to request access.
Data siloData held by one part of an organization in a way others can't readily find, access or combine.
Data stewardThe person who maintains a data set's quality, definitions and documentation day to day.
Deep learningMachine learning using many-layered neural networks, especially strong with images, audio and text.
Denied topicsAn Amazon Bedrock Guardrails safeguard that blocks subjects an application must not discuss.
DistillationTraining a smaller, cheaper "student" model to approach a larger "teacher" model's accuracy for a specific task.

E

TermDefinition
EmbeddingA list of numbers representing the meaning of text or an image, used to search by meaning rather than keywords.
Escalation criteriaWritten conditions under which an AI must hand over to a person.
EU AI ActThe EU's risk-based AI law (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) with four tiers: unacceptable, high, transparency and minimal risk.
Excessive agencyAn agent taking actions beyond its intended scope; controlled by least privilege and confirmation of consequential actions.
ExplainabilityThe responsible AI dimension of understanding and evaluating why a system produced its output.

F

TermDefinition
FairnessThe responsible AI dimension of considering impacts on different groups of stakeholders.
False negativeA case the model failed to flag that should have been flagged (a miss).
False positiveA case the model flagged that shouldn't have been (a false alarm).
FeatureA fact about a case that a model uses to make its prediction.
Feature attribution driftA change in which features a model relies on most after deployment.
Few-shot promptingIncluding a few example inputs and desired outputs in a prompt to teach format, labels or rules.
Fine-tuningTraining an existing foundation model further on labeled examples to change its behavior for specific tasks.
Foundation modelA large model trained on very broad data that can handle many tasks without task-specific training.

G

TermDefinition
Generative AIAI that creates new content such as text, images, code or audio, built on foundation models.
Generative AI Security Scoping MatrixAn AWS model that classifies generative AI uses into five scopes by ownership, from consumer apps to self-trained models, to decide security controls.
Governance (AI governance)The structures, roles, policies and processes that decide how AI is used and hold people accountable for results.
Governance by designBuilding responsible AI practices into project planning from the start rather than reviewing at the end.
Guardrail metricA metric that must not get worse while a target KPI improves, revealing hidden costs.
Guardrails (Amazon Bedrock Guardrails)Configurable safeguards for AI inputs and outputs: content filters, denied topics, word filters, sensitive information filters, contextual grounding and Automated Reasoning checks.

H

TermDefinition
HallucinationFluent, confident AI output that is false.
Human in the loopA person approves each AI decision before it takes effect.
Human on the loopThe AI acts and people monitor, able to intervene or reverse.

I

TermDefinition
InferenceUsing a trained model on a new case to produce a prediction or output.
Inference parametersSettings such as temperature and maximum output tokens that shape how a model generates a response.
Instance-based pricingPricing per hour of reserved capacity, paid whether used or not.
Intangible benefitA benefit that is hard to price reliably, measured through proxies such as satisfaction scores.
ISO/IEC 22989The international standard for AI concepts and terminology, including the AI system life cycle.
ISO/IEC 23053The international framework describing AI systems that use machine learning.
ISO/IEC 23894International guidance on AI risk management.
ISO/IEC 42001The certifiable international standard for an AI management system.

K

TermDefinition
KPI (key performance indicator)A measure showing whether an initiative is achieving its purpose.

L

TermDefinition
LabelThe known answer for a past example used in supervised training.
Lagging indicatorA measure that confirms results after they happen, such as cost savings or ROI.
Large language model (LLM)A foundation model specialized in reading and writing text.
Leading indicatorAn early measure that predicts success, such as adoption or acceptance rates.
Least privilegeGiving people, applications and agents only the permissions they need.

M

TermDefinition
Machine learning (ML)AI that learns patterns from data rather than following explicit rules.
Maturity modelA description of the stages organizations pass through as AI capability grows, such as experimentation to enterprise scale.
ModelWhat an algorithm produces after learning from data: a reusable decision engine.
Model Context Protocol (MCP)An open protocol that standardizes how agents discover and call tools and data.
Model lifecycle (Bedrock)The Active, Legacy and End-of-Life states of Amazon Bedrock models; requests fail after end-of-life.
Multimodal modelA model that works with more than one type of content, such as images and text.

N

TermDefinition
NIST AI Risk Management FrameworkA voluntary US framework for managing AI risk through four functions: Govern, Map, Measure, Manage.

O

TermDefinition
OverfittingA model that memorizes training data and performs poorly on new cases.

P

TermDefinition
ParametersThe internal values a model adjusts during training, which hold what it has learned.
Pilot purgatoryThe state of having many pilots but few AI systems in production delivering measured value.
PrecisionOf everything a model flagged, the share that was truly positive.
PredictionA model's output for a case, usually with a confidence score; a probability, not a fact.
PromptThe input given to a generative AI model to guide its response.
Prompt cachingReusing a long, repeated start of a prompt so it is billed at a lower rate and processed faster.
Prompt engineeringImproving the text given to a model so it produces the response you need.
Prompt injectionText hidden in content that tries to make a model ignore its instructions.
Prompt templateA reusable prompt with fixed approved wording and placeholders filled in for each request.
Proof of concept (POC)A small, short test of whether an idea works on real data and is worth pursuing.
Provisioned ThroughputReserved Amazon Bedrock model capacity, billed by the hour.

R

TermDefinition
RACI matrixA table showing who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted and Informed for each activity.
RAG (retrieval augmented generation)Retrieving relevant information from data sources and adding it to the prompt so the model answers from it.
RecallOf all the truly positive cases, the share the model caught.
Red-teamingDeliberately trying to make an AI system misbehave, to find weaknesses before release.
Reinforcement fine-tuningCustomizing a model using reward functions that score response quality, instead of labeled answers.
Release criteriaMeasurable thresholds across responsible AI dimensions that a system must meet before release.
Responsible AIDesigning, developing and using AI with the goal of maximizing benefits and minimizing risks.
Responsible AI LensAWS Well-Architected guidance for building AI use cases responsibly, organized into eight focus areas.
Risk tierA classification of an AI use by potential harm that sets the depth of review, documentation and monitoring.
RobustnessProducing correct outputs for both expected and unexpected inputs.
ROI (return on investment)(Total benefits minus total costs) divided by total costs.
Rule-based automationSoftware that follows logic written in advance and gives the same output for the same input every time.

S

TermDefinition
SafetyThe responsible AI dimension of preventing harmful output and misuse.
Seat-based pricingPricing per user per month.
Sensitive information filtersAn Amazon Bedrock Guardrails safeguard that blocks or masks personal data in inputs and outputs.
Shadow AIUse of AI tools the organization hasn't reviewed or approved.
Shadow modeRunning a new AI system in parallel with the current process without using its output, to compare results.
Shared responsibility modelThe split of security duties: AWS secures the infrastructure; the customer secures its data, access, configuration and use.
Structured dataData in rows and columns with fixed meaning, such as transaction tables.
Supervised learningLearning from historical examples with known answers (labels).
Sustainable competitive advantageAn advantage competitors can't easily copy, such as proprietary data, feedback loops and redesigned processes.

T

TermDefinition
TemperatureAn inference parameter controlling how varied or focused a model's output is.
ThresholdThe business rule that turns a model's score into an action.
TokenThe unit of text a model reads and writes: a word, part of a word or punctuation; the basis of most generative AI pricing.
Total cost of ownership (TCO)The full cost of an option over several years, including build, usage, people, governance and upgrades.
TransparencyThe responsible AI dimension of enabling people to make informed choices about their engagement with an AI system.

U

TermDefinition
Unstructured dataFree text, documents, images, audio and video whose meaning must be interpreted.
Unsupervised learningLearning patterns, such as groups or unusual cases, from data without labels.
Use caseA specific business task AI will perform or improve, with a measurable outcome.

V

TermDefinition
Vector storeA database that stores embeddings so content can be searched by meaning.
VeracityThe responsible AI dimension of achieving factually correct outputs.

W

TermDefinition
Word filtersAn Amazon Bedrock Guardrails safeguard that blocks specific words or phrases.

Z

TermDefinition
Zero-shot promptingGiving a model an instruction with no examples.

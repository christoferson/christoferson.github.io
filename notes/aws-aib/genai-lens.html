The lens at a glance

What it is

A Well-Architected lens that "provides guidance and best practices for designing, deploying, and operating generative AI applications on AWS. It extends the Well-Architected Framework to address the unique considerations and opportunities of using foundation models and generative AI technologies." It was first published on April 15, 2025; the current version, November 19, 2025, added a section on agentic AI, eight scenarios, an updated responsible AI section and best practice updates across the pillars.

Scope

Generative AI applications that use foundation models on Amazon Bedrock or customer-managed models on Amazon SageMaker AI, and business applications built with Amazon Q, Bedrock and SageMaker AI. Best practices are written "in a cloud- and technology-agnostic way", with AWS-specific implementation steps. Traditional ML is left to the Machine Learning Lens, and pre-training foundation models from scratch is out of scope.

Audience

"Architects, builders, security experts, MLOps engineers, and decision-makers."

How it relates to the other AI lenses

It follows the six Well-Architected pillars, asking how to build generative AI that is well run, secure, reliable, performant, cost-effective and sustainable. The Responsible AI Lens instead follows the ML life cycle and asks whether the system is responsible for its use case. Both are imported into the Well-Architected Tool as custom lenses from AWS's public sample-well-architected-custom-lens GitHub repository. For the framework's pillars, lens types and risk labels, see the Well-Architected basics on the Responsible AI Lens page.

Structure

Six pillars, each with design principles, 29 questions (such as GENOPS01) and 51 best practices (such as GENOPS01-BP01): operational excellence 10, security 10, reliability 10, performance efficiency 8, cost optimization 9, sustainability 4. Each best practice states a desired outcome, the benefits (linked to framework design principles), its risk level, implementation guidance and steps. A recurring instruction is to record decisions in the organization's AI policy, which the lens calls "the central authority for operational questions pertaining to AI workloads".

The lens cites some services whose status has changed since it was published: Amazon Bedrock Agents is now Bedrock Agents Classic, closed to new customers since July 30, 2026 (AWS recommends AgentCore); Amazon Q Business is closed to new customers (use Amazon Quick, where QuickSight continues as Quick Sight); SageMaker Debugger is closed to new customers. The best practices themselves still apply.

The running example

Examples on this page follow an online electronics retailer's customer support assistant built on Amazon Bedrock. It answers questions from product manuals and the returns policy (RAG over a knowledge base) and, as an agent, can look up orders and start returns through internal APIs. It handles about 40,000 conversations a day, peaking around holiday sales.

Definitions

The lens defines 35 terms. They are reproduced here, because the rest of the lens uses them precisely:

Generative AI Lens definitions
Agent
An AI system that can perform tasks autonomously and interact with its environment to achieve specific goals.
Bias and fairness testing
Evaluating and mitigating potential biases or unfair outcomes from AI models, particularly in areas like gender, race, or age.
Continuous pre-training
The process of continuously updating a pre-trained model with new data to improve its performance and adapt to evolving domains or tasks.
Chunking
Breaking up large data files into small chunks to allow the foundation model to fit that data into a context window.
Data management
The process of identifying, collecting, storing, aggregating, searching, tracking, governing, and using data.
Embedding
Transforms chunks of data into vectors that represent semantic meaning.
Fine-tuning
The process of adapting a pre-trained model to a specific task or domain by training it on a smaller, task-specific dataset.
Foundation models
Large language models pre-trained on vast amounts of data, serving as a foundation for downstream tasks and fine-tuning.
Foundation model providers
Companies or organizations that develop and release foundation models for use by others.
Generative AI
AI systems capable of generating new content, such as text, images, or code, based on input data or prompts.
Hallucination
A phenomenon where a generative AI model produces outputs that are inconsistent, factually incorrect, or unrelated to the input prompt.
Human oversight
Mechanisms for human experts to review, validate, and control critical decisions or outputs from AI models.
Indexing
Process of inserting embedded chunks into a vector data store.
Knowledge graph
A structured representation of real-world entities and their relationships, used to enhance the contextual understanding and reasoning capabilities of AI systems.
LLMOps or GenAIOps
Operational practices and principles for managing the lifecycle of large language models, including model selection, data preparation, deployment, monitoring, and governance.
Model card
A document that provides key information about a machine learning model, including its intended use, training data, performance characteristics, and potential limitations or biases.
Model customization
The process of modifying a foundation model using various techniques to control its behavior.
Model distillation
A technique for creating a smaller, more efficient model that mimics the behavior of a larger, more advanced model.
Model evaluation
The process of assessing the performance, robustness, and other characteristics of language models using various metrics and techniques.
Model gateway
An interaction layer offering secure access to the model hub through standardized APIs.
Model hub
A central repository providing access to enterprise foundation models from first-party, third-party, and open-source providers.
Model interpretability
The ability to understand and explain the reasoning behind a model's outputs, increasing transparency and interpretability.
Model orchestration
Encapsulation of multistep workflows which are characteristic of generative AI workflows.
Pre-training
Building a foundation model from scratch. Requires GPU clusters to run continuously for weeks.
Prompt catalog
A centralized repository for storing, managing, and versioning prompts used to interact with generative AI models.
Prompt engineering
The practice of carefully crafting prompts to guide language models to produce desired outputs.
Provisioned throughput
Feature of Amazon Bedrock that allows you to provision a higher level of throughput at a fixed cost for predictable, high-throughput workloads.
Quantization
Techniques for reducing the precision of model parameters, thereby decreasing the memory footprint and computational requirements.
Responsible AI
The practice of developing and deploying AI systems in a manner that prioritizes fairness, transparency, accountability, and adherence to ethical principles.
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
A technique where a language model's output is augmented with relevant information retrieved from a corpus of documents, to ground responses in the documents and reduce hallucination.
Self-hosted models
AI models that are deployed and managed by the organization using them, rather than relying on a third-party provider.
Serverless architecture
An architecture pattern where the cloud provider automatically manages the allocation and provisioning of computational resources, allowing for scalability and cost optimization.
Tokenization
The process of breaking down input text into smaller units called tokens, which can be words, subwords, or characters.
Vector store
A specialized data store for efficient storage and retrieval of high-dimensional vector embeddings, often used in semantic search; for example, Amazon OpenSearch Serverless.
Zero-shot learning
The ability of a model to perform a task on examples it has never seen before, without requiring task-specific training data.

Design principles

Six principles apply across the lens; each pillar then adds its own.

Design for controlled autonomy

Guardrails, boundaries, operational requirements, security controls and failure conditions that keep AI systems "within safe, efficient, and cost-effective parameters". AWS ties this to security, cost and reliability.

Implement comprehensive observability

Metrics across every layer (user feedback, model behavior, resource use, security events), for data-driven improvement and fast problem resolution.

Optimize resource efficiency

Choose and configure components "based on empirical requirements rather than assumptions": right-sized models, optimized data operations, dynamic scaling.

Establish distributed resilience

Redundancy, automated recovery and geographic distribution, so systems stay up through component or Regional failures.

Standardize resource management

Centralized catalogs and controls for prompts, models and access permissions, for security, governance, version control and cost.

Secure interaction boundaries

Least-privilege access, secure communications, input and output sanitization and monitoring on every data flow and interface.

Responsible AI

The lens uses AWS's eight-dimension view: fairness, explainability, privacy and security, safety, controllability, veracity and robustness, governance, and transparency (the Responsible AI Lens splits two of them, giving ten). Its key statement: "Elements of the Responsible AI framework are weighted more heavily for generative AI systems as opposed to traditional machine learning solutions (like veracity or truthfulness). However, the implementation of Responsible AI requires a systematic review of the system along the defined dimensions." The landing page also frames responsibility as shared "between model producers, providers, and consumers".

The section illustrates each dimension with a financial services assistant offering guidance, loan processing and investment advice:

  • Fairness: mortgage decisions on financial criteria, not "unwanted discriminatory factors", with bias testing and outcome audits by customer segment.
  • Explainability: an investment advisor "must be able to articulate the reasoning behind its investment recommendations" to customers and regulators.
  • Privacy and security: systems "access only the information necessary for their specific functions".
  • Safety: a defined use case scope matching risk tolerance, with guardrails against regulatory breaches and escalation for edge cases.
  • Controllability: "clear procedures for adjusting or disabling AI systems when necessary".
  • Veracity and robustness: diverse testing and automated reasoning "to capture and correct hallucinations in real-time".
  • Governance: committees spanning technical, business and risk perspectives.
  • Transparency: "clearly communicate when and how AI is being used, what data informs decisions, and what controls are in place".

AWS's argument is that governance in place from the start speeds deployment: "This is more than risk management."

The generative AI lifecycle

The lens evaluates six phases against the six pillars (its text says "seven key phases" but lists six). The lifecycle mapping shows which best practices belong to each.

1. Scoping

Understand the business problem; define goals, requirements and success measures; and above all "determine the relevance of generative AI in solving the problem". Ask what kinds of model you need, whether an off-the-shelf model will do or needs customizing, and whether one model or several orchestrated ones. Assess feasibility, build a risk profile, check data availability and "create security scoping matrices for different use cases". Costs are driven by "prompt lengths, data architecture and access patterns, model selection, and agent orchestration".

2. Model selection

Evaluate models on "modality, size, accuracy, training data, pricing, context window, inference latency, and compatibility"; choose hosting (batch, real-time or a combination); check the provider's data usage policy; and consider offering several options through model routing and a model catalog.

3. Model customization

Align the model with the application's goals, iteratively: "prompt engineering, RAG, agents, fine-tuning, continuous pre-training, model distillation, and human feedback alignment", with prompt template management and bias mitigation.

4. Development and integration

Integrate the model into the application: conversational interfaces, prompt catalogs, agents and knowledge bases; guardrails against hallucination; APIs, user interface and automated integration tests; load handling; and monitoring set up before production.

5. Deployment

Roll out "in a controlled manner", with CI/CD, infrastructure as code (AWS CDK, CloudFormation or Terraform), versioning for quick rollback, and validated security and privacy.

6. Continuous improvement

Monitor "accuracy, toxicity, and coherence", collect feedback, find biases, update data, try new techniques, train teams and reassess the AI strategy.

Data architecture

Data is "the strategic differentiator": generative AI "thrive[s] on high-quality, context-rich, and well-governed data". The lens distinguishes three uses of data, each with different needs:

Pre-training

Petabyte-scale, diverse, mostly unstructured data on highly scalable infrastructure, with quality, versioning, privacy and long-term storage challenges.

Fine-tuning and customization

Smaller, curated datasets needing "rapid iteration, efficient data preprocessing, and careful versioning to track the relationship between datasets and model performance", and bias reduction.

RAG

"Low-latency data retrieval systems", efficient indexing of large knowledge bases, up-to-date content, and privacy when retrieving at inference time.

Its strategic imperatives are data quality as the foundation (governance, automated validation, monitoring), a unified data architecture that breaks down silos across structured and unstructured data, and a privacy-first approach (differential privacy, federated learning, synthetic data). Success factors are scalable infrastructure, data observability (lineage, anomaly detection), cross-functional collaboration and KPIs such as data quality scores and time to model deployment.

Agentic AI

AI agent

"Autonomous entities that are capable of perceiving their environment, reasoning, and taking actions to achieve defined goals", usually an LLM "augmented with capabilities like retrieval (access to information), tools (interacting with its environment), and memory". "Agentic" comes from agency: "the ability to act independently, make decisions, and take actions".

The key rule: "only increase the agency of the system when the task complexity requires it." Agency is a spectrum, across three patterns:

LLM-augmented workflows (low agency)

Largely deterministic code paths with certain steps decided by an LLM. AWS's example: a document processor that classifies documents as simple or complex and routes them with tool calls. Still agentic, because it decides through tool invocations.

Autonomous agents

An LLM with retrieval, tools and memory "orchestrated in a loop": the ReAct (reason and act) loop. The agent receives tools (internal APIs, data connections, functions, "often connected to the agent using the MCP protocol") and a system prompt with its goal and role, and loops until it reaches a stop reason.

Hybrid (high agency)

The plan-and-solve loop: one LLM creates a plan and task list, a series of ReAct agents resolves the tasks from a queue, and another LLM call decides whether to answer the user or add tasks. It scales with queues, auto scaling and asynchronous execution.

ExampleThe support assistant is an autonomous agent with two tools (order lookup, start return) and one knowledge base. It doesn't need a plan-and-solve design, so it doesn't get one.

Operational excellence

Pillar principles: implement comprehensive observability "across all layers of your generative AI system, from foundation models to user interactions"; automate operational management with infrastructure as code, templates and pipelines; and establish operational controls such as "managing prompt templates, implementing rate limits, and enabling workflow tracing".

GENOPS01: How do you achieve and verify consistent model output quality?

GENOPS01-BP01 Periodically evaluate functional performance High

Foundation models "are inherently non-deterministic with a realistic chance of failure", so run ground truth prompts on a schedule, compare outputs with known answers, and track results over time to catch drift. Use stratified sampling (sample from each category, such as user persona), refresh the ground truth as real usage diverges, and give fine-tuned models the same criteria. Tools: Amazon Bedrock evaluations with model invocation logging, SageMaker AI evaluations, or open-source fmeval and ragas; name a single-threaded workload owner.

ExampleEvery Monday, 600 golden questions (returns, warranties, product specs), stratified by product category, run against the assistant; a drop of more than 3 points pages the owner.

GENOPS01-BP02 Collect and monitor user feedback High

Feedback surfaces degradation "as [it happens] without requiring ground truth data". It can be "as simple as thumbs up or thumbs down", stored and reviewed regularly, with improvements A/B tested. Amazon Q Business offers a feedback API (PutFeedback) and analytics dashboard; for Bedrock, store feedback in S3 and process it with Lambda. The AI policy decides how feedback is used.

GENOPS02: How do you monitor and manage the operational health of your applications?

GENOPS02-BP01 Monitor all application layers High

Instrument the application layer, the service layer ("an optional gateway that negotiates prompts and brokers responses") and the foundation model layer, plus knowledge graphs, flows and agents. Managed services integrate with CloudWatch and CloudTrail; add tracing with AWS X-Ray or agent traces, alarms and dashboards, and GuardDuty for security.

GENOPS02-BP02 Monitor foundation model metrics High

Track invocations, latency, token counts, errors and throttling (on Bedrock, metrics such as InputTokenCount, OutputTokenCount and InvocationThrottles), with warning and error thresholds, anomaly detection, and practiced incident response playbooks.

GENOPS02-BP03 Implement solutions to mitigate the risk of system overload High

Two approaches: scale self-hosted inference with SageMaker AI inference components, or rate limit and throttle managed inference, raising capacity with cross-Region inference or Provisioned Throughput. Know your Bedrock quotas, throttle at API Gateway, retry with exponential backoff and jitter, make operations idempotent, and add circuit breakers.

GENOPS03: How do you maintain traceability for your models, prompts, and assets?

GENOPS03-BP01 Implement prompt template management High

"A robust, versioned prompt template management system": templates with variables, versions that include hyperparameters, variants compared against a ground truth baseline. Amazon Bedrock Prompt Management stores and versions prompts; Bedrock Flows "supports versioning, rollback, and A/B testing".

GENOPS03-BP02 Enable tracing for agents and RAG workflows High

Traces show each reasoning step, exposing poor decisions, unnecessary actions and slow steps. Bedrock Agents return traces (pre-processing, orchestration, post-processing, failure and guardrail traces) when tracing is enabled; Amazon Bedrock AgentCore "supports agent tracing by default", with OpenTelemetry. Store traces centrally and protect them, because they can contain sensitive data.

ExampleTraces show the agent calling order lookup twice per conversation; fixing the prompt halves tool calls and cuts response time by a second.

GENOPS04: How do you automate the lifecycle management of your generative AI workloads?

GENOPS04-BP01 Automate generative AI application lifecycle with infrastructure as code (IaC) High

Define the workload as code (AWS CDK, CloudFormation or Terraform), keep it in Git, deploy through CI/CD with production approval gates, use the same templates with different parameters per environment, and govern with AWS Config and Service Catalog.

GENOPS04-BP02 Implement GenAIOps to optimize the application lifecycle High

"GenAIOps is a specialized subset of machine learning operations (MLOps)", covering two things: operationalizing model consumption (DevOps plus RAG and agent patterns) and operationalizing model training and tuning (FMOps, or LLMOps for language models: experiments, model registry, evaluation, deployment). Tools include SageMaker AI Pipelines, MLflow and model registries, with a feedback loop that triggers retraining.

GENOPS05: How do you determine when to execute Gen AI model customization?

GENOPS05-BP01 Learn when to customize models High

"Prioritize prompt engineering and RAG before model customization." Move to fine-tuning or continued pre-training for a specific task with labeled data, domain language or higher accuracy; build a custom foundation model only when no pre-trained model fits, you have "a vast amount of proprietary data", or you need full control.

Security

Pillar principles: implement comprehensive access controls across models, data, endpoints and agents; secure data and communication flows; monitor and enforce security boundaries on control and data planes; and control AI system behaviors with guardrails, secure prompt catalogs and "clear boundaries for agentic behaviors".

GENSEC01: How do you manage access to generative AI endpoints?

GENSEC01-BP01 Grant least privilege access to foundation model endpoints High

A zero-trust approach: least-privilege IAM roles with session durations and permissions boundaries, private network access, and organization-level controls (service control policies, resource control policies, permission boundaries) that "block or restrict models your organization has not approved", by account and Region.

GENSEC01-BP02 Implement private network communication between foundation models and applications High

A "scoped down data perimeter": reach Bedrock, Amazon Q and SageMaker AI endpoints through AWS PrivateLink with endpoint policies and security groups, and keep vector stores and agent tools private too.

GENSEC01-BP03 Implement least privilege access permissions for foundation models accessing data stores High

"Treat generative AI systems and their foundation models just as you would treat privileged users." Controls at the model layer alone are "insufficient": classify data by use, filter vector stores by metadata, sanitize "at time of ingestion" (a support assistant needs manuals, "not company financials, staff information or HR policies"), and test with curated prompts that the model can't reach sensitive data.

GENSEC01-BP04 Implement access monitoring to generative AI services and foundation models High

CloudTrail plus model invocation logging, centrally stored and alerted on, and "log both name of the generative AI application and the end-user making the request", per agent in agentic workloads.

GENSEC02: How do you stop generative AI applications from generating harmful, biased, or factually incorrect responses?

GENSEC02-BP01 Implement guardrails to mitigate harmful or incorrect model responses High

Guardrails range "from keyword identification and regular expression matching to automated reasoning and constitutional AI". Amazon Bedrock Guardrails provides content filters (including prompt attacks), denied topics, word filters, sensitive information filters and Automated Reasoning checks, and the ApplyGuardrail API works for self-hosted models. Define a fallback action for when a guardrail trips (a disclaimer or a refusal), and add human review or model-as-a-judge for critical responses.

ExampleDenied topics: legal advice and competitor pricing claims. When a guardrail trips, the assistant offers to connect a human agent.

GENSEC03: How do you monitor and audit events associated with your generative AI workloads?

GENSEC03-BP01 Implement control plane and data access monitoring to generative AI services and foundation models High

Track changes to services, infrastructure and data stores (CloudTrail management and data events, CloudWatch), across six monitoring categories: performance, quality and accuracy (including "hallucination rates"), security (authentication, prompt injection, unusual access), cost, audit trail, and compliance (retention, personal data, consent, geography).

GENSEC04: How do you secure system and user prompts?

GENSEC04-BP01 Implement a secure prompt catalog Medium

Prompts take effort to engineer and control behavior, so store them centrally, encrypted, with identity-based permissions and separation of duties. "Prompt catalogs are not meant for the same use as databases": they add versioning and can insert a prompt version into a flow dynamically.

GENSEC04-BP02 Sanitize and validate user inputs to foundation models High

"Prompt injection is the risk of introducing new content or material to a prompt that could impact its behavior." Put a validation layer between user input and the model (guardrails, keyword checks, a separate LLM-as-a-judge), test against curated injection prompts, set context boundaries in the system prompt, and "set character and token size limits on prompts and rate limits on requests".

GENSEC05: How do you avoid excessive agency for models?

GENSEC05-BP01 Implement least privilege access and permissions boundaries for agentic workflows High

"Excessive agency is an Open Worldwide Application Security Project (OWASP) Top 10 security threat for LLMs": an agent taking "actions beyond their intended purpose", usually not malicious but "an unintended consequence of automation". Scope agent execution roles and flow service roles to specific resources, separate the roles of the prompt engineer and the security engineer who writes the agent's IAM role, use permissions boundaries, and require user confirmation for actions.

ExampleThe agent's role can read orders and create return requests for the signed-in customer only; it has no refund permission, and starting a return asks the customer to confirm.

GENSEC06: How do you detect and remediate data poisoning risks?

GENSEC06-BP01 Implement data purification filters for model training workflows High

"Data poisoning is best handled at the data layer before training or customization has taken place"; it "is considered successful if the model has learned from poisoned data". Isolate training environments, filter for biased, incorrect, hateful, violent or irrelevant data (Bedrock Guardrails for text, Amazon Transcribe toxicity detection for voice), and test for toxicity after training.

Reliability

Reliability is "the system's ability to consistently perform its intended functions correctly and deliver expected results under both normal and adverse conditions". Key metrics: inference availability, response time consistency, recovery time and point objectives, error and recovery rates. Pillar principles: design for distributed resilience, implement robust error management ("avoid cascading failures for agent workflows"), standardize resource management through catalogs, and architect for intelligent scalability.

GENREL01: How do you determine throughput quotas (or needs) for foundation models?

GENREL01-BP01 Scale and balance foundation model throughput as a function of utilization Medium

Monitor throughput and quota use; buffer requests in a queue "so that models do not deny or drop requests due to throughput constraints"; handle token limits with token-bucket rate limiting or Provisioned Throughput, concurrency limits with queuing or cross-Region inference, and model loading with warm pools.

ExampleHoliday sales triple traffic, so the retailer requests quota increases a month ahead and routes through a cross-Region inference profile.

GENREL02: How do you maintain reliable communication among different components of your generative AI architecture?

GENREL02-BP01 Implement redundant network connections among model endpoints and supporting infrastructure Medium

Multiple network paths and at least two Availability Zones for endpoints, vector databases, agent APIs and functions; cross-Region connectivity (VPC peering, Transit Gateway, VPC Lattice) with failover; and regular failover drills.

GENREL03: How do you implement remediation actions for generative AI workload loops, retries, and failures?

GENREL03-BP01 Use logic to manage prompt flows and gracefully recover from failure Medium

Define expected behavior before, during and after each prompt; check RAG results for relevance with a fallback when nothing is found; have agents classify tool results as actionable (for example, a query returning at least one result) or inactionable (errors, empty responses) and retry or fail gracefully; use fallback prompt templates and circuit breakers. Bedrock Flows offers condition and iterator nodes for this.

GENREL03-BP02 Implement timeout mechanisms on agentic workflows High

"Detect and terminate long-running unexpected workflows": timeouts on the functions behind agent action groups (allowing for cold starts), agent-level timeouts for sessions waiting on users, event publishing so stalls are visible, dead letter queues, and clear user-facing errors with next steps.

GENREL04: How do you maintain versions for prompts, model parameters, and foundation models?

GENREL04-BP01 Implement a prompt catalog Medium

Release a prompt version only after it passes testing thresholds and roll back when behavior changes; pair prompt versions with hyperparameter ranges (temperature, top_p, top_k) and with test results per model version; govern with approvals and audit trails.

GENREL04-BP02 Implement a model catalog Low

A catalog of first-party, third-party and custom models with versions, lineage, benchmarks and model cards, so "the deployed model is the appropriate model for the given use case" and deployment can be automated and rolled back.

GENREL05: How do you distribute inference workloads over multiple regions of availability?

GENREL05-BP01 Load-balance inference requests across all regions of availability Medium

Bedrock cross-Region inference profiles or multi-AZ SageMaker AI endpoints with auto scaling, health checks and failover, accepting "increased network latency and operational complexity".

GENREL05-BP02 Replicate embedding data across all regions of availability Medium

Replicate embeddings and knowledge bases (S3 Cross-Region Replication, OpenSearch cross-cluster replication, AWS Glue), within the limits of data sovereignty and residency.

GENREL05-BP03 Verify that agent capabilities are available across all regions of availability Medium

Agents need the same or equivalent APIs in each Region: deploy them multi-Region with latency-based routing, and enable model access in every required Region if you don't use a cross-Region profile.

GENREL06: How do you design high-performance distributed computation tasks to maximize successful completion?

GENREL06-BP01 Design for fault-tolerance for high-performance distributed computation tasks High

Customization and training are "long-running, expensive, and brittle", running on dozens or hundreds of machines for days or months. Use managed orchestration (SageMaker HyperPod, with automatic node replacement and auto-resume from checkpoints; Bedrock managed customization; AWS Batch), checkpoint to S3 or FSx for Lustre, and automate remediation.

Performance efficiency

"The ability to consistently deliver high-quality responses and maintain optimal resource utilization." Metrics: inference latency, throughput, response quality, resource utilization and availability. Pillar principles: measure and validate performance systematically, so improvements are "based on actual measurements rather than assumptions"; optimize model and vector operations; and leverage managed services.

GENPERF01: How do you capture and improve the performance of your generative AI models in production?

"Strong performance in one task (for example, summarization) does not indicate strong performance in another task (such as question answering)."

GENPERF01-BP01 Define a ground truth data set of prompts and responses Medium

Ground truth, or a "golden dataset", is "a curated set of prompts and responses that describe the ideal workflow with a model": from dozens to thousands of pairs, with variations and several acceptable answers, and for agents "an ideal prompt flow" through each system. Treat it as "a living artifact", use mock data where production data isn't allowed, and reuse it for fine-tuning and distillation. Store it for lookup by language, domain and use case (for example in S3, cataloged with AWS Glue and queried with Athena).

GENPERF01-BP02 Collect performance metrics from generative AI workloads Medium

Response quality is part of performance: inaccurate or "overly casual, dismissive, or even toxic" answers count as under-performing. Centralize telemetry in CloudWatch, trace whole flows, benchmark against golden data or external benchmarks, alert on thresholds, and record remediation actions (scale out for latency; update and retest the system prompt for hallucinations).

GENPERF02: How do you verify your generative AI workload maintains acceptable performance levels?

GENPERF02-BP01 Load test model endpoints Medium

Test under average and extreme load to find the optimal endpoint load and set alerts below it. Fix serving bottlenecks with customization or bigger endpoints, and usage bottlenecks with cross-Region inference, prompt caching, streaming responses or batch inference.

GENPERF02-BP02 Optimize inference parameters to improve response quality Low

Find task-specific ranges for parameters such as temperature, top p and top k: test the extremes against golden data, then narrow by halving the change until its effect is negligible; use LLM-as-a-judge for large test sets; and document the ranges in the AI policy.

GENPERF02-BP03 Select and customize the appropriate model for your use case Medium

"Not all foundation models are created equal." Test families and sizes against ground truth and hard prompts, with Bedrock Evaluations or fmeval; use Amazon Bedrock intelligent prompt routing when sizes within a family have "no clear leader"; and customize by fine-tuning, continued pre-training or distillation once candidates are narrowed. Record the choice in a per-workload AI usage document.

GENPERF03: How do you optimize computational resources required for high-performance distributed computation tasks?

GENPERF03-BP01 Use managed solutions for model hosting, customization, and data access where appropriate Medium

Choose the least control you need: Bedrock (fully managed, including Custom Model Import), then SageMaker AI endpoints, then self-managed EC2. Adapt models in order: prompt engineering, then RAG, then managed customization. Train proprietary models on SageMaker HyperPod.

GENPERF04: How do you improve the performance of data retrieval systems?

GENPERF04-BP01 Test vector embeddings for latency and relevant performance Medium

Chunking and embedding "can only be addressed before data enters the data store": test fixed-size, hierarchical and semantic chunking. Benchmark search algorithms (HNSW for accuracy, IVF for balance, product quantization for compact storage, LSH for fast indexing), organize indexes hierarchically, use hybrid search, monitor each stage and re-embed as data drifts.

GENPERF04-BP02 Optimize vector sizes for your use case Low

"Start with a more compact encoding, and increase the vector size if warranted": "a well-tuned model with smaller dimensions (like 256) can sometimes outperform a more generic model with larger dimensions (1024 or greater)" on both accuracy and speed.

Cost optimization

Pillar principles: optimize model and inference selection and "avoid over-provisioning"; control resource consumption parameters (prompt length, response size, vector dimensions); and design workflow boundaries with "clear limits and exit conditions" to "avoid runaway resource consumption".

GENCOST01: How do you select the appropriate model to optimize costs?

GENCOST01-BP01 Right-size model selection to optimize inference costs Medium

Managed models charge per input and output token; self-hosted ones charge for uptime, storage and network. Start with the smallest model and "gradually increase model size and capabilities until an acceptable model is selected"; route simpler requests to smaller models (Bedrock intelligent prompt routing works within a model family); use batch inference when real time isn't needed; and re-evaluate continuously.

GENCOST02: How do you select a cost-effective pricing model (for example, provisioned, on-demand, hosted, or batch)?

GENCOST02-BP01 Balance cost and performance when selecting inference paradigms Medium

Provisioned Throughput suits throughput-sensitive workloads: validate with a short commitment first; longer commitments, such as six months, give lower unit cost. For small, periodic single-model workloads, compare SageMaker endpoints with Bedrock Custom Model Import on total cost. "Where performance trade-offs are negligible, deploy to the most cost-effective inference paradigm."

GENCOST02-BP02 Optimize resource consumption to minimize hosting costs Medium

For self-hosting: the smallest instance that meets goals, shutdown outside predictable hours, Savings Plans or reserved capacity after right-sizing (SageMaker Inference Recommender), Spot capacity for development, and techniques such as quantization and LoRA adapters.

GENCOST03: How do you engineer prompts to optimize cost?

GENCOST03-BP01 Optimize prompt token length Medium

Prompts "as short as possible while meeting performance requirements": trim words, use a separate LLM or Amazon Bedrock prompt optimization to shorten, and keep testing. "Reducing even a few tokens off the prompt contributes to cost optimization in the long-run."

GENCOST03-BP02 Control model response length Medium

Set a hard maximum with the response length parameter, ask for brevity in the prompt, and use minimal, deterministic response schemes where possible, such as "True" or "False" with an explanation only for the negative case.

GENCOST03-BP03 Implement prompt caching to reduce token costs Medium

Cache "long and repeated contexts", such as a document many questions refer to. Cache reads are billed at a reduced rate, while writes may cost more than normal input for some models; checkpoints have model-specific minimum token counts, and the cache lives five minutes, resetting on each hit. Monitor hit rates and restructure prompts to reuse more.

ExampleThe 4,000-token system prompt and returns policy start every request, so they are cached; conversation turns follow the cache checkpoint.

GENCOST03-BP04 Annotate user input to enable cost-aware content filtering Medium

Tag only user content for Amazon Bedrock Guardrails evaluation, so system prompts, verified search results and history aren't filtered on every request, using input tags with a random per-request suffix to resist injection. (Input tags aren't supported with the ApplyGuardrail API.)

GENCOST04: How do you optimize vector stores for cost?

GENCOST04-BP01 Reduce vector length on embedded tokens Medium

Use the smallest vector size (and compressed vector types, where supported) that keeps retrieval quality, adjusting chunking if needed, to cut embedding and vector database costs.

GENCOST05: How do you optimize agent workflows for cost?

GENCOST05-BP01 Create stopping conditions to control long-running workflows High

"Maximum costs for an agent's runtime can be predicted based on the implemented stopping conditions." Estimate maximum runtime (model, tools, network), set timeouts on agents and tools, use event-driven asynchronous flows that can be interrupted, and "set token limits on model responses to simulate timeout functionality".

ExampleEach conversation stops after 12 agent steps or 90 seconds, then hands over to a person, so the maximum cost per conversation is known.

Sustainability

Pillar principles: identify if generative AI is the right solution ("search engines can return results with fewer resources required than generative AI"); design for environmental efficiency; and implement dynamic resource optimization, so resources are "only consumed when needed".

GENSUS01: How do you minimize the computational resources needed for training, customizing, and hosting generative AI workloads?

GENSUS01-BP01 Implement auto scaling and serverless architectures to optimize resource utilization Medium

Prefer managed and serverless services (Bedrock, Amazon Q, SageMaker Serverless Inference), auto scale endpoints, let self-hosted models "scale to zero when not in use", shut down idle notebooks, use Inferentia for performance per watt, and track usage with CloudWatch and the Customer Carbon Footprint Tool.

GENSUS01-BP02 Use efficient model customization services Medium

Managed customization (Bedrock, SageMaker HyperPod), Trainium for lower energy use in training, parameter-efficient fine-tuning such as LoRA, managed Spot training with checkpoints, and SageMaker Debugger to stop bad jobs early.

GENSUS02: How can you optimize data processing and storage to minimize energy consumption and maximize efficiency?

GENSUS02-BP01 Optimize data processing and storage to minimize energy consumption Medium

S3 Intelligent-Tiering and lifecycle rules, columnar formats (Parquet) with compression, serverless processing (Athena, Glue, Lambda for jobs under 15 minutes; EMR or AWS Batch for larger ones) and removal of redundant data.

GENSUS03: How do you maintain model efficiency and resource optimization when working with large language models?

GENSUS03-BP01 Leverage smaller models and optimized inference techniques to reduce carbon footprint Medium

Quantization ("reducing the precision of the numbers used to represent the model's weights and activations"), pruning ("removing redundant or unnecessary parameters"), smaller task-specific models, Bedrock Model Distillation, and SageMaker inference optimization (speculative decoding, compilation), each evaluated against the original for accuracy and edge cases.

Scenarios

The lens includes eight reference scenarios showing the best practices applied to real architectures.

Multi-tenant generative AI platform

A governed platform for many lines of business: one gateway with unified APIs across model providers (AWS's implementation uses LiteLLM on ECS or EKS), tenant isolation with per-tenant keys, quotas and cost records for chargeback, central guardrails and evaluation, and a federated pattern where core services are central and lines of business own their orchestration. Its tenet: "build once and deploy anywhere". Models are only promoted when they pass evaluation in CI/CD.

Autonomous call center

Amazon Connect with Amazon Lex for structured requests, escalating to Amazon Q in Connect or Bedrock agents for complex ones, then to a live agent "who has the entire contact context available". Phased rollout: high-volume, simple flows first, then generative AI with guardrails and knowledge bases, then predictive routing and agents. Lesson: "Start with high-volume, low-complexity scenarios."

Generative business intelligence

Natural language exploration of structured and unstructured data, either managed (Amazon Q Business plus Amazon Q in QuickSight, now Amazon Quick and Quick Sight, respecting access controls and row- and column-level security) or custom (Bedrock knowledge bases over documents and Amazon Redshift, with an agent for multi-turn querying). In the custom approach, the application is responsible for authenticating users and enforcing data access.

Code transformation

AWS Transform uses agentic AI to modernize legacy code (for example .NET Framework) across many applications, through ingestion, analysis, transformation, validation and deployment phases, piloted on two or three representative applications with good test coverage.

Knowledge worker co-pilot

A co-pilot over CRM, documents, email and BI with user-selectable modes (quick, balanced, deep research, each mapped to a different model size). It highlights inferential access risk ("the combination of authorized data reveals unauthorized intelligence"), memory contamination, and the rule that users "cannot access information through AI synthesis that they cannot access directly in source systems". It also compares chunking costs: semantic chunking costs 30 to 50% more to process, and hierarchical chunking raises storage by 200 to 400%.

Incident response assistant

Event-driven assistance that correlates alerts, searches runbooks and suggests actions to cut time to resolution, with graceful degradation, prompt injection defenses and disaster recovery drills; it starts with smaller models and scales up on validated need.

Automated code review

A pull request triggers an automated review against company-wide and team standards before human approval, prioritizing "accuracy over real-time processing". "Base models are sufficient for most code review cases"; fine-tune only when prompting can't meet the need.

Automated Kanban workflow

Jira events trigger a serverless Step Functions workflow in which Bedrock reviews a task description and, if it passes, generates subtasks, with versioned prompts and human oversight.

Exam-level summary: the Generative AI Lens follows the six pillars; it starts customization with prompt engineering and RAG (GENOPS05); it controls agents with least privilege against excessive agency (GENSEC05) and stopping conditions (GENCOST05, the only high-risk cost practice); and its cost levers are right-sized models, routing, batch, Provisioned Throughput, shorter prompts and responses, and prompt caching.

The Generative AI Lens is about building generative AI well across the pillars; whether a specific use case is responsible (stakeholders, harms, release criteria) is the job of the Responsible AI Lens. A scenario asking for release criteria or harm assessment points to the Responsible AI Lens.

Key takeaways

  • The Generative AI Lens (first published April 2025; current version November 19, 2025) extends the Well-Architected Framework to foundation-model applications on Bedrock and SageMaker AI, with 29 questions and 51 best practices across six pillars.
  • Six design principles: controlled autonomy, comprehensive observability, resource efficiency, distributed resilience, standardized resource management, secure interaction boundaries.
  • Lifecycle: scoping, model selection, model customization, development and integration, deployment, continuous improvement. Agency is a spectrum: increase it only when task complexity requires.
  • Operations: golden datasets evaluated periodically with stratified sampling, user feedback, monitoring every layer, overload protection, prompt template management, agent and RAG tracing, IaC and GenAIOps, prompt engineering and RAG before customization.
  • Security: least-privilege and private endpoints, models treated as privileged users of data, guardrails with fallback actions, prompt catalogs, input sanitization against prompt injection, limits on agent permissions, data purification against poisoning.
  • Reliability, performance, cost and sustainability: queues and quotas, multi-AZ and cross-Region inference, timeouts and graceful recovery, prompt and model catalogs, load testing, right-sized models and vectors, batch and Provisioned Throughput, prompt caching, stopping conditions, serverless and smaller, optimized models.

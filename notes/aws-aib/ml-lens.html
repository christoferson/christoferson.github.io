The lens at a glance

What it is

A Well-Architected lens of best practices "you can apply ... when designing your ML workloads and after your workloads have entered production as part of continuous improvement". It "complements and builds upon the Well-Architected Framework to address the fundamental differences between traditional application workloads and machine learning workloads": applications follow deterministic instructions, while ML "enable[s] algorithms to learn from data through iterative and continuous cycles", so data must be monitored and models retrained as it changes.

History

First published April 16, 2020; major updates in October 2021 and July 2023 (which added the sustainability pillar); the current version, November 19, 2025, rewrote all best practices "with new structure and guidance".

Scope and audience

The "foundational lens for ML workloads": supervised and unsupervised learning, classification, regression, clustering, forecasting, recommendation, anomaly detection, computer vision, fraud and risk scoring. Content-generating foundation model applications are covered by the Generative AI Lens. The guidance is "cloud- and technology-agnostic", with AWS implementation steps, for CTOs, architects, developers, data scientists and ML engineers. Like the other AI lenses, it is imported into the Well-Architected Tool as a custom lens from AWS's public GitHub repository.

How it is organized

105 best practices: operational excellence 16, security 13, reliability 13, performance efficiency 18, cost optimization 29, sustainability 16. Unlike the other AI lenses, its pages state no question wording: each pillar groups its best practices by lifecycle phase, and the group ID encodes the phase. MLOPS01 is business goal identification, MLOPS02 problem framing, through MLOPS06 for monitoring. Reliability has nothing for the first phase, so its groups run MLREL01 (problem framing) to MLREL05 (monitoring). Each best practice has a desired outcome, common anti-patterns, benefits, a risk level and implementation steps.

The lens names Amazon SageMaker Model Monitor and SageMaker Clarify throughout, and both are now closed to new customers. AWS's replacements are its open-source SageMaker AI monitoring solutions (built on Evidently AI and MLflow, with QuickSight and CloudWatch), standard bias metrics and the open-source SHAP library. The practices themselves (baselines, the four monitoring types, bias and explainability checks) don't depend on those tools.

The running example

Examples follow a mobile network operator's churn model: it scores every contract customer each night for the likelihood of leaving when their contract ends, so the retention team can make offers to those most at risk.

Design principles

"Well-Architected ML design principles are a set of considerations used as the basis for a well-architected ML workload."

Assign ownership

"Apply the right skills and the right number of resources along with accountability and empowerment to increase productivity."

Provide protection

"Apply security controls to systems and services hosting model data, algorithms, computation, and endpoints."

Enable resiliency

"Verify fault tolerance and the recoverability of ML models through version control, traceability, and explainability."

Enable reusability

"Use independent modular components that can be shared and reused", such as features and services.

Enable reproducibility

"Use version control across components, such as infrastructure, data, models, and code. Track changes back to a point-in-time release."

Optimize resources

"Perform trade-off analysis across available resources and configurations to achieve optimal outcome."

Reduce cost

Find savings "through automation or optimization, analyzing processes, resources, and operations".

Enable automation

"Pipelining, scripting, and continuous integration (CI), continuous delivery (CD), and continuous training (CT)". Continuous training is the ML-specific addition to DevOps.

Enable continuous improvement

"Evolve and improve the workload through continuous monitoring, analysis, and learning."

Minimize environmental impact

"Establish sustainability goals and understand the impact of ML models", using managed services and efficient hardware.

The ML lifecycle

"The ML lifecycle is the cyclic iterative process with instructions and best practices to use across defined phases while developing an ML workload." The phases "are not necessarily sequential" and have feedback loops between them.

1. Business goal identification

"The most important phase of the ML lifecycle." Start by "determining if ML is the appropriate approach", involve stakeholders from the beginning, and check that enough relevant, high-quality data exists. AWS's steps:

  • Business considerations: understand requirements, align stakeholders, form a business question, identify must-have features, consider new processes, and decide how business metrics will show the value.
  • Frame the ML problem: define the ML task, review published work in similar domains, and run small, focused proofs of concept where confidence is low.
  • Determine the optimization objective: business performance metrics, such as new business acquired or fraud detection rate.
  • Review data requirements and feasibility.
  • Cost and performance: the cost of data acquisition, training, inference and wrong predictions, and whether external data would help.
  • Production: how ML-generated errors will be handled, and the pathway to production.

2. ML problem framing

Defining "what is observed and what should be predicted (known as a label or target variable)". AWS's example: a manufacturer maximizing profit could forecast demand for existing products, forecast the materials it needs, or predict sales of new products, which are three different ML problems. The steps:

  1. Define successCriteria for a successful outcome, and an observable, quantifiable metric such as accuracy.
  2. Link technical and business metricsDefine how the technical metric relates to the business outcome, and get stakeholders to agree.
  3. Formulate the ML questionInputs, desired outputs and the metric to optimize.
  4. Check ML is right"Some business problems don't need ML as simple business rules can do a much better job"; others lack the data.
  5. Plan dataHow data will be sourced and annotated.
  6. Start simple, then iterateA model that is easy to interpret and debug, mapped to the business outcome, improved with more data, tuning or complexity as needed.

ExampleLabel: did the customer leave within 30 days of contract end? Inputs: usage, bills, complaints, device age. Metric: recall among the top 5% of scores, linked to retained revenue.

3. Data processing

Collection, then preparation (preprocessing and feature engineering). One rule matters throughout: "the same sequence of data processing steps that is applied to the training data needs to also be applied to the inference requests."

Data collection

Identify the data needed, label it if labels are missing ("labeled data is a group of samples that have been tagged with one or more labels"), and ingest and aggregate it from sources such as events, sensors and transactions, in real time or batch, into lakes, warehouses or a lake house.

Data preprocessing

"Puts data into the right shape and quality for training": clean (impute missing values, remove outliers and duplicates); partition into train, validation and test sets (remove duplicates first to avoid data leakage, where test information leaks into training); scale (normalized values fall in [0,1], standardized ones have mean 0 and standard deviation 1); unbias and balance ("biases are imbalances in the accuracy of predictions across different groups"); and augment (synthesize data to reduce overfitting).

Feature engineering

A feature is any attribute of the data (for churn: location, age, income, recent purchases). Feature engineering selects and transforms them: creation (one-hot encoding, binning, calculated features), transformation and imputation, extraction (dimensionality reduction such as PCA) and selection (the subset that minimizes error, using importance scores and correlation). Deep learning largely automates it.

4. Model development

Training and tuning

Select an algorithm by running experiments (weighing success metrics, explainability and compute), train it, and tune its hyperparameters, the "settings that can be tuned to control the behavior of the ML algorithm" (learning rate, epochs, layers). Large jobs use distributed training: data parallelism splits the training set across nodes; model parallelism splits the model itself. A debugger catches problems such as overfitting and vanishing gradients; validation metrics include the confusion matrix for classification and RMSE for regression.

Evaluation

Compare candidates, decide whether the model must be "more sensitive than specific, or more specific than sensitive", check error rates per class, and evaluate offline (historical data) and online (live data). The holdout set "is never used for model training or validation", only to evaluate the final model, so its labels must be verified carefully.

5. Deployment

Deploy through a governed process with quality gates. The production endpoint takes model artifacts from the model registry, features from the feature store and the inference container from a container repository. Release strategies reduce risk:

Blue/green

Two identical environments; test on green, switch traffic to it, keep blue for rollback.

Canary

Release to a small group of users first, then roll out gradually.

A/B testing

A defined share of traffic goes to the new model; like canary but "larger user groups and a longer time scale, typically days or even weeks".

Shadow

Input runs through both versions; the old one serves production, the new one is only analyzed.

6. Monitoring

"The model monitoring system must capture data, compare that data to the training set, define rules to detect issues, and send alerts", on a schedule, on an event or on demand. It watches four things: data quality, model quality, bias drift and feature attribution drift.

Data drift and concept drift

"Data drift is significant changes to the data distribution compared to the data used for training." "Concept drift is when the properties of the target variables change." Retraining is triggered two ways: a scheduler at business-defined intervals, or the alarm manager launching the model update pipeline when monitoring detects violations.

Lifecycle architecture

The lens's reference architecture, "cloud and technology agnostic", adds shared components around the phases:

Architecture components
Online and offline feature store
Consistent, reusable features: the online store for "low-latency retrieval ... ideal for real-time inference", the offline store for history, training and batch scoring.
Model registry
"A repository for storing ML model artifacts including trained model and related metadata", acting as version control and lineage for models.
Performance feedback loop
Feeds model evaluation results back into data preparation during development.
Model drift feedback loop
Feeds production evaluation back into data preparation.
Alarm manager
Receives monitoring alerts and notifies target applications, such as the retraining pipeline.
Scheduler
"Initiates a model re-training at business-defined intervals."
Lineage tracker
Re-creates "the ML environment at a specific point in time", from references to infrastructure as code, data (metadata, values, features), model (algorithm, features, parameters, hyperparameters), code, feature lists and container images.
Pipelines
Data preparation, feature, CI/CD/CT (build, train and release), performance evaluation, inference (prepare data, predict, post-process) and model update pipelines.

Operational excellence

The ability to run and monitor systems to deliver business value and to continually improve supporting processes and procedures.

MLOPS01: Business goal identification

MLOPS01-BP01 Develop the right skills with accountability and empowerment High

Build a skilled, ethically responsible ML workforce: role-based learning paths (AWS Skill Builder, Machine Learning University), cross-functional teams that include ethics, legal and domain experts, fairness checkpoints before production, and model cards documenting intended uses and limitations. Anti-pattern: "assuming ML models are inherently unbiased and fair."

MLOPS01-BP02 Discuss and agree on the level of model explainability High

Agree explainability requirements early, because they constrain model choice: deep neural networks behave as "an opaque system" while decision trees and linear models are transparent, and regulated industries may demand high explainability. Use SHAP-based feature attribution reports and choose SHAP baselines deliberately; for generative AI, add source citations and confidence scores.

MLOPS01-BP03 Monitor model adherence to business requirements High

Tie monitoring to business metrics (revenue impact, satisfaction) as well as technical ones, set drift thresholds from a baseline, and define drift response protocols (retrain, review or fall back). Data drift is "changes in the distribution of input data"; concept drift is "changes in the relationship between inputs and outputs". Review periodically even without alarms, to catch gradual drift.

MLOPS02: ML problem framing

MLOPS02-BP01 Establish ML roles and responsibilities High

Define roles with accountability and least-privilege access: domain expert, data engineer, data scientist, ML engineer, MLOps engineer, IT auditor, model risk manager, cloud security engineer and, for generative AI, prompt engineer. SageMaker AI Role Manager creates persona-based permissions; avoid "a single person doing all ML tasks".

MLOPS02-BP02 Prepare an ML profile template Medium

Keep a current and a target profile for each workload, describing model characteristics (size, models per endpoint, retraining frequency, deployment location), architecture, traffic (steady or spiky, latency class, cold-start tolerance), network and cost options, so maturity and gaps are visible.

MLOPS02-BP03 Establish model improvement strategies Medium

Improve systematically: start from a simple baseline, track every experiment, then try feature engineering, more data, more complex algorithms and ensembles, and automated tuning. Anti-patterns include "arbitrary hyperparameter changes" and advanced techniques before fundamentals.

MLOPS02-BP04 Establish a lineage tracker system High

Track every release across "documentation, environment, model, data, code, and infrastructure", so an unexpected production result can be traced to "the exact version of the model, the data it was trained on, the code used to train it, and the infrastructure configuration". Tools: SageMaker AI ML Lineage Tracking, Pipelines, Feature Store, Model Registry and Git.

MLOPS02-BP05 Establish feedback loops across ML lifecycle phases High

Treat deployment as a loop, not the last step: monitoring detects drift, EventBridge rules trigger retraining pipelines, low-confidence predictions go to human reviewers (Amazon Augmented AI) whose corrections become ground truth, and learnings are documented and reviewed.

MLOPS02-BP06 Review fairness and explainability High

At problem framing, assess whether the algorithm is ethical for the use case and whether the data is representative; measure pre-training bias (class and label imbalance) and fairness metrics such as "disparate impact, difference in positive proportions, or equal opportunity difference"; use SHAP to find protected attributes or proxies driving outcomes; monitor bias drift in production.

MLOPS03: Data processing

MLOPS03-BP01 Profile data to improve quality High

Profile "distribution, descriptive statistics, data types, and patterns" before training, automate it, and baseline the training data for later drift comparison; training and inference schemas must "exactly match (the number and order of the features)". AWS's example: a churn dataset with 15% missing ages and 5% impossible transaction amounts.

MLOPS03-BP02 Create tracking and version control mechanisms High

Track experiments as runs (inputs, parameters, results), version processing code, register each model version in a model registry, and version datasets (for example with DVC), so the best model can be found and rolled back.

MLOPS04: Model development

MLOPS04-BP01 Automate operations through MLOps and CI/CD High

Use "infrastructure as code (IaC) and configuration as code (CaC)" and pipelines (SageMaker AI Pipelines, Step Functions or Apache Airflow) with automated data, model and API tests, staging before production, approval gates, and canary or blue/green rollback.

MLOPS04-BP02 Establish reliable packaging patterns to access approved public libraries Medium

Provide approved libraries through internal repositories and versioned base images per framework (in Amazon ECR), rebuilt automatically for patches, instead of "uncontrolled direct downloads from public repositories".

MLOPS05: Deployment

MLOPS05-BP01 Establish deployment environment metrics High

Monitor endpoints with metrics such as CPU and memory utilization, model latency and invocations per instance, define KPIs (latency for APIs, throughput for batch, token cost for generative AI), alert, and use anomaly detection to "automatically halt or roll back deployments".

MLOPS06: Monitoring

MLOPS06-BP01 Synchronize architecture and configuration, and check for skew across environments High

Keep development, staging and production identical through infrastructure as code, and compare model quality across them, so models deliver "the same range of accuracy regardless of which environment they run in". Skew can come from hardware, software versions or configuration.

MLOPS06-BP02 Enable model observability and tracking High

Monitor data quality, model quality, bias drift and feature attribution drift against baselines, keep lineage and model cards, and use shadow testing, which routes "copies of production inference requests" to a new variant for comparison before promotion.

Security

The ability to protect data, systems, and assets to take advantage of cloud technologies to improve your security posture.

MLSEC01: Business goal identification

MLSEC01-BP01 Validate ML data permissions, privacy, software, and license terms High

Confirm you may use data for ML ("assuming data collected for one purpose can automatically be used for ML training without additional consent" is an anti-pattern), support consent withdrawal and removal from training sets, keep a software license inventory, and use approved package repositories and pre-validated containers.

MLSEC02: ML problem framing

MLSEC02-BP01 Design data encryption and obfuscation High

"Use field level encryption or obfuscation to protect personally identifiable data": find PII, encrypt fields with AWS KMS (with key rotation and separate keys per environment), tokenize or mask, and use differential privacy to reduce memorization.

MLSEC03: Data processing

MLSEC03-BP01 Provide least privilege access High

Protect "data, algorithms, code, hyperparameters, trained model artifacts, and infrastructure" with persona-based roles, a multi-account structure with service control policies, tagging by sensitivity and network isolation per project.

MLSEC03-BP02 Secure data and modeling environment High

Encrypt data at rest and in transit, run notebooks and training in a private VPC, remove root access, keep secrets out of code ("hard-coded credentials in ML code and notebooks" is an anti-pattern), scan containers, and use a private mirror of approved packages.

MLSEC03-BP03 Protect sensitive data privacy High

Discover and classify sensitive data (Amazon Macie), minimize it with "data removal, masking, tokenization, and principal component analysis (PCA)", encrypt it, and consider differential privacy, AWS Clean Rooms and federated learning.

MLSEC03-BP04 Enforce data lineage High

Trace every data element to its source and transformations, with integrity checks ("checksums, statistical analysis, or machine learning-based anomaly detection") to detect manipulation, and version code and infrastructure alongside.

MLSEC03-BP05 Keep only relevant data High

Use only the data the use case needs, redact PII (Amazon Comprehend for text, Rekognition for images, Transcribe for audio), keep production data out of development, and expire data automatically instead of "keeping collected data indefinitely in case it might be useful later".

MLSEC04: Model development

MLSEC04-BP01 Secure governed ML environment High

Separate accounts for development, training and production, preventive guardrails (service control policies), detective controls (AWS Config, GuardDuty), private networking, central logging in a security account, and self-service templates, balancing security with flexibility. For foundation models, curate approved models in a private model hub.

MLSEC04-BP02 Secure inter-node cluster communications High

Encrypt the "model coefficients, gradients, and parameter updates" exchanged between nodes in distributed training (SageMaker AI inter-container traffic encryption, TLS), rather than "assuming internal network communications are inherently secure".

MLSEC04-BP03 Protect against data poisoning threats High

Poisoning happens through "data injection (adding corrupt training data) or data manipulation (changing existing data like labels)", or through compromised third-party libraries. Use trusted sources, audit data changes, watch for distribution shifts, compare new models with previous ones on consistent test sets, and keep versions to roll back.

MLSEC05: Deployment

MLSEC05-BP01 Protect against adversarial and malicious activities High

Test robustness with perturbed inputs, build robustness with ensembles and adversarial training, detect probing such as "model brute-forcing" (systematically varying inputs to learn decision boundaries), validate data before retraining, and secure endpoints with isolation, rate limiting and request validation.

MLSEC06: Monitoring

MLSEC06-BP01 Restrict access to intended legitimate consumers High

"Use least privilege permissions to invoke the deployed model endpoint. For consumers who are external to the workload environment, provide access using a secure API": signed requests with IAM, Amazon Cognito for external users, API Gateway for validation and rate limiting, and private networking.

MLSEC06-BP02 Monitor human interactions with data for anomalous activity High

Log who accessed which data, how and when (CloudTrail data events), classify data (Macie), and flag anomalies against baselines, such as access from unusual locations, outside working hours, excessive volumes, or data the role doesn't need.

Reliability

The ability of a workload to perform its intended function correctly and consistently when it's expected to.

The reliability pillar has no business goal identification practices ("There are no reliability best practices specific to the business goal identification phase"), so its groups start at MLREL01 for problem framing.

MLREL01: ML problem framing

MLREL01-BP01 Use APIs to abstract change from model consuming applications High

Put a versioned API (for example /v1/predict, with Amazon API Gateway in front of the model endpoint) between models and the applications that use them, so models can be updated, replaced or A/B tested "with minimal or no disruption". Anti-patterns: embedding model code in applications, hardcoding model versions.

ExampleThe churn model sits behind a /v2/churn-score API; the CRM never knows which model version answers.

MLREL01-BP02 Adopt a machine learning microservice strategy Medium

Split ML systems into services along business domains ("customer churn prediction", "product recommendation" rather than a generic prediction service) and stages (ingestion, features, training, serving), for fault isolation and independent scaling.

MLREL02: Data processing

MLREL02-BP01 Use a data catalog Medium

A central inventory of ML data assets and transformations (AWS Glue Data Catalog, SageMaker AI Catalog with DataZone) replaces spreadsheets and "tribal knowledge"; AWS notes data preparation "typically consumes 60-80% of a data scientist's time".

MLREL02-BP02 Use a data pipeline High

Automate data movement and transformation with error handling, retries and versioned artifacts (AWS Glue, Amazon EMR, SageMaker AI Processing and Pipelines) instead of manual movement and one-off scripts.

MLREL02-BP03 Automate managing data changes High

Version training data like code, so you can "reproduce model versions exactly as they were originally created", record which datasets trained each model, and test automatically when data versions change.

MLREL03: Model development

MLREL03-BP01 Enable CI/CD/CT automation with traceability High

Pipelines covering continuous integration, delivery and training, tracking "data, model artifacts, and training environments" as well as code; retraining triggered "by data drift, scheduled intervals, or on-demand"; approval gates; and production variants for blue/green and canary rollback.

MLREL03-BP02 Verify feature consistency across training and inference High

Avoid training-serving skew, when "the features used to train a model differ from those used during inference", with a shared feature store: an online store for low-latency inference, an offline store for training, with the same definitions.

MLREL03-BP03 Validate models with relevant data High

Validate on representative real-world data, including edge cases (AWS: a fraud model needs typical and unusual fraud patterns), check distribution mismatch between training, test and live data, use k-fold cross-validation, and revalidate on a schedule even without drift alerts.

MLREL03-BP04 Establish data bias detection and mitigation High

Detect bias before training and monitor it in production, using several metrics rather than "a single bias metric for each use case"; mitigate in preprocessing (resampling, weighting), in-processing (fairness constraints) or post-processing (adjusting outputs).

MLREL04: Deployment

MLREL04-BP01 Automate endpoint changes through a pipeline High

"Versioned pipeline inputs and artifacts allow you to track the changes and automatically rollback after a failed change." Integrate with change management; no manual updates to production endpoints.

MLREL04-BP02 Use an appropriate deployment and testing strategy High

Choose by risk: blue/green ("instantaneous rollback by maintaining two identical environments"), canary (a small share of traffic, with baking periods), linear traffic shifting, A/B testing, or shadow testing (runs alongside production "but not using their predictions"); prefer canary or shadow for high-stakes uses, with automatic rollback on metric thresholds.

ExampleA new churn model runs in shadow for two weeks, then takes 10% of traffic before full rollout.

MLREL05: Monitoring

MLREL05-BP01 Allow automatic scaling of the model endpoint High

Scale endpoints horizontally on CPU, memory, request or latency metrics, with thresholds that trigger "not too early (which wastes resources) or too late (which impacts performance)", and serverless inference for intermittent traffic.

MLREL05-BP02 Create a recoverable endpoint with a managed version control strategy High

Version model artifacts, container images and endpoint configuration, so an endpoint can be reproduced "exactly as [it was] at specific points in time" and reverted quickly; back up and test recovery.

Performance efficiency

The efficient use of computing resources to meet requirements and the maintenance of that efficiency as demand changes and technologies evolve.

MLPERF01: Business goal identification

MLPERF01-BP01 Determine key performance indicators High

"Consider that machine learning inferences are probabilistic and will not provide exact results. Identify a minimum acceptable accuracy and maximum acceptable error in the KPIs." Quantify the business value, the cost of errors and the tolerance for them, and record a baseline before ML is introduced.

ExampleKPI: reduce monthly churn from 2.1% to 1.8% among customers offered retention deals, with no more than 30% of offers going to customers who would have stayed anyway.

MLPERF02: ML problem framing

MLPERF02-BP01 Define relevant evaluation metrics High

Choose metrics by the cost of each error: in predictive maintenance a false positive means "unnecessarily replacing functioning equipment" and a false negative "missing actual failures". Typical metrics: precision, recall, F1, AUC for classification; RMSE, MAPE for regression; NDCG for recommendations.

MLPERF02-BP02 Use purpose-built AI and ML services and resources Medium

Use managed AI services, built-in algorithms, AutoML, pre-trained models and AWS Marketplace before building from scratch, and model optimization ("quantization, compilation, and speculative decoding") for deployment.

MLPERF03: Data processing

MLPERF03-BP01 Use a modern data architecture Medium

A data lake ("a single place to run analytics across mixed data structures") with purpose-built stores for warehousing, streaming, queries and time series, unified governance (AWS Lake Formation), a catalog, and a feature store, instead of "one-size-fits-all storage".

MLPERF04: Model development

MLPERF04-BP01 Optimize training and inference instance types Medium

Match instances to the workload: GPUs for deep learning on images, video or language, while "CPUs are sufficient for many inference scenarios"; benchmark, distribute large jobs, and consider Trainium and Inferentia for price-performance.

MLPERF04-BP02 Explore alternatives for performance improvement Medium

Start from a baseline ("simple architecture, obvious features, and a straightforward algorithm"), then systematically try feature engineering, more data, other algorithms, ensembles (bagging, boosting, stacking) and automated tuning.

MLPERF04-BP03 Establish a model performance evaluation pipeline High

Evaluate automatically on every model or data update, tie metrics to KPIs, and let approval workflows "automatically promote models meeting performance thresholds".

MLPERF04-BP04 Establish feature statistics High

Baseline statistics for the most influential features (from feature importance and sensitivity analysis near decision boundaries), alert when they drift outside expected ranges, and base retraining "on quantitative measures rather than intuition".

MLPERF04-BP05 Perform a performance trade-off analysis High

Select models across accuracy and complexity, bias and variance, precision and recall, fairness, explainability and operational constraints (AWS: credit scoring prioritizes fairness and explainability; real-time recommendation prioritizes speed). "Simpler models are often more explainable."

MLPERF04-BP06 Detect performance issues when using transfer learning High

Inherited problems "often don't manifest as obvious failures but rather as biased or suboptimal predictions": validate pre-trained models, inspect training (vanishing gradients, exploding tensors), test with slight input variations, and align preprocessing with what the pre-trained model expects.

MLPERF05: Deployment

MLPERF05-BP01 Evaluate cloud versus edge options for machine learning deployment Medium

Edge inference "avoids costly roundtrips to cloud API endpoints" and works with limited connectivity (predictive maintenance in factories, vehicles): train in the cloud, compile for the device (SageMaker AI Neo: "trained once and run anywhere"), and deploy with AWS IoT Greengrass.

MLPERF05-BP02 Choose an optimal deployment option in the cloud Medium

Real-time inference for steady low-latency traffic; serverless inference for "inconsistent request patterns or idle periods"; asynchronous inference for "large input files (up to 1GB)" or "extended processing time (up to 15 minutes)"; batch transform for non-urgent bulk scoring.

ExampleChurn scores are needed once a night for the whole customer base, so the model runs as a batch transform job, not a 24/7 endpoint.

MLPERF06: Monitoring

MLPERF06-BP01 Include human-in-the-loop monitoring High

Have subject matter experts label a sample of predictions (low-confidence, random or high-risk) and compare with the model, using the agreement rate to detect drift and the labels for retraining (Amazon Augmented AI).

MLPERF06-BP02 Evaluate model explainability High

Explain predictions globally and locally (SHAP feature attribution), meet requirements such as explanations for credit denials, monitor feature attribution drift, and prefer interpretable models where explanations are mandatory.

MLPERF06-BP03 Evaluate data drift High

Data drift is "when the statistical properties of model inputs change over time"; detect it on a schedule with measures such as "KL divergence, Jensen-Shannon divergence, and population stability index", and retrain on drift rather than on fixed schedules alone.

MLPERF06-BP04 Monitor, detect, and handle model performance degradation High

Degradation comes from "data quality, model quality, model bias, and model explainability"; monitor all four against training baselines, alert by severity, and remediate through retraining pipelines and A/B tests.

MLPERF06-BP05 Establish an automated re-training framework High

Trigger retraining on "scheduled intervals", performance below threshold, drift above limits or "availability of new training data"; validate on holdout data, register versions and deploy safely with automatic rollback.

MLPERF06-BP06 Review for updated data and features for retraining High

Review data and features proactively, "monthly" in volatile environments and "quarterly or biannual" in stable ones, explore new features, and approve retraining through a cross-functional team.

Cost optimization

The continual process of refinement and improvement of a system over its entire lifecycle, building "cost-aware systems that achieve business outcomes and minimize costs".

MLCOST01: Business goal identification

MLCOST01-BP01 Define overall return on investment (ROI) and opportunity cost High

Classify projects as research (future value, "long-term returns") or development (established methods, "near-term returns"); count direct costs (infrastructure, services) and indirect ones (staff time, maintenance, data preparation, production errors); track by project with tags and Cost Explorer; and re-check the cost-benefit as conditions change.

MLCOST01-BP02 Use managed services to reduce total cost of ownership (TCO) High

AWS states that the three-year TCO of SageMaker AI "is much lower than other self-managed cloud-based ML options, such as Amazon EC2 and Amazon EKS"; use pre-trained AI services, Savings Plans for steady components and serverless inference for intermittent ones.

MLCOST02: ML problem framing

MLCOST02-BP01 Identify if machine learning is the right solution High

Compare ML with simpler alternatives on full cost (data preparation, storage, training, labeling, retraining, hosting, maintenance); fraud detection suits ML because fraud "constantly evolv[es]", simple inventory rules may not. Use an existing rules solution as the baseline; data scientists "might be your most constrained resource".

MLCOST02-BP02 Perform a tradeoff analysis between custom and pre-trained models Medium

Pre-trained models suit common tasks but "can be difficult to modify"; custom models suit specialized domains. Consider fine-tuning or transfer learning in between, and for generative AI, RAG "can be more cost-effective than fine-tuning".

MLCOST03: Data processing

MLCOST03-BP01 Use managed data labeling Medium

SageMaker Ground Truth combines ML-assisted (active learning) labeling with a chosen workforce and consensus checks; Ground Truth Plus is a turnkey expert service that AWS says "reduces costs by up to 40 percent".

MLCOST03-BP02 Use no-code or low-code and code generation tools for interactive analysis Medium

Visual data preparation (SageMaker Canvas, Data Wrangler with "over 300 built-in transformations") and AI code assistants cut the "60-80% of data scientists' time" spent preparing data, while generating reproducible code.

MLCOST03-BP03 Use managed data processing capabilities Medium

Managed processing jobs provision, scale and "automatically clean up" resources when finished, with built-in framework containers and Spot capacity for non-urgent work.

MLCOST03-BP04 Enable feature reusability Medium

A feature store with online storage ("low-latency retrieval ... ideal for real-time inference"), offline storage (history "suited for training and batch scoring") and encryption, so teams stop recreating the same features.

MLCOST04: Model development

MLCOST04-BP01 Select optimal computing instance size Medium

"Simple models might not train faster on larger instances ... might even train slower due to high GPU communication overhead." Profile whether jobs are compute-, memory- or I/O-bound, start small, and use Spot (up to 90% savings) with checkpoints.

MLCOST04-BP02 Use managed build environments Medium

Managed notebooks and IDEs (SageMaker Studio, notebook instances, Canvas) instead of local setups, with auto-shutdown for idle notebooks.

MLCOST04-BP03 Select local training for small scale experiments Medium

For small datasets ("typically under a few gigabytes"), train locally with SageMaker local mode before paying for clusters, then right-size cloud training from the profile.

MLCOST04-BP04 Select an optimal ML framework Medium

Benchmark framework and instance combinations (training time, latency, memory, accuracy) rather than choosing by popularity, tracking runs in experiments.

MLCOST04-BP05 Use automated machine learning Medium

AutoML (SageMaker Autopilot, through Canvas) automates preparation, algorithm choice, tuning and cross-validation and ranks candidates transparently; cap cost with maximum runtimes.

MLCOST04-BP06 Use managed training capabilities High

Move training off notebooks to managed clusters; managed Spot training can "lower training costs by up to 90%", with SageMaker AI handling interruptions, and training compiler optimizations can accelerate training "by up to 50%".

MLCOST04-BP07 Use distributed training Medium

Data parallelism splits the dataset across GPUs, each holding "a complete copy of the model"; model parallelism partitions a model "too large to fit on a single GPU". Use built-in libraries and let the cluster terminate after training.

MLCOST04-BP08 Stop resources when not in use Medium

"Commit the work, stop them, and restart when needed": idle-shutdown scripts and extensions, billing alarms and schedules; AWS cites savings "up to 75%" from running only in business hours.

MLCOST04-BP09 Start training with small datasets Medium

Iterate on a representative sample ("aim for 10-20% of your data") on small instances, then run full training on a properly sized cluster.

MLCOST04-BP10 Use warm start and checkpointing hyperparameter tuning Medium

Start new tuning jobs from up to five parent jobs (identical data and algorithm, or transfer learning for related problems), narrow search ranges, and checkpoint so jobs resume "rather than starting from scratch".

MLCOST04-BP11 Use hyperparameter optimization technologies Medium

Hyperparameters are "configuration variables that govern the training process itself"; automatic model tuning uses Bayesian optimization, log scales for wide ranges, parallel jobs and early stopping of poor candidates.

MLCOST04-BP12 Set up a budget and use resource tagging to track costs Medium

Mandatory tags (for example Project, BusinessUnit, Environment, Owner) on notebooks, training jobs, models, endpoints and buckets, activated as cost allocation tags (which can take up to 24 hours), with AWS Budgets on actual and forecast spend and Cost Anomaly Detection.

MLCOST04-BP13 Enable data and compute proximity High

Keep data and compute in the same Region to avoid transfer fees and latency ("the laws of physics still impose latency limitations"); replicate deliberately only for availability or recovery.

MLCOST04-BP14 Select optimal algorithms High

"Start with simple algorithms first" (linear or logistic regression, decision trees, k-means) as a baseline, then weigh data needs, compute, explainability, deployment complexity and maintenance before going deeper.

ExampleLogistic regression on 40 features gets the churn model within 1 point of AUC of a deep network, at a fraction of the cost and with explainable reasons.

MLCOST05: Deployment

MLCOST05-BP01 Use an appropriate deployment option Medium

Match the option to traffic: real-time, serverless, asynchronous or batch; multi-model endpoints that load models on demand into a shared container; multi-container endpoints and inference pipelines.

MLCOST05-BP02 Explore cost effective hardware options High

"Up to 90% of the infrastructure spend for developing and running ML applications is often on inference." Optimize models (SageMaker AI Neo) and use purpose-built chips: AWS Inferentia instances offer much lower cost per inference than comparable GPU instances.

MLCOST05-BP03 Right-size the model hosting instance fleet High

Use Inference Recommender (instance type and count "within minutes", plus managed load tests), auto scaling with cooldown periods to "avoid rapid scaling oscillations", at least two Availability Zones, and smaller models through quantization, distillation or pruning.

MLCOST06: Monitoring

MLCOST06-BP01 Monitor usage and cost by ML activity Medium

Tag costs by ML activity "such as re-training and hosting" (and project, environment, owner), automate and enforce tagging (tag policies, AWS Config rules), and report by activity with Budgets and Cost Explorer.

MLCOST06-BP02 Monitor return on investment for ML models High

Compare business KPIs with a baseline (AWS: new customers acquired and their spend; savings from predictive maintenance), isolate the model's effect with A/B tests, and if ROI is negative, change hosting, rebalance "model accuracy and model complexity", or add "an additional simpler model to triage or filter the cases"; scale or retire models on the evidence.

ExampleRetention offers cost $180,000 a month; customers kept are worth $410,000 against the control group, so the model stays and expands to broadband.

MLCOST06-BP03 Monitor endpoint usage and right-size the instance fleet High

Watch utilization (AWS's example thresholds: consistently above 80% suggests under-provisioning, below 20% over-provisioning), auto scale, and keep storage such as FSx for Lustre in the same Availability Zone to avoid transfer cost.

MLCOST06-BP04 Enable debugging and logging Medium

Capture training state and resource use (SageMaker Debugger detects "vanishing gradients or tensor explosions"), keep logs with retention policies to control storage cost, and use them to right-size.

Sustainability

Environmental impacts, "especially energy consumption and efficiency, since they are important levers for architects to guide direct action on how to reduce resource usage".

MLSUS01: Business goal identification

MLSUS01-BP01 Define the overall environmental impact or benefit High

Set sustainability metrics (for example "carbon emissions per training run or energy efficiency per inference") across the whole life cycle; training is usually more intensive per run, but a model serving millions of predictions a day can have a larger cumulative impact. Measure with the AWS Customer Carbon Footprint Tool and weigh net benefit (ML that reduces energy elsewhere).

MLSUS02: ML problem framing

MLSUS02-BP01 Consider AI services and pre-trained models Medium

Managed AI services and pre-trained models avoid repeating resource-intensive training; fine-tune efficiently (PEFT, LoRA) and prefer RAG to fine-tuning large models.

MLSUS02-BP02 Select sustainable Regions Medium

After meeting non-negotiables (sovereignty, compliance, latency, service availability), prefer Regions near renewable energy projects and with lower grid carbon intensity.

MLSUS03: Data processing

MLSUS03-BP01 Minimize idle resources Medium

Serverless, event-driven pipelines (AWS Glue, Lambda, EMR Serverless, triggered on data arrival) that consume resources only while working, rather than always-on instances.

MLSUS03-BP02 Implement data lifecycle policies aligned with your sustainability goals Medium

Classify data by importance and access, move it through cheaper, more efficient storage classes over time, and delete it when no longer needed.

MLSUS03-BP03 Adopt sustainable storage options Medium

Store less: avoid copies, use columnar formats (Parquet "can reduce your storage by up to 87%" compared with CSV) and compression, keep re-creatable data on lower-redundancy storage, archive audit artifacts.

MLSUS04: Model development

MLSUS04-BP01 Define sustainable performance criteria Medium

"The relationship between model accuracy and complexity is logarithmic at best": a model "95% accurate may provide the same business value as one that is 96% accurate but requires twice the computational resources". Use early stopping and plot accuracy against resources.

MLSUS04-BP02 Select energy-efficient algorithms Medium

Start simple, use distilled models (AWS cites DistilBERT: "40% fewer parameters, runs 60% faster, and preserves 97% of its performance"), prune and quantize.

MLSUS04-BP03 Archive or delete unnecessary training artifacts Medium

Clean up experiments, models, images and logs ("by default, Amazon CloudWatch retains logs indefinitely"), with retention settings, lifecycle rules and expiry tags.

MLSUS04-BP04 Use efficient model tuning methods Medium

Prefer Bayesian search (AWS: "typically ... 10 times fewer jobs than random search") or Hyperband to grid search, narrow ranges, and run fewer concurrent jobs so each learns from the last.

MLSUS05: Deployment

MLSUS05-BP01 Align SLAs with sustainability goals Medium

"Define SLAs to meet your business requirements, not exceed them": use asynchronous or serverless endpoints that scale to zero, batch transform whose "clusters are decommissioned when batch transform jobs finish", and only the redundancy continuity requires.

MLSUS05-BP02 Use efficient silicon Medium

Energy-efficient instances: Graviton for CPU inference, Inferentia for inference and Trainium for training, with the AWS Neuron SDK; compare performance per watt.

MLSUS05-BP03 Optimize models for inference Medium

Compile models (SageMaker AI Neo, Treelite for tree ensembles) and quantize (FP32 to FP16 or INT8) so the same predictions use fewer resources.

MLSUS05-BP04 Deploy multiple models behind a single endpoint Medium

"Sharing endpoint resources is more sustainable and less expensive": multi-model endpoints for many similar models, multi-container endpoints for different frameworks, inference pipelines for chained steps.

MLSUS06: Monitoring

MLSUS06-BP01 Measure material efficiency Medium

Track "provisioned resources per unit of work", for example "vCPU minutes per recommendation delivered", as the baseline for improvement.

MLSUS06-BP02 Retrain only when necessary Medium

"Only retrain when your model's predictive performance has fallen below defined KPIs", detected through drift and quality monitoring, not on an arbitrary schedule.

Exam-level summary: the Machine Learning Lens frames everything by the six-phase ML lifecycle, starting with business goal identification ("the most important phase") and the question of whether ML is the right solution (MLCOST02-BP01). Recurring themes: a simple baseline first, metrics tied to business KPIs and the cost of errors, lineage and versioning of data, code, models and infrastructure, CI/CD plus continuous training, safe deployment strategies, and monitoring of data quality, model quality, bias drift and feature attribution drift.

Retraining on a fixed schedule regardless of evidence, chasing the last point of accuracy at twice the compute, or starting with deep learning before a simple model are all anti-patterns in this lens. So is treating deployment as the final step instead of the start of monitoring.

Key takeaways

  • The Machine Learning Lens (first published 2020; current version November 19, 2025) is the foundational Well-Architected lens for ML workloads, with 105 best practices across six pillars, each grouped by lifecycle phase.
  • Ten design principles: assign ownership, provide protection, enable resiliency, reusability, reproducibility, automation and continuous improvement, optimize resources, reduce cost, minimize environmental impact.
  • Six phases: business goal identification, ML problem framing, data processing (collection, preprocessing, feature engineering), model development (training, tuning, evaluation), deployment and monitoring, with feedback loops between them.
  • Architecture: feature store (online and offline), model registry, lineage tracker, alarm manager, scheduler, and pipelines for data, features, CI/CD/CT, evaluation, inference and model updates.
  • Operations and reliability: roles and skills, explainability agreed early, lineage, feedback loops, CI/CD/CT, APIs in front of models, feature consistency to prevent training-serving skew, blue/green, canary, A/B and shadow deployments, auto scaling and recoverable endpoints.
  • Security: permission to use data, encryption and obfuscation of personal data, least privilege, keeping only relevant data, protection against poisoning and adversarial probing, restricted endpoint access and monitoring of human data access.
  • Performance, cost and sustainability: KPIs with acceptable error, metrics by error cost, evaluation pipelines, drift and degradation monitoring, ROI and TCO, managed and Spot training, right-sized and shared endpoints, efficient silicon, SLAs no stricter than needed, and retraining only when necessary.

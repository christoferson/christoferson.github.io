AI, ML, deep learning and generative AI

TermWhat it isOutputExample
Artificial intelligenceMachines performing tasks that normally need human intelligence; includes rulesDecisions, predictions, contentA rules engine checking eligibility
Machine learningAI that learns patterns from data for one taskA score, class or numberFraud score
Deep learningML with many-layered neural networksInterpretations of images, audio, textDamage estimate from photos
Generative AIDeep learning on foundation models that creates new contentText, images, code, audioDrafting a customer reply

"Predict, score, classify, forecast" points to ML. "Draft, summarize, generate, converse" points to generative AI. A fixed policy applied the same way every time may need no ML.

Rules, ML, generative AI and agents

SolutionChoose whenWatch out for
Rule-based automationLogic is fixed, fully known, structured, and must be identical and auditable every timeRule sets that keep growing and break each other; patterns that change faster than rules
ML modelMany interacting factors; good labeled history; a prediction is neededData quality, bias, drift, explainability
Generative AI assistantUnstructured content; drafting, summarizing, answering in natural languageHallucinations, harmful content, cost per request
AI agentMulti-step tasks whose path varies case by case and require actions in other systemsExcessive agency, compounding errors, runaway cost, audit trail
HybridMost real processes: rules for clear cases and limits, AI for judgment, people for high-impact decisionsClear handoffs between parts

Choose the simplest solution that meets the need. Stable, explicit, auditable logic means rules; unstructured input or learned patterns mean AI.

Fixed workflow, assistant and agent

OptionWho decides the stepsTakes actions?Best for
Fixed workflow (AI inside steps)Designers, in advanceYes, as programmedKnown, repeatable processes; cheapest and most auditable
Assistant or chatbotThe user, one request at a timeNo; a person acts on its outputAnswers, drafts, summaries
AgentThe agent, within limitsYes, through toolsVariable multi-step tasks
Multi-agent systemAn orchestrator and specialist agentsYesComplex tasks needing several kinds of expertise

"Completes multi-step tasks, takes actions in systems, decides which tool to use" points to an agent. If the steps never vary, a fixed workflow is better.

Prompt engineering, RAG, fine-tuning and distillation

TechniqueChanges the model?SolvesCost and effort
Prompt engineeringNoWrong format, tone, length or focusLowest; always first
RAGNoMissing, company-specific or frequently changing knowledge; need for citationsModerate: documents, index, extra tokens
Supervised fine-tuningYesConsistent specialized behavior, style or formatHigher: labeled data, training, storage, retesting
Reinforcement fine-tuningYesAligning answers with what the business scores as goodHigher: reward functions and training
DistillationYes (a smaller model)Cost and speed for a stable, high-volume taskTraining cost, then lower running cost
Training from scratchNew modelOrganizations whose product is the modelHighest

"Doesn't know our latest policies" or "must show sources" means RAG. "Must always write in our format" means fine-tuning. "Same task, huge volume, too expensive" means distillation. Never fine-tune to keep facts current.

Compare multi-year total cost of ownership, test vendor claims on your own data, and check data use, compliance, IP and exit terms.

Data, concept, bias and feature attribution drift

DriftWhat changesExampleDetected by
Data driftInputs no longer look like training dataExpansion into a new regionComparing input distributions with the baseline (early warning)
Concept driftThe link between inputs and the right answerFraudsters change tacticsAccuracy against real outcomes (often delayed)
Bias driftFairness across groupsOne region flagged more and more oftenPer-group outcome and error rates over time
Feature attribution driftWhich factors the model relies onSudden reliance on postal codeExplanation methods such as SHAP, over time
Generative AI quality changeAnswer qualityOutdated documents, new question types, model version changeSampled reviews, grounding-check blocks, evaluation runs

FormHow it worksFits
Human in the loopA person approves each decision before it takes effectHigh-impact decisions about people (claim denials, loans, hiring)
Human on the loopAI acts; people monitor and can intervene or reverseHigh-volume, reversible, lower-risk tasks
Human in commandPeople set the policies, limits and goals, and decide whether to use AIAlways, at the governance level

Generative AI Security Scoping Matrix scopes

ScopeBuy or buildWhat you controlMain focus
1. Consumer appBuy, no agreementOnly what employees put inData employees share; consumer terms
2. Enterprise appBuy, with a contractContract, configuration, accessData-use terms; vendor security
3. Pre-trained modelBuild on an existing modelApplication, data, access, safeguardsApplication security; what reaches the model
4. Fine-tuned modelBuild and customizeScope 3 plus a model containing your dataSensitive data inside the model
5. Self-trained modelBuild from scratchEverythingAll of the above, plus full development cost

The Scoping Matrix classifies by ownership, to choose security controls. Risk tiers (such as the EU AI Act's) classify by potential harm. Use both.

Ways to split data

  • Randomized: independent rows, no time order, classes reasonably balanced.
  • Stratified: a rare label; keeps each class's share in every split (Data Wrangler allows up to 100 distinct values in the column).
  • Ordered (time-based): forecasting or anything where future data must not leak into training.
  • Split by key: several rows per customer or device; a key never appears in two splits.

Covered in Task 1.3: Sources of bias.

Domain 2: ML Model and Foundation Model Development

Prompting, RAG, fine-tuning, continued pre-training and distillation

  • Prompt engineering: hours, no training; instructions, examples, format.
  • RAG: facts that change or must be cited; new documents usable in minutes; AWS's starting point for question answering over your documents.
  • Supervised fine-tuning: behaviour, tone, format or a narrow task from labelled pairs; "how to respond, not what to know".
  • Reinforcement fine-tuning: answers can be graded automatically but labelled answers are expensive.
  • Continued pre-training: very large volumes of unlabelled domain text, then instruction tuning.
  • Distillation: a smaller, cheaper student copies a larger teacher on your prompts.

Covered in Task 2.1: Fine-tuning strategies.

AI services, Bedrock, Canvas, JumpStart and custom training

  • AWS AI services (Textract, Rekognition, Comprehend, Transcribe): a standard task as an API call; least effort.
  • Bedrock foundation models: serverless generative AI per token; prompting, RAG, agents, light customization.
  • Canvas or the Autopilot API: AutoML for tabular, text, image and time-series predictions without writing models.
  • JumpStart or Bedrock Marketplace models on your own endpoint: open or specialised models with control over instances and networking.
  • Custom training on training jobs or HyperPod: most control, most work.

Automatic model tuning strategies

  • Bayesian (default): learns from finished jobs; best with modest parallelism.
  • Random: independent samples; any amount of parallelism.
  • Grid: every combination of categorical values only.
  • Hyperband: stops weak jobs early by epoch; fast for iterative algorithms; has its own early stopping, so the tuner's must be off.

BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR and BERTScore

  • BLEU: n-gram precision against a reference, with a brevity penalty; translation.
  • ROUGE (1, 2, L): n-gram overlap oriented to recall of the reference; summarization (fmeval defaults to ROUGE-2 and reports the F-measure).
  • METEOR: like ROUGE-1 plus stems and synonyms; kinder to paraphrases.
  • BERTScore: contextual embedding similarity; same meaning in different words. Bedrock's automatic jobs use it for summarization, and F1 for question answering.

Covered in Task 2.3: NLP metrics.

Domain 3: Deployment and Orchestration of ML and AI Workflows

Real-time, serverless, asynchronous and batch inference

  • Real-time endpoint: steady, low-latency traffic; 6 MB and 60 seconds per request; streaming responses.
  • Serverless: intermittent traffic, scales to zero, pay per request; 4 MB, 60 seconds, no GPU, no VPC, cold starts.
  • Asynchronous: queued requests up to 1 GB through S3 and up to an hour; results in S3 with SNS; scales to zero.
  • Batch transform: a whole dataset offline with no persistent endpoint. Bedrock batch inference is the foundation model equivalent (about 50% cheaper).

Covered in Task 3.1: Inference strategies.

Inference components, multi-model, multi-container and serial pipelines

  • Inference components: several models (often FMs) on shared instances, each with its own CPU, accelerator and memory reservation and copy count, scaling independently and to zero.
  • Multi-model endpoint: many models with the same framework, loaded from S3 on demand; cheap for thousands of rarely used models, with cold starts.
  • Multi-container endpoint: up to 15 different containers, called directly or in sequence.
  • Serial inference pipeline: 2 to 15 containers in sequence (preprocess, predict, postprocess) at no extra cost.

Covered in Task 3.1: Orchestrators and multi-model hosting.

Ways to serve a foundation model

  • Bedrock serverless: per token, service tiers, no infrastructure.
  • Bedrock custom model deployment: per-token serving of a model fine-tuned on Bedrock, where supported; swap models in place with UpdateCustomModelDeployment.
  • Provisioned Throughput: hourly model units, billed around the clock; for steady volume or custom models that need it.
  • Custom Model Import: your own weights for supported open architectures, serverless, scales to zero after 5 idle minutes.
  • Bedrock Marketplace: specialised models on SageMaker-managed instances you pay for hourly, called through Bedrock APIs.
  • SageMaker AI endpoint, HyperPod or EKS: full control of containers, instances and scaling.

Covered in Task 3.1: FM deployment options.

Blue/green (all at once), canary, linear and rolling

  • All at once (the default blue/green): new fleet, all traffic moves, old fleet waits through the baking period.
  • Canary: a small share (up to 50%) first, then the rest after baking.
  • Linear: equal steps (10 to 50%) with waits between.
  • Rolling: batches of instances replaced on the same fleet; needs less spare capacity; not for serverless or multi-variant endpoints.

All can roll back automatically on CloudWatch alarms. Covered in Task 3.3: Deployment strategies and rollback.

CloudTrail, Config, GuardDuty and Security Hub

  • AWS CloudTrail: who called which API and when (Bedrock invocations are management events; SageMaker InvokeEndpoint is a data event); no payloads.
  • AWS Config: resource settings over time and rule checks (for example models in a VPC, KMS keys on endpoint configs).
  • Amazon GuardDuty: threats in activity, including AI Protection for Bedrock, AgentCore and SageMaker AI (anomalous invocation, cost harvesting).
  • AWS Security Hub: posture controls and findings across accounts in one place.

Covered in Task 4.3: Monitoring, auditing and compliance.

Often confused pairs

  • Data drift and concept drift: The inputs change versus the relationship between inputs and the label changes (Task 2.3).
  • Overfitting and underfitting: Good on training data but poor on validation (regularize, simplify, add data) versus poor on both (more features, more complexity, less regularization).
  • Incremental training and checkpointing: Starting a new job from a previous model (only three image built-ins) versus resuming an interrupted job.
  • Data capture and model invocation logging: SageMaker endpoint requests and responses to S3 versus Bedrock prompts and responses to CloudWatch Logs or S3.
  • Knowledge base logs and invocation logs: Ingestion only (which files were indexed) versus model calls; retrieval queries are logged by your application.
  • Prompt Management versions and Flows aliases: Immutable prompt snapshots the app pins by ARN versus a pointer to one flow version that can be moved.
  • AgentCore DEFAULT and named endpoints: Follows every new runtime version versus stays pinned until you move it; production uses a named endpoint.
  • Model approval status and lifecycle stage: Only Approved lets a version deploy versus a description of where a version is.
  • Savings Plans and training plans: A price commitment versus reserved capacity.
  • Stop reason max_tokens and an error: A truncated answer is a successful call, so no error metric moves.
  • ApplyGuardrail and InvokeGuardrailChecks: Checks content against a guardrail you created versus scores content against chosen categories without a guardrail.
  • Macie and Comprehend PII redaction: Finds personal data in S3 versus removes it from text.
  • Glue Data Quality and Model Monitor: Active data checks in pipelines versus a monitoring service closed to new customers since 30 July 2026.
  • Inspector and CodeGuru: The current AWS answer for scanning images and code versus an older service (CodeGuru Reviewer takes no new repositories).

Overview

Task 2.1

Choose appropriate modeling approaches for ML and AI solutions.

Questions in this task describe a business need and its constraints (explainable decisions, a tight latency budget, little ML expertise, frequently changing documents, a fixed budget) and ask for the approach: a pre-trained AI service, AutoML, a built-in algorithm, a Bedrock model with RAG, or a customized model. AWS's own guidance points the same way each time: start with the simplest, most managed option that meets the quality bar, and move to more control (and more work) only when a requirement forces it.

Running example. The online retailer (account 111111111111) needs a churn model that the retention team can explain, a support assistant that answers from help articles that change weekly, nightly summaries of support tickets, and automatic reading of supplier invoices.

Status changes to know. Several "use the managed service" answers now come with a caveat. Amazon Kendra and Amazon Q Business have been closed to new customers since 30 July 2026 (AWS points to Bedrock Managed Knowledge Bases and to Amazon Quick), as have Bedrock Agents (now "Agents Classic"), SageMaker Clarify and Model Monitor. Amazon Forecast has been closed to new customers since 29 July 2024 (use SageMaker Canvas) and Amazon Fraud Detector since 7 November 2025. All Meta Llama models on Bedrock are Legacy or end-of-life, and Claude 3 Haiku fine-tuning has ended. Autopilot's interface moved into Canvas; its API remains.

Selecting a Bedrock FM

Skill 2.1.1Evaluate and select appropriate FMs from Amazon Bedrock based on task requirements and performance criteria.

The criteria AWS lists in its generative AI decision guide and the Well-Architected Generative AI Lens:

  • Modality: text in and out, images or video in (vision), embeddings, image or video generation, speech. Amazon Nova 2 Lite, Nova Pro and Nova Lite and the Claude models take images; Nova Micro is text only; Titan, Cohere Embed and Nova Multimodal Embeddings produce vectors; Stability AI models generate images.
  • Context window and output length (long documents, long conversations), languages, and reasoning support.
  • Quality of response, which AWS calls the most essential criterion: test candidates on your own prompts with Bedrock model evaluation (automatic metrics, an LLM judge or human reviewers; see the Bedrock evaluation notes).
  • Latency and price: smaller models are faster and cheaper. AWS's cost best practice is to find the minimum quality bar, list the models that meet it, pick the cheapest, and re-evaluate as models change.
  • Features and access: whether the model supports fine-tuning, batch, prompt caching, service tiers, knowledge bases or structured outputs, which Regions and inference profiles offer it, and its lifecycle (Active, then Legacy with an announced end-of-life date).

The model card for each model in the Bedrock user guide is the real selection sheet: context window, maximum output, modalities, supported features per API, service tiers, Regions, and the earliest end-of-life date. The API can only shortlist:

Shortlist Active, on-demand text models in a Region (Python, boto3) 
import boto3

bedrock = boto3.client("bedrock", region_name="us-east-1")  # control plane, not bedrock-runtime
resp = bedrock.list_foundation_models(byOutputModality="TEXT", byInferenceType="ON_DEMAND")

for m in resp["modelSummaries"]:
    # Status is ACTIVE or LEGACY; end-of-life models simply stop working
    if m.get("modelLifecycle", {}).get("status") == "ACTIVE":
        print(m["modelId"], m["inputModalities"], m.get("customizationsSupported", []))
# Context window, price, tiers, batch and caching support are on the model card, not here

Lifecycle status is per Region, and a missing feature can rule a model out (Nova 2 Lite, for example, can't be the target of a Bedrock model evaluation job or a knowledge base, and the newest Claude models support only the Standard tier and no batch). Many newer models are reachable only through a geographic or global cross-Region inference profile, so "pick a Region" often means "pick a profile". Older blogs and guides use models that have since been retired; take current IDs from the model cards.

Fine-tuning strategies

Skill 2.1.2Identify fine-tuning strategies for pre-trained FMs to meet business needs.

Customization is a ladder, from cheapest and fastest to most effort:

  1. Prompt engineering (instructions, examples, output format): hours, no training.
  2. RAG for facts that change. AWS Prescriptive Guidance: for question answering over your own documents, start with RAG; fine-tune when the model must perform a new task such as a particular kind of summarization. RAG takes in new documents in minutes and can cite sources; a fine-tuned model can't cite and goes stale when documents change.
  3. Supervised fine-tuning on labelled prompt and response pairs to change behaviour: tone, format, domain style, a workflow. In the words of the Nova guide, it "teaches the model how to respond, not what to know".
  4. Reinforcement fine-tuning when you can grade answers automatically (a rule or an AI judge) but labelled answers are expensive, or the task needs multi-step reasoning.
  5. Continued pre-training on large volumes of unlabelled domain text (tens of billions of tokens for Nova on SageMaker AI), followed by instruction tuning so the model still follows prompts.
  6. Distillation to make a smaller, cheaper student copy a larger teacher on your prompts (Bedrock: Nova teachers, not Anthropic models).
  7. Training from scratch only for a genuinely new model, on SageMaker training jobs or HyperPod.
  • Parameter-efficient vs full fine-tuning. LoRA (low-rank adaptation) trains small adapter matrices while the base weights stay frozen: much less memory and time, smaller artifacts, and less risk of the model forgetting general skills. QLoRA also quantizes the base model to save memory (offered as a HyperPod recipe option). Full fine-tuning updates every weight: more capacity to change the model, more compute, and more risk of catastrophic forgetting, which AWS's Nova Forge counters by mixing curated general data with your own.
  • Where it runs: Bedrock fine-tuning, reinforcement fine-tuning and distillation for supported models (today mainly Amazon Nova, plus some open-weight models for reinforcement fine-tuning); SageMaker AI serverless customization (LoRA or full, SFT, DPO and RL techniques; in the API, leaving out Peft means full fine-tuning); JumpStart training jobs with lora_r and similar hyperparameters; HyperPod recipes for large jobs. Details are in the Bedrock customization and SageMaker model customization notes.
  • Serving the result: a fine-tuned Nova model with LoRA adapters can use Bedrock on-demand inference; full-rank custom models generally need Provisioned Throughput, which is a fixed hourly cost.

"Answers must reflect policy documents updated every week" means RAG, not fine-tuning. "Responses must always follow the company's tone and a fixed template" means fine-tuning (or a strong prompt first). "Cut cost and latency while keeping quality on one narrow task" means distillation or fine-tuning a smaller model.

Models, algorithms and templates

Skill 2.1.3Compare and select appropriate ML models, generative AI (GenAI) models, algorithms, and solution templates to meet business needs (for example, interpretability, domain-specific performance, latency).

Start from the problem type. SageMaker AI's built-in algorithms, grouped as the documentation groups them:

  • Tabular classification and regression: XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost, AutoGluon-Tabular, TabTransformer, Linear Learner, k-nearest neighbours, Factorization Machines (sparse data such as click predictions and recommendations).
  • Time series: DeepAR, which beats classical methods such as ARIMA and ETS when there are hundreds of related series; start on a single CPU instance, and inference is CPU only. Pre-trained forecasting models (Chronos) and Canvas forecasting are the alternatives now that Amazon Forecast is closed to new customers.
  • Unsupervised: K-Means (clustering), PCA (dimensionality reduction), Random Cut Forest (anomalies), IP Insights (unusual IP use), LDA and NTM (topics).
  • Text: BlazingText (Word2Vec embeddings and fast text classification), Text Classification - TensorFlow, Seq2Seq (translation, summarization), Object2Vec (embeddings of pairs).
  • Images: image classification, object detection and semantic segmentation (GPU training).

Then weigh the constraints the skill names:

  • Interpretability. Linear models (coefficients) and single decision trees explain themselves. Tree ensembles such as XGBoost need SHAP values (tree SHAP is fast and exact); neural networks need methods such as integrated gradients; anything else can use Kernel SHAP, which gets expensive as features grow. Regulated decisions (credit, healthcare, the retention offer someone gets) may justify a slightly less accurate but explainable model. Canvas shows column impact; with Clarify closed to new customers, AWS points to the open-source shap library.
  • Domain-specific performance. A model trained on similar data usually wins: JumpStart offers nearly 1,000 models, and Bedrock Marketplace adds over 100 specialised models that run on SageMaker AI endpoints but are called through Bedrock APIs.
  • Latency. Small tree models and linear models answer in milliseconds on CPU; deep networks and LLMs need GPUs or accelerators and cost more per prediction. A generative model is the wrong tool for a structured prediction a gradient-boosted tree does well (churn, fraud scores, demand).
  • Solution templates. SageMaker AI Projects provide MLOps templates (Service Catalog products for build, train and deploy pipelines with third-party Git; the CodeCommit templates were removed on 28 October 2024). JumpStart solution templates remain only in Studio Classic.

"Tabular data, high accuracy, and the business must understand which factors drive a prediction" points to XGBoost (or LightGBM) with SHAP explanations; "must be explainable without extra tools" points to Linear Learner or a single tree. "Thousands of related product demand series" points to DeepAR or Canvas forecasting.

Build or buy

Skill 2.1.4Evaluate tradeoffs between custom solutions, managed services, pre-trained models, and FMs to meet business needs.

From least to most effort and control:

  • AWS AI services (Textract, Rekognition, Comprehend, Transcribe and others): an API call, no ML skills, priced per request. Right when the task is a standard one (read a form, detect objects, find sentiment). Most can be adapted a little: Textract adapters, Rekognition Custom Labels, Comprehend custom classification, Transcribe custom vocabularies.
  • Bedrock foundation models: serverless, priced per token, with prompting, RAG, agents and light customization. AWS's Bedrock-or-SageMaker decision guide (July 2026) describes a common path: start on Bedrock, and move to SageMaker AI as customization needs grow.
  • AutoML: SageMaker Canvas builds up to 250 candidate models without code (Quick build in minutes on a sample of up to 50,000 rows, Standard build in hours and usually more accurate). The Autopilot API (CreateAutoMLJobV2) does the same from code and is needed for text, image and time-series problems.
  • Pre-trained models on SageMaker AI: JumpStart and Bedrock Marketplace models you deploy on your own endpoint, with control over instances, networking and cost per hour.
  • Custom training: your own code on training jobs or HyperPod, for the most control or a model nobody offers. Expect the most effort for data, tuning, deployment and monitoring.
Let Autopilot build the churn model from code (Python, boto3) 
import boto3

sm = boto3.client("sagemaker")
sm.create_auto_ml_job_v2(
    AutoMLJobName="churn-automl",
    AutoMLJobInputDataConfig=[{
        "ChannelType": "training",
        "ContentType": "text/csv;header=present",
        "DataSource": {"S3DataSource": {
            "S3DataType": "S3Prefix",
            "S3Uri": "s3://retail-ml-111111111111/churn/train/"}},
    }],
    OutputDataConfig={"S3OutputPath": "s3://retail-ml-111111111111/churn/automl/"},
    AutoMLProblemTypeConfig={"TabularJobConfig": {
        "TargetAttributeName": "churned",
        "ProblemType": "BinaryClassification",
        # AUTO picks ENSEMBLING under 100 MB, HYPERPARAMETER_TUNING above
        "Mode": "AUTO",
        "CompletionCriteria": {"MaxCandidates": 50},
    }},
    AutoMLJobObjective={"MetricName": "F1"},
    RoleArn="arn:aws:iam::111111111111:role/SageMakerAutoMLRole",
)

"Least operational overhead" and "no ML expertise" point to the most managed option that does the job: an AI service, Canvas or a Bedrock model, not a custom training job. Check the service is still open to new customers: questions written before 2026 may expect Kendra, Forecast or Fraud Detector, which new accounts can no longer adopt.

RAG architecture patterns

Skill 2.1.5Select Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) architecture patterns based on use case requirements.

  • Managed knowledge base (Bedrock Managed Knowledge Base, GA June 2026, which AWS now recommends for most cases): Bedrock runs the parsing, embedding, storage, hybrid search and reranking, with connectors and document-level permissions. Retrieval is through Retrieve or the agentic AgenticRetrieveStream; it doesn't support RetrieveAndGenerate, SQL or graph retrieval (see the managed knowledge base notes).
  • Customer-managed knowledge base: you choose the vector store (OpenSearch Serverless or managed clusters, Aurora PostgreSQL with pgvector, S3 Vectors for cheap large indexes, Neptune Analytics, partner stores), the chunking and parsing, and you can call RetrieveAndGenerate for answers with citations (see the customer-managed notes).
  • Fully custom RAG: your own pipeline with embeddings from Bedrock or SageMaker AI and any store, when you need full control over every step.

Patterns matched to requirements:

  • Exact terms (product codes, error numbers) as well as meaning: hybrid search (keyword plus vector). It works on OpenSearch Serverless, Aurora and MongoDB stores; elsewhere a request for hybrid quietly falls back to semantic.
  • Relevance not good enough: retrieve more chunks and rerank them with a reranking model (Amazon Rerank or Cohere Rerank 3.5), passing only the best to the model.
  • Complex, multi-part questions: query decomposition splits a question into sub-queries; agentic retrieval plans several retrieval steps.
  • Relationships across documents ("which suppliers of recalled parts also supply the north region?"): GraphRAG with Neptune Analytics.
  • Numbers in a warehouse: a structured knowledge base turns questions into SQL against Amazon Redshift (grant read-only access).
  • Images, audio and video: multimodal knowledge bases, either with multimodal embeddings or by converting media to text with Bedrock Data Automation.
  • Different users may see different documents: filter on metadata or use document-level permissions.
Hybrid search with reranking on a customer-managed knowledge base (Python, boto3) 
import boto3

kb = boto3.client("bedrock-agent-runtime", region_name="us-west-2")
resp = kb.retrieve(
    knowledgeBaseId="KB12345678",
    retrievalQuery={"text": "Can I return a jacket bought on sale?"},
    retrievalConfiguration={"vectorSearchConfiguration": {
        "numberOfResults": 20,
        "overrideSearchType": "HYBRID",
        "filter": {"equals": {"key": "language", "value": "en"}},
        "rerankingConfiguration": {
            "type": "BEDROCK_RERANKING_MODEL",
            "bedrockRerankingConfiguration": {
                "modelConfiguration": {"modelArn": "arn:aws:bedrock:us-west-2::foundation-model/cohere.rerank-v3-5:0"},
                # Keep the 5 best of the 20 retrieved chunks
                "numberOfRerankedResults": 5,
            },
        },
    }},
)
for r in resp["retrievalResults"]:
    print(r["score"], r["content"]["text"][:80])

Amazon Kendra as a retriever (the Kendra knowledge base type) is for existing customers only since 30 July 2026; AWS's migration target is a Bedrock Managed Knowledge Base. From 30 September 2026, customer-managed knowledge bases can't add new Confluence, SharePoint, Salesforce or web crawler connectors.

Training performance, time and cost

Skill 2.1.6Assess tradeoffs between ML model performance, training time, and cost.

  • A simpler model or less data first. A tuned XGBoost often matches a deep network on tabular data at a fraction of the cost; train on a sample to compare approaches, then on everything for the winner. Canvas Quick build versus Standard build is the same trade.
  • Managed Spot Training: up to 90% cheaper than On-Demand, but jobs can be interrupted. Set MaxWaitTimeInSeconds at least as large as MaxRuntimeInSeconds and save checkpoints to S3 so an interrupted job resumes; there is no fallback to On-Demand, and built-in algorithms that can't checkpoint are limited to a one-hour wait.
  • Right instance: GPUs cost more per hour but usually finish sooner, so they are often cheaper per job for deep learning; XGBoost on CPU is memory-bound (general-purpose M instances rather than compute-optimised C); AWS Trainium (Trn1, Trn2) claims up to 50% lower cost to train than comparable instances.
  • Warm pools (KeepAlivePeriodInSeconds) keep instances running between jobs so repeated experiments skip start-up time; you pay while they wait, and they don't work with Spot.
  • Capacity and commitment: SageMaker AI training plans reserve scarce GPU or Trainium capacity for 1 to 182 days (paid upfront, can't be cancelled); SageMaker Savings Plans cut up to 64% for a one- or three-year hourly commitment.
  • Stop early: early stopping in training or automatic model tuning (TrainingJobEarlyStoppingType AUTO) ends runs that are not improving (more in Spot, warm pools and capacity).
Spot training with checkpoints (JSON, part of CreateTrainingJob) 
{
  "EnableManagedSpotTraining": true,
  "StoppingCondition": {
    "MaxRuntimeInSeconds": 7200,
    "MaxWaitTimeInSeconds": 14400
  },
  "CheckpointConfig": {
    "S3Uri": "s3://retail-ml-111111111111/churn/checkpoints/",
    "LocalPath": "/opt/ml/checkpoints"
  }
}

"Reduce training cost; the job can tolerate interruptions" means Managed Spot Training with checkpoints. "Data scientists rerun short experiments all day and wait for instances to start" means warm pools. "Guaranteed GPUs for a training run next month" means a training plan.

Inference performance, latency and cost

Skill 2.1.7Assess tradeoffs between AI model performance, latency, and cost.

  • Model size: the biggest lever. Route easy requests to a small model and hard ones to a large one, or distil a small model for one task. Bedrock Intelligent Prompt Routing does this automatically but supports only a few, older model families.
  • Service tiers per request: Priority (about 75% more than Standard, up to 25% faster output), Standard, Flex (about 50% cheaper, slower and may be delayed when busy) and account-level Reserved capacity. Not every model supports every tier.
  • Batch inference: about 50% cheaper than on-demand for jobs of 100 to 100,000 records with results in hours, not seconds.
  • Prompt caching: reuse a long, unchanging prompt prefix (instructions, documents) for up to 90% lower cost and lower time to first token; the cache lasts 5 minutes by default.
  • Cross-Region inference: geographic profiles keep data within an area; global profiles add capacity at about 10% lower price, without data residency.
  • Provisioned Throughput: fixed hourly model units for steady, high volume or for custom models that need it.
  • Perceived latency: stream responses (users see the first words sooner), set a realistic maxTokens, and keep prompts short. Watch TimeToFirstToken (streaming only) and InvocationLatency in CloudWatch.
Nightly ticket summaries on the cheaper Flex tier (Python, boto3) 
import boto3

runtime = boto3.client("bedrock-runtime", region_name="us-east-1")
resp = runtime.converse(
    modelId="global.amazon.nova-2-lite-v1:0",
    messages=[{"role": "user", "content": [{"text": "Summarize this ticket: ..."}]}],
    inferenceConfig={"maxTokens": 300},
    # priority | default | flex | reserved; the response reports the tier used
    serviceTier={"type": "flex"},
)
print(resp["serviceTier"], resp["usage"]["outputTokens"], resp["metrics"]["latencyMs"])

More on each lever in the Bedrock service tiers, prompt caching and batch notes.

Match the lever to the need: "results by tomorrow morning, lowest cost" means batch or Flex; "interactive chat feels slow" means streaming, a smaller model, prompt caching or Priority; "same 20-page policy in every prompt" means prompt caching; "steady high volume of a custom model" means Provisioned Throughput.

AWS AI services

Skill 2.1.8Apply AWS AI services to solve specific business problems (for example, Amazon Textract, Amazon Rekognition, Amazon Comprehend, Amazon Transcribe).

  • Amazon Textract (documents): DetectDocumentText for plain OCR; AnalyzeDocument with FeatureTypes FORMS, TABLES, QUERIES, SIGNATURES or LAYOUT for structure (Queries answer questions such as "What is the invoice total?"); AnalyzeExpense for invoices and receipts; AnalyzeID for US driving licences and passports; Analyze Lending for mortgage packets. Multipage PDFs need the asynchronous StartDocumentAnalysis with an SNS notification.
  • Amazon Rekognition (images and video): labels for objects and scenes, text in images, unsafe-content moderation, face detection, comparison and search, Face Liveness, PPE detection, and Custom Labels for your own classes (logos, product defects) from a few hundred images. Streaming video analysis and bulk image analysis closed to new customers on 30 April 2026, and People Pathing ended on 31 October 2025.
  • Amazon Comprehend (text): entities, key phrases, language, sentiment and targeted sentiment, PII, toxicity, and custom classification or entity recognition (real-time custom classification needs an endpoint that is billed until you delete it). Comprehend Medical extracts medical entities and PHI.
  • Amazon Transcribe (speech to text): batch or streaming, custom vocabularies and language models, speaker labels, PII redaction, and Call Analytics for contact centres (sentiment, categories, summaries; categories must exist before the job). Transcribe Medical for clinical speech.
  • Others: Amazon Translate (translation with custom terminology), Amazon Polly (text to speech), Amazon Lex (chatbots), Amazon Personalize (recommendations).
  • Bedrock Data Automation is the generative AI alternative for documents, images, audio and video: standard output (summaries, transcripts) or custom fields defined in blueprints, with confidence scores, instead of stitching several services together.
Read the total and due date from a supplier invoice (Python, boto3) 
import boto3

textract = boto3.client("textract")
resp = textract.analyze_document(
    Document={"S3Object": {"Bucket": "retail-docs-111111111111", "Name": "invoices/inv-2041.png"}},
    FeatureTypes=["QUERIES", "TABLES"],
    QueriesConfig={"Queries": [
        {"Text": "What is the invoice total?", "Alias": "TOTAL"},
        {"Text": "What is the payment due date?", "Alias": "DUE_DATE"},
    ]},
)
for block in resp["Blocks"]:
    if block["BlockType"] == "QUERY_RESULT":
        print(block["Text"], block["Confidence"])

Human review through Amazon A2I (the HumanLoopConfig option in Textract and Rekognition) fails for accounts that aren't already A2I customers since July 2026. A question that needs people to check low-confidence results now needs your own review step, for example a queue and a small review app.

Key takeaways

  • Bedrock FM choice: modality, context, quality on your own prompts, latency and price, features and lifecycle; the model card is the selection sheet; start with the smallest model that meets the bar.
  • Customization ladder: prompt, RAG for changing facts, fine-tuning for behaviour, reinforcement fine-tuning when answers can be graded, continued pre-training for lots of domain text, distillation for a cheaper copy; LoRA saves cost and limits forgetting.
  • Algorithms: XGBoost and friends for tabular, DeepAR for many related series, K-Means, PCA and Random Cut Forest unsupervised, BlazingText for text; linear models and single trees explain themselves, ensembles need SHAP.
  • Build or buy: AI services, then Bedrock, then Canvas or Autopilot, then JumpStart, then custom training; check the service is still open to new customers.
  • RAG: managed knowledge base by default; customer-managed for your own store, RetrieveAndGenerate or SQL and graph retrieval; hybrid search for exact terms, reranking for relevance, decomposition for complex questions.
  • Training cost: simpler model and sampled data first, Spot with checkpoints (up to 90%), warm pools for fast iteration, training plans for guaranteed capacity, Savings Plans for steady use.
  • Inference cost and latency: smaller model, Flex or batch for offline work, Priority for speed, prompt caching for repeated prefixes, streaming and a sensible maxTokens.
  • AI services: Textract for documents (queries, tables, forms, expenses, IDs), Rekognition for images, Comprehend for text, Transcribe for speech; Bedrock Data Automation for generative extraction.

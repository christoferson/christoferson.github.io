Control types

Domain 1. See topic 1.2.

TypePurposeCluster example
PreventativeStop a vulnerability being usedRBAC, admission policy, NetworkPolicy, seccomp
DeterrentDissuade a threat actorAcceptable-use policy, login banners
DetectiveWarn of an attempted or successful attackAudit logs, Falco alerts, scanning
CorrectiveReduce the impact after the eventRollback, redeploying a patched image, restoring etcd
CompensatingMake up for a weakness in another controlStrict seccomp plus extra monitoring when a workload can't be sandboxed

gVisor and Kata Containers

Domain 1. See topic 1.2.

RuntimeIsolationProject

Both are chosen per Pod with a RuntimeClass. Kubernetes has no "sandboxed" flag of its own.

Often confused pairs

Short distinctions that exam questions like to test.

PairDifferenceSee
Encoding and encryption of SecretsSecret values are base64-encoded; etcd stores them unencrypted unless encryption at rest is configuredTopic 3.3
Encryption at rest and API accessEncryption protects etcd and backups; anyone RBAC lets read a Secret still gets plaintextTopic 4.3
get and list on SecretsBoth return contents; there's no "names only" permissionTopic 3.2
Authentication and authorization orderAuthenticators run in no guaranteed order; authorizers run in the configured orderTopic 3.2
system:serviceaccount and system:serviceaccountsSingular is one ServiceAccount's username; plural is the group of all of themTopic 3.2
PSA enforce on Pods and DeploymentsEnforce rejects Pods; a violating Deployment is accepted and its ReplicaSet failsTopic 3.1
PodSecurityPolicy and Pod Security AdmissionPSP mutated Pods and was removed in v1.25; PSA only validates, per namespace labelTopic 3.1
Admission and read requestsAdmission never sees get, list or watch; exec and port-forward (CONNECT) it can blockTopic 5.4
Audit log and EventsAudit records who did what (off by default); Events are hints kept one hourTopic 5.2
mTLS and authorizationmTLS authenticates; anyone with a valid certificate still gets in without an authorization policyTopic 5.2
Keyless and keyed signingKeyless still signs with a key, used once; trust comes from the identity in a short-lived certificateTopic 5.1
Tag and digestTags can be repointed (Trivy Action, March 2026); digests are immutableTopic 1.3
RWO and RWOPRWO is one node, possibly several Pods; RWOP is exactly one Pod. Neither is write protectionTopic 2.3
STRIDE and LINDDUN non-repudiationA property to protect in STRIDE; a privacy threat in LINDDUNTopic 6.2
Compliant and secure"A compliant system is not guaranteed to be secure, nor a secure system is guaranteed to be compliant"Topic 6.1
CSF and SP 800-53CSF describes outcomes (six functions); SP 800-53 is the control catalogueTopic 6.1
NIS2 and DORANIS2 is a directive each country transposes; DORA is a regulation that applies directlyTopic 6.1

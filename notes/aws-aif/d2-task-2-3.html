Task 2.3: Describe AWS infrastructure and technologies for building GenAI applications

The AWS toolbox for generative AI and when to reach for each tool: Amazon Bedrock for foundation models, SageMaker AI and JumpStart for building and hosting your own, Amazon Quick for business users, Kiro for developers, Strands Agents and Bedrock AgentCore for agents. Then why build on AWS at all, what its infrastructure provides for security, compliance and safety, and how each choice trades cost against speed, availability and control.

Overview

Task 2.3

Describe AWS infrastructure and technologies for building GenAI applications.

Many exam questions are "which service?" questions. The key is the level of control each service offers. At one end, Amazon Quick and Kiro are finished applications you use. In the middle, Amazon Bedrock gives you foundation models as a managed API to build your own applications, and AgentCore runs your agents. At the other end, SageMaker AI lets you train, customize and host models yourself. The more control, the more work and responsibility.

AWS services for GenAI

Skill 2.3.1Identify AWS services and features to develop GenAI applications (for example, Amazon Bedrock, Amazon SageMaker AI, SageMaker JumpStart, Amazon Quick, Kiro, Strands Agents, Amazon Bedrock AgentCore)

Amazon Bedrock

What it is

A fully managed service that gives secure access to high-performing foundation models from Amazon and leading AI companies through a single API. It is serverless: there are no servers or GPUs to manage, you pay for what you use, and your prompts and outputs aren't used to train the base models. It is the default answer for "build a generative AI application on AWS without managing infrastructure".

Models include Amazon Nova (text, multimodal, image, video and speech), Amazon Titan (embeddings) and models from Anthropic, Meta, Mistral AI, Cohere, AI21 Labs, Stability AI, DeepSeek, Qwen, OpenAI and others. You can switch models with little code change, so you can try several and move to better or cheaper ones as they appear.

Main features

  • Playgrounds: try models and prompts in the console before writing code.
  • Knowledge Bases: fully managed RAG. Connect data sources (Amazon S3, SharePoint, Confluence, websites and others); Bedrock splits, embeds and stores the content and retrieves relevant passages to generate cited answers. A Managed Knowledge Base option, added in 2026, also runs the storage and search for you.
  • Guardrails: filters for harmful content, denied topics, prompt attacks and personal data, plus grounding checks against hallucination, applied to inputs and outputs of any model.
  • Prompt Management and Flows: store, version and reuse prompts; chain prompts, knowledge bases and functions into workflows.
  • Evaluations: compare models and RAG systems with automatic metrics, human reviewers or an LLM as judge.
  • Customization: fine-tuning, reinforcement fine-tuning and distillation for supported models; Custom Model Import for open-weight models you customized elsewhere.
  • Inference options: on-demand, batch, cross-Region inference, service tiers and Provisioned Throughput (see Cost trade-offs).
  • Bedrock Data Automation: extract structured information from documents, images, audio and video.

Amazon Bedrock Agents is now Amazon Bedrock Agents Classic and has been closed to new customers since July 30, 2026. It let you configure an agent in the console with action groups (APIs described by an OpenAPI schema and run by Lambda functions) and knowledge bases, and supported supervisor-led multi-agent collaboration. Existing agents keep working, with no planned end date, but get no new features or models. AWS recommends AgentCore for new agents. Knowledge Bases and Guardrails are unaffected.

Amazon SageMaker AI and SageMaker JumpStart

SageMaker AI for generative AI

The managed ML platform for when you need more control than Bedrock gives: training or fine-tuning models with your own code and data, hosting open-weight or custom models on endpoints where you choose the instance types (including GPUs and AWS's own Trainium and Inferentia chips), and running the full MLOps lifecycle. It suits teams with ML skills who need a model or configuration Bedrock doesn't offer, full control over training, or to keep hosting entirely in their own setup.

SageMaker JumpStart

A hub inside SageMaker AI of hundreds of pre-trained, mostly open source models (foundation models from providers such as Meta, Mistral AI and Hugging Face, plus vision and language models), with solution templates and example notebooks. You can deploy a model to a SageMaker AI endpoint, or fine-tune it on your data, in a few clicks or lines of code. The model runs in your account on instances you choose.

Bedrock or JumpStart?Bedrock is a serverless API, pay per token, nothing to manage. JumpStart deploys the model onto infrastructure you choose and pay for by the hour, with more control over the model and hosting.

Ready-made GenAI applications

Amazon Quick

An AI-powered service for business users to automate tasks, analyze data, build web applications and conduct research, mainly through chat with AI agents that work over connected company data and applications. Launched as Amazon Quick Suite in October 2025. Its parts:

  • Quick Sight: business intelligence dashboards and natural-language questions about data (evolved from Amazon QuickSight, whose APIs continue to work).
  • Quick Index: connects documents and data sources so answers are grounded in company information.
  • Quick Research: in-depth research across company data and the web, delivered as a cited report.
  • Quick Flows: workflows that any user describes in plain language.
  • Quick Automate: automation of complex, multi-step business processes with agents, for technical teams, including human approval steps.
  • Spaces and custom chat agents: shared collections of documents, dashboards and connectors, and agents configured with their own instructions, knowledge and tools. Connectors can be built from MCP servers.

When it fitsBusiness users who want AI over their company's data without building anything. Amazon Q Business is no longer open to new customers, and AWS directs its users to Quick.

Kiro

An AI-powered development environment, available as an IDE, a command-line tool and on the web, that helps developers build software from prototype to production with AI agents. Generally available since November 2025. Its distinctive approach is spec-driven development: from a description of a feature, Kiro writes requirements.md (user stories with acceptance criteria), design.md (architecture and technical design) and tasks.md (a list of implementation tasks), which the developer reviews before Kiro implements them. Steering files give it persistent knowledge of a project's conventions; hooks trigger agent actions on events such as saving a file; MCP connects it to external tools and data.

When it fitsDevelopers writing and maintaining code, including the code of GenAI applications. Kiro is the successor to Amazon Q Developer; the Q Developer CLI became the Kiro CLI, and Q Developer's IDE plugins reach end of support on April 30, 2027.

Building and running agents

Strands Agents

An open-source SDK (Apache 2.0 license) for building AI agents in a few lines of code, released by AWS in May 2025 and used in production by AWS teams. It is model-driven: instead of scripting each step, you give the agent a model, a system prompt and a set of tools, and the model plans, calls tools and iterates until the task is done. Any Python function becomes a tool with the @tool decorator; MCP servers can supply tools too. It works with models on Bedrock and other providers, supports multi-agent patterns (agents as tools, swarm, graph, workflow) and the A2A protocol, and emits OpenTelemetry traces for observability.

A minimal Strands agent 
from strands import Agent, tool

# any Python function becomes a tool the model can call
@tool
def order_status(order_id: str) -> str:
    """Look up the delivery status of an order."""
    return lookup_in_orders_db(order_id)

# model + system prompt + tools = an agent
agent = Agent(
    system_prompt="You are a helpful order support agent.",
    tools=[order_status],
)

# the agent decides itself whether to call order_status
agent("Where is my order 55120?")

Amazon Bedrock AgentCore

An agentic platform for building, deploying and operating agents securely at scale, using any framework (Strands Agents, LangGraph, CrewAI, LlamaIndex and others) and any foundation model, on Bedrock or elsewhere. Generally available since October 2025. It solves the problems that appear when an agent moves from a demo to production: hosting, memory, connecting to tools, authentication, observability, evaluation and control. Its services can be used together or separately:

  • Runtime: secure, serverless hosting for agents and tools, with each session isolated in its own microVM, sessions of up to 8 hours, and support for MCP and A2A.
  • Memory: short-term memory within a session and long-term memory across sessions (facts, preferences, summaries, episodes), extracted automatically.
  • Gateway: a single, secure entry point that turns APIs, Lambda functions and services into MCP tools agents can discover and call, and fronts existing MCP servers.
  • Identity: identity and credential management for agents: who may invoke an agent, and secure access tokens for the tools and services it acts on (Task 5.1).
  • Code Interpreter and Browser: sandboxes where agents run code and use websites safely.
  • Observability: traces, metrics and dashboards of each agent step, through Amazon CloudWatch.
  • Evaluations: automated quality scoring of agents (correctness, helpfulness, tool selection, goal success), online and on demand.
  • Policy: deterministic rules, written in Cedar or generated from plain language, that are checked on every tool call through the Gateway, outside the agent's own code (Task 5.1).

Newer additionsIn 2026 AWS added further AgentCore services, including a managed agent harness, a registry of agents and tools, and optimization tools. They postdate most exam material.

Advantages of AWS GenAI services

Skill 2.3.2Describe the advantages of using AWS GenAI services to build applications (for example, accessibility, lower barrier to entry, efficiency, cost-effectiveness, speed to market, ability to meet business objectives)

Accessibility

Foundation models that cost hundreds of millions of dollars to train are available to any AWS customer through an API, alongside ready-made applications for non-technical staff. A small company gets the same models as a global bank, and business users can use AI through Amazon Quick without engineering help.

Lower barrier to entry

No need to buy GPUs, hire a research team, collect training data or train models. A developer can call a model with a few lines of code; managed features (Knowledge Bases, Guardrails, Evaluations) replace components that teams would otherwise build themselves. Pay-as-you-go pricing means no large upfront investment to experiment.

Efficiency

AWS runs, scales, patches and secures the infrastructure, so teams spend their time on the application rather than operations. Integration with the rest of AWS (S3 for data, IAM for access, CloudWatch for monitoring, Lambda for logic) avoids building connections from scratch.

Cost-effectiveness

Pay only for what you use (per token or per request), with no idle hardware; choose from many models at different price points and switch to cheaper ones as they appear; and cut costs further with batch inference, the Flex tier, prompt caching and distillation. For steady, high volume, Provisioned Throughput and AWS's own AI chips (Trainium for training, Inferentia for inference) lower cost further.

Speed to market

A prototype in days rather than months: pick a model in the playground, connect documents to a knowledge base, add guardrails, deploy. Managed services remove the slowest steps (infrastructure, model training, building retrieval and safety components), so the business sees results and can learn from real users sooner.

Ability to meet business objectives

A broad choice of models and customization options lets the solution fit the requirement, whether lowest cost, highest accuracy, lowest latency or a specific Region, rather than the requirement bending to one model. Built-in security, compliance and governance features let regulated businesses use generative AI, and evaluations and monitoring let them prove it delivers.

Benefits of AWS infrastructure

Skill 2.3.3Describe the benefits of AWS infrastructure for GenAI applications (for example, security, compliance, responsibility, safety)

Security

Generative AI applications handle sensitive prompts, documents and outputs. On AWS:

  • Data privacy: on Amazon Bedrock, your prompts and outputs aren't used to train the base models and aren't shared with model providers; when you fine-tune, you get a private copy of the model.
  • Encryption: data is encrypted in transit (TLS) and at rest, with keys you can manage in AWS Key Management Service (KMS).
  • Access control: AWS Identity and Access Management (IAM) policies control who can call which models and features.
  • Network isolation: AWS PrivateLink lets applications in your Amazon VPC reach Bedrock without going over the public internet.
  • Auditing: AWS CloudTrail records API calls; model invocation logging can record prompts and responses.

Compliance

AWS services are assessed against many standards and regulations, for example SOC reports, ISO 27001, PCI DSS, HIPAA eligibility for health data, FedRAMP for US government, and GDPR support. AWS also holds ISO/IEC 42001 certification, the international standard for AI management systems, for a specified set of its AI services. Customers download the audit reports and certificates from AWS Artifact to support their own compliance. Choosing the Region (and geographic cross-Region inference profiles that keep processing within, for example, the EU) supports data residency rules.

Responsibility

Two meanings, both relevant:

  • The shared responsibility model: AWS is responsible for security of the cloud (data centers, hardware, the managed service and the models' hosting); the customer is responsible for security in the cloud (who has access, what data they send, how prompts and outputs are used, configuring guardrails, encryption choices). The more managed the service, the more AWS takes on.
  • Responsible AI: AWS commits to developing AI responsibly (fairness, explainability, privacy, safety, controllability, veracity, governance, transparency), publishes AI Service Cards documenting intended uses and limitations of its AI services, and provides tools for customers to build responsibly (Guardrails, Evaluations, SageMaker Model Cards).

Safety

Built-in protections help keep applications from producing harmful output: Bedrock Guardrails filters hate, insults, sexual content, violence, misconduct and prompt attacks, blocks denied topics, masks personal data and checks answers against sources; Amazon Nova models include built-in safety measures and watermarking of generated images; Evaluations tests models for toxicity and harmful content before launch.

Global infrastructure Extra

AWS runs in many Regions, each with multiple Availability Zones, giving reliability and the ability to place applications near users and within required jurisdictions. Purpose-built chips (AWS Trainium and AWS Inferentia) and GPU instances on Amazon EC2 provide the compute for training and inference.

"Who is responsible for the data a company sends to Bedrock and how it is used?" The customer. "Who is responsible for the physical security and patching of Bedrock's infrastructure?" AWS. "Where do you get AWS's compliance reports?" AWS Artifact.

Cost trade-offs

Skill 2.3.4Describe cost tradeoffs of AWS GenAI services (for example, responsiveness, availability, redundancy, performance, regional coverage, token-based pricing, provision throughput, custom models)

Every choice that makes a GenAI application faster, more available or more tailored usually costs more. The exam expects you to recognize which pricing option fits which need.

The pricing options on Amazon Bedrock

On-demand, token-based pricing

Pay per input and output token, with no commitment. Ideal for getting started, variable or unpredictable traffic, and most applications. You share capacity with other customers, so throughput is limited by account quotas (tokens and requests per minute), and very high steady volume can become expensive.

Service tiers

For on-demand requests you can choose how they are served: Priority (fastest responses, served ahead of other traffic, at a premium), Standard (the default) or Flex (a discount for work that can tolerate longer processing, such as evaluations and summarization). A Reserved tier lets customers reserve token capacity for one or three months through their AWS account team.

Batch inference

Submit a file of prompts and get results later, at about 50% less than on-demand prices for supported models. The trade-off is responsiveness: jobs can take hours. Right for bulk work such as tagging a catalog or summarizing archives.

Provisioned Throughput

Buy dedicated capacity in model units, each giving a set number of tokens per minute, billed by the hour, with no commitment or discounted one-month and six-month commitments. You pay whether or not you use it. Right for high, steady, predictable volume that needs guaranteed throughput, and the way to serve many custom models.

Prompt caching

Repeated prompt prefixes (long instructions, documents, tool definitions) are cached for a few minutes, and cache reads are billed at a lower rate and return faster. It reduces cost and latency for applications that resend the same context.

The trade-offs the exam names

Responsiveness

Faster answers cost more: larger capacity, the Priority tier, latency-optimized inference, or a larger always-on endpoint. Accepting slower responses (Flex, batch) cuts cost. Choose based on whether a person is waiting.

Availability

Guaranteeing that capacity is there when needed (Provisioned Throughput, Reserved tier) costs more than sharing on-demand capacity, where peak demand can hit quota limits and throttling. Cross-Region inference raises available throughput by routing requests to other Regions with spare capacity, at no extra charge; global profiles even reduce price slightly.

Redundancy

Running in multiple Regions or keeping fallback models protects against outages but multiplies some costs: duplicated knowledge bases and vector stores, provisioned capacity in a second Region, extra data transfer. Decide based on how critical the application is.

Performance

Larger, more capable models give better quality but cost more per token and respond more slowly. Fine-tuning or distilling a smaller model can give similar quality on a specific task at lower inference cost, in exchange for the customization cost. Measure whether the quality difference is worth the price difference.

Regional coverage

Not every model is available in every Region, and prices can differ between Regions. Data residency rules may force a specific Region with fewer models or higher prices. Geographic cross-Region inference profiles (for example, US or EU) spread load across Regions within one geography while keeping data in it.

Token-based pricing

Cost scales with usage: longer prompts, more retrieved context, longer answers and more agent steps all raise the bill, and output tokens cost more than input. Control it with model choice, concise prompts, fewer retrieved chunks, output limits, caching and batch.

Provisioned Throughput

Predictable cost and guaranteed capacity, but you pay for every hour whether used or not, and longer commitments lower the hourly rate but lock you in. Only worth it when usage is high and steady enough to keep the capacity busy.

Custom models

Customizing a model adds costs at three points: training (charged per token processed during fine-tuning, multiplied by the number of passes over the data), storage of the custom model (a monthly fee), and inference. Many custom models need Provisioned Throughput to serve them; some newer Bedrock models support on-demand, pay-per-token deployment of custom versions, and imported models are billed per minute of active compute, scaling to zero when idle. Before customizing, check whether prompt engineering or RAG would meet the need at far lower cost.

Choosing the option

A startup's new assistant with unknown, spiky traffic

On-demand token pricing.

Summarizing 2 million archived support tickets this month

Batch inference.

A high-volume production chatbot with steady traffic and a fine-tuned model

Provisioned Throughput (or on-demand custom model deployment if the model supports it).

Requests failing with throttling at peak times

Cross-Region inference to reach more capacity, or Provisioned Throughput for guaranteed capacity.

Provisioned Throughput isn't a discount for occasional use. It charges by the hour for reserved capacity; for low or unpredictable traffic, on-demand is cheaper.

Key takeaways

  • Bedrock: serverless API to many foundation models, plus Knowledge Bases, Guardrails, Prompt Management, Flows, Evaluations, customization and flexible inference. Bedrock Agents is now Agents Classic, closed to new customers.
  • SageMaker AI and JumpStart: train, fine-tune and host models yourself on instances you choose. Quick: AI for business users over company data. Kiro: spec-driven AI development, successor to Q Developer.
  • Strands Agents: open-source, model-driven SDK to build agents. AgentCore: run any framework's agents in production with Runtime, Memory, Gateway, Identity, Code Interpreter, Browser, Observability, Evaluations and Policy.
  • AWS GenAI advantages: accessibility, lower barrier to entry, efficiency, cost-effectiveness, speed to market, meeting business objectives.
  • AWS infrastructure: security (data not used for training, KMS, IAM, PrivateLink, CloudTrail), compliance (certifications including ISO/IEC 42001, AWS Artifact, Region choice), shared responsibility and responsible AI, safety (Guardrails).
  • Cost trade-offs: faster, guaranteed, redundant or customized costs more. On-demand for variable traffic, batch for bulk, Flex for work that can wait, Priority for speed, Provisioned Throughput for steady high volume and custom models, cross-Region inference for throughput.

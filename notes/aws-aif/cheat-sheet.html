The exam

AIF-C01 at a glance
Questions
65: 50 scored and 15 unscored (not identified). Multiple choice, multiple response, ordering and matching.
Scoring
Scaled 100 to 1,000; pass at 700. Compensatory: you don't need to pass each domain. No penalty for guessing.
Domains
1 AI and ML 20% · 2 GenAI 24% · 3 Foundation models 28% · 4 Responsible AI 14% · 5 Security, compliance and governance 14%
Candidate
Up to 6 months of exposure to AI/ML on AWS; uses, but doesn't necessarily build, AI solutions.
Out of scope
Coding models, data or feature engineering, hyperparameter tuning, building pipelines, math and statistics, implementing security protocols or governance frameworks.

Domain 1: Fundamentals of AI and ML

Kinds of AI (Task 1.1)

TermWhat it isClue words
AIAny technique for intelligent behavior, including rules"Rule-based", "if-then", "deterministic logic"
Machine learningLearns patterns from data instead of written rules"Learns from historical data", "predicts"
Deep learningML with many-layered neural networks; learns its own features"Images, speech, unstructured", "neural network"
Generative AICreates new content with foundation models"Generate", "prompt", "foundation model"
Agentic AIA foundation model with goals, tools, memory and a loop, so it acts"Plans steps", "calls APIs", "takes actions", "multi-step"
  • Algorithm is the method; model is the trained result; training adjusts parameters; inference uses the model on new data (the running cost).
  • Overfitting: high training accuracy, low test accuracy (high variance). Underfitting: low on both (high bias).
  • Bias in fairness questions means unfair outcomes for groups; in "bias and variance" questions it means an over-simple model.

TypeHow it worksClue words

Data and learning types (Task 1.1)

  • Labeled data has answers (supervised learning); unlabeled doesn't (unsupervised learning, FM pre-training).
  • Structured: rows and columns (tabular, time-series). Unstructured: text, images, audio, video. Time-series must be split by time, never shuffled.
  • Supervised: classification (category) and regression (number). Unsupervised: clustering, anomaly detection, dimensionality reduction. Reinforcement: an agent learns a policy from rewards; RLHF applies it to LLMs.

Choosing a technique (Task 1.2)

Answer the business wantsTechnique
A number (price, demand, minutes)Regression
A predefined category, with labeled examples (fraud or not, ticket type)Classification, even if the output is a probability
Groups nobody has defined (customer segments)Clustering
Rare unusual items with few labelsAnomaly detection
An exact result from known rules (tax, invoice total)No ML: write the rule
  • Traditional ML for structured data, regulated or explained decisions, millisecond latency, huge volume, consistent output. Foundation models for open-ended language, content and unstructured documents with little labeled data.

Lifecycle and metrics (Task 1.3)

  1. Order of the ML pipelineBusiness goal, data collection, EDA, pre-processing, feature engineering, training, hyperparameter tuning, evaluation, deployment, monitoring, re-training.
  • Managed API (Bedrock, AI services): no infrastructure, pay per use. Self-hosted (SageMaker AI endpoint, EC2, EKS, ECS): control and any model, you pay for capacity.
  • SageMaker AI features: Canvas (no-code, includes Data Wrangler), Feature Store, automatic model tuning, MLflow (experiments), Model Registry (versions, approval), Model Cards (documentation), Pipelines (MLOps), JumpStart (pre-trained models).
  • MLOps: experiment tracking, versioned repeatable pipelines, scalability, technical-debt control, production readiness, drift monitoring, triggered re-training.
  • Kiro builds the application code (spec-driven); Amazon Quick serves business users (dashboards, research, agents).

Domain 2: Fundamentals of GenAI

Core concepts (Task 2.1)

TermIn one line
TokenThe unit models read, write and bill; about 4 characters or 0.75 words in English
Context windowMaximum tokens of prompt plus response per request
ChunkingSplitting documents into passages for retrieval (fixed-size, hierarchical, semantic)
EmbeddingA vector capturing meaning; similar meaning, similar vectors; searched in vector databases
TransformerArchitecture using self-attention over the whole sequence; behind LLMs
Foundation modelHuge model pre-trained on broad data, adaptable to many tasks
Multimodal modelTakes or produces several data types (text, image, audio, video)
Diffusion modelGenerates images by removing noise step by step
  • FM lifecycle: data selection, model selection, pre-training, fine-tuning, evaluation, deployment, feedback.
  • Token pricing: input and output priced separately, output higher; retrieved context and history often dominate input. Latency grows with output length.
  • Cut token cost: smaller model, less context, max tokens, prompt caching (5-minute default lifetime), batch (about 50% off), Flex tier, Provisioned Throughput for steady volume, distillation.
  • Context engineering: deciding what enters the context window (instructions, history, retrieved data, memory, tools); techniques select, compress, isolate, externalize.

Capabilities and limitations (Task 2.2)

  • Advantages: adaptability, responsiveness, conversation, content generation.
  • Disadvantages: hallucinations (fix with RAG and grounding checks), interpretability, inaccuracy, nondeterminism (lower temperature).
  • Model selection: type, performance, capabilities, constraints, compliance, cost, latency, complexity. Pick the smallest model that meets the bar.
  • Business metrics: cross-domain performance, ROI, efficiency, conversion rate, ARPU, accuracy, customer lifetime value.

Customization, cheapest first (Task 3.1)

  1. In-context learningPrompts and examples; no training; more tokens per call.
  2. RAGAdds private, changing knowledge with citations.
  3. Fine-tuningLabeled examples change behavior and style; training, storage and hosting costs.
  4. DistillationLarge teacher trains small student: cheaper inference at scale.
  5. Continued pre-training / pre-trainingUnlabeled domain data / from scratch; most expensive.

Prompting (Task 3.2)

  • Parts: instruction, context (and role), input data, output format, negative prompts.
  • Techniques: zero-shot (no examples), single-shot (one), few-shot (several), chain-of-thought (step by step, for logic and math), templates (reusable with variables).
  • Attacks: exposure (leaking prompts or data), poisoning (bad training or knowledge-base data), hijacking (prompt injection, direct or hidden in content), jailbreaking (bypassing safety).
  • Bedrock Prompt Management: templates with {{variables}}, variants to compare, draft versus immutable numbered versions for production and rollback.

Training (Task 3.3)

  • Instruction tuning (instruction-response pairs), domain adaptation, transfer learning (reuse learned knowledge), continued pre-training (unlabeled domain text).
  • Data: curated, governed (rights, privacy, lineage), right-sized, consistently labeled, representative. RLHF: humans rank answers, a reward model learns preferences, the model is optimized toward them.

Evaluation (Task 3.4)

MetricMeasuresUsed for
ROUGERecall of reference words (n-grams, longest sequence)Summarization
BLEUPrecision of n-grams, with a brevity penaltyTranslation
BERTScoreSemantic similarity using embeddingsMeaning, paraphrases
LLM-as-a-judgeA model scores outputs against a rubricOpen-ended outputs at scale
  • Approaches: human-in-the-loop, benchmark datasets, Amazon Bedrock Evaluations (automatic, human, LLM-as-a-judge, RAG).
  • RAG: retrieval (context relevance, coverage) and generation (faithfulness, correctness, citations). Agents: goal success, tool selection and parameter accuracy, efficiency, safety, from traces (AgentCore Evaluations).
  • Business alignment: task completion rate, user satisfaction, cost per interaction; also productivity and engagement.

Domain 4: Responsible AI

Features and tools (Task 4.1)

  • Features: bias, fairness, inclusivity, robustness, safety, veracity. AWS dimensions: fairness, explainability, privacy and security, safety, controllability, veracity and robustness, governance, transparency.
  • Datasets: inclusive, diverse, curated, balanced. Detect bias: label quality analysis, human audits, subgroup analysis.
  • Legal risks: IP infringement (AWS indemnifies outputs of GA Nova models), biased outputs, loss of trust, end-user harm, hallucinations.
  • Sustainable selection: smallest adequate model, reuse over retraining, distillation, Inferentia and Trainium, batching and caching.
Bedrock Guardrails policyStops
Content filtersHate, insults, sexual, violence, misconduct, prompt attacks
Denied topicsSubjects the app must not discuss
Word filtersSpecific words, profanity
Sensitive information filtersPII (block or mask), custom regex
Contextual grounding checksAnswers not supported by sources or not relevant
Automated Reasoning checksAnswers that contradict a formal policy (detects)

Transparency (Task 4.2)

  • Transparent: how it was built and tested. Explainable: why this output. Interpretable models (linear, trees) versus black boxes (deep networks, FMs) explained with SHAP, citations, traces.
  • Tools: SageMaker Model Cards (intended use, risk rating, training and evaluation), Bedrock Evaluations, open source models, data documentation, licensing, AWS AI Service Cards.
  • Trade-offs: interpretability versus performance (measure both); transparency versus safety (don't reveal what enables misuse).
  • Human-centered design: disclose AI use, explain in the user's terms, feedback, correction and appeal, human oversight.

Formulas and numbers

Formulas
Accuracy
(TP + TN) ÷ all predictions; misleading on imbalanced data
Precision
TP ÷ (TP + FP): how many flagged items were right
Recall
TP ÷ (TP + FN): how many real positives were caught
F1 score
2 × precision × recall ÷ (precision + recall)
ROI
(benefit − cost) ÷ cost
Conversion rate
Conversions ÷ visitors
ARPU
Revenue ÷ users
Customer lifetime value
Average order value × orders per year × years as a customer
Task completion rate
Completed tasks ÷ attempted tasks
Cost per interaction
Total running cost ÷ interactions
Numbers worth knowing
Tokens
About 4 characters or 0.75 words in English; 1,000 tokens ≈ 750 words
SageMaker AI inference
Real-time 6 MB and 60 s; serverless 4 MB, 60 s, 1–6 GB; asynchronous 1 GB and 1 hour
Bedrock
Batch about 50% cheaper; prompt cache lifetime 5 minutes by default; Provisioned Throughput with no commitment, 1 or 6 months
Guardrails grounding threshold
0 to 0.99; responses below it are blocked

1.8: Personnel security policies and procedures

Managing the risk that comes with people: screening them lawfully before they are trusted, binding them to clear agreements, controlling their access as they join, move and leave, and holding vendors, consultants and contractors to the same standards.

Overview

Objective 1.8

1.8 Contribute to and enforce personnel security policies and procedures. Sub-topics: candidate screening and hiring; employment agreements and policy-driven requirements; onboarding, transfers and termination; vendor, consultant and contractor agreements and controls (for example SLAs).

HR owns most personnel processes; security contributes requirements and enforces the security parts. Account management in detail is on the 5.5 page, and supplier risk on the 1.11 page. The official outline is on isc2.org. This page is general information, not legal or employment advice.

Running example. The retail group: about 4,000 staff, 300 stores in the US and the EU, its own card payment processing, a data center plus two cloud providers, an in-house development team and AI services. It hires 1,500 seasonal staff each autumn and uses about 200 contractors in IT.

Why personnel security matters

Objective 1.8Understanding the people-related risks that personnel controls reduce.

Every control eventually depends on people: someone has the administrator password, approves the payment, or can carry data out of the building. Personnel security reduces the risk of malicious insiders (fraud, theft, sabotage), negligent insiders (mistakes, falling for phishing), and compromised insiders (people coerced or whose credentials are stolen). It works across the whole employment life cycle: before hiring, during employment and after departure.

NIST SP 800-53's personnel security family (PS) sets the controls, all in the low, moderate and high baselines: policy and procedures (PS-1), position risk designation (PS-2), screening (PS-3), termination (PS-4), transfer (PS-5), access agreements (PS-6), external personnel (PS-7), sanctions (PS-8) and security responsibilities in position descriptions (PS-9).

Candidate screening and hiring

Objective 1.8Screening people in proportion to the risk of their role, lawfully and consistently.

Screening starts with the role, not the person. PS-2 requires assigning "a risk designation to all organizational positions" and establishing screening criteria for each; PS-3 requires screening individuals "prior to authorizing access to the system" and rescreening under defined conditions. A store cashier, a payment platform administrator and the CFO warrant different depth of checks.

  • Typical checks: identity and right to work, employment history and references, education and professional certifications, criminal records where lawful and relevant, credit history for roles handling money where lawful, and sanctions lists.
  • Hiring practices that help security: job descriptions that state security responsibilities (PS-9), interviews that test integrity as well as skill, and verification of claimed certifications with the issuer.
  • Rescreening: on promotion to a higher-risk role, periodically for sensitive roles, or after events that raise concern.

Screening is regulated. In the US, when an employer uses a background screening company, the Fair Credit Reporting Act applies (FTC and EEOC guidance):

  1. DisclosureTell the candidate in writing, in a stand-alone document, that a report may be used.
  2. AuthorizationGet written permission before obtaining the report.
  3. Pre-adverse actionBefore rejecting someone because of the report, give them a copy and a summary of their rights.
  4. Adverse action noticeAfter deciding, notify them, name the screening company, and explain their right to dispute.
  5. DisposalSecurely destroy the report when no longer needed.

Anti-discrimination law also applies: the EEOC warns that checks must not be based on protected characteristics, must apply the same standards to everyone, and that excluding people for criminal records must be "job related and consistent with business necessity". In the EU, GDPR Article 10 allows processing of criminal conviction data only under official authority or where EU or member state law authorizes it with safeguards, so criminal checks are far more limited.

Screening is HR and legal's process. Security defines which roles need which checks and why; HR runs the process and legal confirms it is lawful in each country. A security manager who searches a candidate's social media or orders checks directly can break the law and expose the organization.

Employment agreements and policy requirements

Objective 1.8Binding people to clear obligations before access, and designing roles so no one person can misuse them.

PS-6 requires individuals to "sign appropriate access agreements prior to being granted access", and to re-sign when the agreements change. Its examples are "nondisclosure agreements, acceptable use agreements, rules of behavior, and conflict-of-interest agreements". PL-4 requires documented acknowledgment that people "read, understand, and agree to abide by the rules of behavior."

  • Non-disclosure agreement (NDA): protects confidential information and trade secrets during and after employment (objective 1.4); PS-6(3) adds informing people of legally binding post-employment obligations.
  • Acceptable use policy and rules of behavior: how systems and data may be used, including monitoring notice and social media rules (PL-4(1)).
  • Conflict-of-interest declaration: outside business interests, relationships with suppliers, gifts.
  • Intellectual property assignment: work created on the job belongs to the employer.
  • Non-compete and non-solicitation clauses: governed by local law. In the US, the FTC's 2024 rule banning most non-competes never took effect; a court stopped its enforcement, and in February 2026 the FTC formally removed it, so state law still decides.

Policy-driven requirements built into how jobs work:

  • Separation of duties: split critical tasks so one person cannot both perform and approve them, such as creating a supplier and approving payments to it.
  • Least privilege and need-to-know: access granted by role and reviewed.
  • Job rotation: moving people through roles reduces dependence on individuals and exposes hidden problems.
  • Mandatory vacations: US banking examiners describe two consecutive weeks of absence as "highly effective in preventing embezzlements", because someone else does the work in the meantime and irregularities surface.

Onboarding, transfers and termination

Objective 1.8Controlling access and obligations as people join, move and leave.

  1. OnboardingScreening complete and agreements signed before access; accounts, devices and badges issued by role; security awareness training in the first days; a named manager accountable for the new starter's access.
  2. TransferPS-5 requires reviewing and confirming ongoing need for existing access when someone moves; add new role access and remove the old, so privileges do not accumulate.
  3. TerminationPS-4 requires disabling access within a defined time, revoking credentials and authenticators, conducting exit interviews that include security topics, retrieving organizational property, and keeping access to the information the person held.

Termination needs extra care when it is involuntary or high risk: disable access at the moment the person is told, have them escorted, collect badges and devices in the meeting, change shared passwords and keys they knew, and review their recent activity. For friendly departures, a handover period is fine, but access still ends on the last day. Exit interviews are a chance to remind leavers of continuing obligations (NDA, return of data) and to learn about security concerns.

The retail group's seasonal staff are a test of this process: 1,500 accounts are created from HR data in October with an expiry date already set for January, so leavers are disabled automatically even if a store manager forgets to report them.

Sanctions and enforcement

Objective 1.8Applying consequences for policy violations through a fair, formal process.

PS-8 requires "a formal sanctions process for individuals failing to comply" with security and privacy policies, and notifying defined people when a sanctions process starts. Good practice:

  • Consequences are defined in advance in policy and agreements, and proportionate (retraining for a first honest mistake; dismissal for deliberate misuse).
  • Enforcement is consistent at every level; exempting senior staff undermines the whole program.
  • Decisions follow an investigation with evidence, involve HR and legal, and give the person a chance to respond (objective 1.5).
  • Security's role is to provide facts and evidence, not to decide the penalty.

Vendors, consultants and contractors

Objective 1.8Extending personnel security to external people and services through agreements, SLAs and monitoring.

External personnel often have the same access as staff but are hired, managed and released by someone else. PS-7 requires organizations to set personnel security requirements for external providers, require providers to comply with the organization's policies, document the requirements, require providers to notify the organization when their staff with credentials, badges or privileges transfer or leave, and "monitor provider compliance". SA-9 covers external system services the same way: providers must meet the organization's requirements, oversight roles are defined, and compliance is monitored on an ongoing basis.

A service level agreement "defines the specific responsibilities of the service provider and sets the customer expectations" (CNSSI 4009). NIST's SP 800-47 describes it as covering responsibilities, the type of service, expected performance (reliability, quality, response times), reporting, resolution and termination. SA-9's discussion adds that SLAs describe measurable outcomes and remedies for non-compliance, while risk accountability stays with the organization.

Security terms in a managed service contract (retail group)
Screening
Provider staff with access to group systems are screened to the group's standard for that role
Agreements
Each named engineer signs the group's acceptable use and confidentiality terms
Joiners and leavers
Provider notifies the group within 24 hours when any engineer with access leaves or changes role
Service levels
99.9% availability; critical incidents responded to in 15 minutes; RTO 2 hours, RPO 15 minutes; service credits for misses
Incidents
Security incidents affecting group data reported within 24 hours; cooperation with investigations
Audit and evidence
Annual SOC 2 Type 2 report; right to audit; vulnerability scan results shared quarterly
Subcontractors
Same requirements flow down to subcontractors; group approval needed for new ones
Exit
Return and deletion of data, access removal and transition support at contract end

Other useful sources: SP 800-53 SR-8 (notification agreements for supply chain compromises) and SR-3(3) (flowing contract controls down to subcontractors); the EU's DORA Article 30, which lists required clauses in financial entities' ICT contracts, including service levels, data location, access and return of data, incident assistance, audit rights and exit strategies; and CISA's Secure by Demand guide, which suggests security questions to ask suppliers before, during and after procurement.

You can outsource the work, not the accountability. If a contractor's employee steals customer data, regulators and customers still hold the organization responsible. Contracts, SLAs and monitoring are how the organization exercises due care over people it does not employ.

Scenario check

PracticeApplying personnel security to situations like those in exam questions.

Scenario 1: the dismissal meeting
Situation
A database administrator is to be dismissed for misconduct at 3 p.m. today.
Best response
Coordinate with HR so access is disabled as the meeting starts, badges and devices are collected in the meeting, shared credentials the administrator knew are changed, and recent activity is reviewed.
Why
Hostile terminations need access removed at the moment of notice; waiting until the end of the day gives a privileged insider time to cause damage.
Scenario 2: the contractor who left
Situation
An audit finds that 30 accounts belong to contractors who left the provider months ago; the provider never told you.
Best response
Disable the accounts and review their activity; enforce the contract's notification clause, or add one; and add expiry dates and periodic reviews for all external accounts.
Why
PS-7 expects providers to notify transfers and terminations and the organization to monitor compliance; expiry dates and reviews catch what notifications miss.
Scenario 3: the fast hire
Situation
A business unit wants a new payment platform engineer to start on Monday with full access, before background checks finish.
Best response
Allow the start with limited, supervised access to non-sensitive systems, and grant privileged access only after screening and agreements are complete.
Why
PS-3 requires screening before authorizing access, in proportion to the position's risk; a compromise keeps the business moving without skipping the control.

Key takeaways

  • Personnel security addresses malicious, negligent and compromised insiders across the employment life cycle; SP 800-53 PS-1 to PS-9.
  • Screening: designate position risk (PS-2), screen before access and rescreen (PS-3); FCRA disclosure, authorization, pre-adverse and adverse action notices, disposal; EEOC consistency and job-relatedness; GDPR Art. 10 limits criminal checks. HR and legal run it.
  • Agreements before access (PS-6, PL-4): NDA, acceptable use and rules of behavior, conflict of interest, IP assignment; non-competes depend on local law (FTC rule removed 2026).
  • Job design: separation of duties, least privilege, job rotation, mandatory vacations.
  • Joiners, movers, leavers: access only after screening and agreements; review on transfer (PS-5); disable promptly and revoke credentials on termination (PS-4); immediate removal for hostile terminations; exit interviews.
  • Sanctions (PS-8): formal, proportionate, consistent, evidence-based; security supplies facts.
  • External personnel: PS-7 and SA-9 requirements, notification of leavers, monitoring; SLAs define responsibilities, measurable service levels and remedies; flow-down to subcontractors; accountability stays with the organization.

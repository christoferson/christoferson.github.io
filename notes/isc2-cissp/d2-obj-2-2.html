Overview

Objective 2.2

2.2 Establish information and asset handling requirements. Sub-topics: physical; digital/electronic; secure media destruction; data encryption (at rest, in transit/motion).

Classification is on the 2.1 page; retention and remanence on the 2.4 page; cryptographic algorithms on the 3.6 page. Sanitization basics are in CC 5.1. The official outline is on isc2.org.

Running example. The retail group: about 4,000 staff, 300 stores in the US and the EU, its own card payment processing, a data center plus two cloud providers, an in-house development team and AI services. Stores still print daily reports, and backup tapes go to an offsite vault weekly.

Handling requirements by level

Objective 2.2Writing rules for each classification level that people and systems can follow.

Handling requirements are the standards that make classification useful: for each level they say how information is marked, stored, transmitted, shared, printed, used in tools and destroyed. They are usually published as a handling matrix, one row per activity and one column per level, so a person can look up what is allowed without interpreting policy. SP 800-53's media protection family (MP) and access control controls (AC-16 labels, AC-21 information sharing, AC-4 information flow) provide the building blocks.

Handling rules: Restricted (retail group)
Marking
"RESTRICTED" in header and footer; metadata label applied automatically
Paper storage
Locked cabinet in an access-controlled room; clear desk at all times
Electronic storage
Approved encrypted repositories only; no personal devices or personal cloud
Transport
Paper by tracked courier in sealed envelopes; removable media encrypted and logged out and back in
Transmission
Encrypted channels only; email only with message encryption to named recipients
Sharing
Named individuals only; external sharing needs owner approval and an agreement
Printing
Secure print release; collect immediately
AI tools
Not to be entered into any AI service
Destruction
Cross-cut shredding for paper; purge or destroy for media

Rules apply to every copy, including backups, exports, test environments and printouts; a backup must be protected at the same level as the original. They also apply to suppliers through contracts.

Physical handling

Objective 2.2Protecting paper, media and devices as they are marked, stored and moved.

  • Marking: SP 800-53 MP-3 requires marking media with "distribution limitations, handling caveats, and applicable security markings". Labels on folders, drives and tapes let people handle them correctly without opening them.
  • Storage (MP-4): locked cabinets, safes or controlled rooms appropriate to the level; media vaults with environmental protection for backups.
  • Transport (MP-5): protect and control media outside controlled areas, keep accountability (who has it, when it moved), use trusted couriers with tamper-evident packaging, and encrypt digital media so loss in transit is not a breach.
  • Use: clear desk and clear screen rules, secure print release, visitor escorts where sensitive material is visible, and restrictions on photography.
  • Mobile devices and laptops: physical assets that carry data; full-disk encryption, screen locks and procedures for loss.

The retail group's backup tapes travel in locked cases with numbered seals; the courier and the vault both log the seal number, and any mismatch is an incident.

Digital handling

Objective 2.2Enforcing handling rules for electronic information with labels, sharing controls and automation.

  • Machine-readable labels (AC-16) attached "in storage, in process, and/or in transmission", so tools can enforce rules: encryption on save, blocking external sharing, watermarking.
  • Sharing and flow control (AC-21, AC-4): who may share with whom; data loss prevention that identifies, monitors and protects data in use, in motion and at rest.
  • Retention and disposal (SI-12): keep information as long as required and dispose of it securely (SI-12(3)).
  • Test and development data: SI-12(2) points to "de-identification or synthetic data" rather than copies of production; masking replaces values "in a way that does not preserve the analytic utility of the value", while pseudonymization replaces identifiers with pseudonyms. NIST warns that "some de-identified data can sometimes be re-identified".
  • Cloud and SaaS: apply the same rules to cloud storage, collaboration tools and email; a cloud access security broker can enforce them across services.
  • AI tools: the handling matrix says which levels may be used in which approved AI services, and AI outputs inherit the input's classification.

Automate where you can. Handling rules that rely only on people remembering are weak. Default labels, encryption by policy, DLP and blocked uploads make the safe behavior automatic, and leave training to cover judgment calls.

Secure media destruction

Objective 2.2Choosing and recording the right sanitization method for each medium, level and destination.

NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 2 (September 2025) defines sanitization as rendering "access to target data on the media infeasible for a given level of effort", at three levels:

  • Clear: logical techniques that protect against "simple, non-invasive data recovery techniques using the same interface that is available to the user", such as overwriting or a factory reset; for reuse inside the organization.
  • Purge: makes "target data recovery infeasible using state-of-the-art laboratory techniques", including cryptographic erase and the drive's built-in sanitize commands; for media leaving the organization's control.
  • Destroy: purge-level protection that also makes the media unusable for storing data: shredding, disintegrating, pulverizing, melting or incinerating.

Rev. 2 changes worth knowing: it focuses on an enterprise sanitization program rather than technique tables; multi-pass overwrites are no longer needed for clear; degaussing works only on magnetic media and "does not currently constitute a destroy sanitization technique"; overwriting SSDs gives "very little confidentiality protection" because of how flash memory remaps data; and cryptographic erase (destroying the encryption key) is unsafe if copies of the key exist elsewhere. Each sanitized item should get a certificate of sanitization recording the media, method, verification, date and person. SP 800-53 MP-6 requires sanitizing media "prior to disposal, release out of organizational control, or release for reuse."

Paper is destroyed by cross-cut shredding, pulping or incineration. Cloud data cannot be physically destroyed by the customer, so it relies on cryptographic erase and contractual commitments (objective 2.4).

Match the method to the destination. Media staying inside the organization at the same level: clear may be enough. Media leaving the organization (resale, return to a leasing company, recycling): purge or destroy. Highly sensitive media, or media that cannot be purged reliably: destroy. Deleting files or formatting a drive is not sanitization.

Data encryption at rest and in transit

Objective 2.2Using encryption as a handling control for stored and transmitted data, with keys managed separately.

  • At rest (SP 800-53 SC-28, CSF PR.DS-01): full-disk encryption on laptops and servers; database, file or field-level encryption for the most sensitive data; encrypted backups and removable media. NIST notes SC-28 is about "the state of the information", not the type of storage.
  • In transit or in motion (SC-8, PR.DS-02): TLS 1.2 or 1.3 for web and APIs, IPsec or other VPNs between sites, SSH for administration, and encrypted email for sensitive messages. Apply it inside networks too, not only at the edge.
  • Algorithms: AES (128, 192 or 256-bit keys) for data, preferably in an authenticated mode such as GCM; symmetric algorithms are far less exposed to future quantum computers.
  • Keys: the protection is only as good as key management; keep keys separate from the data, ideally in an HSM or key management service, with access control, rotation and recovery (objective 3.6).

Encryption also simplifies other handling rules: a lost encrypted laptop is often not a reportable breach, and encrypted media can be sanitized by cryptographic erase. Data in use is covered on the 2.6 page.

Handling in special situations

Objective 2.2Applying handling rules where they are most often broken: sharing outside, working remotely, losing devices and making mistakes.

  • Sharing with third parties: share only what the recipient needs, under an agreement (NDA or data processing terms), through an approved secure channel (a managed file transfer service or shared workspace with expiring links), not personal email or consumer file-sharing. Record what was shared and when access ends.
  • Remote and hybrid work: printing restricted material at home, working in public places, and family members using work devices are common gaps. Handling rules should say what may be printed at home (often nothing above Internal), require privacy screens and screen locks, and keep work data in managed apps.
  • Lost or stolen devices: report immediately; remotely lock or wipe managed devices; assess whether data was encrypted and whether the incident is notifiable (objective 1.4). Encryption often turns a reportable breach into a non-event.
  • Mishandling incidents: a misdirected email, an open cloud folder, restricted documents left on a printer. Treat them as security incidents: contain (recall, revoke the link, retrieve the paper), assess the data involved and notification duties, and fix the cause (address autocomplete, default sharing settings, secure print).
  • Disposal of consumer data: in the US the FTC Disposal Rule requires "reasonable measures" to dispose of consumer report information, which applies to paper and electronic records alike.
  • Repairs and maintenance: devices sent for repair leave the organization's control; remove or sanitize storage first, or use on-site repair under supervision.

Handling rules work only if people can follow them in the moment. Short, task-based guidance ("sending customer data to a partner: use the secure transfer portal, never email") and tools that make the approved route the easiest one prevent more incidents than long policies.

At the retail group, a buyer emailed a supplier price list to the wrong external address. The recall failed, so the security team contacted the recipient for deletion confirmation, the owner assessed the impact as low, and the company added a warning banner for external recipients on messages labeled Confidential or above.

Scenario check

PracticeApplying handling rules to situations like those in exam questions.

Scenario 1: the leased servers
Situation
Leased servers with SSDs that held customer data are due back to the leasing company next week.
Best answer
Purge the drives (cryptographic erase or the drive's sanitize command, verified) or, if the contract allows, keep and destroy them; record a certificate for each drive.
Why
Media leaving the organization needs purge or destroy; overwriting SSDs gives little protection under SP 800-88 Rev. 2.
Scenario 2: the test database
Situation
Developers ask for a copy of the production customer database to test a new loyalty feature.
Best answer
Provide masked, de-identified or synthetic data instead; if real data is unavoidable, protect the test environment at the production level.
Why
Handling rules follow the data wherever it is copied; SI-12(2) points to de-identified or synthetic test data.
Scenario 3: the degausser
Situation
A team plans to degauss a mixed batch of hard disks and SSDs before sending them for recycling.
Best answer
Degaussing does nothing to SSDs and is not a destroy method; purge the SSDs by cryptographic erase or sanitize commands, or physically destroy all the drives.
Why
Sanitization must suit the medium; degaussing affects only magnetic media.
Scenario 4: printing at home
Situation
A manager working from home wants to print restricted store closure plans to read on paper over the weekend.
Best response
Refuse under the handling rules (no printing above Internal at home); offer a managed device or secure viewing instead, and explain why.
Why
Handling rules apply in every location; paper at home has no secure disposal or physical access control.

Key takeaways

  • Handling matrix: per level, rules for marking, storage, transport, transmission, sharing, printing, AI use and destruction; applies to every copy, including backups and suppliers.
  • Physical: marking (MP-3), secure storage (MP-4), controlled and accountable transport (MP-5), clear desk, secure printing, device encryption.
  • Digital: machine-readable labels (AC-16), sharing and flow control (AC-21, AC-4, DLP), retention (SI-12), masked or synthetic test data, cloud and AI rules; automate.
  • Destruction (SP 800-88 Rev. 2): clear, purge, destroy by medium and destination; no multi-pass needed; degaussing is not destroy; SSD overwrites are weak; cryptographic erase only if no key copies; certificates; MP-6.
  • Encryption: at rest (SC-28) and in transit (SC-8), AES and TLS, keys separate and managed; it reduces breach impact and enables cryptographic erase.
